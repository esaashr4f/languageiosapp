## The idea

You now have every piece of the script: consonants, vowel points, vowel letters, shewa, dagesh, and the marks around them. This lesson puts the transliteration together as one system and uses it to read the first three verses of the Bible aloud.

A **transliteration** writes one script in the letters of another, sign by sign. It is not a pronunciation guide in disguise. The scheme used here, the academic style of the *SBL Handbook of Style*, is designed to be **reversible**: from *bərēʾšîṯ* you can rebuild בְּרֵאשִׁית exactly, letter by letter and point by point. That is why it uses so many marks. Each one records something on the page:

- a line under or over b g d k p t (ḇ ḡ ḏ ḵ p̄ ṯ) records a letter without dagesh, the soft sound;
- a doubled letter records dagesh forte;
- a macron (ā ē ō) records qameṣ, ṣere or ḥolem written without a vowel letter;
- a circumflex (â ê î ô û) records a vowel written with a vowel letter;
- ə records vocal shewa, and ă ĕ ŏ the three hatef vowels;
- ʾ and ʿ record alef and ʿayin, which are consonants.

What the scheme does not record is equally important. It does not mark stress (this guide adds an acute only in part 1 and in notes). It does not show the accents. And it uses the school values for the vowels, not the Tiberian ones: *ā* stands for qameṣ, which the Tiberian reading said as open o. When you read aloud, read the signs, not the Latin letters: qameṣ as [ɔ], waw as [v], resh as a uvular r.

Two decisions are made for you by the transliteration. Qameṣ ḥaṭuf is written *o* (lesson 6). And where a word has a ketiv and a qere, the transliteration gives the qere (lesson 9).

## How it is built

To transliterate a pointed word, work through it from the right, one consonant at a time.

1. Write the consonant. For b g d k p t, check the dot: with dagesh, plain letter; without, soft letter.
2. If the dot is dagesh forte (a vowel comes before), write the consonant twice.
3. Write its vowel. Is there a vowel letter after it (ו, י, final ה)? Use the circumflex form, and skip the vowel letter. Otherwise use the plain or macron form.
4. For shewa, decide vocal (ə) or silent (nothing), by lesson 5's rules. For qameṣ, decide ā or o, by lesson 6's.
5. Join words with maqqef by a hyphen. Separate other words by spaces.

To read a verse aloud, add three steps.

6. Divide each word into syllables and find the stress (lessons 6 and 8; in a printed Bible the accent shows it).
7. Find the pauses with the accents: a small break at atnaḥ, a full stop at sof pasuq (lesson 9).
8. Say each sign with its Tiberian value (lessons 2 and 3).

### Reading Genesis 1:1–3

Here are the first three verses. The first two have been glossed before; this time the aim is to read them.

:::example
orig: בְּרֵאשִׁית בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים אֵת הַשָּׁמַיִם וְאֵת הָאָרֶץ׃
translit: bərēʾšîṯ bārāʾ ʾĕlōhîm ʾēṯ haššāmayim wəʾēṯ hāʾāreṣ
morphemes: bə=rēʾšîṯ bārāʾ ʾĕlōhîm ʾēṯ ha=ššāmayim wə=ʾēṯ hā=ʾāreṣ
gloss: in=beginning create.QTL.3SG.M God OBJ DEF=heavens and=OBJ DEF=earth
translation: In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.
note: Preview. Stresses: bərēʾšî́ṯ bārā́ʾ ʾĕlōhî́m ʾḗṯ haššāmáyim wəʾḗṯ hāʾā́reṣ. Khan's reconstruction of the Tiberian reading, slightly simplified: [baʀeːˈʃiːiθ bɔːˈʀɔː ʔɛloːˈhiːim ˈʔeːeθ haʃʃɔːˈmaːjim veˈʔeːeθ hɔːˈʔɔːʀɛsˁ].
source: Genesis 1:1 (WLC); khan2020 §I.5.4.1
confidence: attested
preview: QTL, 3, SG, M, OBJ, DEF
:::

Look at what the Tiberian transcription shows. The vocal shewa of *bə-* is an [a], its default. The shewa of *wə-* before alef copies the next vowel and comes out [e]. Stressed vowels are long, so *haššāmáyim* has a long [aː] though it is written with pataḥ. A long vowel in a closed syllable takes a short echo before the final consonant: [ʔeːeθ], [ʔoːoʀ]. Qameṣ is [ɔ] throughout. And the final word, *hāʾā́reṣ*, carries silluq and the pause.

## Watch out

**Transliteration is not pronunciation.** *Bārāʾ* looks like "ba-ra" but the Tiberian reading said [bɔːˈʀɔː]. Read from the signs.

**The same word can look different in transliteration.** *Təhôm* and *ṯəhôm*, *kēn* and *ḵēn*, are one word each. The soft letter depends on what stands before the word in the verse. Look words up by their dictionary form.

**ê is ambiguous.** Ṣere with yod and segol with yod are both *ê*. It is the one place where the transliteration does not let you rebuild the pointing; the Hebrew settles it.

**Familiar names stay familiar in prose.** The guide writes *Torah*, *Shema*, *Genesis* in running English, and *tôrâ*, *šəmaʿ* only in examples and new words.

## Connections

English Bibles keep the old transliterations of many of these words through Greek and Latin: *Genesis* is the Greek title, but Jewish tradition names the book from its first word, *Bereshit*.

## Exercises

1. Transliterate הַשָּׁמַיִם and explain each mark you used.
2. Write in Hebrew letters (with points) the word transliterated *ʾôr*.
3. Transliterate וַיְהִי־אוֹר and mark the stress.
4. Why is the taw of תְּהוֹם written *t* in the dictionary but *ṯ* in Genesis 1:2?
5. In Genesis 1:1, which word carries the main pause of the verse, and which the final pause?
6. A student transliterates בָּרָא as *barā*. Name two errors.
7. Read Genesis 1:3 aloud in the Tiberian manner, and say how waw, the doubled yod and the vocal shewa sound in it.

## Worked answers

1. *haššāmayim*. h for he; a for pataḥ; šš for shin with dagesh forte (a vowel comes before the dot, so it doubles); ā for qameṣ in an open syllable; m; a for pataḥ; y for yod with its own vowel; i for ḥireq; m for final mem.
2. אוֹר: alef, waw with ḥolem dot, resh.
3. *wayhî-ʾôr*, stressed *wayhî-ʾṓr*: maqqef makes the two one unit, and only the second word keeps its stress.
4. Standing alone, the word begins with a dagesh lene and the taw is hard, *təhôm*. In Genesis 1:2 the word before, *pənê*, ends in a vowel and is closely joined to it, so the taw is soft and has no dot: *ṯəhôm*.
5. The main pause is atnaḥ on *ʾĕlōhîm*; the final pause is silluq on *hāʾāreṣ*.
6. The first vowel is qameṣ, written *ā*, not *a*. And the final alef must be written, *ʾ*: *bārāʾ*.
7. [vaɟˈɟoːmɛʀ ʔɛloːˈhiːim jiˈhiː ˈʔoːoʀ vajhiː-ˈʔoːoʀ]. Waw is [v] in *wayyōʾmer* and *wayhî*. The doubled yod of *wayyōʾmer* is a stop, [ɟɟ]. The vocal shewa of *yəhî* is [i]: before a guttural (here h) it copies the vowel that follows.
