## The idea

A **preposition** is a word that sets a noun in relation to something else: "*in* the land", "*to* the king", "*like* a lion", "*from* Haran". Hebrew has many, and most are ordinary words: *ʿal* "on", *bên* "between", *ʿim* "with". Four of the commonest, though, have shrunk to a single consonant written on the front of the noun, like the article and "and" (Gesenius §102a, c):

- *bə-* "in, at; with, by means of";
- *lə-* "to, for; belonging to";
- *kə-* "like, as; about, according to";
- *min* "from, out of; than", usually reduced to *mi-* with doubling.

Gesenius says the shewa of *bə-*, *lə-*, *kə-* is the weakened remnant of an original short *a* (§102d). That *a* comes back when the article follows, and that is why "in the land" is *bāʾāreṣ*, not *\*bəhāʾāreṣ*. *Min* is different. It is a full word that is still written separately in some places, and its *n* assimilates to the next consonant, just as the *l* of Arabic *al-* does before the sun letters: *min* + *bêṯ* → *mibbêṯ* (Gesenius §102a).

These prefixes are glossed with the English preposition, joined by `=`: *bə=rēʾšîṯ* `in=beginning`. When the article has merged into the prefix, one piece carries both: *bā=ʾāreṣ* `in.DEF=land`.

## How it is built

### bə-, lə-, kə-

The three behave alike. Look at the start of the noun (Gesenius §102d–i).

1. **Before a consonant with a full vowel: shewa.** *lə=śārâ* "to Sarah", *kə=ʿammî* "like my people".
2. **Before a consonant with shewa: ḥireq.** Two shewas cannot open a word (lesson 1.5), so the old vowel returns as *i*: *li=šmûʾēl* "to Samuel" (§102d). Before *yə*, the *i* merges with the yod: *bə* + *yərûšālayim* → *bîrûšālayim* "in Jerusalem".
3. **Before a ḥaṭep̄ vowel: that vowel in full.** *ka=ʾărî* "like a lion" (§102d). With *ʾĕlōhîm* the two syllables contract to *ē*: *bēʾlōhîm*, *lēʾlōhîm*, *kēʾlōhîm* (§102d).
4. **Before the divine name: *a*.** The name is read *ʾăḏōnāy*, so the prefix is pointed for that reading: *laYHWH*, read *laʾḏōnāy* (§102m).
5. **Before the article, the *h* drops out and the prefix takes the article's vowel and doubling.** *bə* + *haššāmayim* → *baššāmayim*; *lə* + *hāʾôr* → *lāʾôr*; *bə* + *hehārîm* → *behārîm* (Gesenius §35n). A guttural keeps its effect on the vowel: *lāʾôr*, not *\*laʾʾôr*.
6. **Before a few short words and stressed syllables, *qameṣ*.** *lāḵem* "to you", *lāneṣaḥ* "for ever" (§102f–i). These are learned as they come.

So "to the light" goes: *lə* + *hāʾôr*; the article is there, so drop the *h*, keep its vowel *ā*: *lāʾôr* (Genesis 1:5).

### min

1. **Before the article, keep the full word, joined by maqqef:** *min-hāʾăḏāmâ* "from the ground" (§102b).
2. **Before an ordinary consonant, *mi-* + doubling:** *mibbêṯ* "from the house of", *mimmôlaḏtəḵā* "from your kindred". The doubled letter stays with the noun: *mi=bbêṯ*.
3. **Before a guttural or *r*, which cannot double, *mē-*:** *mēʾereṣ*, *mēḥārān*, *mēʿîr*. The vowel lengthens to make up for the lost doubling, as in the article (§102b).
4. **Before *yə*, usually *mî-*:** *mîḏê* "from the hands of" (§102b).

Before YHWH, *min* is *mē-*, because the alef of *ʾăḏōnāy* is heard after it (Gesenius §102m).

## Where it applies

**bə-**

- **Place, "in, at, on":** *bîrûšālayim*, *bāʾāreṣ* "in the land", *baššāmayim* "in the heavens".
- **Time, "in, on, at":** *bərēʾšîṯ* "in the beginning", *bayyôm* "on the day".
- **Means, "with, by":** "with the sword" is *baḥereḇ*.
- **Material and possession of wealth, "in":** *kāḇēḏ ... bakkesep̄* "rich in silver" (Genesis 13:2, lesson 2.3).

**lə-**

- **Direction, "to, towards":** "to the city" *lāʿîr*. For "to" a place, *ʾel* is commoner; *lə-* is commoner for "to" a person in "say to", "give to".
- **Purpose and benefit, "for":** *ləʾoḵlâ* "for food" (lesson 1.6).
- **Possession, "belonging to", "have":** *ûləlāḇān šətê ḇānôṯ*; *bēn ləśārâ*. This is how Hebrew says "have".
- **Naming:** *wayyiqrāʾ ... lāʾôr yôm*, "called the light Day".
- **"Become"**, with the verb *hāyâ*: "became a living being" is *wayhî ... lənep̄eš ḥayyâ* (Genesis 2:7; part 4).

**kə-**

- **Likeness, "like, as":** *kēʾlōhîm*, *kəʿammî ḵəʿammeḵā*.
- **Approximation, "about":** with numbers, "about ten" (part 5).
- **"As ... so"**, in pairs (1 Kings 22:4).

**min**

- **Origin, "from, out of":** *mēḥārān*, *min-hāʾăḏāmâ*, *mibbêṯ ʾāḇîḵā*.
- **Comparison, "than":** *ṣaddîq ʾattâ mimmennî*. Hebrew adjectives have no "-er" form.
- **"Some of":** *min* can pick out a part of a whole, as in "some of the fruit" (part 5).

**All four** can form the predicate of a verbless clause: location (*mēḥārān ʾănāḥnû*), possession (*ləlāḇān šətê ḇānôṯ*), likeness (*kəʿammî ḵəʿammeḵā*).

## Watch out

**Do not keep the article's *h* after these prefixes.** By analogy with *wəhāʾāreṣ* (lesson 2.3), you might write *\*bəhāʾāreṣ*. The prepositions absorb the article: *bāʾāreṣ*. Only "and" keeps the *h* (Gesenius §35n).

**So the article can be hidden.** *Bəʾereṣ* is "in a land"; *bāʾāreṣ* is "in the land". The vowel of the prefix is the only sign of the article. Read it carefully.

**Do not prefix *min* before the article.** Before the article the usual form is the separate *min-hāʾăḏāmâ* (§102b), not *\*mēhāʾăḏāmâ*. Gesenius notes a few exceptions, so the rule is not absolute, but follow it when you build.

**Not *\*mibḥārān*.** Gutturals cannot double, so *mi-* + ḥ gives *mē-*: *mēḥārān*.

**Not *\*ləʾĕlōhîm*.** The prefix and the ḥaṭep̄ segol contract: *lēʾlōhîm*, *bēʾlōhîm*, *kēʾlōhîm*.

**Do not use a verb for "have".** Spanish *tener* and French *avoir* tempt you to look for a verb, but there is none for plain possession. Hebrew says "to Laban (are) two daughters": *ləlāḇān šətê ḇānôṯ*.

**Do not use *lə-* for every "to".** "To" with verbs of motion towards a place or person is usually *ʾel*; *ʿal* is "on, over". Spanish *a* and French *à* cover all of these; Hebrew splits them.

**Do not take *kə-* for *kî*.** *Kə-* is a prefix "like" on a noun. *Kî* is a separate word, "that, because" (lesson 2.1).

**Do not translate *min* after an adjective as "from".** *Ṣaddîq ʾattâ mimmennî* is not "you are righteous from me" but "more righteous than I".

**Begadkefat after the prefix.** A prefix ends in a vowel, so a following b g d k p t is soft unless it has a dagesh: *ûləlāḇān šətê ḇānôṯ*. The second *kə* in 1 Kings 22:4 is *ḵə* for the same reason: the word before ends in a vowel.

## Connections

Arabic has the same prefixed prepositions: *bi-* "in, with" (*bi-smi llāh* "in the name of God") and *li-* "to, for", and *ka-* "like". Its *min* "from" is a separate word, as Hebrew *min* is before the article. Arabic also says "have" with a preposition: *ʿindī* "with me" = "I have", where Hebrew uses *lə-*.

## Exercises

1. Write "in the land", "to the land" and "from the land".
2. Write "from a city" and "from a house", and explain the difference.
3. Why is "like God" *kēʾlōhîm* and not *kəʾĕlōhîm*?
4. Build "Sarah has a son" as a verbless clause.
5. Translate *mēḥārān ʾănāḥnû* and say which part is the predicate.
6. Translate *ṣaddîq ʾattâ mimmennî*.
7. A student writes *\*bəhaʿîr* for "in the city". Correct it.
8. *Bəʾereṣ* or *bāʾāreṣ*: which is "in the land" and which "in a land"? How can you tell?
9. Write "in Judah" from *yəhûḏâ*.

## Worked answers

1. *bāʾāreṣ*, *lāʾāreṣ*, *min-hāʾāreṣ*. With *bə-* and *lə-*, the article's *h* drops and its *ā* moves to the prefix; the noun keeps its pausal shape *ʾāreṣ* (lesson 2.2). *Min* stays separate before the article (Gesenius §102b).
2. *mēʿîr*: ʿayin cannot double, so *mi-* becomes *mē-*. *mibbayiṯ*: *b* is an ordinary consonant, so *mi-* + doubling, and the doubled *b* is hard.
3. *Kə-* stands before a ḥaṭep̄ segol, and the two contract to *ē* with this word (Gesenius §102d).
4. *bēn ləśārâ* (or *ləśārâ bēn*). There is no verb "have": "to Sarah (is) a son".
5. "We are from Haran." The predicate is *mēḥārān*, the prepositional phrase; *ʾănāḥnû* is the subject, here in its pausal form.
6. "You are more in the right than I." *Min* after an adjective expresses comparison.
7. *bāʿîr*. The article's *h* drops after *bə-*; before ʿayin the article is *hā-*, so the prefix takes *ā*.
8. *Bāʾāreṣ* is "in the land": the qameṣ under the prefix is the article's vowel, and the noun has the pausal shape it always takes after the article. *Bəʾereṣ* is "in a land": plain shewa, no article.
9. *bîhûḏâ*. *Bə* + *yə*: the shewas cannot stand together; the prefix takes *i*, which merges with the yod.
