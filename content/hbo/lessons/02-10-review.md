## Before you start

This review covers part 2: sentences without a verb (lesson 2.1), the article (2.2), "and" (2.3), the personal pronouns (2.4), the prefixed prepositions (2.5), the third-person qatal (2.6), *lōʾ*, *yēš* and *ʾên* (2.7), yes/no questions (2.8) and the object marker (2.9). Part 1 is assumed throughout: every answer expects correct transliteration, including the soft b g d k p t.

Do each exercise before you read its answer. Every answer names the lesson to reread if you went wrong.

## Passage

Jacob has fled from his brother Esau and reached a well in the east, where shepherds are waiting with their flocks. It is the scene in which he first meets Rachel. The passage is attested, Genesis 29:4–6, word for word from the Leningrad Codex.

The narrative verbs *wayyōʾmer* "and he said" and *wayyōʾmərû* "and they said" are taught in part 4; you have met them as words. The other new forms are glossed in the notes.

## Exercises

1. Translate line 1, and name the kind of clause in Jacob's question.
2. Translate line 2. Why does the predicate come first, and why is the preposition *mē-* and not *mi-* with doubling?
3. Translate line 3. Identify the question marker and explain its vowel; then explain *ʾeṯ-*.
4. Translate line 4. How do the shepherds say "yes"?
5. Translate line 5, and say what the clause would be as a statement.
6. Translate line 6. What is *wə-* doing before *hinnēh*, and why is the article on *haṣṣōʾn* written with doubled *ṣ*?
7. In line 3 the object has no article. Why does it still take *ʾēṯ*?
8. Build "We are servants", with the predicate first.
9. Build "Is there a prophet in the city?" and give the answer "No."
10. Build "Laban has a daughter."
11. Build "The men came to the land", using *ʾănāšîm* "men" and *bāʾû*.
12. Build "Rachel did not hear the voice of Jacob."
13. Build "The LORD is holy, and the people are wicked."
14. Find and fix the error: *\*hāʾereṣ ṭôḇâ məʾōḏ* for "the land is very good".
15. Find and fix the error: *\*ʾên yāḏaʿ lāḇān* for "Laban did not know".
16. Find and fix the error: *\*šāmaʿ hāʾîš haqqôl* for "the man heard the voice".
17. Find and fix the error: *\*bəhāʿîr* for "in the city".
18. Find and fix the error: *\*ʾattâ ʾiššâ* said to a woman.
19. Find and fix the error: *\*wəḇāqār* for "and cattle".
20. Explain the difference between *ṭôḇ haddāḇār* and *haddāḇār haṭṭôḇ*.

## Worked answers

1. "Jacob said to them, 'My brothers, where are you from?'" *mēʾayin ʾattem* is a verbless clause: the predicate is a prepositional phrase with a question word, "from where", and the subject is the pronoun *ʾattem* (lessons 2.1, 2.4, 2.5).
2. "They said, 'We are from Haran.'" With a pronoun subject the predicate usually comes first, because it is the new information (lesson 2.4; Gesenius §141n). Ḥet cannot double, so *min* takes the form *mē-* (lesson 2.5).
3. "He said to them, 'Do you know Laban, the son of Nahor?'" *Ha-* is the question marker. It has pataḥ without doubling because the next consonant, *y*, has shewa (lesson 2.8; Gesenius §100l). *ʾeṯ-* marks the definite object, Laban; the maqqef shortens it from *ʾēṯ* (lesson 2.9).
4. "They said, 'We know (him).'" There is no word for "yes"; they repeat the verb of the question, with "you" turned into "we" (lesson 2.8; Gesenius §150n).
5. "He said to them, 'Is he well?'" As a statement, without the marker: *šālôm lô* "he is well", a verbless clause with *lə-* (lessons 2.1, 2.5, 2.8).
6. "They said, 'He is well. And look, Rachel his daughter is coming with the flock.'" *wə-* joins the new clause and *hinnēh* points to what the speakers see (lessons 2.3, 2.1 on *hēn*). The article doubles the next consonant, and *ṣ* is an ordinary consonant, so *ha=ṣṣōʾn* (lesson 2.2).
7. Laban is a name, and a name is definite. *ʾēṯ* goes before any definite object: names, nouns with the article, and nouns made definite in other ways (lesson 2.9; Gesenius §117a).
8. *ʿăḇāḏîm ʾănaḥnû*. Pronoun subject, predicate first, no copula (lessons 2.1, 2.4).
9. *hăyēš nāḇîʾ bāʿîr*. Answer: *ʾayin*. *Hă-* before *y*; *bə-* + the article before ʿayin gives *bā-* (lessons 2.5, 2.7, 2.8).
10. *yēš ləlāḇān baṯ*, or *ləlāḇān baṯ* with no *yēš* (as in Genesis 29:16). Possession is "there is to" (lessons 2.5, 2.7).
11. *bāʾû hāʾănāšîm ʾel-hāʾāreṣ* (or *lāʾāreṣ*). Third person plural qatal, verb first; the article before alef is *hā-*; "the land" is always *hāʾāreṣ* (lessons 2.2, 2.6).
12. *lōʾ šāməʿâ rāḥēl ʾeṯ-qôl yaʿăqōḇ*. *Lōʾ* before the verb; feminine *-â* with the reduced vowel; the object is definite because it is followed by a name, so *ʾeṯ-* (lessons 2.6, 2.7, 2.9).
13. *qāḏôš YHWH wəhāʿām rāšāʿ*. Two verbless clauses joined by *wə-*; the predicate adjectives have no article (lessons 2.1, 2.2, 2.3). *ʿam* is singular in form; Exodus 9:27 uses a plural predicate with "my people", *wəʿammî hārəšāʿîm*, and when a collective noun takes a plural is part 3.
14. *ṭôḇâ hāʾāreṣ məʾōḏ*. With the article the noun always has its pausal vowel, *hāʾāreṣ* (lesson 2.2; Gesenius §35o). The order with the predicate first is the attested one (Numbers 14:7), though subject first is not wrong.
15. *lōʾ yāḏaʿ lāḇān*. *ʾên* negates noun clauses; a verb takes *lōʾ* (lesson 2.7; Gesenius §152a).
16. *šāmaʿ hāʾîš ʾeṯ-haqqôl*. The object is definite, so it needs *ʾeṯ-* (lesson 2.9). Verb before subject is the plain order (lesson 2.6).
17. *bāʿîr*. After *bə-* the article's *h* drops and its vowel moves to the prefix (lesson 2.5; Gesenius §35n).
18. *ʾatt ʾiššâ* (or *ʾiššâ ʾatt*). Speaking to a woman, "you" is *ʾatt* (lesson 2.4).
19. *ûḇāqār*. Before *b*, *m*, *p* the conjunction is *û-*, and the *b* after the vowel is soft (lesson 2.3; Gesenius §104e).
20. *ṭôḇ haddāḇār* is a sentence, "the word is good": the adjective is the predicate and has no article. *Haddāḇār haṭṭôḇ* is a phrase, "the good word": both words are definite, so the adjective describes the noun (lessons 2.1, 2.2; Gesenius §126i; adjectives in part 3).
