## The idea

Lesson 3.5 joined two nouns: *dəḇar YHWH*. Two things remain. First, the bound forms of feminine nouns and of plurals, which have endings of their own. Second, chains of more than two nouns, and adjectives added to a chain.

Both follow from what you know. The bound noun loses its stress to the next word, so its vowels shrink (lesson 3.3). And a chain is definite or indefinite as a whole, depending on its last noun (§127a).

The construct endings are three:

- feminine singular **-aṯ** for absolute *-â*: *tôrâ* → *tôraṯ* "the instruction of";
- masculine plural and dual **-ê** for *-îm* and *-ayim*: *bānîm* → *bənê* "the sons of", *yāḏayim* → *yəḏê* "the hands of";
- feminine plural **-ôṯ**, the same as the absolute: *bānôṯ* → *bənôṯ* "the daughters of".

The feminine *-aṯ* is the old ending kept before a following word (lesson 3.1; §80b). The *-ê* has two sources. In the dual it is a contraction of *-ay*, still visible in *yāḏ-ayim*. Where the plural *-ê* comes from is disputed: Gesenius sets out two views, that *-ay* was borrowed from the dual, or that it was an old collective ending, and leaves the matter open (§89d).

## How it is built

### The feminine singular

Replace *-â* with *-aṯ*. The stress has gone, so a changeable vowel before the ending reduces, and if two shewas would meet, the first becomes a short vowel, usually *i* (§95a):

- *tôrâ* → *tôraṯ*: the *ô* is unchangeable.
- *šānâ* "year" → *šənaṯ*.
- *ʿēḏâ* "congregation" → *ʿăḏaṯ*, with a hatef vowel under the guttural.
- *ṣəḏāqâ* → *ṣiḏqaṯ*: *ṣə-dā-* cannot become *ṣə-də-*; the first shewa returns as *i* and closes the syllable (§95a).
- *malkâ* → *malkaṯ*, *mišpāḥâ* → *mišpaḥaṯ*.
- Some take *-eṯ* in segholate shape: *milḥāmâ* → *milḥemeṯ*, *məlāʾḵâ* "work" → *məleʾḵeṯ* (BDB).

Nouns that already end in *-eṯ*, *-îṯ*, *-ûṯ* do not change: *kəṯōneṯ*, *bərîṯ*, *malḵûṯ*.

### The masculine plural and dual

Replace *-îm* or *-ayim* with *-ê*. Now the stress moves two places, so the pretonic vowel kept in the plural also reduces, and the first vowel returns as a short vowel (§92d):

- *dəḇārîm* → *diḇrê*: *də-ḇā-* would become *də-ḇə-*; the first shewa becomes *i*.
- *zəqēnîm* → *ziqnê* "the elders of"; *ḥăḵāmîm* → *ḥaḵmê*.
- *məlāḵîm* → *malḵê*: segholates show their base (lesson 3.4).
- *ʾănāšîm* → *ʾanšê*; *bānîm* → *bənê*; *yāmîm* → *yəmê*; *šānîm* → *šənê*.
- Unchangeable vowels hold: *sûsîm* → *sûsê*, Gesenius's *sûsê p̄arʿōh* "Pharaoh's horses" (§89c).
- Duals: *yāḏayim* → *yəḏê*, *ʿênayim* → *ʿênê*, *raḡlayim* → *raḡlê*.

### The feminine plural

The ending stays *-ôṯ*; only the vowels before it change: *bānôṯ* → *bənôṯ*, *mišpāḥôṯ* → *mišpəḥôṯ*.

### Longer chains

Every noun except the last is in the construct state. The last is absolute and decides definiteness for the whole chain.

To say "the word of the man of God":

1. "Word", the first noun, is construct: *dəḇar*.
2. "Man", the middle noun, is also bound to what follows: construct *ʾîš* (unchanged).
3. "God", the last noun, is absolute and definite: *hāʾĕlōhîm*.
4. *dəḇar ʾîš-hāʾĕlōhîm*. As an object it takes *ʾeṯ*: *ʾeṯ-dəḇar ʾîš-hāʾĕlōhîm* (1 Kings 13:4).

Gesenius cites chains of four and more, such as *yəmê šənê ḥayyê ʾăḇōṯay* "the days of the years of the life of my fathers" (Genesis 47:9; §128a). He also notes that the language avoids hanging two nouns joined by "and" on one construct noun; it repeats the construct noun instead: *ʾĕlōhê haššāmayim wēʾlōhê hāʾāreṣ* "the God of heaven and the God of the earth" (Genesis 24:3; §128a).

### Adjectives after a chain

An adjective cannot stand inside the chain. It goes at the end, after the last noun, whichever noun it describes. Agreement in gender and number shows which: in *kap̄ hakkōhēn haśśəmāʾlîṯ* "the priest's left palm", the feminine adjective goes with *kap̄* (feminine), not with *kōhēn*. When both nouns have the same gender and number, only sense decides.

The adjective takes the article if the chain is definite, even when the noun it describes has none of its own: *kap̄* has no article, but *haśśəmāʾlîṯ* does, because the chain is definite.

## Where it applies

- **"Sons of", "men of", "daughters of" + a place or people,** for its inhabitants or members: *bənê yiśrāʾēl* "the Israelites", *ʾanšê səḏōm* "the men of Sodom", *bənôṯ hāʾāḏām*.
- **Titles and offices:** *malkaṯ šəḇāʾ*, *ziqnê yiśrāʾēl* "the elders of Israel".
- **The object of a feeling or act:** *yirʾaṯ YHWH* "the fear of the LORD", *tôraṯ YHWH* "the instruction of the LORD".
- **Time:** *bəyôm milḥemeṯ*, *šənaṯ* + a number, *yəmê* + a name: *bîmê ʾaḇrāhām* "in the days of Abraham" (Genesis 26:18).
- **Body parts:** *yəḏê ʿēśāw*, *ʿênê lēʾâ*, *raḡlê* + owner.
- **Groups:** *ʿăḏaṯ yiśrāʾēl* "the congregation of Israel", *mišpəḥōṯ hāʾăḏāmâ*.
- **Every place a two-noun chain applies** (lesson 3.5), now with longer chains.
- **With adjectives:** the adjective goes last and agrees with its own noun; if the chain is definite, the adjective takes the article.
- **Later in the guide:** the plural construct *-ê* reappears before the plural possessive suffixes (lesson 3.8), and the construct before a whole clause (part 6).

## Watch out

**Do not keep *-îm* or *-â* in the construct.** *\*bānîm yiśrāʾēl* and *\*tôrâ YHWH* are not chains. Use *bənê yiśrāʾēl*, *tôraṯ YHWH*.

**Do not add *-ê* to feminine plurals.** *\*bənôṯê hāʾāḏām* is wrong. The feminine plural construct ends in *-ôṯ*: *bənôṯ hāʾāḏām*.

**Do not forget the second reduction.** *\*dəḇārê hāʿām* keeps a vowel that the construct plural loses: *diḇrê hāʿām*. The stress has moved two places (§92d).

**No article inside the chain.** *\*dəḇar hāʾîš hāʾĕlōhîm* breaks the chain in two. Only the last noun takes the article: *dəḇar ʾîš-hāʾĕlōhîm*.

**Do not put the adjective next to its noun.** French *la main gauche du prêtre* puts "left" by "hand". Hebrew cannot: *\*kap̄ haśśəmāʾlîṯ hakkōhēn* is impossible. The adjective follows the last noun: *kap̄ hakkōhēn haśśəmāʾlîṯ*.

**Do not drop the article from the adjective of a definite chain.** *\*kap̄ hakkōhēn śəmāʾlîṯ* would read as a sentence, "the priest's palm is left" (lesson 2.1). A describing adjective matches the definiteness of the chain.

**Agreement may be ambiguous.** In *diḇrê hammeleḵ haṭṭôḇîm* the plural adjective must go with *diḇrê*. But with two singular masculine nouns, an adjective at the end could describe either; read by sense.

**Do not read *šənê* as "years of" every time.** *šənê* is also the construct of the number "two": *šənê hamməʾōrōṯ* "the two lights" (Genesis 1:16). Numbers are part 5.

## Connections

Arabic *iḍāfa* chains work the same way: *bāb bayt al-malik* "the door of the king's house", with only the last noun carrying the article. Arabic also puts the adjective after the whole chain and makes it agree with its own noun. The sound masculine plural even loses its *-na* in the bound form (*muʿallimū l-madrasa* "the teachers of the school"), a shortening much like Hebrew *-îm* → *-ê*.

## Exercises

1. Give the construct forms of *šānâ*, *ʿēḏâ* and *tôrâ*.
2. Give the construct plurals of *dəḇārîm*, *zəqēnîm* and *məlāḵîm*.
3. Build "the daughters of Moab".
4. Build "the elders of the city", and say why the article appears only once.
5. Translate *wəʾēlleh šəmôṯ bənê yiśrāʾēl*. How many nouns are in the chain, and which make it definite?
6. Build "the work of the house of the LORD".
7. Translate *ʿal-kap̄ hakkōhēn haśśəmāʾlîṯ*. How do you know which noun "left" describes?
8. Find and fix the error: *\*hadiḇrê hammeleḵ* "the words of the king".
9. Find and fix the error: *\*bənôṯê yiśrāʾēl* "the daughters of Israel".

## Worked answers

1. *šənaṯ*, *ʿăḏaṯ* (hatef under the guttural), *tôraṯ* (ô unchangeable). The feminine takes its old *-aṯ* before a following noun (Gesenius §§80b, 95a).
2. *diḇrê*, *ziqnê*, *malḵê*. The stress moves two places; *-îm* becomes *-ê*, the pretonic vowel reduces, and the first vowel returns short (§92d). *malḵê* shows the segholate base (lesson 3.4).
3. *bənôṯ môʾāḇ*. The feminine plural keeps *-ôṯ*; the name makes the chain definite.
4. *ziqnê hāʿîr*. The last noun alone takes the article, and that makes the whole chain definite (§127a).
5. "These are the names of the sons of Israel." Three nouns: *šəmôṯ*, *bənê*, *yiśrāʾēl*. Only the last, a name, decides definiteness.
6. *məleʾḵeṯ bêṯ-YHWH*. Two construct nouns, *məleʾḵeṯ* (from *məlāʾḵâ*) and *bêṯ*, then the name.
7. "On the priest's left palm." The adjective is feminine (*-îṯ*); *kap̄* is feminine and *kōhēn* masculine, so it describes *kap̄*.
8. *diḇrê hammeleḵ*. No article on a construct noun (lesson 3.5).
9. *bənôṯ yiśrāʾēl*. The feminine plural construct has no *-ê*.
