## The idea

Hebrew has no separate words for "my", "your", "his". It adds a short ending to the noun: *qôl* "voice", *qôlî* "my voice", *qôlô* "his voice", *qôlām* "their voice". These endings are the **pronoun suffixes**. Each one marks person, number and gender, like the pronouns of lesson 2.4, and is glossed the same way: *qôl-î* `voice-1SG`.

Gesenius explains what a suffix is in grammatical terms: it "stands in a genitive relation" to the noun (§91a, §33c). *qôlî* is "the voice of me". So the suffix behaves like the second noun of a construct chain (lessons 3.5–3.6):

- the noun with a suffix is **definite**, as a chain ending in a definite noun is (§127a). *qôlî* is "my voice", never "a voice of mine";
- it never takes the article: *\*haqqôlî* is impossible;
- as an object it takes *ʾēṯ*: *wayyišmaʿ YHWH ʾeṯ-qōlēnû* "the LORD heard our voice".

Many suffixes are visibly related to the independent pronouns: *-nû* "our" and *ʾănaḥnû* "we", *-ḵem* "your (pl.)" and *ʾattem*, *-hem* "their" and *hēm*. Gesenius describes them as the same pronouns in forms "usually shorter, joined to the end of verbs, nouns, and particles" (§33a).

## How it is built

### The suffixes

After a singular noun that ends in a consonant, the suffixes are (Gesenius §91b):

- **1SG** *-î* "my": *qôlî*
- **2SG.M** *-əḵā* "your" (to a man): *qôləḵā*. In pause, *-eḵā*.
- **2SG.F** *-ēḵ* "your" (to a woman): *qôlēḵ*
- **3SG.M** *-ô* "his, its": *qôlô*. After a vowel, *-hû* or *-w* (lesson 3.12).
- **3SG.F** *-āh* "her, its", with mappiq in the *h*: *qôlāh*
- **1PL** *-ēnû* "our": *qōlēnû*
- **2PL.M** *-əḵem* "your" (pl.): *qôləḵem*; **2PL.F** *-əḵen* (rare)
- **3PL.M** *-ām* "their": *qôlām*; **3PL.F** *-ān* (rare)

The vowels *-ā-*, *-ē-*, *-ə-* before the consonant of the suffix are **connecting vowels**. Gesenius traces them to old vowels of the noun's stem (§91d, §58f). Learn the suffixes with them.

Next to a guttural the shewa of *-əḵā* and *-əḵem* becomes ḥaṭep̄ pataḥ: *rûḥăḵā* "your spirit" (§91d).

### What happens to the noun

The suffix pulls the stress forward, so lesson 3.3 applies. Gesenius divides the suffixes into two groups (§93b).

**Light suffixes:** all except *-ḵem* and *-ḵen*. They move the stress one place. The noun has the same shape as before the plural ending *-îm*:

- *dāḇār* → *dəḇārî*, *dəḇārəḵā*, *dəḇārô*, *dəḇārēnû* (compare *dəḇārîm*);
- *šēm* "name" → *šəmô*, *šəmāh*, *šəmām*;
- *zāqēn* → *zəqēnî* (§93, paradigm II).

**Heavy suffixes:** *-ḵem* and *-ḵen*. They carry their own stress, so the noun has its construct shape: *lēḇāḇ* "heart" → *ləḇaḇḵem* "your heart" (Deuteronomy 11:16), as *dāḇār* → *dəḇar*.

**Segholates** take every suffix on their base (lesson 3.4): *ʾereṣ* → *ʾarṣəḵā*, *ṣelem* "image" → *ṣalmēnû*.

**Geminate nouns** double the last consonant: *ʿam* → *ʿammî* "my people", *ʾēm* → *ʾimmô* "his mother" (lesson 3.3).

**Unchangeable vowels** hold: *qôl* → *qôlî*, *sûs* → *sûsî*.

**Feminine nouns in *-â*** use the old *-aṯ*. Before a light suffix it is *-āṯ-*: *šip̄ḥâ* → *šip̄ḥāṯāh* "her maidservant", *tôrâ* → *tôrāṯî* "my instruction". Before a heavy suffix it is *-aṯ-* (§95). Nouns in *-eṯ* show their segholate base: *môleḏeṯ* → *môlaḏtəḵā* "your kindred". Nouns in *-ûṯ*, *-îṯ* add the suffix directly: *dəmûṯ* "likeness" → *dəmûṯēnû*.

### Building "her son"

1. The noun is *bēn*. The owner is "she": 3SG.F, *-āh*.
2. *bēn* has a changeable ṣere; before a light suffix it reduces: *bən-*.
3. *bənāh* "her son". It is definite, so an adjective with it takes the article: *bənāh haqqāṭān* "her younger son" (Genesis 27:15).

### Words that look like *ʾāḇ*

*ʾāḇ* "father" and *ʾāḥ* "brother" add the suffixes to a form ending in *-î*: *ʾāḇîḵā* "your father", *ʾāḇînû* "our father", *ʾāḥîw* "his brother". Learn these from the examples; lesson 3.12 explains them.

## Where it applies

- **Possession of every kind:** *ʾarṣəḵā*, *bənî*, *šip̄ḥāṯāh*.
- **Family and relations:** *ʾîšāh* "her husband", *ʾištô* "his wife" (lesson 3.12), *bənāh*.
- **Names:** *ûšəmô X* "and his name was X".
- **Polite address:** *ʾăḏōnî* "my lord" to a man (lesson 2.2). The divine title *ʾăḏōnāy* is a plural noun with "my" (§124i; lesson 3.8).
- **A body part shared by many:** the singular with a plural suffix, *ləḇaḇḵem*.
- **In place of a second noun:** wherever a construct chain can stand, a suffix can (lesson 3.5).
- **Later in the guide:** suffixes on plural nouns (lesson 3.8), on prepositions and *ʾēṯ* (lesson 3.9), on verbs as objects (lesson 4.19), and on infinitives as subjects (lesson 4.10).

## Watch out

**No article on a suffixed noun.** *\*haqqôlî* or *\*habbənî* is impossible; the suffix makes it definite. Spanish says *mi hijo* and French *mon fils* without an article too, but Italian *il mio libro* "my book" would mislead you.

**But do put the article on its adjective.** *bənāh haqqāṭān*, not *\*bənāh qāṭān*, which would mean "her son is small" (lesson 2.1).

**Do not confuse *-āh* "her" with *-â* (feminine).** *sûsâ* is "a mare"; *sûsāh* is "her horse" (built by pattern; *sûs* is rare with suffixes). In the script the difference is the dot in the *h*: סוּסָה versus סוּסָהּ.

**Do not add the suffix to the plain segholate.** *\*ʾereṣəḵā* keeps the helping vowel. Use the base: *ʾarṣəḵā*.

**Do not forget the old *t* of feminine nouns.** *\*šip̄ḥâāh* or *\*tôrâî* cannot exist; the suffix goes on *-āṯ-*: *šip̄ḥāṯāh*, *tôrāṯî*.

**Heavy suffixes take the construct shape.** *\*lēḇāḇḵem* keeps a vowel that must shorten: *ləḇaḇḵem*. With *dāḇār* the expected form is *dəḇarḵem*.

**"Your" depends on who is spoken to, not on what is owned.** *-əḵā* to a man, *-ēḵ* to a woman, *-əḵem* to a group. The gender of the noun does not matter, unlike French *son*/*sa*, which agree with the thing owned.

**"His" and "her" depend on the owner too.** *sûsô* "his horse", *sûsāh* "her horse". French *sa jument* is "his or her mare"; Hebrew always says whose.

**Small words change their vowel unpredictably.** *šēm* gives *šəmô* "his name" but *šimḵā* "your name". Such forms are listed in BDB; check before you write them.

## Connections

Arabic attaches the same set of suffixes to nouns: *kitābī* "my book", *kitābuka* / *kitābuki* "your (m./f.) book", *kitābuhu* "his book", *kitābuhā* "her book", *kitābunā* "our book", *kitābuhum* "their book". The likeness is close: Arabic *-nā* and Hebrew *-nû*, *-hā* and *-āh*, *-hum* and *-hem*/*-ām*. As in Hebrew, the suffixed noun is definite and takes no article. Korean and Japanese use separate possessive words (*nae*, *watashi no*); Hebrew, like Arabic, builds the owner into the noun.

## Exercises

1. Add "my", "his" and "our" to *qôl*.
2. Add "your" to *dāḇār*, once for a man and once for a woman.
3. Build "her land" from *ʾereṣ*, and explain the vowels.
4. Build "his maidservant" from *šip̄ḥâ*.
5. Translate *ûšəmô ʾelqānâ*, and say why there is no verb.
6. Why is "your heart" (to a group) *ləḇaḇḵem* and not *lēḇāḇḵem*?
7. Find and fix the error: *\*habbənî haqqāṭān* "my younger son".
8. Translate *haʿôḏ lānû ḥēleq wənaḥălâ bəḇêṯ ʾāḇînû*, and identify both suffixes.
9. Translate *sûsâ* and *sûsāh*, and say how the script tells them apart.

## Worked answers

1. *qôlî*, *qôlô*, *qōlēnû* (or *qôlēnû*). The *ô* is unchangeable; the suffixes are added with their connecting vowels (Gesenius §91b).
2. *dəḇārəḵā* (to a man) and *dəḇārēḵ* (to a woman). Light suffixes: the noun takes its plural shape, *dəḇār-* (§93b).
3. *ʾarṣāh*. *ʾereṣ* is a segholate; the suffix goes on the base *ʾarṣ-*, and the helping vowel disappears (lesson 3.4).
4. *šip̄ḥāṯô*. The feminine ending returns to its old *t*, here *-āṯ-* before a light suffix (§95).
5. "And his name was Elkanah." A verbless clause: subject *šəmô*, predicate the name (lesson 2.1).
6. *-ḵem* is a heavy suffix with its own stress, so the noun takes its construct shape, as *dāḇār* → *dəḇar* (§93b).
7. *bənî haqqāṭān*. The suffix makes the noun definite, so it takes no article; its adjective takes the article.
8. "Do we still have a share or inheritance in our father's house?" *lānû* "to us" (the 1PL suffix on *lə-*, lesson 3.9) and *ʾāḇînû* "our father".
9. *sûsâ* "a mare", with the feminine ending; *sûsāh* "her horse", with the 3SG.F suffix. The suffix has a mappiq dot in the *h* (סוּסָהּ); the ending has none (סוּסָה).
