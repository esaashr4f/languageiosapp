## The idea

"My word" is *dəḇārî*. "My words" is *dəḇāray*. "His word" is *dəḇārô*, "his words" *dəḇārāyw*. A plural or dual noun takes a second set of suffixes, and the difference lies in one sound: a **y**, the remains of a vowel *-ay-*, between the noun and the pronoun.

Where does it come from? The plural construct ends in *-ê* (lesson 3.6), and Gesenius points out that the plural noun before suffixes, as in *sûsay*, matches an older *-ay* (§89d). Lesson 3.6 noted that the source of this *-ay* in the plural is disputed; in the dual it is clearly the *-ay* of *-ayim*. Whatever its origin, the suffix is added to that *-ay-*, and the *y* is written in almost every form. It is the mark you look for: a *y* before the suffix means "more than one thing owned".

The owners are the same as in lesson 3.7: the suffixes still say *whose*. Only the link to the noun changes.

## How it is built

### The suffixes on a masculine plural

Remove *-îm*. Add (Gesenius §91; see the reference table):

- **1SG** *-ay* "my": *dəḇāray* "my words"
- **2SG.M** *-êḵā* (written ־ֶיךָ): *dəḇārêḵā*
- **2SG.F** *-ayiḵ*: *dəḇārayiḵ*
- **3SG.M** *-āyw* (written ־ָיו; the *y* is not pronounced): *dəḇārāyw*
- **3SG.F** *-êhā*: *dəḇārêhā*
- **1PL** *-ênû*: *dəḇārênû*
- **2PL.M** *-êḵem*, **2PL.F** *-êḵen*: *diḇrêḵem*
- **3PL.M** *-êhem*, **3PL.F** *-êhen*: *diḇrêhem*

The transliteration *ê* stands for the written *-e-* or *-ē-* with *y* (lesson 1.10 and the dossier's scheme): ־ֶי and ־ֵי are both *ê*.

### What the noun does

The same two groups as in lesson 3.7 (Gesenius §92b, d; §93b):

1. **Light suffixes** (all but the four *-ḵem*, *-ḵen*, *-hem*, *-hen*): the noun keeps its plural shape. *dəḇārîm* → *dəḇār-ay*, *dəḇār-āyw*. Segholates keep their plural qameṣ: *məlāḵîm* → *məlāḵay* "my kings" (Gesenius's paradigm, §92e).
2. **Heavy suffixes:** the noun takes its construct plural shape, as before *-ê*. *diḇrê* → *diḇrêḵem*, *diḇrêhem*; *malḵê* → *malḵêhem* "their kings" (§92d).

### Feminine plurals

A feminine plural keeps its *-ôṯ* and then adds the same plural set (§91m, §95): *bānôṯ* → *bənōṯay* "my daughters", *bənôṯêḵā* "your daughters", *ʾāḇôṯ* → *ʾăḇōṯênû* "our fathers". So the feminine plural shows plurality twice: once in *-ôṯ*, once in the *y*. Gesenius notes this "double indication of the plural" (§87s).

### Duals

Duals take the plural set: *yāḏayim* → *yāḏay* "my hands", *yāḏêḵā*, *yāḏāyw*, *yāḏêhā*, *yāḏênû*, and with the heavy suffixes *yəḏêḵem*, *yəḏêhem*. *ʿênayim* → *ʿênêḵā* "your eyes".

### Building "her hands"

1. *yāḏ* "hand". Two hands: dual, *yāḏayim*.
2. "Her": a light suffix on a plural or dual, so *-êhā*.
3. The noun keeps its dual shape *yāḏ-*: *yāḏêhā*.

To read a form, work backwards: is there a *y* before the pronoun? Then the noun is plural or dual. Then identify the pronoun from its consonants: *-ḵā*, *-hā*, *-nû*, *-ḵem*, *-hem*, or the bare *-ay*, *-āyw*.

## Where it applies

- **Every plural noun,** masculine or feminine: *bānāyw*, *bənōṯay*, *ʾăḇōṯênû*.
- **Every dual:** *ʿênêḵā*, *yāḏêhā*, *ʾappāyw*.
- **Plural-only nouns,** even with a singular meaning: *pānāyw* "his face", *ḥayyay* "my life", *mêmêḵā* "your water" (lesson 3.12).
- **The plural of majesty:** *ʾĕlōhêḵā* "your God", *ʾăḏōnāyw* "his master" (Gesenius §124i). So "my God" is *ʾĕlōhay*, and the title *ʾăḏōnāy* "the Lord" is in form "my lords" (§124i; lesson 2.1).
- **Collective nouns are singular:** *ṣōʾnî* "my flock", with the singular set (example 1).
- **Prepositions that are old plural nouns** take the same set: *ʾēlāyw* "to him", *ʿālêhā* "on her", *lip̄nêḵem* "before you" (lesson 3.9).
- **Later in the guide:** *hinnēh* and some particles with suffixes (lesson 5.10).

## Watch out

**One letter separates singular and plural.** *dəḇārēnû* is "our word"; *dəḇārênû*, with yod, "our words". In transliteration the difference is *ē* against *ê*. In unpointed or defectively written texts you must read the context.

**Do not pronounce the *y* of *-āyw*.** *dəḇārāyw* is said *dəḇārāw*. The *y* is written to show the plural, not sounded (dossier, transliteration table).

**Do not keep *-îm* before a suffix.** *\*dəḇārîmô* is impossible. The plural marker of the suffixed form is the *-ay-*, not *-îm*.

**Do not drop *-ôṯ* from a feminine plural.** *\*bənay* would be "my sons". "My daughters" is *bənōṯay*: *-ôṯ*, then *-ay*.

**Heavy suffixes need the construct shape.** *\*dəḇārêhem* keeps the pretonic *ā*; the form is *diḇrêhem* (§92d).

**A singular-meaning plural still takes plural suffixes.** By analogy with "his face", *\*pānô* would be natural; Hebrew says *pānāyw*. Likewise *ʾĕlōhāyw* "his God", never *\*ʾĕlōhô*.

**Spanish and French mark the plural on the possessive word** (*mis hijos*, *mes fils*). Hebrew marks it on the noun's link to the suffix. Do not look for the plural in the pronoun: *-ḵā* in *dəḇārêḵā* is the same "your" as in *dəḇārəḵā*.

## Connections

Arabic has no equivalent *-ay-* link: it puts the same suffixes on singular and plural alike (*kitābuhu* "his book", *kutubuhu* "his books"). But the Arabic dual shows the same history as Hebrew: *yadā-hu* or *yaday-hi* "his two hands", with the *-ay* of the dual before the suffix. Hebrew spread that *-ay-* from the dual, or from an old ending, to all its plurals (Gesenius §89d).

## Exercises

1. Give "my words", "his words" and "our words" from *dəḇārîm*.
2. Give "her hands" and "their hands" from *yāḏayim*.
3. Translate *habbānôṯ bənōṯay wəhabbānîm bānay*, and explain why "my" has the same form in both.
4. What is the difference between *bənô* and *bānāyw*?
5. Build "your daughters" (to a man).
6. Why is "their words" *diḇrêhem* and not *dəḇārêhem*?
7. Translate *ʾĕlōhê ʾăḇōṯênû*. Why does *ʾăḇōṯênû* have a *y* although *ʾāḇôṯ* ends in *-ôṯ*?
8. A student writes *\*pānô* for "his face". Correct it and explain.

## Worked answers

1. *dəḇāray*, *dəḇārāyw*, *dəḇārênû*. Light suffixes; the noun keeps its plural shape *dəḇār-* (Gesenius §92b).
2. *yāḏêhā* (light: the dual shape *yāḏ-*), *yəḏêhem* (heavy: the construct shape *yəḏ-*, as in *yəḏê*).
3. "The daughters are my daughters and the sons are my sons." Both nouns are plural, so both take *-ay*; the feminine plural keeps *-ôṯ* before it (§91m).
4. *bənô* "his son": the singular with *-ô*. *bānāyw* "his sons": the plural with *-āyw*.
5. *bənôṯêḵā*. The feminine plural *-ôṯ*, then *-êḵā*.
6. *-hem* is heavy, so the noun has its construct plural shape, as in *diḇrê* (§92d).
7. "The God of our fathers." Suffixes on any plural take the plural set; *ʾāḇ* is masculine with an *-ôṯ* plural, and *-ôṯ* is followed by *-ênû* (§87s, §91m).
8. *pānāyw*. *pānîm* is plural in form, so it takes the plural suffixes, even though it means one face (lesson 3.2).
