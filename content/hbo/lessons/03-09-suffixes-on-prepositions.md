## The idea

"To me", "with him", "from them", "him" as an object: Hebrew says all of these with the suffixes of lessons 3.7 and 3.8 attached to a preposition or to *ʾēṯ*. *lî* "to me", *ʿimmô* "with him", *mēhem* "from them", *ʾōṯô* "him". There are no separate object pronouns like English "me" and "him".

Why the same endings? Gesenius explains that the prepositions "were originally nouns" (§101, §103a). *taḥaṯ* "under" was "the place below", *ʾaḥărê* "after" was "the hinder parts". A preposition with a suffix is therefore a noun with a possessive: *ʾeṣlî* "by me" is properly "at my side", *taḥtām* "instead of them" is "in their place" (§103a). The same holds for *ʾēṯ*, whose suffixed forms *ʾōṯî*, *ʾōṯô* you met in part 2.

This has a consequence you have already seen with nouns. Some prepositions take the singular set of suffixes, some the plural set with its *y*: *lô* "to him" but *ʾēlāyw* "to him (towards him)". Gesenius gives two reasons for the plural set. Some prepositions really are old plural nouns (*lip̄nê*, *ʾaḥărê*). Others, *ʾel*, *ʿal*, *ʿaḏ*, had an old form ending in *-ay*, which looks like a plural (§103n).

## How it is built

### Prepositions with the singular set

These take the suffixes of lesson 3.7, sometimes with a changed vowel (Gesenius §103f–i).

- ***lə-*** "to, for": *lî*, *ləḵā* (in pause *lāḵ*), *lāḵ* (2SG.F), *lô*, *lāh*, *lānû*, *lāḵem*, *lāhem*. Gesenius notes a tendency to "lengthen the preposition, so as to give it more strength" when a short suffix follows (§103e): *lə-* becomes *lā-* in *lānû*, *lāhem*.
- ***bə-*** "in, with": the same pattern: *bî*, *bəḵā*, *bô*, *bāh*, *bānû*, *bāḵem*, *bāhem* or *bām*.
- ***kə-*** "like" adds *-mô-* before the suffix: *kāmônî* "like me", *kāmôḵā*, *kāmōhû* "like him". Gesenius takes the *mô* to be an old word "what" (§103k).
- ***min*** "from" doubles itself: *mimmennî* "from me", *mimməḵā*, *mimmēḵ*, *mimmennû* "from him", *mimmennâ* "from her", *mikkem*, *mēhem*. Gesenius gives two explanations for *mimmen-*: a doubled *min-min-*, or an older *minnen-* (§103m).
- ***ʿim*** "with" doubles its *m*: *ʿimmî*, *ʿimməḵā*, *ʿimmô*, *ʿimmāh*, *ʿimmānû*, *ʿimmāḵem*, *ʿimmām* (§103c).
- ***ʾēṯ*** "with" (the preposition) takes *ʾitt-*: *ʾittî*, *ʾittəḵā*, *ʾittô*, *ʾittānû*, *ʾittām* (§103b).
- ***bên*** "between" has the singular set for one owner: *bênî ûḇênêḵā* "between me and you" (with the plural set on the second; §103o).

### The object marker ʾēṯ

*ʾēṯ* takes *ʾōṯ-* before light suffixes and *ʾeṯ-* before heavy ones (§103b):

- *ʾōṯî* "me", *ʾōṯəḵā* (pause *ʾōṯāḵ*) "you", *ʾōṯāḵ* (2SG.F), *ʾōṯô* "him", *ʾōṯāh* "her", *ʾōṯānû* "us", *ʾeṯḵem* "you (pl.)", *ʾōṯām* "them".

It looks like the preposition *ʾēṯ* "with" but is pointed differently: *ʾōṯô* "him" against *ʾittô* "with him" (§103b). Gesenius explains the *ō* as an original long *ā* obscured to *ō* (§103b).

*ʾōṯô* is used when the object is a pronoun and the verb does not take the pronoun as its own suffix. Verbs can also carry object suffixes directly (*yədāʿāh* "he knew her", lesson 4.19); in prose both are common.

### Prepositions with the plural set

These take the suffixes of lesson 3.8 (Gesenius §103n–o):

- ***ʾel*** "to, towards": *ʾēlay*, *ʾēlêḵā*, *ʾēlāyw*, *ʾēlêhā*, *ʾēlênû*, *ʾălêḵem*, *ʾălêhem*.
- ***ʿal*** "on, over": *ʿālay*, *ʿālêḵā*, *ʿālāyw*, *ʿālêhā*, *ʿālênû*, *ʿălêḵem*, *ʿălêhem*.
- ***ʿaḏ*** "as far as": *ʿāḏay*, *ʿāḏêḵā*.
- ***taḥaṯ*** "under, instead of": *taḥtay*, *taḥtāyw*; ***ʾaḥărê*** "after": *ʾaḥăray*, *ʾaḥărāyw*.
- ***lip̄nê*** "before": *ləp̄ānay*, *ləp̄ānêḵā*, *ləp̄ānāyw*, *lip̄nêḵem*, *lip̄nêhem*: this is *lə-* + *pānîm* "face", "to the face of".

### Building "I am with you"

1. "With": *ʿim* or *ʾēṯ*. Take *ʿim*.
2. "You", a man: 2SG.M, *-əḵā*; *ʿim* doubles its *m*: *ʿimməḵā*. In pause it becomes *ʿimmāḵ* (Genesis 28:15).
3. "I": *ʾānōḵî*. A verbless clause: *ʾānōḵî ʿimməḵā*.

### Building "they hated him"

1. The verb: *wayyiśnəʾû* "and they hated" (the narrative form, part 4).
2. "Him" is a definite object, and a pronoun: *ʾēṯ* + 3SG.M = *ʾōṯô*.
3. *wayyiśnəʾû ʾōṯô*.

## Where it applies

- **Pronoun objects:** *ʾōṯô*, *ʾōṯām*, *ʾōṯî*, wherever the object is "me, you, him, her, us, them" and the verb has no object suffix.
- **Possession with *lə-*:** *yēš lî* "I have", *ʾên lānû* "we have not" (lesson 2.7 with the suffixes).
- **Becoming:** *wattəhî-lô ləʾiššâ*.
- **Company:** *ʿimmô*, *ʾittô*, with no difference in meaning that you need to track in prose.
- **Direction and position:** *ʾēlāyw*, *ʿālāyw*, *taḥtāyw*, *ʾaḥărāyw*, *ləp̄ānāyw*.
- **Comparison:** *kāmōhû*, and *min* "than" with suffixes, *mimmennî* "than I" (lesson 5.5).
- **Reflexive sense:** Hebrew has no word for "himself"; *lô* can mean "for himself": *wayyiqqaḥ-lô* "and he took for himself" (common in narrative).
- **With *hinnēh* and other particles:** *hinnēnî* "here I am" (Genesis 22:1), *ʾênennû* "he is not": lesson 5.10.
- **Later in the guide:** object suffixes on the verb itself (lesson 4.19) and the fuller prepositions (lesson 5.7).

## Watch out

**Do not use the independent pronoun as an object.** *\*wayyiśnəʾû hûʾ* for "they hated him" is wrong. *hûʾ* is the subject form. Use *ʾōṯô*.

**Do not confuse *ʾōṯô* and *ʾittô*.** *ʾōṯô* is "him"; *ʾittô* is "with him" (§103b).

**Do not give *ʾel* and *ʿal* the singular set.** By analogy with *lô*, you might write *\*ʾēlô* or *\*ʿālô*. They take the plural set: *ʾēlāyw*, *ʿālāyw*.

***mimmennû* has two meanings.** "From him" and "from us" are the same form. *lānû* is only "to us", but with *min* you must read the context.

**Pausal forms can look like the other gender.** *ʿimmāḵ* and *lāḵ* are 2SG.F out of pause but 2SG.M in pause (§103e). Check who is addressed.

**Do not translate *lô* as "his" in a noun phrase.** *bēn lô* is "he has a son" or "a son of his"; "his son" is *bənô*. The preposition makes a statement or an indefinite owner; the noun suffix makes a definite possessive (lesson 3.5 on *bēn ləyišay*).

**French and Spanish clitics go before the verb** (*il le hait*, *lo odian*). Hebrew *ʾōṯô* follows the verb, normally right after it.

## Connections

Arabic attaches the same suffixes to its prepositions: *lī* "to me", *lahu* "to him", *maʿī* "with me", *minhu* "from him". Arabic *ʿalā* "on" and *ʾilā* "to" end in a long vowel and take *-ay-* before suffixes, *ʿalayhi* "on him", *ʾilayhi* "to him": exactly the old *-ay* that Gesenius sees behind Hebrew *ʿālāyw*, *ʾēlāyw* (§103n). Arabic has an object-pronoun particle too, *iyyā-* (*iyyāhu* "him"), used when the pronoun cannot be a suffix on the verb.

## Exercises

1. Give "to me", "to him", "to us" and "to them" with *lə-*.
2. Give "with him" in two ways.
3. Build "God blessed him".
4. Why is "to him" with *ʾel* *ʾēlāyw* and not *\*ʾēlô*?
5. Translate *lōʾ ṯōʾḵal mimmennû*. What else could *mimmennû* mean?
6. Build "There is no one like you" (to a man).
7. A student writes *\*wayyiśnəʾû hûʾ*. Correct it.
8. Translate *wayyišlaḥ yaʿăqōḇ malʾāḵîm ləp̄ānāyw*, and explain the form *ləp̄ānāyw*.
9. Translate *wəhinnēh ʾānōḵî ʿimmāḵ*. Is Jacob addressed as a woman?

## Worked answers

1. *lî*, *lô*, *lānû*, *lāhem* (Gesenius §103f).
2. *ʿimmô* and *ʾittô*. Both mean "with him" (§103b–c).
3. *wayḇāreḵ ʾōṯô ʾĕlōhîm*, or *wayḇāreḵ ʾĕlōhîm ʾōṯô*. A pronoun object with *ʾēṯ*: *ʾōṯô*.
4. *ʾel* goes back to a form ending in *-ay*, so it takes the plural set of suffixes (§103n).
5. "You must not eat from it." *mimmennû* is also "from us" (§103m).
6. *ʾên kāmôḵā*. *ʾên* negates existence (lesson 2.7); *kə-* takes *-mô-* before suffixes.
7. *wayyiśnəʾû ʾōṯô*. An object pronoun is a suffix on *ʾēṯ*, never the subject pronoun.
8. "Jacob sent messengers ahead of him." *lip̄nê* is *lə-* + *pānîm* in the construct, a plural noun, so it takes the plural set on the plural shape: *ləp̄ānāyw* (§103n).
9. "Look, I am with you." No: *ʿimmāḵ* is the pausal form of *ʿimməḵā* (2SG.M). Out of pause the same form would be feminine (§103e).
