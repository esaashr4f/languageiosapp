## The idea

A **demonstrative** is a pointing word: "this", "that", "these", "those". Hebrew has two sets.

- **"This", "these":** *zeh* (masculine), *zōʾṯ* (feminine), *ʾēlleh* (plural, both genders).
- **"That", "those":** the third-person pronouns of lesson 2.4: *hûʾ*, *hîʾ*, *hēm* or *hēmmâ*, *hēnnâ*.

The difference is not only near and far. Gesenius puts it this way: *zeh* "almost always points out a (new) person or thing present", while *hûʾ* "refers to a person or thing already mentioned or known" (§136a). His example is the pair *hayyôm hazzeh* "this day", the day on which someone is speaking, and *hayyôm hahûʾ* "that day", a day the story has just told of (§136b). So *zeh* points at the scene; *hûʾ* points back into the text.

The two sets are used in two ways, and the position tells you which:

- **After a noun, with the article, it describes:** *hammāqôm hazzeh* "this place", *bayyôm hahûʾ* "on that day".
- **Without the article, usually first, it is a pronoun or a subject:** *zeh hammāqôm* "this is the place", *zōʾṯ happaʿam* "this, this time".

Gesenius states the rule: the demonstrative "takes the article according to the same rule as adjectives": *hāʾîš hazzeh* "this man", but *zeh hāʾîš* "this is the man" (§34e). Lesson 2.1 taught the same contrast for adjectives, and lesson 3.11 completes it.

The feminine *zōʾṯ* carries the feminine *-t* (lesson 3.1): Gesenius derives it from *zāʾ* + *-t*, with *ā* darkened to *ō*, and compares Arabic *hāḏā* "this" (§34b). The plural *ʾēlleh* comes from a different stem; it serves as the plural "by usage, though not etymologically" (§34b).

## How it is built

### As a describer, after the noun

1. Take the noun and give it the article: *hammāqôm*.
2. Choose the demonstrative by gender and number, and by "this" or "that": masculine singular, "this": *zeh*.
3. Put it after the noun, with the article too: *hammāqôm hazzeh*.

The article on the demonstrative follows lesson 2.2: *hazzeh*, *hazzōʾṯ*, *hāʾēlleh* (alef: *hā-*), *hahûʾ*, *hahîʾ* (virtual doubling before *h*), *hāhēm*, *hāhēmmâ*, *hāhēnnâ* (before *hē-*, *hā-*).

The noun can be made definite in other ways too: by a name in a chain or by a suffix. The demonstrative still takes the article: *kol-haddəḇārîm hāʾēlleh* "all these words".

### As a pronoun or subject

Without the article, *zeh*, *zōʾṯ*, *ʾēlleh* stand for the thing itself: "this one", "this (thing)", "these". As the subject of a verbless clause they usually come first: *ʾēlleh šəmôṯ* "these are the names" (lesson 3.6). *zōʾṯ* is the usual word for an abstract "this", a fact or event, where Latin uses the neuter (§136b).

As an object it takes *ʾēṯ* like any definite noun: *ʾeṯ-zōʾṯ* "this" (§34c). After a preposition it is the object of the preposition: *ləzōʾṯ* "this one" (Genesis 2:23).

### Other forms

- ***kāzeh*, *kāzōʾṯ*** "like this, such": *kə-* + demonstrative.
- ***hallāzeh*** "that one over there": a stronger, rarer form (§34f). Genesis 24:65 has it.
- ***zeh* after a question word** strengthens it, like "now" or "on earth": *mah-zzōʾṯ* "what is this?" (§136c). Question words are lesson 6.1.
- ***zeh* before a number of times or years:** *zeh paʿămayim* "twice now", *zeh ʿeśrîm šānâ* "these twenty years" (§136d).

### Choosing between zeh and hûʾ

To translate "In those days there were giants on the earth":

1. "Those days" refers to a time the story is describing, not the speaker's present: *hûʾ*-set.
2. Plural masculine: *hēm*. With the article before *hē*: *hāhēm*.
3. *bayyāmîm hāhēm* "in those days" (Genesis 6:4).

## Where it applies

- **"This" for something present to the speaker:** *hammāqôm hazzeh* (example 2), *hayyôm hazzeh* "this very day".
- **"That" for something in the story:** *bayyôm hahûʾ*, *bayyāmîm hāhēm*.
- **Set phrases.** Gesenius notes fixed habits that cut across the rule: almost always *haddāḇār hazzeh*, *haddəḇārîm hāʾēlleh*, but *bayyāmîm hāhēm* (§136b).
- **As subject of a verbless clause:** *zeh hûʾ*, *ʾēlleh tôləḏôṯ*, *zōʾṯ happaʿam ʿeṣem mēʿăṣāmay*.
- **"This" for an act or fact:** *mah-zzōʾṯ ʿāśîṯ* (feminine).
- **Contempt.** *zeh* can sound scornful, like Latin *iste*: "this fellow" (§136b).
- **After question words** for emphasis: *mah-zzeh*, *lāmmâ zeh* "why ever?" (§136c).
- **Before numbers of times or years:** *zeh paʿămayim* (§136d).
- **"Such":** *kāzeh*, *kāzōʾṯ*.
- **"That" as a pronoun subject:** *hûʾ*, *hîʾ*, *hēm* standing alone are ordinary pronouns (lesson 2.4); with the article after a noun they are demonstratives.

## Watch out

**Do not put the demonstrative before the noun to say "this X".** Spanish *este lugar*, French *ce lieu* would suggest *\*zeh hammāqôm* for "this place". That is a sentence: "this is the place". Say *hammāqôm hazzeh*.

**Do not drop the article from either word.** *\*māqôm zeh* and *\*hammāqôm zeh* are not phrases. Both the noun and the demonstrative take the article (§34e).

**Do not use *zeh* for a feminine noun.** *\*hāʾāreṣ hazzeh* is wrong; *ʾereṣ* is feminine: *hāʾāreṣ hazzōʾṯ* (Genesis 15:18). And *laylâ* is masculine: *hallaylâ hazzeh* (lesson 3.1).

**Do not make *ʾēlleh* feminine.** There is no separate feminine plural "these": *ʾēlleh* serves both (§34a).

**Do not use *zeh* for "that" in a narrative.** "On that day" in a story is *bayyôm hahûʾ*, not *\*bayyôm hazzeh*, which would mean "today" from the speaker's point of view.

**Do not read *hahûʾ* as "the he".** With the article after a noun, the pronoun is a demonstrative: "that".

**Do not confuse *zeh* the demonstrative with the enclitic *zeh*.** In *wayyaʿqəḇēnî zeh paʿămayim* it does not mean "this one deceived me"; it marks the count, "now twice" (§136d). Its position before a number tells you.

## Connections

Arabic has the same two-way system: *hāḏā* / *hāḏihi* "this" and *ḏālika* / *tilka* "that", and like Hebrew it puts the demonstrative with a definite noun: *hāḏā l-bayt* "this house", *al-bayt hāḏā*. Gesenius links Hebrew *zeh* to the *ḏā* of *hāḏā* (§34b). Japanese *kono*, *sono*, *ano* and Korean *i*, *geu*, *jeo* divide space three ways; Hebrew divides two ways, by present scene and story.

## Exercises

1. Build "this man", "this woman" and "these men".
2. Translate *zeh hāʾîš* and *hāʾîš hazzeh*, and explain the difference.
3. Build "that day" and "those days" as they would appear in a narrative.
4. Why is "this land" *hāʾāreṣ hazzōʾṯ*, not *\*hāʾāreṣ hazzeh*?
5. Translate *ʾaḥar haddəḇārîm hāʾēlleh*.
6. A student writes *\*zeh hammāqôm hazzeh* for "this place". What is wrong?
7. Translate *mah-zzōʾṯ ʿāśîṯ*. Why is the demonstrative feminine?
8. Translate *hănimṣāʾ ḵāzeh ʾîš*, and explain *ḵāzeh*.
9. Build "This is the book of the covenant", using *sēp̄er* and *habbərîṯ*.

## Worked answers

1. *hāʾîš hazzeh*, *hāʾiššâ hazzōʾṯ*, *hāʾănāšîm hāʾēlleh*. Noun and demonstrative both take the article; the demonstrative agrees (Gesenius §34e).
2. *zeh hāʾîš* "this is the man": a verbless clause, demonstrative as subject without the article. *hāʾîš hazzeh* "this man": a phrase, both definite.
3. *bayyôm hahûʾ*, *bayyāmîm hāhēm* (or *hāhēmmâ*). In narrative, "that" points back with the *hûʾ*-set (§136a–b).
4. *ʾereṣ* is feminine (lesson 3.1).
5. "After these things".
6. Two demonstratives: *zeh* at the front makes a sentence, "this is this place". Write *hammāqôm hazzeh*.
7. "What is this that you have done?" *zōʾṯ* is used for an act or fact, where Latin would use the neuter (§136b).
8. "Can we find a man like this?" *kāzeh* is *kə-* "like" + *zeh*.
9. *zeh sēp̄er habbərîṯ*. Demonstrative first, without the article, as subject; then the construct chain as predicate (lessons 3.5, 2.1).
