## Before you start

This review covers part 3: gender (lesson 3.1), plural and dual (3.2), how noun vowels change (3.3), segholates (3.4), the construct state and chains (3.5–3.6), pronoun suffixes on singular and plural nouns (3.7–3.8) and on prepositions and *ʾēṯ* (3.9), demonstratives (3.10), adjectives (3.11) and the irregular nouns (3.12). Part 2 is assumed: verbless clauses, the article, *wə-*, the prepositions, the third-person qatal, negation, questions and *ʾēṯ*.

Do each exercise before reading its answer. Each answer names the lesson to reread if you went wrong.

## Passage

The passage is attested: 1 Samuel 25:2–3, word for word from the Leningrad Codex, leaving out the last clause of each verse. It introduces Nabal and his wife Abigail, whom David will meet. It is made almost entirely of verbless clauses, so nearly every word is a noun, an adjective or a suffix.

New words are glossed in the notes. The numbers are part 5.

## Exercises

1. Translate line 1. What kind of clause is each half, and what does the suffix in *ûmaʿăśēhû* refer to?
2. Translate line 2. Why does *gāḏôl* have no article, and what does *wəlô* do?
3. Translate line 3, and name the construct chains.
4. Translate line 4. Explain the form *ṭôḇaṯ*, and why *yāp̄â* appears as *-p̄aṯ*.
5. Translate line 5. How does it contrast with line 4, form for form?
6. In line 1 the suffix is *-hû*, not *-ô*. Why?
7. Give the singular of *ʿizzîm* and explain the doubled *z*. What gender is the noun, and what is odd about its plural ending?
8. Build "the man's wife was wise", using *ḥăḵāmâ*.
9. Build "his daughters were beautiful".
10. Build "These are the names of the sons of Nabal."
11. Build "the great city" and "the city is great".
12. Build "I am with you" (to a woman), using *ʿim*.
13. Build "the kings of the land" from *meleḵ*.
14. Build "the elders of the city" from *zāqēn*.
15. Give "my words" and "my word", and say how they differ in the script.
16. Find and fix the error: *\*hāʾîš haggāḏôl* for "the man was great".
17. Find and fix the error: *\*ʾāḇô* for "his father".
18. Find and fix the error: *\*hammāqôm zeh* for "this place".
19. Find and fix the error: *\*habbêṯ hammeleḵ* for "the king's house".
20. Find and fix the error: *\*ʿārîm gəḏōlîm* for "great cities".
21. Find and fix the error: *\*wayyiśnəʾû hûʾ* for "they hated him".
22. Why is "the land" in "this land" followed by *hazzōʾṯ* and not *hazzeh*?

## Worked answers

1. "There was a man in Maon whose business was in Carmel." Two verbless clauses, each with a prepositional predicate (lesson 2.1). The suffix "his" refers back to the man (lesson 3.7). An indefinite subject first, *ʾîš*, is the usual way to introduce a new character.
2. "The man was very rich; he had three thousand sheep and a thousand goats." *gāḏôl* is the predicate: no article (lessons 2.1, 3.11). Here "great" means rich, as the next clause shows. *wəlô* "and to him" is the possession construction with *lə-* and a suffix: "he had" (lessons 2.7, 3.9).
3. "The man's name was Nabal, and his wife's name Abigail." *šēm hāʾîš*, construct + definite noun (lesson 3.5); *šēm ʾištô*, construct + a noun with a suffix, so definite (lessons 3.5, 3.7, 3.12).
4. "The woman was sensible and beautiful." *ṭôḇaṯ* is the construct of the feminine *ṭôḇâ*: the old *-t* returns before a bound noun (lessons 3.1, 3.6). *yāp̄â* has the construct *yəp̄aṯ*; after *wə-* (here *wî-*, lesson 2.3) the *y* has shewa, so *wîp̄aṯ*. Adjectives in the construct, bound to the noun that says in what respect (lesson 3.11).
5. "But the man was harsh and his deeds were evil." Line 4 has two feminine adjectives in the construct; line 5 has a masculine adjective *qāšeh* and a masculine construct adjective *raʿ* bound to *maʿălālîm*, "bad of deeds". Same structure, opposite meaning, with gender following each subject (lessons 3.1, 3.11).
6. *maʿăśeh* ends in *-eh*; its construct ends in *-ēh*, and such nouns add *-hû* to that vowel (Gesenius §91d: *śāḏēhû* "his field"; lessons 3.5, 3.7).
7. *ʿēz* "she-goat". The root's last consonant is doubled, and the doubling returns before the ending (lesson 3.3). It is feminine but takes *-îm*: plural endings do not follow gender strictly (lesson 3.2).
8. *ʾēšeṯ hāʾîš ḥăḵāmâ*, or *ḥăḵāmâ ʾēšeṯ hāʾîš*. *ʾēšeṯ* is the construct of *ʾiššâ*; the predicate adjective is feminine and has no article (lessons 3.11, 3.12).
9. *bənōṯāyw yāp̄ôṯ*. *bānôṯ* + the plural suffix *-āyw*; the predicate is feminine plural (lessons 3.8, 3.11, 3.12).
10. *ʾēlleh šəmôṯ bənê nāḇāl*. *ʾēlleh* first, without the article, as subject; then a chain of three (lessons 3.6, 3.10; compare Exodus 1:1).
11. *hāʿîr haggəḏôlâ* "the great city"; *gəḏôlâ hāʿîr* "the city is great". *ʿîr* is feminine without an ending (lessons 3.1, 3.11).
12. *ʾānōḵî ʿimmāḵ* (or *ʾănî ʿimmāḵ*). *ʿim* doubles its *m*; 2SG.F is *-āḵ* (lesson 3.9).
13. *malḵê hāʾāreṣ*. The segholate base *malk-* with the plural construct *-ê*; the *k* is soft (lessons 3.4, 3.6).
14. *ziqnê hāʿîr*. *zəqēnîm* → *ziqnê*: the stress moves two places (lesson 3.6).
15. *dəḇāray* "my words", *dəḇārî* "my word". The plural has *-ay*, written with yod after pataḥ: דְּבָרַי against דְּבָרִי (lessons 3.7, 3.8).
16. *hāʾîš gāḏôl* (or *gāḏôl hāʾîš*). With the article on the adjective it is a phrase, "the great man" (lessons 2.1, 3.11).
17. *ʾāḇîw*. *ʾāḇ* adds suffixes to *ʾāḇî-* (lesson 3.12).
18. *hammāqôm hazzeh*. The demonstrative after a definite noun takes the article (lesson 3.10).
19. *bêṯ hammeleḵ*. No article on the construct noun (lesson 3.5).
20. *ʿārîm gəḏōlôṯ*. *ʿîr* is feminine; the adjective follows the gender of the singular (lessons 3.2, 3.11).
21. *wayyiśnəʾû ʾōṯô*. A pronoun object is a suffix on *ʾēṯ* (lesson 3.9).
22. *ʾereṣ* is feminine, so the demonstrative is *zōʾṯ*: *hāʾāreṣ hazzōʾṯ* (Genesis 15:18; lessons 3.1, 3.10).
