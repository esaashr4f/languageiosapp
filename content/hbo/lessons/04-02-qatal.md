## The idea

Lesson 2.6 taught the third person of the qatal: *šāmar* "he guarded", *šāmərâ* "she guarded", *šāmərû* "they guarded". This lesson adds "I", "you" and "we". These are the forms of conversation. In a story the main line is carried by *wayyiqtol* (lesson 4.4), but when people speak to each other they report, deny, confess and promise, and for that they use the qatal: "I did not laugh", "you listened to your wife", "we have sinned".

The new endings are worth looking at closely, because you have seen them before. *-tā* "you" resembles *ʾattâ* "you"; *-tem* "you (plural)" resembles *ʾattem*; *-nû* "we" resembles *ʾănaḥnû*. That is not chance. Gesenius explains the qatal as a verbal stem with "certain forms of the personal pronoun" added to its end (§44a). *šāmartā* is, in origin, something like "guarding-you": a description with its subject attached.

Two endings are less obvious. *-tî* "I" does not match *ʾănî*; Gesenius connects it to the longer pronoun *ʾānōḵî*, with *t* in place of *k* (§44a). And the second person feminine *-t* once ended in *-tî* too: that older vowel still appears in some written forms and comes back before object suffixes (§44h; lesson 4.19).

## How it is built

### The endings

Start from the stem *šāmar* and add:

- **1SG "I"**: *-tî*. *šāmartî* "I guarded".
- **2SG.M "you", a man**: *-tā*. *šāmartā* "you guarded".
- **2SG.F "you", a woman**: *-t*. *šāmart* "you guarded". The final *t* carries a shewa in the script, תְּ, which marks the end of the word, not a vowel.
- **1PL "we"**: *-nû*. *šāmarnû* "we guarded".
- **2PL.M "you", several men or a mixed group**: *-tem*. *šəmartem*.
- **2PL.F "you", several women**: *-ten*. *šəmarten*. Rare.

With the third person from lesson 2.6, that is the whole qatal: nine forms, three of them already known.

### What happens to the vowels

Two things change, and both follow from where the stress falls.

**Before a consonant ending, the pataḥ stays.** The third-person endings *-â* and *-û* begin with a vowel, so the second *a* of *šāmar* reduced to shewa: *šāmərû*. The new endings begin with a consonant, so *r* closes the syllable and the *a* stays: *šā-mar-tî* (Gesenius §44b).

**The stress stays on the stem with *-tî*, *-tā*, *-t*, *-nû*.** These four endings are unstressed, so the word is stressed on the next-to-last syllable: *šāmártî*, *šāmártā* (Gesenius §44n). The acute is the guide's mark, not part of the spelling. This matters in lesson 4.5, where the stress moves.

**Before *-tem* and *-ten*, the first vowel reduces.** These endings take the stress themselves. The *ā* of the first syllable is now two places from the stress, and like any such vowel it reduces to shewa: *šəmartém* (Gesenius §44b, §43b). If the first root letter is a guttural, it takes a hatef vowel instead of plain shewa (lesson 1.5): *ḥăšaḇtem* "you planned".

### Verbs of state

Some qal verbs name a state, not an action: *kāḇēḏ* "be heavy", *zāqēn* "be old", *qāṭōn* "be small". Gesenius notes that they have *ē* or *ō* in the second syllable, where action verbs have *a* (§43a). In the inflected forms:

- **ē usually becomes *a***: *zāqēn* → *zāqantî* "I am old" (Gesenius §44c).
- **ō stays when stressed**: *qāṭōn* → *qāṭṓntî* "I am small" (Gesenius §44e).

### Root letters that meet the ending

When the last root letter is *t* or *n*, it can merge with the *t* of the ending. *Nāṯan* "give" + *-tî* gives *nāṯattî*: the *n* has assimilated, and the dagesh in the *t* records it (lesson 4.13). *Kāraṯ* "cut" + *-tî* gives *kārattî* (Gesenius §44o).

### Building a sentence

"We have forsaken the LORD":

1. The root is *ʿ-z-b* "leave, forsake". The act is complete and its result stands: qatal.
2. "We" is 1PL: *-nû*, a consonant ending, so the pataḥ stays: *ʿāzaḇnû*.
3. No pronoun is needed. The object is definite: *ʾeṯ-YHWH*.
4. *ʿāzaḇnû ʾeṯ-YHWH* (1 Samuel 12:10).

## Where it applies

- **Past events in speech:** denials, reports, accusations. *lōʾ ṣāḥaqtî*, *kî-šāmaʿtā ləqôl ʾišteḵā*. Dialogue is full of first- and second-person qatals, because there the speaker reports rather than narrates.
- **Done, with the result still standing:** "have done". *ḥāṭāʾnû* "we have sinned", *wəlōʾ ḥāśaḵtā* "you have not withheld" (Gesenius §106g).
- **States of mind and body, as a present:** *yāḏaʿtî* "I know", *zāḵarnû* "we remember", *zāqantî* "I am old", *qāṭōntî* "I am small" (Gesenius §106g). With a negative and a past context the same form is a past: *lōʾ yāḏaʿtî* can be "I did not know".
- **Things done by saying them:** a deal, an oath, a ruling. *nāṯattî* "I give (hereby)" (Gesenius §106i, m).
- **After *kî*, *yaʿan* "because", and in questions and negations:** *kî ʿāzaḇnû*, *yaʿan māʾastā ʾeṯ-dəḇar YHWH* "because you rejected the word of the LORD" (1 Samuel 15:23).
- **In prophecy,** a future can be stated in the qatal as if already seen (Gesenius §106n). This is mainly poetic and prophetic; lesson 7.6 returns to it.
- **Later:** after *wə-* the second- and first-person qatal often means "and you will do", the *weqatal* (lesson 4.5). In relative clauses the qatal gives "which I had done" (lesson 6.1).

## Watch out

**Do not keep the first vowel before *-tem*.** *\*šāmartem* is wrong. The stress is on *-tem*, so the *ā* reduces: *šəmartem*. With a guttural first letter: *ḥăšaḇtem*, not *\*ḥəšaḇtem*.

**Do not reduce the second vowel before a consonant ending.** By analogy with *šāmərû* you might write *\*šāmərtî*. The ending begins with a consonant, so the pataḥ stays: *šāmartî*.

**Do not mix up 2SG.M and 2SG.F.** *-tā* is "you" to a man, *-t* "you" to a woman. The masculine is written תָּ, with no vowel letter after it (rarely תָּה, Gesenius §44g), and the feminine is תְּ; look at the vowel under the last *t*. Sarah is *ṣāḥāqt*, not *\*ṣāḥaqtā*.

**Do not add a pronoun for plain "I did".** *\*ʾănî ṣāḥaqtî* is not wrong Hebrew, but it means "*I* laughed", in contrast to someone else. Sarah says *lōʾ ṣāḥaqtî*. Joseph's *wəʾattem ḥăšaḇtem* has the pronoun because he is about to contrast his brothers with God.

**Do not translate every qatal of a state verb as a past.** *Yāḏaʿtî* in Genesis 22:12 is "I know", not "I knew". Spanish *supe* and French *je sus* would be wrong here; *sé*, *je sais* are right.

**Do not translate a qatal of saying-as-doing as a past.** *Haśśāḏeh nāṯattî lāḵ* is not "I gave you the field" (he has not yet). It is "I give you the field", like a signature.

**Do not keep *ē* in the inflected forms of *zāqēn*.** *\*zāqēntî* is wrong; Hebrew levels it to *zāqantî* (Gesenius §44c). The *ō* of *qāṭōn* behaves differently and stays under the stress.

**Do not read *wə* + second-person qatal at the start of a clause as a past.** *Wəšāmartā* in an instruction is "and you shall keep" (lesson 4.5). The past "and you guarded" is rare in that position.

**Do not confuse *-nû* "we" with *-ēnû* "our".** On a noun, *-ēnû* is "our" (*dəḇārēnû*, lesson 3.7); on a qatal stem, *-nû* is the subject "we". On verbs *-nû* can also be the object "us" (lesson 4.19); there the vowel before it differs.

## Connections

The Arabic suffix conjugation has the same endings: *katabtu* "I wrote", *katabta* "you (m.) wrote", *katabti* "you (f.) wrote", *katabnā* "we wrote", *katabtum* "you (pl.) wrote". Arabic keeps the final vowels that Hebrew dropped. Hebrew *-tî* "I" differs in its consonant-vowel shape, but Gesenius links it to the pronoun *ʾānōḵî* (§44a), and *-t* "you (f.)", once *-tî*, matches Arabic *-ti*.

8. Translate *haśśāḏeh nāṯattî lāḵ*, explain the English tense, and say what the double *t* records.

10. In *wəʾattem ḥăšaḇtem*, why is the pronoun there, when the verb already says "you"?

## Worked answers

1. *zāḵartî*, *zāḵartā*, *zāḵarnû*, *zəḵartem*. In the last, the stressed ending *-tem* puts the first *ā* two places from the stress, so it reduces to shewa (Gesenius §44b).
2. *kāṯaḇt*. The 2SG.F ending is *-t*; the pataḥ stays before a consonant ending.
3. *bāḥartî*. The choice is made and stands; "I" is *-tî*.
4. "We have forsaken the LORD." *ʿāzaḇ-nû* `leave.QTL-1PL`: the root *ʿ-z-b* in the qatal, with *-nû* "we".
5. Knowing is a state reached and still holding. With such verbs the qatal states the present (Gesenius §106g).
6. *šāmartā*. The ending *-tā* begins with a consonant, so *r* closes the syllable and the pataḥ stays. Reduction to shewa happens only before the vowel endings *-â*, *-û*.
7. *ḥăšaḇ-tem* `plan.QTL-2PL.M`: the stem of *ḥ-š-b* with *-tem*. The first vowel reduces before the stressed ending; a guttural cannot carry plain shewa and takes the hatef *ă* (lesson 1.5).
8. "I give you the field." The gift is made by the words themselves, so English uses the present (Gesenius §106m). The root's final *n* has assimilated to the *t* of *-tî*: *nāṯan* + *tî* → *nāṯattî*.
9. *zāqantî*. Verbs of state with *ē* usually change it to *a* before a consonant ending (Gesenius §44c).
10. For contrast. The pronoun points at "you" as against someone else; the verse continues with God, who meant it for good.
