## The idea

The qatal puts the person at the end: *šāmar-tî* "I guarded". The second conjugation puts it at the front: *ʾe-šmōr* "I will guard", *yi-šmōr* "he will guard". Its name, *yiqtol*, is the 3SG.M of the model verb, as *qatal* is. Older grammars call it "the imperfect".

The two conjugations view an event differently. Gesenius describes the qatal as presenting an event "from the point of view of completion" (§106a) and the yiqtol as presenting it as "still continuing, or in process of accomplishment, or even as just taking place" (§107a). In practice that gives the yiqtol three large uses:

- **the future**: "you shall keep my covenant";
- **what happens repeatedly or habitually**, in the past as well as the present: "it used to lie in his bosom";
- **the possible, the wished and the required**: "who is the LORD, that I should obey him?"

So the yiqtol is not "the future tense". It covers what an English speaker would split between *will*, *would*, *used to*, *may*, *should* and the simple present. Lesson 4.6 discusses how scholars describe this; here you learn the forms and the main uses.

### Where the prefixes come from

As with the qatal, the person markers are pieces of pronouns. Gesenius connects *ʾ-* "I" with *ʾănî*, *n-* "we" with *(ʾă)naḥnû*, and the *t-* of "you" with *ʾattâ*, *ʾattem* (§47b–c). The feminine *-î* of "you (f.)" matches the old pronoun *ʾattî*; the plural *-û* is the same plural mark as in the qatal. The *y-* of "he" and the *t-* of "she" have "not yet met with any satisfactory explanation" (§47d). Because the prefix alone cannot always show both gender and number, some persons also need an ending: that is why the yiqtol has prefixes *and* suffixes (§47a).

## How it is built

### The stem and the prefixes

The yiqtol stem of a strong action verb is *-qṭōl*: the first root letter closes a syllable with the prefix, and the second carries *ō*. *Š-m-r* gives *-šmōr*. Put a prefix with *i* in front:

- **yi-** for 3SG.M and 3PL.M: *yišmōr* "he will guard".
- **ti-** for 3SG.F and every second person: *tišmōr* "she will guard" or "you (m.) will guard".
- **ʾe-** for 1SG: *ʾešmōr* "I will guard". The ʾ prefers *e* (Gesenius §47b).
- **ni-** for 1PL: *nišmōr* "we will guard".

The prefix vowel *i* comes from an older *a*: Gesenius gives the ground forms *yaqṭul*, *taqṭul*, as in Arabic (§47b, d). The *-ōl* comes from an older *-ul*, lengthened under the stress (§47f).

### The endings

Three persons add an ending:

- **2SG.F -î**: *tišmərî* "you (f.) will guard".
- **3PL.M and 2PL.M -û**: *yišmərû* "they will guard", *tišmərû* "you (pl.) will guard".
- **3PL.F and 2PL.F -nâ**: *tišmōrnâ* "they (f.) / you (f. pl.) will guard".

The vowel endings *-î* and *-û* take the stress, and the *ō* before them reduces to shewa: *tišmōr* → *tišmərû* (Gesenius §47e). The ending *-nâ* begins with a consonant and is not stressed, so the *ō* stays: *tišmōrnâ*. This is the same logic as the qatal (lesson 4.2).

Notice the overlaps. *Tišmōr* is both "she will guard" and "you (m.) will guard"; *tišmōrnâ* is both "they (f.)" and "you (f. pl.)". The context, and often an explicit subject, decides.

### Action verbs and state verbs: *ō* or *a*

Gesenius notes that *ō* "is to be found almost exclusively with transitive verbs" (§47h). Verbs of state, and many verbs of motion or posture, take *a* instead: *šāḵaḇ* "lie down" → *yiškaḇ*; *gāḏal* "grow" → *yiḡdal*; *qāṭōn* "be small" → *yiqṭan*; *lāmaḏ* "learn" → *yilmaḏ* (§47h). Verbs whose last root letter is a guttural also take *a*, because gutturals prefer it (lesson 4.11): *šāmaʿ* → *yišmaʿ*; *rāṣaḥ* → *yirṣaḥ*.

So learn each verb's yiqtol vowel with the verb, as you learn the gender of a French noun. The vocabulary lists give it.

### The longer plural *-ûn*

Instead of *-û*, the plural sometimes ends in *-ûn*, always stressed, "especially in the older books" and mostly before a pause (Gesenius §47m). The vowel before it is then often kept: *yišʾālûn* "they will ask", where the plain ending would give *yišʾălû* by pattern. Gesenius notes that *-ûn* is the regular ending in Aramaic and Arabic. In Hebrew it is an older, fuller variant with the same meaning.

### Building a sentence

"You shall not steal", said to one man:

1. A command for all time, with the strongest expectation of obedience: *lōʾ* + yiqtol (Gesenius §107o).
2. The root is *g-n-b*, an action verb: yiqtol with *ō*, *-ḡnōḇ*.
3. "You", a man: prefix *ti-*: *tiḡnōḇ*.
4. *lōʾ tiḡnōḇ* (Exodus 20:15). To a group: *lōʾ tiḡnəḇû* (Leviticus 19:11).

## Where it applies

- **The future, from the speaker's now:** *hammeleḵ ʾăšer yimlōḵ ʿălêḵem*; *naʿăśeh wənišmāʿ* (Gesenius §107i).
- **The future seen from a point in the past:** "the king who was to reign" (Gesenius §107k, citing 2 Kings 3:27 *ʾăšer-yimlōḵ*).
- **Repeated or habitual action in the past:** "used to", "would". *ûḇəḥêqô ṯiškāḇ*; Moses "used to take" the tent, *yiqqaḥ* (Exodus 33:7; Gesenius §107e).
- **Continuous action in the past, or after *ʾāz* "then" and *ṭerem* "before":** Gesenius cites Exodus 15:1 *ʾāz yāšîr-mōšeh* "then Moses sang" (§107c). Lesson 6.6 returns to these time words.
- **The present, for what is going on or always true:** *mah-təḇaqqēš* "what are you looking for?" (Gesenius §107f–g).
- **Prohibitions with *lōʾ*:** *lōʾ tiḡnōḇ*. This is the firm, lasting form; *ʾal* with the short form is the urgent "don't!" (lesson 4.8; Gesenius §107o).
- **Obligation, permission, possibility:** "should", "may", "can". *ʾăšer ʾešmaʿ bəqōlô* "that I should obey him"; Genesis 2:16, *ʾāḵōl tōʾḵēl* "you may freely eat" (Gesenius §107s).
- **Purpose clauses** after *ləmaʿan* "so that" and *pen* "lest" (Gesenius §107q; lesson 6.4).
- **Later:** after *wa-* with doubling the short yiqtol becomes the wayyiqtol "and he did" (lesson 4.4); the jussive and cohortative (lesson 4.8) are yiqtol forms used for wishes.

## Watch out

**Do not translate every yiqtol as a future.** Spanish and French learners expect a future tense. *Ûḇəḥêqô ṯiškāḇ* is not "it will lie in his arms": the parable is in the past, and the yiqtol says it did so habitually. Use the English that fits: "used to", "would", "should", "is doing".

**Do not read *tišmōr* as "she" without checking.** *Ti-* marks 3SG.F and every second person. In *wəʾattâ ... ṯišmōr* the pronoun *ʾattâ* shows it is "you". Look for an explicit subject or a vocative.

**Do not keep *ō* before *-û* and *-î* out of pause.** *\*tišmōrû* is wrong in ordinary position; the form is *tišmərû*. Only before *-nâ*, or at a pause, does the *ō* stay.

**Do not give every verb *ō*.** *\*yiškōḇ* for "he will lie down" is wrong; the verb is *yiškaḇ*. State and posture verbs, and verbs ending in a guttural, take *a*.

**Do not take *-ûn* for a different person.** *Yišʾālûn* is an ordinary 3PL, with an older, fuller ending. It is not a suffix "them".

**Do not confuse the 1PL prefix with the niphal.** *Nišmōr* "we will guard" (qal yiqtol 1PL) and *nišmar* "he was guarded" (niphal qatal 3SG.M) both start with *niš-*. The stem vowel tells you: qal yiqtol has *ō* (or *a*) and is "we"; the niphal qatal has *a* and is "he" (lesson 4.20).

**Do not use *ʾal* with the yiqtol for "you shall not" in a law.** The commandments use *lōʾ*. *\*ʾal tiḡnōḇ* would be a warning in the moment, "don't steal!", not a law (Gesenius §107o).

**Do not add a pronoun out of habit.** As with the qatal, the prefix already says who. *ʾĂnî ʾemlōḵ* has *ʾănî* because Adonijah sets himself against his brother.

## Connections

Arabic has the same prefix conjugation: *yaktubu* "he writes", *taktubu* "you write / she writes", *ʾaktubu* "I write", *naktubu* "we write", *taktubīna* "you (f.) write", *yaktubūna* "they write". Gesenius's ground forms *yaqṭul*, *taqṭul* are close to these, and the Arabic plural *-ūna* is the fuller ending that Hebrew keeps in *-ûn*. The overlap of "you (m.)" and "she" in *t-* is the same in both languages.

## Exercises

1. Give the yiqtol of *zāḵar* "remember" for "I", "you (m.)", "you (f.)" and "they (m.)", and explain what happens to the *ō*.
2. Build "We will guard the covenant".
3. Build "He will lie down", from *šāḵaḇ*, and explain the vowel.
4. Translate *lōʾ tirṣāḥ* and say why the verb has *a*.
5. *Tišmōr* stands alone. Give two possible translations and say how you would decide.
6. Take apart *yišʾālûn* and say how it differs from *yišʾălû*.
7. Translate *ûḇəḥêqô ṯiškāḇ* and explain why it is not a future.
8. A student writes *\*ʾal tiḡnōḇ* for the commandment "You shall not steal". What is wrong?
9. Build "Who is the king, that I should listen to his voice?"

## Worked answers

1. *ʾezkōr*, *tizkōr*, *tizkərî*, *yizkərû*. The stressed endings *-î* and *-û* begin with a vowel, so the *ō* before them reduces to shewa (Gesenius §47e). The *k* of the stem is hard after the closed syllable *ʾez-*, *tiz-*.
2. *nišmōr ʾeṯ-habbərîṯ*. 1PL prefix *ni-*, stem *-šmōr*, definite object with *ʾeṯ*.
3. *yiškaḇ*. *Šāḵaḇ* is a verb of posture, and such verbs take *a* in the yiqtol (Gesenius §47h). The *k* is hard after the closed syllable *yiš-*.
4. "You shall not murder" (Exodus 20:13). The last root letter *ḥ* is a guttural, and gutturals prefer *a*; at the pause it is lengthened to *ā*.
5. "She will guard" or "you (m.) will guard". Look for a subject: a feminine noun or *hîʾ* gives "she", *ʾattâ* or the person spoken to gives "you".
6. *yišʾālûn* `ask.YQTL.3PL.M`: prefix *yi-*, stem *-šʾāl-* with its vowel kept, and the longer ending *-ûn* (Gesenius §47m). It means the same as the plain plural (*yišʾălû* by pattern); the long ending is older and fuller and often stands before a pause.
7. "And it used to lie in his arms." The parable is set in the past, and the yiqtol describes a repeated habit (Gesenius §107e).
8. The commandments use *lōʾ* with the yiqtol, which states a lasting law with the strongest expectation of obedience; *ʾal* belongs with the short form and gives an urgent warning (Gesenius §107o). The right form is *lōʾ tiḡnōḇ*.
9. *mî hammeleḵ ʾăšer ʾešmaʿ bəqōlô*. The question is a verbless clause; *ʾešmaʿ* is 1SG yiqtol with *a* because of the final guttural, used for "should", as in Exodus 5:2.
