## The idea

Open any page of Genesis or Kings and you will see the same word-shape again and again at the start of clauses: *wayyōʾmer*, *wayyēleḵ*, *wayyiqqaḥ*, *wayhî*. This is the **wayyiqtol**, the form that carries a Hebrew story forward. *Wayyōʾmer* "and he said" occurs about 1,950 times in the Bible, more than almost any other word.

Gesenius calls its behaviour "one of the most striking peculiarities" of Hebrew: in a series of past events, "only the first verb stands in the perfect, and the narration is continued in the imperfect" (§49a). A story begins, if it begins with a verb at all, with a qatal or a time expression; after that, each new event is a wayyiqtol, each one the next step. Gesenius calls this the "narrative tense" (§111a).

The form looks like *wə-* "and" + yiqtol, but it is not. It has its own prefix, *wa-* with doubling, and it uses a shorter stem than the yiqtol wherever a shorter one exists. Older grammars call it the "imperfect with waw consecutive" or "waw conversive", as if the *wa-* turned a future into a past. That is one theory of where the form came from; lesson 4.6 sets out the others. This guide calls it by its shape.

In the gloss the prefix is WAYY, and the rest is glossed without YQTL, because the form is not a yiqtol in meaning: *way-yōʾmer* `WAYY-say.3SG.M`.

## How it is built

### The prefix

Start from the yiqtol: *yizkōr* "he will remember". Then:

1. Put *wa-* in front. Gesenius notes that this *wa* is the older form of "and", the one Arabic still uses as its ordinary copula (§49f; lesson 2.3).
2. Double the prefix consonant: *wa-yyizkōr*, written *wayyizkōr*. The doubling, Gesenius explains, comes from the close bond of *wa* with the verb; the *a* could not stand in an open syllable (§49f).

So the sign of the wayyiqtol is **pataḥ under *w* + dagesh in the next letter**: וַיִּ, וַתִּ, וַנִּ.

Two adjustments:

- **Before ʾ (1SG)** the doubling is impossible, because ʾ cannot double (lesson 1.7). The *a* lengthens to *ā* instead: *wā-ʾešmōr* "and I guarded" (Gesenius §49c).
- **Before *yə-*** the dagesh is dropped, as it regularly is in a *y* with shewa: *way-hî* "and it was", *way-ḏabbēr* "and he spoke" (Gesenius §49c).

### The short stem

Where the yiqtol has a long and a short form, the wayyiqtol takes the short one, and the stress moves back towards the prefix (Gesenius §49d). In the strong qal there is nothing to shorten, so *yizkōr* → *wayyizkōr*. But in weak verbs the difference is large:

- *yāqûm* "he will rise" → *wayyā́qom* "and he rose";
- *yihyeh* "he will be" → *wayhî* "and he was";
- *yirʾeh* "he will see" → *wayyarʾ* "and he saw".

In the first person singular the shortening is "not usual" (Gesenius §49e): "and I rose" is *wāʾāqûm* (Nehemiah 2:12), against *wayyāqom* "and he rose".

These short forms belong to the weak verbs, taught in lessons 4.13 to 4.17. Until then, learn the commonest ones as words: they are in the vocabulary below, each with its root and the lesson that explains it.

### Building a story

"God remembered Noah ... and God made a wind pass over the earth, and the waters subsided" (Genesis 8:1):

1. Each clause is the next event in a past story: wayyiqtol.
2. "Remember", *z-k-r*, 3SG.M yiqtol *yizkōr*; with *wa-* and doubling: *wayyizkōr*.
3. The verb comes first; the subject *ʾĕlōhîm* follows; then the object with *ʾeṯ*.
4. Each following event repeats the pattern: verb first, then subject.

## Where it applies

- **The main line of a past story,** each verb the next event: *wayyizkōr ... wayyaʿăḇēr ... wayyāšōkkû* (Genesis 8:1; Gesenius §111a).
- **Long chains:** there is no limit. Gesenius counts forty-nine wayyiqtols in Genesis 1 (§111c).
- **After a qatal or a time phrase that sets the scene:** *wəyaʿăqōḇ nāṯan ... wayyōʾḵal* (Genesis 25:34); a qatal with its subject first gives the background, and the wayyiqtols that follow are the events (lesson 2.6).
- **Past events in speech:** messages, reports and accusations narrate with wayyiqtol too: *wanniṣʿaq ... wayyišmaʿ* (Numbers 20:16).
- **"When X happened, Y happened":** of two wayyiqtols the first is often background to the second. Gesenius gives Genesis 28:8–9, *wayyarʾ ʿēśāw ... wayyēleḵ*, "when Esau saw ..., he went" (§111d).
- **"So", a result or a summary:** *wayyiḇez ʿēśāw ʾeṯ-habbəḵōrâ* "so Esau despised his birthright" (Gesenius §111k–l).
- **First-person stories:** *wāʾîrāʾ*, *wāʾēḥāḇēʾ* "and I hid" (Genesis 3:10).
- **Opening an episode with *wayhî* "and it happened":** *wayhî ʾaḥar haddəḇārîm hāʾēlleh* "after these things" (Genesis 22:1). Lesson 4.18 teaches *wayhî*.

## Watch out

**Do not put anything between *wa-* and the verb.** If a subject, an object, an adverb or "not" comes first, the wayyiqtol is impossible and the verb becomes qatal: *wəlaḥōšeḵ qārāʾ*, not *\*wəlaḥōšeḵ wayyiqrāʾ*. For the same reason a wayyiqtol cannot be negated: "and he did not let the people go" is *wəlōʾ šillaḥ ʾeṯ-hāʿām* (Exodus 8:28), *wə* + *lōʾ* + qatal, never *\*wayyišlaḥ lōʾ*.

**Do not read a wayyiqtol as a future.** It contains a yiqtol shape, but *wayyišmaʿ* is "and he heard", not "and he will hear". Spanish learners should think *pretérito*, not *futuro*.

**Do not confuse it with *wə* + yiqtol.** The sign is pataḥ + doubling: וַיִּשְׁמַע *wayyišmaʿ* "and he heard". With ordinary *wə-* and no doubling, וְיִשְׁמַע *wəyišmaʿ* is "and he will hear" or "and let him hear" (lesson 4.8).

**Do not double before ʾ.** *\*wayʾīrāʾ* or *\*waʾʾîrāʾ* is impossible; the 1SG is *wāʾîrāʾ*, with *ā* and no doubling.

**Do not build the weak forms from the yiqtol.** *\*wayyāqûm*, *\*wayyihyeh*, *\*wayyirʾeh* are wrong. The wayyiqtol uses the short forms: *wayyāqom*, *wayhî*, *wayyarʾ*. Learn them as words until lessons 4.13 to 4.18.

**Do not translate every *wa-* as "and".** A string of "and he ... and he ... and he" is faithful but flat English. Use "then", "so", "when", a full stop, or nothing, as the sense requires. Gesenius notes that a wayyiqtol can even mean "and yet" (§111e).

**Do not read *wattiqqaḥ* as "you took" in a story about a woman.** *Wat-* is both 3SG.F and 2SG.M, as *ti-* is in the yiqtol. In narrative it is nearly always "she".

**Do not expect wayyiqtol to give background.** For "now the serpent was cunning" or "now Laban had gone", Hebrew steps out of the chain with *wə* + subject + qatal or a verbless clause (lesson 2.6; Gesenius §111a cites Genesis 3:1).

## Connections

Arabic has no wayyiqtol, but it has something comparable: *lam yaktub* "he did not write" uses a short prefix form with a past meaning. The *wa-* of the Hebrew form is the Arabic *wa* "and" (Gesenius §49f). Lesson 4.6 discusses whether the two prefix forms are related. Modern Hebrew (`heb`) has lost the wayyiqtol entirely; it narrates with the qatal.

## Exercises

1. Build "and he wrote" from *kāṯaḇ*, and "and we wrote".
2. Build "and I remembered" and explain why it has no doubling.
3. Turn *yišməʿû* into a wayyiqtol and translate.
4. Translate *wayyiškaḇ šəlōmōh ʿim-ʾăḇōṯāyw*.
5. Why is the second verb in *wayyiqrāʾ ʾĕlōhîm lāʾôr yôm wəlaḥōšeḵ qārāʾ lāylâ* a qatal?
6. A student writes *\*wayyišlaḥ lōʾ ʾeṯ-hāʿām* for "and he did not let the people go". Correct it.
7. What is the difference between וַיִּזְכֹּר and וְיִזְכֹּר?
8. Translate *wayyōʾḵal wayyēšt wayyāqom wayyēlaḵ* into natural English, and say what the next clause in Genesis 25:34 does.
9. In *ʾeṯ-qōləḵā šāmaʿtî baggān wāʾîrāʾ*, why does the speaker start with a qatal and then switch?

## Worked answers

1. *wayyiḵtōḇ* (from *yiḵtōḇ*) and *wanniḵtōḇ* (from *niḵtōḇ*). *Wa-* + doubling of the prefix consonant; the *k* is soft after the vowel of the prefix syllable.
2. *wāʾezkōr*. The prefix of 1SG is ʾ, which cannot be doubled, so *wa-* lengthens to *wā-* (Gesenius §49c).
3. *wayyišməʿû* "and they heard" (Genesis 3:8).
4. "Solomon slept with his ancestors", that is, he died. The next event in the account of his reign: wayyiqtol of *šāḵaḇ*, with the yiqtol vowel *a*.
5. Because *wə* is followed by *laḥōšeḵ*, not by the verb. A wayyiqtol must stand directly after its *wa-*; when anything intervenes the verb is a qatal (Gesenius §111c).
6. *wəlōʾ šillaḥ ʾeṯ-hāʿām* (Exodus 8:28). A negative cannot stand inside a wayyiqtol; *lōʾ* comes after *wə*, and the verb becomes qatal (here a piel).
7. וַיִּזְכֹּר *wayyizkōr* has pataḥ and doubling: the wayyiqtol "and he remembered". וְיִזְכֹּר *wəyizkōr* is *wə-* + yiqtol, "and he will (or may) remember".
8. "He ate and drank, then got up and left." The next clause, *wayyiḇez ʿēśāw ʾeṯ-habbəḵōrâ*, sums up: "so Esau despised his birthright", a wayyiqtol of conclusion (Gesenius §111k).
9. He puts the object, "your voice", first for emphasis, so the verb cannot be a wayyiqtol and is a qatal. The following event, being afraid, continues the chain as a wayyiqtol.
