## The idea

"Hear, O Israel." "Go, lie down." "Sell me your birthright." The **imperative** is the form for telling someone directly to do something. It is built from the yiqtol: take *tišmōr* "you will guard", remove the prefix *ti-*, and what is left, *šəmōr*, is the command "guard!".

Gesenius puts it the other way round: the bare stem *qəṭōl* is the "ground-form", and the yiqtol is built on it by adding prefixes (§46a). Either way, the imperative and the yiqtol share a stem, and the imperative takes the same endings for "you (f.)" and "you (pl.)" (§46b). If you know a verb's yiqtol, you know its imperative.

The imperative has limits that English does not. It exists only for the second person, "you": commands to a third person, "let him do it", use another form (lesson 4.8). And it is never negated. Hebrew does not say "do not hear!" with an imperative; a negative command uses *ʾal* or *lōʾ* with a prefix form (Gesenius §46a).

In the gloss: `guard.IMP.2SG.M`, one piece, like the yiqtol.

## How it is built

### The four forms

Start from the yiqtol of the second person and drop the prefix:

- **2SG.M**: *tišmōr* → *šəmōr* "guard!". The first root letter, which closed the prefix syllable *tiš-*, now begins the word, so it takes a shewa: *šə-*.
- **2SG.F**: *tišmərî* → *šimrî* "guard!" (to a woman). Two shewas cannot begin a word, so the first becomes *i* (lesson 1.5).
- **2PL.M**: *tišmərû* → *šimrû* "guard!" (to several).
- **2PL.F**: *tišmōrnâ* → *šəmōrnâ* "guard!" (to several women). Rare.

The stem vowel follows the yiqtol. Verbs with *ō* in the yiqtol have *ō* here: *šəmōr*. Verbs with *a* in the yiqtol have *a*: *yišmaʿ* → *šəmaʿ* "hear!"; *yiškaḇ* → *šəḵaḇ* "lie down!" (Gesenius §46c).

### The soft letter in *šiḵḇî*

In *šiḵḇî* "lie down!" (to a woman) and *mišḵû* "draw out!", the second root letter is soft, though it follows a shewa. Gesenius explains that in these forms the first shewa was pronounced, *qĭṭelî*, so the following letter counts as coming after a vowel (§46d). It is the one place in the strong verb where you see this; read the pointing as it stands.

### The longer form with *-â*

The masculine singular can take an extra *-â*, as the first person yiqtol can (lesson 4.8). Gesenius gives *šomrâ* "guard!", *šiḵḇâ* "lie down!", and *miḵrâ* "sell!" in Genesis 25:31 (§48i). The ending is "frequently emphatic" (§48k): "do sell me", "come on, get up". The same ending in the first person is the cohortative, lesson 4.8.

### *Nāʾ* after the imperative

The particle *nāʾ* often follows a command. Gesenius says it serves "sometimes to soften down a command, or to make a request in a more courteous form" (§110d): *šimʿû-nāʾ* "please listen". It is often left untranslated; lesson 4.8 returns to it.

### Weak verbs

The commonest imperatives come from weak roots and are short: *lēḵ* "go!" (from *h-l-k*), *qaḥ* "take!" (*l-q-ḥ*), *tēn* "give!" (*n-t-n*), *šēḇ* "sit!" (*y-š-b*), *qûm* "get up!" (*q-w-m*), *bōʾ* "come!" (*b-w-ʾ*), *ʿăśēh* "do!" (*ʿ-ś-h*). Learn them as words now; lessons 4.13 to 4.17 explain each. Gesenius notes that *lēḵ*, *qûm* and their plurals, before a second imperative, are "only equivalent to interjections, come! up!" (§110h).

### Building a command

"Keep all these words", to one man:

1. A direct positive command to "you": imperative.
2. *Š-m-r*, yiqtol *tišmōr*; drop the prefix: *šəmōr*.
3. The object: *ʾēṯ kol-haddəḇārîm hāʾēlleh*.
4. To continue the command with a second act, use a weqatal (lesson 4.5): *šəmōr wəšāmaʿtā*, "keep and obey" (Deuteronomy 12:28).

## Where it applies

- **Orders:** *šəmōr*, *lēḵ šəḵāḇ* (Gesenius §110a).
- **Requests and prayers,** often with *nāʾ*: *šimʿû-nāʾ*, *dabbēr YHWH* (§110a, d).
- **Permission:** "go ahead, then": Gesenius cites David's "run!" in 2 Samuel 18:23 (§110b).
- **A promise or result, as the second of two imperatives:** *zōʾṯ ʿăśû wiḥyû* "do this and you will live" (§110f).
- **Sarcastic challenge:** "ask for the kingdom too, then!" (1 Kings 2:22; Gesenius §110a).
- **Followed by weqatal** for further steps of the same command: *šəmōr wəšāmaʿtā*; *lēḵ wəʾāmartā* (lesson 4.5).
- **Followed by a cohortative or jussive** for purpose: "come, let us ..." (lesson 4.8).
- **In every stem that has an active voice:** piel *dabbēr*, hiphil, niphal, hithpael (lessons 4.20 to 4.23). The passives (pual, hophal) have no imperative (Gesenius §46a).

## Watch out

**Do not negate an imperative.** By analogy with English "don't listen!" or Spanish *¡no escuches!*, a student writes *\*ʾal šəmaʿ*. Hebrew uses a prefix form instead: *ʾal tišmaʿ* "don't listen!" or, as a lasting rule, *lōʾ tišmaʿ* (Gesenius §46a; lesson 4.8). Spanish learners will recognise the logic: Spanish also switches to another form (*no escuches*, subjunctive) for negative commands.

**Do not use the imperative for "let him" or "let us".** There is no third- or first-person imperative. *\*šəmōr hûʾ* is impossible; "let him guard" is the jussive *yišmōr* (lesson 4.8).

**Do not keep the prefix.** *Tišmōr* is a statement, "you will guard", or a firm instruction. The imperative is *šəmōr*. Both can command, but they are different forms.

**Do not make the feminine *\*šəmərî*.** Two shewas cannot stand at the start of a word; the form is *šimrî*.

**Do not give every verb *ō*.** "Lie down!" is *šəḵaḇ*, not *\*šəḵōḇ*; "hear!" is *šəmaʿ*. Use the vowel of the verb's yiqtol.

**Do not read the soft *ḵ* in *šiḵḇî* as an error.** It is the pointing of the Masoretes, explained by a pronounced shewa (Gesenius §46d).

**Do not confuse *šəmōr* with the infinitive.** The infinitive construct has the same shape, *šəmōr*, and appears after prepositions: *lišmōr* "to guard" (lesson 4.10). Gesenius notes the two "are the same in pronunciation" (§46a). Context tells you: an imperative begins its clause and gives an order.

**Do not translate *lēḵ* every time as "go".** Before another imperative it often means "come on" (Gesenius §110h): *lēḵ šəḵāḇ* is not "go and lie down somewhere else", it is "go on, lie down".

## Connections

Arabic builds its imperative the same way: from *yaktubu* it drops the prefix, and because the bare stem would begin with two consonants it adds a helping vowel: *uktub* "write!". Hebrew solves the same problem with a shewa, *kəṯōḇ*. Arabic also never negates the imperative; "don't write!" is *lā taktub*, prefix form with *lā*, just as Hebrew uses *ʾal* + prefix form.

## Exercises

1. Give the four imperatives of *zāḵar* "remember".
2. Build "Hear, O Jacob!".
3. Build "Lie down!" to a woman, and explain the vowel of the first syllable.
4. Translate *šimʿû-nāʾ haḥălôm hazzeh* and explain the role of *nāʾ*.
5. A student writes *\*ʾal šəmōr* for "don't guard!". What is wrong? Give the right form.
6. Why is "let him remember" not an imperative?
7. Translate *zōʾṯ ʿăśû wiḥyû*. What does the second imperative express?
8. What is the difference between *šəmōr* at the start of a command and *lišmōr* after a verb?
9. Explain the form *miḵrâ* in Genesis 25:31.

## Worked answers

1. *zəḵōr*, *ziḵrî*, *ziḵrû*, *zəḵōrnâ*. Drop the prefix from *tizkōr*, *tizkərî*, *tizkərû*, *tizkōrnâ*; where two shewas would begin the word, the first becomes *i*. As in *šiḵḇî*, the *k* after that *i* is soft (Gesenius §46d).
2. *šəmaʿ yaʿăqōḇ*. Singular masculine; the final guttural gives *a*.
3. *šiḵḇî*. The yiqtol *tiškəḇî* loses its prefix; *\*šəḵəḇî* would begin with two shewas, so the first becomes *i* (lesson 1.5).
4. "Please listen to this dream." *Nāʾ* softens the command into a request (Gesenius §110d).
5. Hebrew never negates the imperative. A negative command uses *ʾal* + the prefix form: *ʾal tišmōr* (Gesenius §46a; lesson 4.8).
6. The imperative exists only in the second person. For "let him" Hebrew uses the jussive, a prefix form: *yizkōr* (lesson 4.8).
7. "Do this, and you will live." The second imperative, joined by *wə-*, states the result the speaker intends (Gesenius §110f).
8. *Šəmōr* at the start is the imperative, "guard!". *Lišmōr* is *lə-* + the infinitive construct, which has the same shape: "to guard" (Gesenius §46a; lesson 4.10).
9. The long imperative of *māḵar*, with *-â*: an emphatic "sell!" (Gesenius §48i). Its first vowel is *i*, though the yiqtol of *māḵar* has *ō*; Gesenius notes this as an exception.
