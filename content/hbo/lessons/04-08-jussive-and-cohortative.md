## The idea

The imperative covers "you, do it!". Hebrew has two more forms for what the speaker wants, built on the yiqtol:

- the **jussive**, for "let him do it", "may it happen", and, with *ʾal*, "do not!";
- the **cohortative**, for "let me do it", "let us do it": a resolve or an invitation.

Together with the imperative they are called the **volitives**, the forms of the will.

Gesenius describes them as two modifications of the yiqtol: "a lengthened form of it (the cohortative), and a shortened form (the jussive)" (§48b). The cohortative adds *-â*: *ʾeʿĕḇōr* "I will pass" → *ʾeʿbərâ* "let me pass". The jussive is the yiqtol cut short where possible: *yihyeh* "it will be" → *yəhî* "let it be".

That "where possible" matters. Gesenius warns that the jussive is often not visibly shorter: "in the imperfect forms with afformatives, always", and in the strong qal too, it "coincides with the ordinary imperfect" (§48b, g). So *yišpōṭ* can be "he will judge" or "may he judge". The context decides: a wish, a blessing, *ʾal* "do not", or a request with *nāʾ*.

Arabic makes these distinctions visible throughout. Gesenius notes that classical Arabic has the indicative *yaqtulu*, the jussive *yaqtul*, and an "energetic" form whose pausal shape *yaqtulā* corresponds to the Hebrew cohortative (§48b). Hebrew has lost most of the final vowels that kept them apart, so the forms often merge.

## How it is built

### The cohortative: first person + *-â*

Take the 1SG or 1PL yiqtol and add stressed *-â*. As before the endings *-û* and *-î*, the stem vowel before it reduces to shewa (Gesenius §48c):

- *ʾešmōr* "I will keep" → *ʾešmərâ* "let me keep, I will keep";
- *naʿăḇōr* "we will pass" → *naʿbərâ* "let us pass";
- *niśrōp̄* "we will burn" → *niśrəp̄â* "let us burn".

In verbs ending in *-â* (lesson 4.16) there is no separate cohortative; the plain form does the work (Gesenius §108a, note).

Gesenius says the cohortative "lays stress on the determination underlying the action, and the personal interest in it", where the plain yiqtol only announces it (§108a). *ʾĀsurâ-nnāʾ* is not "I shall turn aside" but "I must turn aside and look".

### The jussive: second and third person, shortened if it can be

- **Strong qal:** no visible difference. *yišpōṭ* "may he judge".
- **Verbs ending in *-â*:** the ending is cut off. *yihyeh* → *yəhî* "let there be"; *yaʿăleh* → *yaʿal* "let him go up"; *taʿăśeh* → *taʿaś* "do (not)".
- **Hollow verbs and the hiphil:** the long vowel shortens. *yāqûm* → *yāqōm*; hiphil *yaqṭîl* → *yaqṭēl* (Gesenius §48g; lessons 4.15, 4.22).

These are the same short stems that the wayyiqtol uses (lesson 4.4). Lesson 4.6 discussed what that sharing may mean.

### Negative commands: *ʾal* and *lōʾ*

The imperative cannot be negated (lesson 4.7). For "do not!" use:

- ***ʾal* + jussive**: a warning or request in the moment. *ʾal-tîrāʾ* "don't be afraid", *ʾal-tišpəḵû-ḏām* "don't shed blood" (Gesenius §109c).
- ***lōʾ* + yiqtol**: a lasting prohibition, a law. *lōʾ tiḡnōḇ* "you shall not steal" (Gesenius §107o; lesson 4.3).

### *Nāʾ*

BDB calls *nāʾ* the "particle of entreaty or exhortation". It follows an imperative, a jussive or a cohortative, usually joined by maqqef, and it often doubles its *n* after a vowel (*ʾāsurâ-nnāʾ*). It may soften an order into a request, or press an exhortation (Gesenius §110d). Translate it "please", "now", or leave it out.

### Chains of wishes

A volitive followed by *wə-* + another volitive often expresses purpose or result: "bring it to me, *and let me eat*", that is, "so that I may eat" (Gesenius §108d, §109f). Lesson 6.4 returns to this.

### Building a resolve

"Let us pass through your land":

1. The speaker includes himself and wants something: cohortative, 1PL.
2. *ʿ-b-r*, yiqtol *naʿăḇōr*; add *-â*, and the *ō* reduces: *naʿbərâ*.
3. A polite request to a king: add *nāʾ*: *naʿbərâ-nnāʾ*.
4. *naʿbərâ-nnāʾ ḇəʾarṣeḵā* (Numbers 20:17).

## Where it applies

- **Commands and wishes for a third person:** *yəhî ʾôr*; *yēšeḇ-nāʾ ʿaḇdəḵā* (Gesenius §109b).
- **Blessings and curses:** "may the LORD judge", "may the LORD bless you" (Numbers 6:24–26; Gesenius §109b).
- **Negative commands, urgent or polite:** *ʾal* (*-nāʾ*) + jussive (§109c).
- **Resolves and soliloquies:** *ʾāsurâ-nnāʾ wəʾerʾeh* (§108b).
- **Invitations, "let us":** *nilbənâ*, *niśrəp̄â* (§108b).
- **Requests for permission, "let me":** *ʾeʿbərâ ḇəʾarṣeḵā* (§108c).
- **Purpose after another volitive:** "send me, and let me go" = "so that I may go" (§108d, §109f; lesson 6.4).
- **In conditions:** the jussive and cohortative can mark "should I ..., then ..." (§108e, §109h; lesson 6.7).
- **Polite first person in the third:** *ʿaḇdəḵā* "your servant" with a jussive is a courteous "let me".

## Watch out

**Do not look for a visible jussive in every case.** In the strong qal and in any form with an ending, the jussive equals the yiqtol (Gesenius §48b). *Yišpōṭ YHWH* is a wish because of the situation, not the form.

**Do not use *lōʾ* with the jussive for "don't!".** *\*lōʾ tîrāʾ* means "you will not fear" or "you shall not fear". For "don't be afraid!" Hebrew says *ʾal-tîrāʾ*.

**Do not use *ʾal* with the imperative.** *\*ʾal šəmaʿ* is impossible (lesson 4.7). *ʾal* takes the prefix form.

**Do not put *-â* on a second or third person.** The cohortative is a first-person form "with few exceptions" (Gesenius §48b). *\*yišmərâ* is not "let him guard"; that is the jussive *yišmōr*.

**Do not confuse the cohortative *-â* with the feminine *-â* or the directional *-â*.** *Nilbənâ* "let us make bricks" is a verb: it starts with the prefix *ni-*. *Ləḇēnâ* "brick" is a feminine noun; *miṣráymâ* "to Egypt" has the unstressed directional ending (lesson 5.2). The cohortative *-â* is stressed.

**Do not read the short jussive as a different verb.** *Yaʿal* and *yaʿăleh*, *yəhî* and *yihyeh*, *taʿaś* and *taʿăśeh* are the same verbs. The short ones are jussive (or, with *wa-*, wayyiqtol).

**Do not translate every *nāʾ* as "please".** In *ʾāsurâ-nnāʾ*, Moses talks to himself; "please" would be odd. "Now", "come", or nothing.

**Do not forget that a jussive can be second person.** *ʾal-tišlaḥ*, *ʾal-tîrāʾ* are jussives of "you". English grammar's "let him" covers only part of the form's use.

## Connections

Arabic keeps all of this visible: the jussive *yaktub*, the indicative *yaktubu*, and *lā* + jussive for "don't" (*lā taktub*). Hebrew *ʾal* + jussive is the same construction. The Arabic *li-* + jussive, *li-yaktub* "let him write", is another way of saying what Hebrew says with the bare jussive *yiḵtōḇ*. Spanish learners can think of the subjunctive: *¡que venga!* "let him come", *¡no vengas!* "don't come!": a separate form for wishes and negative commands, as in Hebrew.

## Exercises

1. Give the cohortative of *šāmar*, 1SG and 1PL.
2. Build "Let us remember the LORD".
3. Build "Let me go up", using the yiqtol *ʾeʿĕleh* of *ʿālâ*, and explain why there is no *-â*.
4. Translate *yəhî ʾôr* and say how you know it is a jussive.
5. *Yišpōṭ YHWH bênî ûḇênêḵā*: is the verb a yiqtol or a jussive? How do you decide?
6. A student writes *\*lōʾ-tîrāʾ* for "Don't be afraid!". Correct it and explain.
7. Take apart *ʾal-nāʾ ṯaʿăḇōr* and translate.
8. Why does *ʾal-tišpəḵû-ḏām* not show a shorter form?
9. Translate *hāḇâ nilbənâ ləḇēnîm* and identify each verb form.

## Worked answers

1. *ʾešmərâ*, *nišmərâ*. Add stressed *-â* to *ʾešmōr*, *nišmōr*; the *ō* reduces to shewa before it (Gesenius §48c).
2. *nizkərâ ʾeṯ-YHWH*. Cohortative 1PL of *zāḵar*: *nizkōr* + *-â* → *nizkərâ*.
3. *ʾeʿĕleh*. Verbs whose third root letter is *h* end in *-eh* and have no separate cohortative; the plain form serves (Gesenius §108a, note; compare *wəʾerʾeh* in Exodus 3:3).
4. "Let there be light." *Yəhî* is shorter than the yiqtol *yihyeh*; that short form, at the head of a command from God, is the jussive (Gesenius §109b).
5. In form it could be either. It is a jussive by context: Sarai is not predicting but appealing to God, as an oath or curse would (Gesenius §109b).
6. *ʾal-tîrāʾ*. A negative command in the moment uses *ʾal* with the jussive. *Lōʾ* with the yiqtol makes it a statement or a law: "you will not fear" (Gesenius §107o, §109c).
7. *ʾal* "not", *nāʾ* "please", *ṯaʿăḇōr* `pass.JUSS.2SG.M`: "Please do not pass by." A polite negative request (Gesenius §109c).
8. Because the form has an ending, *-û*, and with endings the jussive always coincides with the yiqtol (Gesenius §48b).
9. "Come, let us make bricks." *Hāḇâ*: imperative used as "come on!"; *nilbənâ*: cohortative 1PL of *lāḇan*; *ləḇēnîm*: the noun "bricks".
