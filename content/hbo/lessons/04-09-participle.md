## The idea

You have already met participles as nouns: *šōmēr* "guard", *rōʿeh* "shepherd", *šōp̄ēṭ* "judge", *ʾōyēḇ* "enemy". Each is a verb turned into a word for "someone who does it". The same forms also describe what someone is doing: *ʾānōḵî bōraḥaṯ* "I am fleeing".

Gesenius puts the participle "between the noun and the verb" (§116a). In form it is an adjective: it has gender and number, it takes the article, it can stand in the construct state. It has no person and no tense. But it describes an action in progress, not a permanent quality: "the participle active indicates a person or thing conceived as being in the continual uninterrupted exercise of an activity" (§116a).

That gives three uses: as a **noun** ("a guard"), as an **adjective** ("the man who is sitting"), and as the **predicate of a verbless clause** ("he is sitting"). The last is how Hebrew says "is doing", "was doing", "is about to do".

The qal also has a **passive participle**, *šāmûr* "guarded", which describes a state brought about by an action: *kāṯûḇ* "written", *bārûḵ* "blessed" (Gesenius §116a, e).

## How it is built

### The active participle: *qōṭēl*

The qal active participle has *ō* after the first root letter and *ē* after the second: *šōmēr*, *yōšēḇ*, *hōlēḵ*, *ʾōmēr*. Gesenius calls this shape *pôʿēl* after the model verb, and explains that its *ō* comes from an older *ā* (§50a–b). That *ō* is unchangeable: it is kept in every form. The *ē* is changeable and reduces when an ending takes the stress (§50d).

The endings are those of adjectives (lesson 3.11):

- **SG.M**: *šōmēr*.
- **SG.F**: either *šōmərâ* or, more often, the segolate *šōmereṯ* (Gesenius §50c). With a guttural: *bōraḥaṯ* "fleeing" (f.).
- **PL.M**: *šōmərîm*, the *ē* reduced before the stressed ending.
- **PL.F**: *šōmərôṯ*.
- **Construct**: *šōmēr* (SG), *šōmərê* (PL): "guards of".

The *ō* is often written with *waw*, *šômēr*; both spellings occur. Verbs of state do not form this participle; for them the qatal shape itself serves as an adjective: *zāqēn* "old", *mālēʾ* "full" (Gesenius §50b, §116b).

### The passive participle: *qāṭûl*

*Qāmeṣ* after the first root letter, *û* after the second: *šāmûr* "guarded", *kāṯûḇ* "written", *bārûḵ* "blessed", *ʾārûr* "cursed". The *ā* is pretonic and reduces when an ending is added: *kəṯûḇîm* "written (pl.)", *ʾărûrâ* "cursed (f.)".

### Saying "is doing"

The participle has no person, so the subject must be named, as in any verbless clause (lesson 2.1):

1. "I am fleeing": subject *ʾānōḵî* "I" + participle, feminine because Hagar speaks: *ʾānōḵî bōraḥaṯ*.
2. The time comes from the context. The same clause could be "I was fleeing" in a past narrative, or "I am about to flee".
3. Negate it with *ʾên* "there is not", as you negate other verbless clauses (lesson 2.7).

In the gloss: `flee.PTCP.SG.F`; the plural ending is its own piece, `sit.PTCP-PL.M`; the passive is `write.PASS.PTCP-PL.M`.

## Where it applies

- **As a noun for the doer:** *šōmēr* "guard", *ʾōyēḇ* "enemy", *rōʿeh* "shepherd", *pōṯēr* "interpreter" (Gesenius §116f–g).
- **With the article, as "the one who":** *hāʾōmēr ʾēlay* (§116o; lesson 6.3).
- **As the predicate of a verbless clause, "is doing":** *ʾānōḵî bōraḥaṯ* (§116n).
- **For what was going on when something else happened:** *wəhûʾ yōšēḇ*, *wəlôṭ yōšēḇ*, after or before a wayyiqtol (§116o).
- **For what is about to happen,** often after *hinnēh* or a pronoun: *ʾānōḵî hōlēḵ* (§116p; lesson 5.10).
- **With *hāyâ*, for a continuing action in the past:** *hāyâ rōʿeh* (§116r).
- **With *ʾên* or *yēš* and a suffix,** for "you are (not) doing": *ʾim-ʾênəḵā məšallēaḥ* "if you will not send" (Genesis 43:5; Gesenius §116q). Lesson 6.7.
- **The passive participle as adjective or predicate:** *kəṯuḇîm*, *bārûḵ*, *ʾārûr* (§116e).
- **In every stem:** the derived stems form participles with *m-*: *məḇaqqēš* (piel), *maqdîš* (hiphil), *niqdāš* (niphal). Lessons 4.20 to 4.23.

## Watch out

**Do not add a person to the participle.** *Bōraḥaṯ* is "fleeing", not "I flee". Without *ʾānōḵî* the clause has no subject. Spanish learners: it is like *huyendo*, but it agrees in gender and number like an adjective.

**Do not read the participle as present by default.** *Wəhûʾ yōšēḇ* in Genesis 18:1 is "as he was sitting". The participle states duration; the narrative gives the time (Gesenius §116d).

**Do not use *lōʾ* to negate a participle clause.** It is verbless; the negative is *ʾên*: *ûp̄ōṯēr ʾên ʾōṯô*. *\*lōʾ pōṯēr* is not the normal way to say "no one interprets".

**Do not confuse the participle and the qatal of verbs of state.** *Zāqēn* is both "he is old" (qatal) and "old" (adjective); there is no separate participle (Gesenius §116b). For action verbs the two differ: *šāmar* "he guarded", *šōmēr* "guarding".

**Do not lose the *ō*.** *\*šəmərîm* for "guards" is wrong: the *ō* of the participle never reduces; the *ē* does. The form is *šōmərîm*.

**Do not mix up the passive participle and a noun of the *qāṭôl* shape.** *Gāḏôl* "great" and *qāḏôš* "holy" look similar but have *ô*, and are adjectives, not participles. The passive participle has *û*: *šāmûr*, *kāṯûḇ*.

**Do not expect the participle to replace the wayyiqtol.** Gesenius notes that using the participle for the plain narrative past "is nevertheless quite foreign to Hebrew", though it became normal in Aramaic (§116c). In Hebrew prose the participle is background, not the next event.

**Do not read *ʾeṯ* after a participle as always the object marker.** In *rōʿeh ʾeṯ-ʾeḥāyw* it is the preposition "with" (lesson 2.9).

## Connections

Arabic has the same two participles in the plain verb: *kātib* "writing, a writer" (Hebrew *kōṯēḇ*) and *maktūb* "written" (Hebrew *kāṯûḇ*). Gesenius notes that the Hebrew *ō* of *qōṭēl* goes back to the long *ā* that Arabic *qātil* keeps (§50d). Modern Hebrew (`heb`) has turned the participle into its present tense: *ani kotev* "I write"; Biblical Hebrew is on the way there, but the participle is not yet a tense.

## Exercises

1. Give the four forms of the active participle of *kāṯaḇ*.
2. Build "the king is sitting in the gate".
3. Build "the men who are guarding the city", with the participle and the article.
4. Translate *wəhûʾ yōšēḇ peṯaḥ-hāʾōhel* and explain how you know the time.
5. Why is *hăšōmēr ʾāḥî* a noun use of the participle?
6. A student writes *\*bōraḥaṯ mippənê śāray* for "I am fleeing from Sarai". What is missing?
7. Build "Cursed is the man" with the passive participle of *ʾārar*.
8. Give the passive participle plural of *kāṯaḇ* and explain the first vowel.
9. Translate *ûp̄ōṯēr ʾên ʾōṯô* and explain the negative.

## Worked answers

1. *kōṯēḇ*, *kōṯeḇeṯ* (or *kōṯəḇâ*), *kōṯəḇîm*, *kōṯəḇôṯ*. The *ō* stays; the *ē* reduces before the stressed endings (Gesenius §50d).
2. *hammeleḵ yōšēḇ baššaʿar*. A verbless clause: subject, then participle as predicate, without the article (lesson 2.1).
3. *hāʾănāšîm haššōmərîm ʾeṯ-hāʿîr*. The participle takes the article and agrees with *ʾănāšîm*; it keeps its object with *ʾeṯ* (Gesenius §116f, o).
4. "As he was sitting at the entrance of the tent." The participle shows that the sitting was going on; the story it stands in is past, so English needs "was sitting" (Gesenius §116d, o).
5. *Šōmēr* is "a keeper", the person who does the guarding, bound to *ʾāḥî* "my brother" as its object (Gesenius §116g).
6. The subject. A participle has no person, so the clause needs *ʾānōḵî* (or *ʾănî*): *mippənê śāray ʾānōḵî bōraḥaṯ*. It is also feminine, so it must be a woman speaking.
7. *ʾārûr hāʾîš*. The passive participle as predicate, placed first, as in *bārûḵ ʾaḇrām*.
8. *kəṯûḇîm* (*kəṯuḇîm* in 1 Kings 11:41). The *ā* of *kāṯûḇ* is pretonic; when the ending takes the stress it is two places away and reduces.
9. "And there is no one to interpret it." The clause is verbless, with the participle as noun, so it is negated with *ʾên* "there is not" (lesson 2.7).
