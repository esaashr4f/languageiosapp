## The idea

English uses "to guard" after verbs and for purpose: "he went to guard". Hebrew has a form that does this, the **infinitive construct**: *šəmōr*, usually with a preposition in front: *lišmōr* "to guard".

The name tells you how it behaves. It is an infinitive, a noun naming the action. And it is *construct*: like a noun in the construct state, it can be bound to what follows it, either the doer or the thing done, and it can take a suffix. *Šomrô* is "his guarding". Gesenius calls it a verbal noun "of a much more flexible character" than the other infinitive (§114a). Its suffixes are normally those of nouns, which can name either the doer or the thing done (§61a), the infinitive absolute, which lesson 7.3 teaches.

Because it is a noun, it goes where nouns go: after prepositions. That gives Hebrew a compact way to say things English says with whole clauses:

- *lə-* + infinitive: "to do", "in order to do" (purpose);
- *bə-* or *kə-* + infinitive: "when (he) did", "as (he) was doing" (time);
- *min* + infinitive: "from doing", often "so as not to".

Gesenius notes that a preposition with the infinitive "may usually be resolved in English into the finite verb with a conjunction" (§114d): *bəḇorḥăḵā* "in your fleeing" = "when you fled".

## How it is built

### The form

In the strong qal the infinitive construct is the same shape as the masculine imperative: *šəmōr*, *zəḵōr*, *šəmaʿ* (Gesenius §45a, §46a). Verbs with *a* in the yiqtol often have *a* here too: *šəḵaḇ* (Gesenius §45c).

### With prepositions

The prepositions *bə-*, *lə-*, *kə-* meet a shewa at the start of the infinitive, and two shewas cannot stand together, so the preposition takes *i* (lesson 2.5):

- *lə-* + *šəmōr* → *lišmōr* "to guard";
- *bə-* + *šəmōr* → *bišmōr* "when guarding";
- *kə-* + *šəmōaʿ* → *kišmōaʿ* "when hearing".

*Min* assimilates its *n* and doubles the next letter, as before nouns: *mē-* before a guttural or *r*: *mērəʾōṯ* "from seeing".

### With a subject or object

The infinitive can be followed by a noun naming the doer: *kišmōaʿ lāḇān* "when Laban heard" (literally "as the hearing of Laban"). It can also take an object, with or without *ʾeṯ*, as a verb would: *kišmōaʿ lāḇān ʾeṯ-šēmaʿ yaʿăqōḇ* "when Laban heard the news of Jacob" (Gesenius §115).

### With suffixes

A suffix on the infinitive can be the doer ("his guarding" = "when he guarded") or the thing done ("to guard it"). The context decides. Before a suffix the infinitive takes the shape *šomr-*, with qameṣ ḥaṭuf, like the segolate nouns (lessons 3.4, 3.7; Gesenius §45b, §61):

- *šomrô* "his guarding" or "guarding him";
- *ləšomrāh* "to keep it (f.)";
- *bəḇorḥăḵā* "when you fled".

Before *-ḵā* and *-ḵem* a guttural gives *ă*: *bəḇorḥăḵā*.

### Weak verbs

Many common verbs have infinitives that look quite different: *lirʾōṯ* "to see" (*r-ʾ-h*), *laʿăśôṯ* "to do" (*ʿ-ś-h*), *lāḇôʾ* "to come" (*b-w-ʾ*), *lāṯeṯ* "to give" (*n-t-n*), *lāleḵeṯ* "to go" (*h-l-k*), *lāšeḇeṯ* "to sit" (*y-š-b*), *hĕyôṯ* "being" (*h-y-h*). Learn them now; lessons 4.13 to 4.18 explain them.

The commonest of all is ***lēʾmōr***, *lə-* + *ʾĕmōr* "to say", with the ʾ silent. It introduces direct speech and means "saying", or acts as a colon: *wayḇāreḵ ʾōṯām ʾĕlōhîm lēʾmōr* "God blessed them, saying ...".

In the gloss: `to=guard.INF`, with suffixes `guard.INF-3SG.F`.

### Building a clause

"When Laban heard the news":

1. A time clause, "when": *kə-* + infinitive.
2. *Š-m-ʿ*, infinitive *šəmōaʿ* (furtive pataḥ, lesson 1.4); with *kə-*: *kišmōaʿ*.
3. The doer follows it: *lāḇān*.
4. The thing heard is its object: *ʾeṯ-šēmaʿ yaʿăqōḇ*.
5. A time clause in narrative is usually introduced by *wayhî*: *wayhî kišmōaʿ lāḇān ʾeṯ-šēmaʿ yaʿăqōḇ*, and the main event follows in a wayyiqtol (Gesenius §111g, §114e).

## Where it applies

- **Purpose, "to", "in order to":** *lirʾōṯ ʾeṯ-hāʿîr*; *ləʿoḇdāh ûləšomrāh* (Gesenius §114g).
- **After verbs, as their object:** "be able to", "refuse to", "begin to", "finish": *lōʾ ṯûḵəlû laʿăḇōḏ* (§114m).
- **Introducing speech:** *lēʾmōr* "saying", after almost any verb of speaking or commanding.
- **Time, "when", with *bə-* or *kə-*:** *bəḇorḥăḵā*, *kišmōaʿ lāḇān*. Gesenius notes this use is "especially frequent" (§114e). Lesson 6.5.
- **After *wayhî*:** *wayhî kišmōaʿ ...* opens a scene; the main event follows as wayyiqtol (§111g).
- **"From doing", "too ... to":** *min* + infinitive: *mērəʾōṯ* (§114d).
- **"Be about to", "be to be done":** *hāyâ* + *lə-* + infinitive: *wayhî haššemeš lāḇôʾ* (§114h–k).
- **As a noun subject:** *hĕyôṯ hāʾāḏām ləḇaddô* (§114a).
- **Cause, with *yaʿan* or *ʿal*:** "because you said", *ʿal-ʾomrēḵ* (Jeremiah 2:35; §114d). Lesson 6.6.
- **Negative purpose** uses *ləḇiltî* + infinitive, "so as not to" (lesson 6.4).
- **In every stem:** *ləqaddəšô* "to keep it holy" (piel), *ləhaqrîḇ* "to offer" (hiphil); lessons 4.20 to 4.23.

## Watch out

**Do not put a person ending on the infinitive.** By analogy with French *pour qu'il garde* or Spanish *para que guarde*, you might want a finite verb after "to". Hebrew uses the infinitive, and if it needs a subject it adds a noun or a suffix: *bəḇorḥăḵā* "when you fled", not *\*bəḇāraḥtā*.

**Do not read a suffix on an infinitive as always the object.** *Bəḇorḥăḵā* is "your fleeing": the suffix is the doer. In *ləšomrāh* "to keep it" it is the object. Ask which makes sense.

**Do not confuse the infinitive and the imperative.** Both are *šəmōr*. After a preposition it is the infinitive; at the head of a command it is the imperative.

**Do not keep *lə-* with shewa before the infinitive.** *\*ləšəmōr* is impossible; two shewas cannot stand at the start of a word. The form is *lišmōr*.

**Do not translate *lēʾmōr* literally every time.** "Saying" is often too heavy in English. *Wayḏabbēr YHWH ʾel-mōšeh lēʾmōr* is "The LORD said to Moses:". Treat *lēʾmōr* as quotation marks.

**Do not use the infinitive construct as a finite verb.** *\*Lišmōr hammeleḵ* alone is not "the king guards". Gesenius stresses that the infinitive and participle are nouns in form (§114a, §116a). For a statement you need qatal, yiqtol or a participle in a verbless clause.

**Do not mistake *bə-* + infinitive for *bə-* + noun.** *Bəḡan* "in a garden" and *bišmōr* "when guarding" have the same preposition; the second is followed by a verb form. If the word after *bə-* looks like an imperative, it is an infinitive.

**Do not translate *kə-* + infinitive as "like".** *Kišmōaʿ lāḇān* is not "like Laban's hearing" but "when Laban heard".

## Connections

Arabic has nothing quite like the infinitive construct: after "in order to" it uses *li-* + a subjunctive finite verb (*li-yaktuba* "so that he writes"), and for "when" it uses *lammā* + a finite verb. Hebrew *lə-* and Arabic *li-* are the same preposition, used differently. Spanish *al* + infinitive is a closer match to *bə-* or *kə-* + infinitive: *al oír Labán la noticia* is exactly *kišmōaʿ lāḇān ʾeṯ-šēmaʿ*.

## Exercises

1. Build "to remember" and "when remembering" from *zāḵar*.
2. Build "Moses went up to write the words".
3. Build "when Jacob heard" with *bə-* or *kə-*, and say why the preposition has *i*.
4. Translate *ləʿoḇdāh ûləšomrāh* and say what the suffix refers to.
5. In *bəḇorḥăḵā*, is the suffix the doer or the thing done? How do you know?
6. A student writes *\*wayyēreḏ YHWH wəyirʾeh ʾeṯ-hāʿîr* for "the LORD came down to see the city". What would be more natural, and why?
7. Translate *wayhî haššemeš lāḇôʾ*.
8. What does *min* add in *wattiḵhênā ʿênāyw mērəʾōṯ*?
9. Explain the form *lēʾmōr*.

## Worked answers

1. *lizkōr*, *bizkōr* (or *kizkōr*). The infinitive *zəḵōr*; the preposition takes *i* before the shewa, and the *k* becomes hard after the closed syllable *liz-*.
2. *wayyaʿal mōšeh liḵtōḇ ʾeṯ-haddəḇārîm*. *Wayyaʿal* "and he went up" (lesson 4.6 vocabulary); purpose with *lə-* + infinitive *kəṯōḇ*.
3. *kišmōaʿ yaʿăqōḇ* (or *bišmōaʿ*). The infinitive begins with a shewa; *kə-* before it would make two shewas, so it becomes *ki-* (lesson 2.5).
4. "To work it and to keep it." The suffix *-āh* "her, it" refers to *gan* "garden", here feminine.
5. The doer: "your fleeing" = "when you fled". Jacob was the one who fled; Esau is named separately as the one fled from.
6. *wayyēreḏ YHWH lirʾōṯ ʾeṯ-hāʿîr*. Purpose is normally *lə-* + infinitive (Gesenius §114g). *Wə* + yiqtol can express purpose, but here the infinitive is the usual construction.
7. "As the sun was about to set ..." *Hāyâ* + *lə-* + infinitive: an action on the point of happening (Gesenius §114i).
8. "Away from": the dimness kept him from seeing. English says "too dim to see" (Gesenius §114d).
9. *Lə-* + *ʾĕmōr*, the infinitive of *ʾāmar*. The ʾ falls silent and the shewa of *lə-* lengthens to *ē*: "to say", used as "saying" to introduce direct speech.
