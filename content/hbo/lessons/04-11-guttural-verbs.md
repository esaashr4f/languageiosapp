## The idea

You have already seen many verbs whose vowels did not quite match *šāmar*: *ḥăšaḇtem* not *\*ḥəšaḇtem*, *yišmaʿ* not *\*yišmōr*-style *\*yišmōʿ*, *šaḥăṭû* not *\*šiḥṭû*. These are not new conjugations. They are the strong verb, adjusted for the **gutturals** ʾ, h, ḥ, ʿ, and in one respect r.

Lesson 1.2 gave the two facts behind every adjustment (Gesenius §22):

1. **A guttural cannot be doubled.** Where the pattern doubles a letter, a guttural refuses, and the vowel before it usually lengthens to make up (*compensatory lengthening*, lesson 2.2). R behaves like the gutturals here.
2. **A guttural prefers *a*-vowels, and takes a hatef instead of plain shewa.** Where the pattern puts shewa under the letter, a guttural takes *ă* (or *ĕ*, *ŏ*); where the pattern has *i*, *ē* or *ō* next to it, a guttural tends to pull it to *a*.

Gesenius describes the results under three headings, by the position of the guttural in the root: first (§63), middle (§64) and last (§65). That is the order of this lesson. Once you know the two facts, you can predict nearly every form, and you can find the root of a strange-looking form by reversing them.

Why does this matter so much? Because gutturals are common in roots. Of the verbs you have learned, *ʿāmaḏ*, *ʿāzaḇ*, *ʾāsap̄*, *ḥāzaq*, *šāḥaṭ*, *šāmaʿ*, *šālaḥ* and *māṣāʾ* all have one.

## How it is built

### First guttural: *ʿāmaḏ* "stand"

**Hatef for shewa.** Where *šāmar* has shewa under the first letter, a first guttural takes a hatef: *šəmartem* → *ʿămaḏtem*; infinitive *šəmōr* → *ʿămōḏ* (Gesenius §63a).

**The prefix syllable.** In the yiqtol the first root letter closes the prefix syllable: *yiš-mōr*. A guttural there does one of two things (Gesenius §63b–e):

- it closes the syllable firmly: *yaḥmōḏ* "he will desire", common with *ḥ*;
- or it takes a hatef that echoes the prefix vowel: *yaʿămōḏ* "he will stand", *yeʾĕsōp̄* "he will gather", *yeḥĕzaq* "he will be strong".

The prefix vowel also changes. Before a guttural, *i* becomes *a* (*yaʿămōḏ*) or *e* (*yeʾĕsōp̄*, *yeḥĕzaq*), because the guttural prefers those sounds (Gesenius §63e). As a rule, *ʿ* gives *ya-ʿă-*; *ʾ* gives *ye-ʾĕ-* or, in five verbs, *yō-* (lesson 4.13); *ḥ* gives either *yaḥ-* or *ye-ḥĕ-*.

**Hatef before shewa becomes a full vowel.** When a hatef would stand before another shewa, it becomes the full short vowel: *yaʿămōḏ* + *-û* → *yaʿamḏû* "they will stand" (lesson 1.5). *Wattaʿaḇḏû* "and you served" in Judges 10:13 is another example.

### Middle guttural: *šāḥaṭ* "slaughter"

**Hatef for vocal shewa.** Where the stem vowel reduces before an ending, the guttural takes *ă*: *šāmərû* → *šāḥăṭû*; *yišmərû* → *yišḥăṭû* (Gesenius §64a).

**The imperative and the infinitive.** Before *-î* and *-û* the imperative has *a* + hatef: *zaʿăqû* "cry out!", *šaḥăṭû* "slaughter!" (Gesenius §64a), not *\*ziʿqû*.

**The stem vowel.** The yiqtol and imperative mostly take *a*: *yišḥaṭ*, *šəḥaṭ*; *yizʿaq*; *yiḇḥar* (Gesenius §64b). The infinitive keeps *ō*: *šəḥōṭ*.

**Doubling.** In the piel, where the middle letter should double, a guttural cannot. Before *ʾ* and *r* the preceding vowel lengthens: *bērēḵ* "he blessed", not *\*birrēḵ* (lesson 4.21). Before *h*, *ḥ*, *ʿ* the vowel usually stays short and the guttural is "virtually" doubled (Gesenius §64d).

### Last guttural: *šālaḥ* "send"

***a* in the yiqtol and imperative.** *yišlaḥ*, *šəlaḥ*, *yišmaʿ*, *šəmaʿ* (Gesenius §65b). This is why *šāmaʿ* behaves like a verb of state in lesson 4.3.

**Furtive pataḥ after a long vowel.** Where the form needs an unchangeable long vowel before the guttural, the vowel stays and a furtive pataḥ is slipped in before the guttural (lesson 1.4): participle *šōlēaḥ*, passive participle *šālûaḥ*, infinitive *šəlōaḥ* (Gesenius §65a).

**Before a consonant ending.** A final guttural with silent shewa is unproblematic: *šālaḥtî*, *šāmaʿtā*. The 2SG.F, which would end in two consonants, takes a helping *a*: *hălôʾ šāmaʿat* "have you not heard?" (Ruth 2:8).

### Last letter *ʾ*: *māṣāʾ* "find"

When *ʾ* ends a syllable it falls silent, and the vowel before it is lengthened (Gesenius §74). So *māṣāʾṯî* "I found" keeps *ā*, and the *t* after it is soft, because it now follows a vowel. The yiqtol has *a*, lengthened: *yimṣāʾ*, *ʾemṣāʾ*. The same holds for *qārāʾ*, *nāśāʾ*, *yārēʾ*, *ḥāṭāʾ*, *ṭāmēʾ*.

### Reading backwards

Faced with *yeʾĕsōp̄*, ask: a prefix with *e* and a hatef under *ʾ*? That is a first-guttural yiqtol of *ʾ-s-p*. Faced with *šaḥăṭû*, the *a* + *ă* pattern before *-û* points to an imperative with a middle guttural, *š-ḥ-ṭ*.

## Where it applies

- **Every stem and every form.** The two rules work in the qal, and also in the niphal, piel, hiphil and the rest: niphal *neʿĕzaḇ* (with *e*), piel *bērēḵ* (no doubling), hiphil *heʿĕmîḏ* "he set up" (Gesenius §63e). Lessons 4.20 to 4.24.
- **Nouns too:** *naʿar* "boy" (not *\*neʿer*), *ḥereḇ*, *ʾōhel*, *zeraʿ* show the same preference for *a* and the helping vowel (lesson 3.4).
- **Prefixes and the article:** the article *hā-* before *ʾ* and *r* and *ha-* before *ḥ* (lesson 2.2) is the same refusal to double.
- **r**: only the no-doubling rule applies; for vowels r behaves as an ordinary letter (Gesenius §22b).
- **Combined with other weaknesses:** *ʾāmar* and *ʾāḵal* are first-guttural verbs with a special yiqtol (*yōʾmar*, lesson 4.13); *ʿāśâ*, *ʿālâ*, *rāʾâ* are guttural verbs that also end in *-â* (lesson 4.16). Apply the guttural rules first, then the weak-letter rules.

## Watch out

**Do not put plain shewa under a guttural.** *\*ʿəzaḇtem*, *\*šāḥəṭû* are impossible. The forms are *ʿăzaḇtem*, *šāḥăṭû*.

**Do not double a guttural.** *\*birrēḵ* for "he blessed" is wrong; *r* cannot double, and the *i* lengthens: *bērēḵ*.

**Do not expect *ō* in the yiqtol of a last-guttural verb.** *\*yišlōḥ*, *\*yišmōʿ*: the forms are *yišlaḥ*, *yišmaʿ* (Gesenius §65b).

**Do not drop the furtive pataḥ.** *\*šōlēḥ* is a misreading of שֹׁלֵחַ; the pataḥ is pronounced before the *ḥ*: *šōlēaḥ*.

**Do not read the hatef as a separate syllable that can carry stress.** *Yaʿămōḏ* is still two main syllables, *yaʿ-mōḏ*, in rhythm; the hatef is a short glide (lesson 1.5).

**Do not make the *t* hard after silent *ʾ*.** *\*māṣāʾtî* is wrong in this scheme: the *ʾ* is silent, the *ā* is the last sound before *t*, so the *t* is soft, תִי without dagesh: *māṣāʾṯî*.

**Do not mistake a first-guttural form for a different stem.** *Yeʾĕsōp̄* begins with *ye-* like a piel *yə-*, but the hatef under *ʾ* and the *ō* show a qal yiqtol.

**Do not apply the rules to *ʾ* when it is silent at the start of a syllable after a prefix.** In *yōʾmar* "he will say" the *ʾ* has gone silent and the prefix has *ō*. That is a separate small class (lesson 4.13).

## Connections

Arabic has the same gutturals but no hatef vowels; its vowels stay where the pattern puts them: *yaʿbudu* "he worships" beside Hebrew *yaʿăḇōḏ*. The Hebrew colouring of vowels near gutturals is a Masoretic feature of the reading tradition (lesson 1.3). Arabic does share the preference for *a* near a final guttural in the prefix conjugation: *yaftaḥu* "he opens", *yasmaʿu* "he hears", with *a* where most verbs have *u* or *i*.

## Exercises

1. Give the qatal 2PL.M of *ʿāzaḇ* and explain the first vowel.
2. Build "he will stand" and "they will stand" from *ʿāmaḏ*.
3. Build "he heard" (qatal), "he will hear" (yiqtol) and "hearing" (participle) from *šāmaʿ*, and explain each vowel near the *ʿ*.
4. Translate *wayyeʾĕsōp̄ raḡlāyw* and identify the root.
5. Why is the *t* soft in *māṣāʾṯî*?
6. A student writes *\*šimʿû* for "slaughter!" (plural) from *šāḥaṭ*. Correct it.
7. What root lies behind *yaʿamḏû*? Explain the vowels.
8. Why does the piel of *b-r-k* have *ē* in the first syllable?
9. Translate *pen-yišlaḥ yāḏô* and explain the *a*.

## Worked answers

1. *ʿăzaḇtem*. The stressed *-tem* reduces the first vowel, as in *šəmartem*; a guttural cannot take plain shewa, so it takes *ă* (Gesenius §63a).
2. *yaʿămōḏ*, *yaʿamḏû*. The prefix takes *a* before *ʿ*, which echoes it as *ă*; before the shewa of the plural the hatef becomes a full *a* (Gesenius §63b–d).
3. *šāmaʿ*: *a* in the qatal, as normal. *yišmaʿ*: *a* in the yiqtol instead of *ō*, because the last letter is a guttural (§65b). *šōmēaʿ*: the participle keeps *ē* and adds furtive pataḥ (§65a).
4. "He drew up his feet." *ʾ-s-p*: first guttural, with prefix *ye-* and *ĕ* under *ʾ* (Gesenius §63e).
5. The *ʾ* at the end of the syllable is silent, so the *t* follows the vowel *ā* directly and is soft (Gesenius §74).
6. *šaḥăṭû*. A middle guttural cannot take the shewa that *šimrû* has; the imperative takes *a* + hatef pataḥ (Gesenius §64a). *Šimʿû* is "hear!" from *š-m-ʿ*.
7. *ʿ-m-d*, "stand". Prefix *ya-*; the *ʿ* would have *ă*, but before the shewa under *m* it becomes a full *a*; the stem vowel has reduced before *-û*.
8. The piel doubles the middle letter, but *r* cannot double; the *i* of the first syllable is lengthened to *ē* instead (Gesenius §64d; lesson 4.21).
9. "Lest he stretch out his hand." The final *ḥ* gives the yiqtol *a* instead of *ō* (Gesenius §65b).
