## Before you start

This review covers the first half of part 4: the stems in outline (lesson 4.1), the full qatal (4.2) and yiqtol (4.3), the wayyiqtol and weqatal chains (4.4, 4.5), what the forms mean (4.6), the imperative (4.7), jussive, cohortative and *nāʾ* (4.8), the participle (4.9), the infinitive construct (4.10) and the guttural verbs (4.11). Parts 2 and 3 are assumed.

The weak verbs, the object suffixes and the derived stems come in the second half of part 4. Where the passage uses them, the notes gloss them, and the forms of common weak verbs from the vocabulary of lessons 4.4, 4.7 and 4.10 are treated as known words.

Do each exercise before you read the answer. Each answer names the lesson to reread.

## Passage

The passage is attested: Genesis 37:12–17, word for word from the Leningrad Codex. Jacob sends Joseph to look for his brothers, who are pasturing the flocks far from home. It is a short scene with a narrative chain, dialogue, commands, participles and infinitives. In verse 12 WLC marks the word *ʾeṯ* with dots above the letters (puncta extraordinaria); they are left out here (lesson 7.4).

## Exercises

1. Translate line 1. What does *lirʿôṯ* express, and which preposition carries that meaning?
2. Translate line 2. What kind of clause is *hălôʾ ʾaḥêḵā rōʿîm bišḵem*, and what does the participle say about time?
3. Translate line 3. Name the three verb forms *leḵ*, *rəʾēh* and *wahăšiḇēnî*, and say what *nāʾ* adds.
4. Translate line 4. Why is the narrative carried by wayyiqtols here?
5. Translate line 5. What does *wəhinnēh ṯōʿeh* do in the story?
6. Translate line 6. Why does *ʾeṯ-ʾaḥay* come first?
7. Translate line 7. Identify *nāsəʿû*, *šāmaʿtî*, *ʾōmərîm* and *nēləḵâ*.
8. In line 7 the man uses qatal (*nāsəʿû*, *šāmaʿtî*), but the narrator uses wayyiqtol (*wayyēleḵ*). Why?
9. Build "They were pasturing the flock in Shechem", with *hāyâ* and a participle.
10. Build "Go and see the welfare of your brothers", using an imperative and then a weqatal instead of a second imperative.
11. Build "When Joseph heard, he went after his brothers", with *wayhî* and *kə-* + infinitive.
12. Build "Let us go to Shechem" with the cohortative, and "Let him go to Shechem" with the jussive *yēlēḵ*.
13. Build "Do not send the boy!", to one man.
14. Build "You (pl.) have forsaken the LORD" from *ʿāzaḇ*.
15. Build "I will keep your flock" (yiqtol), and "Keep the flock!" to a woman.
16. Find and fix the error: *\*wayyišlaḥ lōʾ ʾeṯ-yôsēp̄* for "and he did not send Joseph".
17. Find and fix the error: *\*ʾal šəlaḥ ʾeṯ-hannaʿar* for "do not send the boy".
18. Find and fix the error: *\*ʿəmaḏtem* for "you (pl.) stood".
19. Find and fix the error: *\*yiškōḇ* for "he will lie down".
20. Find and fix the error: *\*ləšəmōr ʾeṯ-haṣṣōʾn* for "to guard the flock".
21. Find and fix the error: *\*rōʿîm* for "I am pasturing", said by Joseph alone.
22. Explain why *ṯōʿeh* in line 5 begins with a soft *ṯ*.

## Worked answers

1. "His brothers went to pasture their father's flock at Shechem." *Lə-* + infinitive construct expresses purpose (lessons 4.10; Gesenius §114g).
2. "Israel said to Joseph, 'Are not your brothers pasturing the flock at Shechem? Come, I will send you to them.' He said to him, 'Here I am.'" *Hălôʾ ʾaḥêḵā rōʿîm* is a verbless question with a participle as predicate (lessons 2.8, 4.9). The participle gives an action in progress; the time, now, comes from the context.
3. "He said to him, 'Go, please, see how your brothers are and how the flock is, and bring me word.'" *Leḵ* and *rəʾēh* are imperatives (lesson 4.7); *wahăšiḇēnî* is also an imperative, of the hiphil, with a suffix (lessons 4.19, 4.24, previewed). *Nāʾ* softens the command into a request (lesson 4.8).
4. "He sent him from the valley of Hebron, and he came to Shechem." These are the next events of the story, so they are wayyiqtols (lesson 4.4).
5. "A man found him, and there he was, wandering in the field. The man asked him, 'What are you looking for?'" *Wəhinnēh* + participle shows the scene through the man's eyes: Joseph is wandering at that moment (lessons 4.9, 5.10). It is background to the chain of wayyiqtols.
6. "He said, 'I am looking for my brothers. Please tell me where they are pasturing.'" The object is put first for emphasis: "my *brothers* I am looking for". *ʾĀnōḵî məḇaqqēš* is pronoun + participle, "I am seeking" (lesson 4.9).
7. "The man said, 'They have moved on from here, for I heard them saying, "Let us go to Dothan".' So Joseph went after his brothers and found them at Dothan." *Nāsəʿû*: qatal 3PL; *šāmaʿtî*: qatal 1SG (lesson 4.2); *ʾōmərîm*: participle plural, "saying" (lesson 4.9); *nēləḵâ*: cohortative 1PL, "let us go" (lesson 4.8).
8. The man is speaking, reporting completed events: qatal is the form of reports in speech (lesson 4.2). The narrator continues the main line of the story: wayyiqtol (lesson 4.4).
9. *hāyû rōʿîm ʾeṯ-haṣṣōʾn bišḵem*. *Hāyâ* + participle for an action going on over a period in the past (lesson 4.9; Gesenius §116r).
10. *leḵ wərāʾîṯā ʾeṯ-šəlôm ʾaḥêḵā*. The imperative begins the command and the weqatal continues it (lesson 4.5). *Rāʾîṯā* is the qatal 2SG.M of *rāʾâ* (lesson 4.16); with a strong verb the pattern would be *wəšāmartā*.
11. *wayhî kišmōaʿ yôsēp̄ wayyēleḵ ʾaḥar ʾeḥāyw*. *Wayhî* + *kə-* + infinitive + subject, then the main event as wayyiqtol (lesson 4.10; Gesenius §111g, §114e).
12. *nēləḵâ šəḵemâ*; *yēlēḵ šəḵemâ*. First person: cohortative with *-â*; third person: jussive (lesson 4.8). The directional *-â* on the place name is lesson 5.2.
13. *ʾal-tišlaḥ ʾeṯ-hannaʿar*. A negative command uses *ʾal* + jussive, which for *šālaḥ* is the same as the yiqtol, with *a* because of the final guttural (lessons 4.8, 4.11).
14. *ʿăzaḇtem ʾeṯ-YHWH*. 2PL.M *-tem*; the first vowel reduces, and the guttural takes *ă* (lessons 4.2, 4.11).
15. *ʾešmōr ṣōʾnəḵā* (Genesis 30:31 has *ṣōʾnəḵā ʾešmōr*); *šimrî ʾeṯ-haṣṣōʾn*. Yiqtol 1SG with prefix *ʾe-* (lesson 4.3); imperative 2SG.F *šimrî*, with *i* because two shewas cannot begin a word (lesson 4.7).
16. *wəlōʾ šālaḥ ʾeṯ-yôsēp̄*. Nothing can stand inside a wayyiqtol; with *lōʾ* the verb becomes a qatal after *wə* (lesson 4.4; Exodus 8:28).
17. *ʾal-tišlaḥ ʾeṯ-hannaʿar*. The imperative is never negated; use *ʾal* + jussive (lessons 4.7, 4.8; Genesis 22:12).
18. *ʿămaḏtem*. A guttural cannot take plain shewa; the first guttural takes *ă* (lesson 4.11; Gesenius §63a).
19. *yiškaḇ*. *Šāḵaḇ* is a verb of posture and takes *a* in the yiqtol (lesson 4.3; Gesenius §47h).
20. *lišmōr ʾeṯ-haṣṣōʾn*. Two shewas cannot stand at the start of a word, so *lə-* becomes *li-* before the infinitive (lesson 4.10).
21. *ʾănî rōʿeh* (or *ʾānōḵî rōʿeh*). The participle agrees with its subject, here one man, and needs an explicit subject pronoun (lesson 4.9).
22. *Ṯōʿeh* follows *wəhinnēh*, which ends in a vowel and is read closely with it, so the *t* is soft, as begadkefat letters are after a vowel (lesson 1.7).
