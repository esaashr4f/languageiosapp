## The idea

Since lesson 4.4 you have used *wayyōʾmer* "and he said" and *wayyittēn* "and he gave" as words. Now you can take them apart. Both come from roots whose first letter is weak: *ʾ-m-r* and *n-t-n*. A **weak verb** is one in which a root letter assimilates, falls silent or drops out in some forms. Gesenius notes that the weakness of these two classes shows mainly in the forms with a prefix (§66a, §68a), that is, the yiqtol, wayyiqtol, jussive and cohortative, and for *n* also the imperative and infinitive. The qatal and the participle are regular: *ʾāmar*, *ʾōmēr*, *nāp̄al*, *nōp̄ēl*.

The causes are sound changes, not new rules:

- **First *n* assimilates.** When *n* closes a syllable before another consonant, it is absorbed into that consonant, which is then doubled: *yinpōl* → *yippōl* "he will fall" (Gesenius §66d). The dagesh in the second root letter is the trace of the lost *n*.
- **First *ʾ* goes silent in five verbs.** In *ʾāmar*, *ʾāḵal*, *ʾāḇaḏ*, *ʾāḇâ* and *ʾāp̄â* the ʾ after the prefix lost its consonant value and merged with the prefix vowel: *ya-ʾ* → *yâ* → *yō* (Gesenius §68b). So *yōʾmar* "he will say", *yōʾḵal* "he will eat". The ʾ is still written, but not pronounced.

All other verbs beginning with ʾ (*ʾāsap̄*, *ʾāhaḇ*, *ʾāsar*, *ʾāḥaz* mostly) behave as first-guttural verbs (lesson 4.11): *yeʾĕsōp̄*.

## How it is built

### First *n*: *nāp̄al* "fall", *nāgaš* "come near", *nāṯan* "give"

**Yiqtol, wayyiqtol, jussive.** The prefix closes on the *n*; the *n* assimilates:

- *nāp̄al* → *yippōl*, *wayyippōl*, plural *wayyippəlû*;
- *nāgaš* (yiqtol vowel *a*) → *yiggaš*, *wayyiggəšû*;
- *nāśāʾ* "lift" → *yiśśāʾ*, *wayyiśśāʾ*;
- *nāsaʿ* "set out" → *yissaʿ*, *wayyissaʿ*.

**Imperative and infinitive.** In verbs with *a* in the yiqtol the *n* is dropped altogether (Gesenius §66b–c): imperative *gaš*, *gəšû* "come near!"; infinitive *gešeṯ*, a short stem lengthened by a feminine *-t*. In verbs with *ō* it stays: infinitive *nəp̄ōl* (Gesenius §66b).

**Where *n* stays.** Before a guttural the *n* cannot be absorbed, since a guttural cannot double: *yinḥal* "he will inherit" (Gesenius §66f).

**Nāṯan.** This verb is special three times over (Gesenius §66h–i):

- its yiqtol has *ē*, the only first-*n* verb that does: *yittēn* (from *yintēn*), *wayyittēn*; before maqqef *yitten-*;
- its imperative is *tēn* (before maqqef *ten-*), or the longer *tənâ*; feminine *tənî*;
- its *last* *n* assimilates too, in the qatal: *nāṯan* + *-tî* → *nāṯattî*;
- its infinitive is *tēṯ*, from *tint* → *titt*: *lāṯēṯ* "to give", before a suffix *tittô*.

**Lāqaḥ** "take" follows the first-*n* pattern though its first letter is *l* (Gesenius §66g): *yiqqaḥ*, *wayyiqqaḥ*, imperative *qaḥ*.

### First *ʾ*: *ʾāmar* "say", *ʾāḵal* "eat"

**Yiqtol.** Prefix + silent ʾ with *ō*, then *a* or *ē* in the second syllable, never *ō* (Gesenius §68c): *yōʾmar*, *tōʾḵal*, *yōʾmərû*. In pause *ē* is usual: *tōʾḵēl*.

**1SG.** Where two alephs would meet, the root's ʾ is dropped in writing: *ʾōmar* "I will say", not *\*ʾōʾmar* (Gesenius §68g).

**Wayyiqtol.** The stress moves back, and the last vowel shortens: *wayyṓʾmer* with segol (Gesenius §68d). At a pause it is *wayyōʾmar* (§68e). The 1SG keeps the long vowel: *wāʾōmar*, *wāʾōḵēl*.

**Infinitive.** *ʾĕmōr* with *lə-* becomes *lēʾmōr* (Gesenius §68f), and *ʾĕḵōl* gives *leʾĕḵōl*.

### Building a form

"And they came near" from *nāgaš*:

1. Wayyiqtol 3PL: *wa-* + doubling + *yi-* + stem + *-û*.
2. The *n* closes the prefix syllable, *\*yin-gəšû*, and assimilates to *g*: *yiggəšû*.
3. With *wa-*: *wayyiggəšû*. At a pause the stem vowel returns: *wayyiggāšû* (Genesis 45:4).

## Where it applies

- **All first-*n* verbs with a prefix:** *nāp̄al*, *nāgaš*, *nāśāʾ*, *nāsaʿ*, *nāṭaʿ*, *nāṭâ*, *nāḡaʿ*. Look for a dagesh in the second root letter right after the prefix.
- **The same assimilation in other stems:** niphal *niggaš* (from *ningaš*), hiphil *higgîḏ* "he told", hophal *huggaḏ* (Gesenius §66d). Lesson 4.24.
- **Short imperatives and infinitives** of *a*-verbs: *gaš*, *gešeṯ*, *tēn*, *tēṯ*; and of *lāqaḥ*: *qaḥ*, *qaḥaṯ*.
- **First-*ʾ* verbs with *ō*:** only *ʾāmar*, *ʾāḵal*, *ʾāḇaḏ*, *ʾāḇâ*, *ʾāp̄â* regularly, and *ʾāḥaz* mostly (*yōʾḥēz*) (Gesenius §68b).
- **Nouns** from these roots keep or lose the *n* by the same rule: *mappāl* "what falls", *mattānâ* "gift" (lesson 7.9).

## Watch out

**Do not look for a root with a doubled letter.** *Yippōl* is not from *\*p-p-l*; *yittēn* is not from *\*t-t-n*. A dagesh in the first visible root letter after a prefix often means a lost *n* (or the *l* of *lāqaḥ*).

**Do not apply the loss to the qatal.** *\*pālû* for "they fell" is wrong; the qatal is *nāp̄əlû*. The *n* goes only where it closes a syllable.

**Do not drop the *n* of *ō*-verbs in the imperative.** "Fall!" is *nəp̄ōl*, not *\*pōl* (Gesenius §66b–c). Only verbs with *a* in the yiqtol (and *nāṯan*) lose it.

**Do not read the silent ʾ of *yōʾmar*.** It is written but not pronounced: *yō-mar*. The transliteration shows it for the spelling.

**Do not expect every first-ʾ verb to have *ō*.** *Yeʾĕsōp̄* "he will gather", *yeʾĕhaḇ* "he will love" follow the guttural rules. Only the five verbs (and *ʾāḥaz*) take *yō-*.

**Do not double the ʾ in the 1SG.** *\*ʾōʾmar* is wrong; it is *ʾōmar*, *wāʾōmar*.

**Do not confuse *tēn* with the 2SG.M prefix *t-*.** *Ten-lî* "give me!" is an imperative; *titten-lî* "you will give me" is a yiqtol.

## Connections

Arabic keeps the *n* of such roots: *yanzilu* "he descends", *yanṣuru* "he helps". Hebrew assimilates it, as Aramaic often does. The silent ʾ of *yōʾmar* has an Arabic parallel in spoken forms such as Moroccan *yaklu*, *yākul* "he eats" for Classical *yaʾkulu*: the glottal stop of *ʾ-k-l* weakens after the prefix in both languages.

## Exercises

1. Give the yiqtol 3SG.M and 3PL.M of *nāp̄al*.
2. Build "and he came near" from *nāgaš*.
3. Build "I will give" and "you gave" (2SG.M) from *nāṯan*.
4. What root lies behind *wayyiśśāʾ*? Explain the dagesh and the final ʾ.
5. Translate *ten-lî hannep̄eš wəhārəḵuš qaḥ-lāḵ* and explain both imperatives.
6. Why is "I will say" *ʾōmar* and not *\*ʾōʾmar*?
7. A student writes *\*yeʾĕmōr* for "he will say". Correct it and explain.
8. Why does *nāḥal* keep its *n* in *yinḥal*?
9. Build "to give them bread" with the infinitive of *nāṯan*.

## Worked answers

1. *yippōl*, *yippəlû*. The *n* closes the prefix syllable and assimilates to *p*; the *ō* reduces before *-û* (Gesenius §66d).
2. *wayyiggaš*. Yiqtol *yiggaš* (from *yingaš*), with *wa-* and doubling (lesson 4.4).
3. *ʾettēn*; *nāṯattā*. The yiqtol has *ē* and the first *n* assimilated; in the qatal the last *n* assimilates to the *t* of the ending (Gesenius §66h).
4. *n-ś-ʾ*, "lift". The dagesh in *ś* replaces the first *n*; the final ʾ is silent, so the *a* lengthens to *ā* (lessons 4.11, 4.13).
5. "Give me the people, and take the goods for yourself." *Ten-* is the imperative of *nāṯan*, without either *n* (Gesenius §66h); *qaḥ* is the imperative of *lāqaḥ*, which drops its *l* in the same way (§66g).
6. Two alephs would meet, the prefix and the root letter; the root's ʾ is dropped in writing (Gesenius §68g).
7. *yōʾmar*. *ʾāmar* is one of the five verbs whose ʾ goes silent after the prefix and merges with it into *ō* (Gesenius §68b).
8. The second root letter is a guttural, *ḥ*, which cannot be doubled, so the *n* cannot assimilate to it (Gesenius §66f).
9. *lāṯēṯ lāhem leḥem*. The infinitive *tēṯ* with *lə-* (lesson 4.10); "to them" is *lāhem*.
