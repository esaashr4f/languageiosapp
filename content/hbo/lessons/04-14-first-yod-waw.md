## The idea

*Wayyēšeḇ* "and he sat", *wayyēṣēʾ* "and he went out", *wattēleḏ* "and she bore": in these forms the first root letter, *y*, has vanished, and the prefix has *ē* instead of *i*. Lessons 4.4 and 4.10 gave you some of them as words. This lesson explains them.

The explanation is historical, and Gesenius states it plainly (§69a). Most Hebrew verbs that begin with *y* did not begin with *y* originally. They began with *w*, as their Arabic relatives still do: Hebrew *yālaḏ* "bear a child", Arabic *walada*. At the start of a word Hebrew turned this *w* into *y*. After a prefix it went one of three ways (§69a): it disappeared, it became *y*, or it came back as a vowel.

So there are two groups, and a few strays:

- **Original *w*, dropped after the prefix:** *yāšaḇ*, *yāṣāʾ*, *yālaḏ*, *yāraḏ*, *yāḏaʿ*, and *hālaḵ*, which joins them (lesson 4.17). Gesenius lists these as the verbs that "always" lose the first letter (§69h). Yiqtol *yēšēḇ*, imperative *šēḇ*, infinitive *šeḇeṯ*.
- **Original *w* turned *y*, or original *y*:** *yārēʾ* "fear", *yāraš* "possess", *yāʿaṣ* "advise", *yāšēn* "sleep", and true first-*y* verbs such as *yāṭaḇ* "be good", *yāqaṣ* "wake". The *y* merges with the prefix vowel into *î*: *yîrāʾ*, *yîraš*, *yîṭaḇ* (§69d).
- **Assimilated *y*:** a few verbs treat *y* like *n* (lesson 4.13), with doubling: *yiṣṣaṯ* "it will burn", and some forms of *yāṣaq* "pour" (§71).

The qatal and the participle are regular in all of them: *yāšaḇ*, *yāšaḇtî*, *yōšēḇ*.

## How it is built

### Group 1: *yāšaḇ* "sit, dwell"

**Yiqtol, wayyiqtol, jussive.** The *w* drops; the prefix has an unchangeable *ē*; the stem has *ē* (from an older *i*), or *a* next to a guttural (Gesenius §69b):

- *yēšēḇ* "he will sit", *wayyēšeḇ* "and he sat" (the stress moves back and *ē* shortens to *e*, lesson 4.4);
- *yēṣēʾ* "he will go out", *wayyēṣēʾ*;
- *yērēḏ* "he will go down", *wayyēreḏ*;
- *tēlēḏ* "she will bear", *wattēleḏ*;
- *yēḏaʿ* "he will know", with *a* before the guttural; *ʾēḏaʿ* "I will know".

**Imperative.** The first letter drops entirely: *šēḇ* "sit!", *ṣēʾ* "go out!", *rēḏ* "go down!", *daʿ* "know!" (Gesenius §69b). With endings: *šəḇî*, *ṣəʾû*.

**Infinitive.** A short stem lengthened by feminine *-t* into a segolate (Gesenius §69b–c): *šeḇeṯ*, *lāšeḇeṯ* "to dwell"; *lāreḏeṯ* "to go down"; *daʿaṯ*, *lāḏaʿaṯ* "to know"; *lāṣēʾṯ* "to go out". Sometimes *-â* instead: *rəḏâ* "going down". This is the same compensation as *gešeṯ* from *nāgaš* (lesson 4.13).

### Group 2: *yārēʾ* "fear", *yāraš* "possess"

The *y* stays and merges with the prefix *i* into *î* (Gesenius §69d): *yîrāʾ* "he will fear", *tîrāʾ*, *ʾal-tîrāʾ* "do not fear"; *yîraš* "he will possess". The stem vowel is *a*. Imperative *yərāʾ*, *rəšû* (both kinds occur with *yāraš*).

True first-*y* verbs (Gesenius §69a, "originally began with Yôdh") look the same in the qal: *yîṭaḇ* "it will go well", *wayyîqaṣ* "and he woke".

### The *w* comes back

In the derived stems the old *w* reappears (Gesenius §69e, i), and that is how you can tell which group a verb belonged to:

- **niphal**: *nôlaḏ* "he was born" (*naw-* → *nô-*); infinitive *hiwwāleḏ*, with the *w* as a full doubled consonant;
- **hiphil**: *hôṣîʾ* "he brought out", *hôlîḏ* "he fathered", *hôšîḇ* "he settled" (*haw-* → *hô-*);
- **hophal**: *hûšaḇ*.

Lesson 4.24 returns to these. For now, *hô-* or *nô-* at the start of a verb is a strong hint of a first-*w* root.

### Building a form

"And he went down to Egypt":

1. Wayyiqtol 3SG.M of *yāraḏ*: Group 1, so the *y* drops and the prefix is *yē-*.
2. Yiqtol *yērēḏ*; with *wa-* and the stress pulled back: *wayyēreḏ*.
3. Destination with the directional ending: *miṣráymâ* (lesson 5.2).
4. *wayyēreḏ miṣráymâ*.

## Where it applies

- **Group 1, y lost:** *yāšaḇ*, *yāṣāʾ*, *yālaḏ*, *yāraḏ*, *yāḏaʿ* (and the rarer *yāḥaḏ*, *yāqaʿ*), and *hālaḵ* (Gesenius §69h, x; lesson 4.17).
- **Group 2, y kept as î:** *yārēʾ*, *yāraš*, *yāʿaṣ*, *yāšēn*, *yāʿēp̄* "be weary" (§69h), and true first-y verbs: *yāṭaḇ*, *yāqaṣ*, *yānaq* "suck".
- **The *w* in the derived stems:** niphal *nô-*, hiphil *hô-*, hophal *hû-*; *hôṣîʾ*, *hôlîḏ*, *nôlaḏ*. Lesson 4.24.
- **The piel keeps *y*:** the participle *məyalleḏeṯ* "midwife" (§69k).
- **Nouns:** the old *w* survives in *wālāḏ* "child" (lesson 2.7); *môšāḇ* "dwelling" and *môʿēḏ* "appointed time" have *ô* from *maw-*.
- **yāḵōl "be able"** is irregular: yiqtol *yûḵal*, *tûḵəlû* (lesson 4.10). Learn it as a word.

## Watch out

**Do not look for a two-letter root.** *Yēšēḇ* is from *y-š-b*, not *\*š-b*. When the prefix vowel is *ē* and only two root letters follow, suspect a lost first *w*.

**Do not confuse *wayyēšeḇ* "he sat" with *wayyāšoḇ* "he returned".** The first is *y-š-b*; the second is the hollow root *š-w-b* (lesson 4.15). The vowels decide: *ē* + *e* against *ā* + *o*.

**Do not give the Group 1 verbs a Group 2 yiqtol.** *\*yîšaḇ* is wrong; the form is *yēšēḇ*. Conversely *\*yērāʾ* for "he will fear" is wrong: it is *yîrāʾ*.

**Do not read the infinitive *šeḇeṯ* as a noun.** It looks like a segolate noun (*šeḇeṯ* is also "sitting, rest"), but after *lə-* it is the infinitive: *lāšeḇeṯ* "to dwell".

**Do not drop the *y* in the qatal.** *\*šaḇtî* for "I sat" is wrong; the qatal is regular: *yāšaḇtî*.

**Do not take *hô-* for the article.** *Hôṣîʾ* "he brought out" is a hiphil of *y-ṣ-ʾ*; the article is *ha-* with doubling.

**Do not confuse *ʾēḏaʿ* "I will know" with *ʾeḏaʿ*-like nouns.** The 1SG yiqtol of *yāḏaʿ* has *ʾē-*; the ʾ is the prefix, not a root letter.

## Connections

Arabic keeps the *w*: *walada* "bear", *waḍaʿa* "put", *wariṯa* "inherit" (Hebrew *yāraš*). In the Arabic prefix form it often drops, as in Hebrew group 1: *yalidu* "he begets", *yaḍaʿu* "he puts". Gesenius makes the same comparison (§69e). Hebrew *yālaḏ* and Arabic *walada* are the same root; so are Hebrew *yôm* and Arabic *yawm*, which has nothing to do with this class, because there the *y* was original.

## Exercises

1. Give the yiqtol 3SG.M, the imperative and the infinitive with *lə-* of *yāšaḇ*.
2. Build "and he went down" from *yāraḏ*.
3. Build "and she bore a son" from *yālaḏ*.
4. Why is "he will fear" *yîrāʾ* and not *\*yērāʾ*?
5. What root lies behind *hôṣîʾ*? What does the *ô* tell you?
6. A student writes *\*šaḇnû* for "we sat". Correct it.
7. Translate *ʾal-tîrāʾ mērəḏâ miṣraymâ* and explain both verb forms.
8. *Wayyēšeḇ* or *wayyāšoḇ*: which is "he sat", and how can you tell?
9. Translate *šəḇî ʾalmānâ ḇêṯ-ʾāḇîḵ* and identify the verb.

## Worked answers

1. *yēšēḇ*; *šēḇ*; *lāšeḇeṯ*. The *w* drops after the prefix and in the imperative; the infinitive adds *-t* to the short stem (Gesenius §69b–c).
2. *wayyēreḏ*. Yiqtol *yērēḏ*; the wayyiqtol pulls the stress back and shortens *ē* to *e* (lesson 4.4).
3. *wattēleḏ bēn*. 3SG.F *wat-*; the *y* of *y-l-d* has dropped (Genesis 4:25 has *wattēleḏ bēn*).
4. *Yārēʾ* belongs to the group that keeps its first letter as *y*, which merges with the prefix *i* into *î* (Gesenius §69d, h).
5. *y-ṣ-ʾ*, "go out": a hiphil, "bring out". The *ô* comes from *haw-*, the old first *w* reappearing (Gesenius §69i).
6. *yāšaḇnû*. The qatal of first-*y* verbs is regular (Gesenius §69k).
7. "Do not be afraid to go down to Egypt." *tîrāʾ*: jussive of *yārēʾ*, with *î*; *rəḏâ*: infinitive of *yāraḏ* with *-â*, after *min* (lessons 4.8, 4.10).
8. *Wayyēšeḇ* "he sat": *ē* in the prefix, from *y-š-b*. *Wayyāšoḇ* "he returned" has *ā* in the prefix and comes from the hollow root *š-w-b* (lesson 4.15).
9. "Remain a widow in your father's house." *Šəḇî*: imperative 2SG.F of *yāšaḇ*, with the *y* dropped.
