## The idea

*Wayyāqom* "and he rose", *wayyāśem* "and he put", *wayyāḇōʾ* "and he came", *mēṯ* "he died", *bāʾ* "he came": you have met all of these. Their roots are written with three letters, *q-w-m*, *ś-y-m*, *b-w-ʾ*, *m-w-t*, but in most forms you see only two consonants, with a long vowel between them. These are the **hollow verbs**: verbs whose middle "letter" is really a vowel, *û*, *î* or *ô*.

Gesenius describes them as stems with one syllable that were brought into line with the three-consonant system "by strengthening the vocalic element" (§72a). In other words, the root is in effect two consonants and a long vowel: *q-û-m*, *ś-î-m*. For this reason the dictionary does not list them by the qatal 3SG.M, *qām*, but by the infinitive, which shows the vowel: *qûm*, *śîm*, *bôʾ*, *šûḇ*, *mûṯ* (Gesenius §39b, §72a). BDB lists them under the three-letter root, *qwm*.

There are three kinds, by the vowel:

- **û verbs** (the large majority): *qûm* "rise", *šûḇ* "return", *mûṯ* "die", *rûṣ* "run", *nûs* "flee", *sûr* "turn aside";
- **î verbs**: *śîm* "put", *šîṯ* "set", *rîḇ* "quarrel", *dîn* "judge", *šîr* "sing";
- **ô verbs**: *bôʾ* "come", *bôš* "be ashamed".

## How it is built

### Qatal: two consonants and *ā*

The qatal has no middle letter at all. The stem is *qām*, with *ā*, short *a* in a closed syllable before a consonant ending (Gesenius §72b):

- *qām* "he rose", *qāmâ* "she rose", *qāmû* "they rose";
- *qamtā* "you rose", *qamtî* "I rose", *qamnû*;
- *śām* "he put"; *šāḇ* "he returned"; *bāʾ* "he came", *bāʾṯā* "you came".

The stress stays on the stem in *qā́mâ*, *qā́mû*; the endings do not take it, unlike *šāmərâ* (lesson 4.2).

Verbs of state have *ē* or *ô*: *mēṯ* "he died, is dead", *mēṯâ* "she died"; *bôš* "he was ashamed" (Gesenius §72c).

### Yiqtol: the long vowel inside

The prefix has *ā* (from an original *a* in an open syllable) and the stem keeps its long vowel (Gesenius §72d, f):

- *yāqûm* "he will rise", *yāšûḇ*, *yāmûṯ*, *yārûṣ*;
- *yāśîm* "he will put";
- *yāḇôʾ* "he will come".

### Jussive and wayyiqtol: the vowel shortens

The short form shortens the long vowel, and the wayyiqtol pulls the stress back onto the prefix (Gesenius §72f, §49d):

- jussive *yāqōm*; wayyiqtol *wayyā́qom*, written וַיָּקָם, with qameṣ ḥaṭuf (*wayyāqōm* in pause);
- *yāšûḇ* → *wayyāšoḇ*; *yāmûṯ* → *wayyāmoṯ* (in pause *wayyāmōṯ*);
- *yāśîm* → *wayyāśem*;
- *yāḇôʾ* → *wayyāḇōʾ* (the ô verbs barely change).

With an ending the long vowel comes back: *wayyāqumû* "and they rose" (with *u*).

### Imperative and infinitive

Both have the full vowel: *qûm* "get up!" and "rising", *šûḇ*, *śîm*, *bôʾ*. Feminine *qûmî*, plural *qûmû*. The infinitive takes suffixes like a noun: *šûḇəḵā* "your returning".

### Participle

The active participle is the same shape as the qatal, with *ā*: *qām* "rising", *bāʾ* "coming", *mēṯ* "dead", feminine *bāʾâ*, plural *qāmîm* (Gesenius §72g). Gesenius explains the *ā* as the long *â* of the participle (Arabic *qāʾim*), so it does not reduce. Qatal and participle can look alike; the stress differs in the feminine: qatal *bā́ʾâ* "she came", participle *bāʾā́* "coming" (the acute is the guide's). Context usually decides.

### Building a form

"And they returned":

1. Root *š-w-b*, a hollow *û* verb.
2. Yiqtol 3PL.M: prefix *yā-*, stem *-šûḇ-*, ending *-û*: *yāšûḇû*.
3. Wayyiqtol: *wa-* + doubling: *wayyāšuḇû*, with *u* before the ending.

## Where it applies

- **All hollow verbs in the qal,** with the three vowel types. Common ones: *qûm*, *šûḇ*, *mûṯ*, *bôʾ*, *śîm*, *sûr*, *rûṣ*, *nûs*, *lûn*, *gûr*, *bôš*, *šîṯ*, *rîḇ*, *dîn*, *nûaḥ*, *šîr*.
- **The derived stems** have their own shapes: hiphil *hēqîm* "he set up", *hēḇîʾ* "he brought", *hēšîḇ* "he brought back", *hēmîṯ* "he killed"; niphal *nāḵôn* "it is firm" (Gesenius §72d, i). Lesson 4.24.
- **Instead of the piel,** hollow verbs use a special stem, the polel, which repeats the last letter: *qômēm* "raise up", *mōṯēṯ* "put to death" (Gesenius §72m; lesson 7.2).
- **Nouns** from hollow roots: *māqôm* "place" (*q-w-m*), *mānôaḥ* "resting place" (*n-w-ḥ*), *môṯ* "death" in construct.

## Watch out

**Do not look for three consonants in every form.** *Qām* is a full qatal 3SG.M; the root *q-w-m* never shows its *w* in the qal except as the vowel *û*.

**Do not cite the verb as *qām*.** The dictionary form of a hollow verb is the infinitive: *qûm*, *śîm*, *bôʾ* (Gesenius §72a). (This guide still lists *mēṯ* under its qatal, as lesson 2.6 introduced it.)

**Do not reduce the stem vowel before vowel endings in the qatal.** *\*qəmû* is wrong; the form is *qā́mû*, stressed on the stem.

**Do not keep the long vowel in the wayyiqtol.** *\*wayyāqûm*, *\*wayyāśîm* are wrong: *wayyāqom*, *wayyāśem*. With an ending the vowel comes back short: *wayyāqumû*.

**Do not confuse *wayyāšoḇ* "he returned" with *wayyēšeḇ* "he sat".** Hollow *š-w-b* has *ā* in the prefix; first-*y* *y-š-b* has *ē* (lesson 4.14).

**Do not confuse *wayyāśem* (*ś-y-m*) with *wayyāšeḇ*-like forms.** The *ś* (dot on the left) and the *m* tell you: *śîm* "put".

**Do not read every *bāʾ* or *bāʾâ* as past.** It may be the participle "coming"; look at the context and the stress (Gesenius §72g).

**Do not mistake the prefix *ā* for the article.** *Yāqûm* is a yiqtol; nothing here is "the".

## Connections

Arabic hollow verbs work the same way: *qāma* "he rose", *yaqūmu* "he rises", *qum!* "rise!", *lam yaqum* "he did not rise", with the vowel shortened in the short form, as in Hebrew *wayyāqom*. Gesenius points to the Arabic *yaqūmu* for the Hebrew *yāqûm* (§72f). The Arabic participle *qāʾim* "standing" shows the long *ā* that Hebrew keeps in *qām*.

## Exercises

1. Give the qatal 3SG.M, 3PL and 1SG of *šûḇ*.
2. Build "he will return" and "and he returned".
3. Build "and he put" from *śîm*, and "and they put".
4. Translate *kî-ʿāp̄ār ʾattâ wəʾel-ʿāp̄ār tāšûḇ*.
5. Why is the dictionary form *qûm* and not *qām*?
6. A student writes *\*wayyāmûṯ* for "and he died". Correct it.
7. *Bāʾâ* can be "she came" or "coming (f.)". How do you decide in *wəhinnēh rāḥēl bittô bāʾâ*?
8. What is the difference between *wayyāšoḇ* and *wayyēšeḇ*?
9. Build "Get up!" to a man and to a group, and "Turn aside!" to a man.

## Worked answers

1. *šāḇ*, *šā́ḇû*, *šaḇtî*. No middle consonant; *ā* in the open syllable, *a* in the closed one before *-tî* (Gesenius §72b).
2. *yāšûḇ*; *wayyāšoḇ*. The yiqtol keeps *û*; the wayyiqtol shortens it to *o* and pulls the stress back (Gesenius §72f).
3. *wayyāśem*; *wayyāśîmû*. The singular shortens *î* to *e*; with an ending the vowel stays (*wayyāśîmû* is attested 30×).
4. "For you are dust, and to dust you shall return." A verbless clause, then a yiqtol of the future with the place fronted (lessons 2.1, 4.3).
5. The qatal 3SG.M hides the middle vowel; the infinitive and imperative show it, *qûm*, so grammars and dictionaries use that form (Gesenius §39b, §72a).
6. *wayyāmoṯ* (in pause *wayyāmōṯ*, Genesis 5:5). The short form shortens *û* (Gesenius §72f).
7. With *wəhinnēh* the scene is presented as happening before the speakers' eyes: Rachel is approaching, so the participle "coming" (lessons 4.9, 5.10).
8. *Wayyāšoḇ* "and he returned" is from hollow *š-w-b*, with *ā* in the prefix; *wayyēšeḇ* "and he sat" is from first-*y* *y-š-b*, with *ē* (lesson 4.14).
9. *qûm*, *qûmû*; *sûr*. Imperatives of hollow verbs have the full vowel.
