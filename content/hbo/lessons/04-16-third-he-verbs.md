## The idea

*Bānâ* "build", *ʿāśâ* "do, make", *rāʾâ* "see", *hāyâ* "be", *ʿālâ* "go up", *ʿānâ* "answer", *šāṯâ* "drink": many of the commonest verbs end in *-â*, written with a final *ה*. This is the largest weak class. Its short forms, *wayyiḇen*, *wayyaʿaś*, *wayyarʾ*, *wayhî*, *wayyaʿal*, have been in the vocabulary since lesson 4.4.

The *h* is not a root consonant. Gesenius explains that these roots ended in *y* (a few in *w*), and that the *h* is "a purely orthographic indication of a final vowel" (§75a): the vowel letter of lesson 1.4. Where the old *y* stood at the end of the word it was lost and a vowel remained. That gives the class its two habits:

- **The final vowel is fixed by the form**, not by the root (Gesenius §75c): *-â* in every qatal, *-eh* in every yiqtol and participle, *-ēh* in every imperative, and *-ôṯ* in the infinitive construct.
- **Before a consonant ending, the old *y* comes back as a vowel**, usually *î* in the qal: *bānîṯî* "I built" (Gesenius §75f).

And one more, which makes them hard to recognise: **in the short forms the ending is cut off entirely** (lesson 4.8): *yiḇneh* → *wayyiḇen*.

## How it is built

### Qatal

- 3SG.M *bānâ*; 3PL *bānû* (the *-û* replaces the final vowel).
- 3SG.F *bānəṯâ*: the old feminine *-t* plus the ordinary *-â*, a doubled feminine ending (Gesenius §75i, by way of §44f).
- 1st and 2nd person: *î* + the ending: *bānîṯî*, *bānîṯā*, *bānîṯ*; plural *bānînû*, *bənîṯem*. *Rāʾîṯî* "I saw", *ʿāśîṯā* "you did".

### Yiqtol and participle: *-eh*

*yiḇneh* "he will build", *tiḇneh*, *ʾeḇneh*, *niḇneh*; *yiḇnû* "they will build" (the ending replaces *-eh*). With a first guttural the guttural rules apply as well (lesson 4.11): *yaʿăśeh*, *yaʿăleh*. With *rāʾâ*: *yirʾeh*. Participle *bōneh*, *ʿōśeh*, *rōʾeh*, *rōʿeh* "shepherd".

### Imperative *-ēh*, infinitive *-ôṯ*

Imperative *bənēh* "build!", *ʿăśēh* "do!", *rəʾēh* "see!"; plural *bənû*. Infinitive construct *bənôṯ*, *liḇnôṯ* "to build"; *laʿăśôṯ*, *lirʾôṯ*, *lihyôṯ*. The *-ôṯ* is a feminine ending, like the *-eṯ* of *šeḇeṯ* (Gesenius §75c).

### The short forms: jussive and wayyiqtol

Drop *-eh*. What is left is two consonants after the prefix, and Hebrew then adjusts it:

- *yiḇneh* → *wayyiḇen* "and he built": a helping *e* between the last two consonants, like a segolate (lesson 3.4);
- *yaʿăśeh* → *wayyaʿaś* "and he made"; *yaʿăleh* → *wayyaʿal* "and he went up";
- *yirʾeh* → *wayyarʾ* "and he saw" (with *a* in the prefix);
- *yihyeh* → *wayhî* "and it was", jussive *yəhî*: here the old *y* shows as *î*;
- *yēṭ* from *nāṭâ*, *wayyēṭ* "and he stretched out" (first *n* too, lesson 4.13).

The 1SG wayyiqtol often keeps the long form: *wāʾerʾeh* "and I saw" (Gesenius §49e). The plural keeps its ending: *wayyiḇnû*, *wayyaʿăśû*.

### Two weaknesses at once

Many verbs of this class have a weak first letter as well. Apply both rules: *nāṭâ* (first *n*, last *h*): *yiṭṭeh*, *wayyēṭ*; *ʿālâ* (first guttural): *yaʿăleh*, *wayyaʿal*; *hāyâ*: lesson 4.18.

### Building a form

"And they built a city":

1. Root *b-n-h*, wayyiqtol 3PL.M: the plural ending replaces *-eh*: *yiḇnû*.
2. With *wa-* and doubling: *wayyiḇnû*.
3. *wayyiḇnû ʿîr*.

## Where it applies

- **The whole class:** *bānâ*, *ʿāśâ*, *rāʾâ*, *hāyâ*, *ʿālâ*, *ʿānâ*, *šāṯâ*, *kālâ* "finish", *gālâ* "uncover, go into exile", *bāḵâ* "weep", *pānâ* "turn", *ḥānâ* "camp", *rāḇâ* "be many", *qānâ*, *rāʿâ*, *nāṭâ*, *ḥārâ*.
- **The derived stems:** the same endings in every stem: piel *ṣiwwâ* "he commanded", *yəṣawweh*, wayyiqtol *wayṣaw*; hiphil *heʿĕlâ* "he brought up", wayyiqtol *wayyaʿal* (Genesis 8:20 has this hiphil *wayyaʿal ʿōlōṯ* "he offered burnt offerings"). Lessons 4.21, 4.22, 4.24.
- **Nouns** in *-eh* from these roots: *maʿăśeh* "deed", *marʾeh* "sight", *miqneh* "livestock", *śāḏeh* "field" (lesson 3.1).
- **Passive participle** brings back the old *y*: *bānûy* "built" (Gesenius §75c).

## Watch out

**Do not read the final *h* as a consonant.** *Bānâ* has three root letters *b-n-h* by convention, but the *h* is a vowel letter (Gesenius §75a). A real consonantal *h* has mappiq, *gāḇah* "be high", and behaves like a guttural.

**Do not build the 1st and 2nd person qatal on *bānā-*.** *\*bānātî* is wrong; the form is *bānîṯî*, with the old *y* back as *î* (Gesenius §75f).

**Do not put *-â* on a yiqtol.** *\*yiḇnâ* is wrong; every yiqtol of this class ends in *-eh*.

**Do not expect the short forms to show the third root letter.** *Wayyarʾ* is from *r-ʾ-h*, *wayyaʿaś* from *ʿ-ś-h*, *wayyiḇen* from *b-n-h*. If a wayyiqtol has only two consonants after the prefix and no long vowel, try adding *-h*.

**Do not confuse *wayyarʾ* "and he saw" with *wayyîrāʾ* "and he feared".** Same consonants *r* and *ʾ*, different roots: *r-ʾ-h* and *y-r-ʾ* (lesson 4.14).

**Do not confuse the 3SG.F qatal *bānəṯâ* with a noun.** The double feminine ending looks like a noun in *-ṯâ*; it is a verb.

**Do not use the long form for "and he did".** *\*wayyaʿăśeh* is not the narrative form (except occasionally in the 1SG); the narrative wayyiqtol is *wayyaʿaś*.

## Connections

Arabic has the same class, with the third letter still visible as a vowel: *banā* "he built", *yabnī* "he builds", *lam yabni* "he did not build", with the vowel shortened in the short form, like Hebrew *wayyiḇen*. Arabic *ramā* "he threw", *raʾā* "he saw" are cognate with Hebrew *rāmâ*, *rāʾâ*. The Hebrew *-â* is spelled with *h* where Arabic writes *y* or *ʾalif*.

## Exercises

1. Give the qatal 3SG.F, 1SG and 3PL of *ʿāśâ*.
2. Build "he will see" and "and he saw".
3. Build "and they made" and "and she made".
4. Build "to see the city" and "to build the city".
5. What root lies behind *wayyiḇen*? How do you know?
6. A student writes *\*ʿāśātî* for "I did". Correct it.
7. Translate *ʾĕlōhîm yirʾeh-llô haśśeh ləʿōlâ*.
8. Explain the difference between *wayyarʾ* and *wayyîrāʾ*.
9. Why is the infinitive of *bānâ* *bənôṯ*, with a *t*?

## Worked answers

1. *ʿāśəṯâ*, *ʿāśîṯî*, *ʿāśû* (Gesenius §75). The 3SG.F has the double feminine ending; the 1SG has *î* before the ending.
2. *yirʾeh*; *wayyarʾ*. The yiqtol ends in *-eh*; the wayyiqtol drops it.
3. *wayyaʿăśû*; *wattaʿaś*. The plural keeps its ending; the 3SG.F short form is like the masculine with *t-* (*wat-*).
4. *lirʾôṯ ʾeṯ-hāʿîr*; *liḇnôṯ ʾeṯ-hāʿîr* (or *liḇnōṯ hāʿîr*, Genesis 11:8). The infinitive construct ends in *-ôṯ*.
5. *b-n-h*, "build". Two consonants after the prefix, with a helping *e* and no long vowel: the short form of *yiḇneh*.
6. *ʿāśîṯî*. Before a consonant ending the old *y* returns as *î* (Gesenius §75f).
7. "God will provide the lamb for the burnt offering." *Yirʾeh* is a yiqtol of *rāʾâ*; *rāʾâ lə-* is "provide" (BDB).
8. *Wayyarʾ* "and he saw": short form of *yirʾeh*, root *r-ʾ-h*. *Wayyîrāʾ* "and he feared": wayyiqtol of *yārēʾ*, root *y-r-ʾ*, with *î* (lesson 4.14).
9. The infinitive construct of this class always ends in *-ôṯ*, a feminine ending added where the root ends in a vowel (Gesenius §75c).
