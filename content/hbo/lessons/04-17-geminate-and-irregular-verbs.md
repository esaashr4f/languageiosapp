## The idea

This lesson completes the weak verbs of the qal with three groups that look like something else.

**Doubled roots** (*geminates*) have the same consonant second and third: *s-b-b* "go round", *t-m-m* "be complete", *q-l-l* "be slight", *ḥ-n-n* "be gracious". Gesenius explains them as old two-consonant stems, *sb*, *tm*, that were made to fit the three-letter system (§67a). In many forms you see only two consonants, the second doubled when an ending follows: *tam* "it is finished", *tammû* "they are finished", *qallû* "they have abated". These forms are easily mistaken for hollow verbs (lesson 4.15) or first-*n* verbs (lesson 4.13).

***Hālaḵ* "go"** has a regular qatal, *hālaḵ*, but in the yiqtol, imperative and infinitive it behaves exactly like a first-*w* verb (lesson 4.14): *yēlēḵ*, *wayyēleḵ*, *lēḵ*, *leḵeṯ*. Gesenius notes that it "belongs in some respects to the פ״ו class", and that the regular forms with *h* are rare and mostly late or poetic (§69x).

***Lāqaḥ* "take"** has a regular qatal, *lāqaḥ*, but treats its *l* like the *n* of a first-*n* verb (lesson 4.13): *yiqqaḥ*, *wayyiqqaḥ*, *qaḥ*, *qaḥaṯ* (Gesenius §66g).

So none of these needs a new rule. Each borrows the pattern of a class you know.

## How it is built

### Doubled roots: *sāḇaḇ* "go round", *tāmam* "be finished"

**Qatal.** Verbs of action often show all three letters in the 3rd person: *sāḇaḇ*, *sāḇəḇû* "they went round". Verbs of state use the two-letter stem: *tam*, *tammû*; *qallû* (Gesenius §67a). Before a consonant ending an *ô* is inserted, which keeps the doubling audible: *sabbôṯā* "you went round", *wəsabbōṯem* "and you shall go round" (Gesenius §67d).

**Yiqtol and wayyiqtol.** The prefix takes a long vowel in the open syllable, and the stem has one vowel: *yāsōḇ* "he will go round", *yāḥōn* "he will be gracious" (Gesenius §67f). With an ending, the second letter doubles: *wayyāsōbbû* "and they went round". Some verbs instead double the *first* letter after the prefix, as a first-*n* verb would: *wattittōm* "and it came to an end" (Gesenius §67g), so the form looks like one from *n-t-m*.

**Imperative and infinitive.** *Sōḇ* "go round!", infinitive *sōḇ*.

### *Hālaḵ*

- qatal regular: *hālaḵ*, *hālaḵtî*, *hāləḵû*;
- yiqtol *yēlēḵ*, *tēlēḵ*, *ʾēlēḵ*; 2SG.F *tēləḵî*;
- wayyiqtol *wayyēleḵ*, in pause *wayyēlaḵ*; 1SG *wāʾēlēḵ*;
- imperative *lēḵ*, before maqqef *leḵ-*, longer *ləḵâ* "come!", plural *ləḵû*;
- infinitive *leḵeṯ*, *lāleḵeṯ* "to go";
- participle regular: *hōlēḵ*.

The hiphil is *hôlîḵ* "lead", again as if from *w-l-k* (Gesenius §69x).

### *Lāqaḥ*

- qatal regular: *lāqaḥ*, *lāqaḥtî*, *lāqəḥû*;
- yiqtol *yiqqaḥ*, wayyiqtol *wayyiqqaḥ*; 1SG *ʾeqqaḥ*;
- imperative *qaḥ*, plural *qəḥû*; feminine *qəḥî*;
- infinitive *qaḥaṯ*, *lāqaḥaṯ* "to take";
- participle regular: *lōqēaḥ*.

### Telling them apart

Faced with a prefix + two consonants, ask:

1. Is there a long vowel between them, *û*, *î*, *ô*? Then hollow: *yāqûm*.
2. Is the first of them doubled, *yiqqaḥ*, *yippōl*? Then first-*n*, *lāqaḥ*, or (rarely) a doubled root.
3. Is the prefix vowel *ē*, *yēlēḵ*, *yēšēḇ*? Then first-*w* or *hālaḵ*.
4. Does the stem end in a vowel or *-eh*? Then third-*h*.
5. Is the prefix vowel *ā* with a single stem vowel and doubling before endings, *yāsōḇ*, *yāsōbbû*? Then a doubled root.

## Where it applies

- **Doubled roots:** *sāḇaḇ* "go round", *tāmam* "be complete", *qālal* "be slight", *ḥānan* "be gracious", *ʾārar* "curse", *rāḇaḇ* "be many", *šāmam* "be desolate", *rāʿaʿ* "be bad", *māḏaḏ* "measure". Nouns share them: *ḥēn* "favour" (*ḥ-n-n*), *tām* "complete" (*t-m-m*), *rāḇ* "many".
- **Doubled roots in the derived stems:** hiphil *hēsēḇ* "turn", wayyiqtol *wayyassēḇ*; and a special stem with the last letter repeated, *sôḇēḇ* "go about" (Gesenius §67a; lesson 7.2).
- ***hālaḵ*:** every prefix form and the imperative and infinitive behave as first-*w*; the hithpael *hiṯhallēḵ* "walk about" keeps *h* (lesson 4.23).
- ***lāqaḥ*:** qal prefix forms, imperative, infinitive; the niphal *nilqaḥ* keeps the *l*.

## Watch out

**Do not read *wayyiqqaḥ* as from *\*q-q-ḥ* or *\*n-q-ḥ*.** It is *l-q-ḥ*, with the *l* treated like *n* (Gesenius §66g).

**Do not look for *h* in *wayyēleḵ*.** The root is *h-l-k*, but the prefix forms behave as if from *w-l-k*. *\*wayyahălōḵ* is late and rare (Gesenius §69x).

**Do not take *tam*, *qallû* for hollow verbs.** A hollow verb has a long vowel: *qām*, *qā́mû*. A doubled root has a short vowel and a doubled consonant before an ending: *tam*, *tammû*.

**Do not drop the inserted *ô*.** *\*sabtem* is wrong; the doubled root keeps its doubling before *-tem* with *ô*: *sabbôṯem* (here *sabbōṯem*).

**Do not mistake *wattittōm* for a first-*n* verb.** Some doubled roots double the first letter after the prefix (Gesenius §67g); *t-m-m* "be finished" fits the meaning, *\*n-t-m* does not exist.

**Do not use *hālaḵ* forms for "walk" in the sense of conduct.** That is the hithpael *hiṯhallēḵ*, "walk about, live" (lesson 4.23), as in Genesis 5:22.

## Connections

Arabic doubled roots are treated the same way: *madda* "he stretched" (for *madada*), *madadtu* "I stretched", with the two forms Hebrew shows as *tam* and *sāḇaḇ*. The Hebrew *ô* before consonant endings has no Arabic counterpart; Arabic undoes the doubling (*madadtu*). *Hālaḵ* has a cousin in Arabic *halaka* "perish", but the Hebrew prefix forms from *w-l-k* have no clear Arabic parallel.

## Exercises

1. Give the yiqtol 3SG.M, wayyiqtol 3SG.M and imperative of *hālaḵ*.
2. Give the same three forms of *lāqaḥ*.
3. Build "and they went round the city" from *sāḇaḇ*.
4. Build "to take" and "to go" with *lə-*.
5. What root lies behind *wayyiqqaḥ*, and why does it double the *q*?
6. *Qallû* or *qāmû*: which is a doubled root, and how do you tell?
7. A student writes *\*wayyahălōḵ* for "and he went". What is the normal form?
8. Translate *hăṯēləḵî ʿim-hāʾîš hazzeh* and identify the verb.
9. Use the checklist to identify the root type of *yāsōḇ*, *yiqqaḥ*, *yēšēḇ*, *yāqûm* and *yiḇneh*.

## Worked answers

1. *yēlēḵ*, *wayyēleḵ*, *lēḵ*. As if from first-*w* *w-l-k* (Gesenius §69x; lesson 4.14).
2. *yiqqaḥ*, *wayyiqqaḥ*, *qaḥ*. The *l* behaves like the *n* of a first-*n* verb (Gesenius §66g; lesson 4.13).
3. *wayyāsōbbû ʾeṯ-hāʿîr* (Joshua 6:15). Prefix *yā-*, one stem vowel, the *b* doubled before *-û*.
4. *lāqaḥaṯ*; *lāleḵeṯ*. Both infinitives lose the first root letter and add *-t*.
5. *l-q-ḥ*. The *l* assimilates to *q* when it closes the prefix syllable, as *n* does (Gesenius §66g).
6. *Qallû* is the doubled root *q-l-l*: short *a* and a doubled *l*. *Qāmû* is hollow *q-w-m*: long *ā*, no doubling.
7. *wayyēleḵ*. The forms with *h* are rare and late (Gesenius §69x).
8. "Will you go with this man?" *Tēləḵî*: yiqtol 2SG.F of *hālaḵ*, with *hă-* for the question (lesson 2.8).
9. *yāsōḇ*: doubled root (prefix *ā*, one short stem vowel). *yiqqaḥ*: *lāqaḥ* (first letter doubled away). *yēšēḇ*: first-*w* (prefix *ē*). *yāqûm*: hollow (long *û*). *yiḇneh*: third-*h* (*-eh*).
