## The idea

Lesson 2.1 showed that Hebrew says "Samuel is a prophet" with no verb at all: *šəmûʾēl nāḇîʾ*. A verbless clause has no tense; the context gives it. So why does Hebrew have a verb *hāyâ* "be"?

Because *hāyâ* is not a copula that every sentence needs. BDB gives its meanings as "fall out, come to pass, become, be". It is used where something **happens**, **comes into being** or **changes**, and where the time must be fixed: "the earth *was* formless", "the man *became* a living being", "I *will be* with you". In the plain present, "Samuel is a prophet", there is nothing to happen and no time to fix, so Hebrew leaves it out. Lesson 2.1 warned that *\*šəmûʾēl hāyâ nāḇîʾ* means "Samuel was (or became) a prophet". This lesson gives the full verb.

*Hāyâ* is a third-*h* verb (lesson 4.16), and nearly every form follows that class. Its peculiarity is its first letter, *h*, which after a prefix often loses its vowel. And its wayyiqtol, *wayhî*, is so common, about 780 times, that it works almost as a particle.

## How it is built

### Forms

Apply lesson 4.16 to *h-y-h*:

- **qatal:** *hāyâ*, *hāyəṯâ*, *hāyîṯā*, *hāyîṯî*, *hāyû*;
- **yiqtol:** *yihyeh* "he/it will be", *tihyeh*, *ʾehyeh*, *yihyû*; the *h* closes the prefix syllable;
- **jussive:** *yəhî* "let there be", *təhî*; here the short form ends in *î* (the old *y*);
- **wayyiqtol:** *wayhî* "and it was, and it happened" (the *y* with shewa does not double, lesson 4.4), *wattəhî*, *wayyihyû*, 1SG *wāʾĕhî*;
- **imperative:** *hĕyēh* "be!", plural *hĕyû*;
- **infinitive:** *hĕyôṯ*, *lihyôṯ* "to be";
- the **participle** is almost unused (*hōweh* twice): Hebrew does not need "being" for the present.

### Uses

**1. Past and future being.** *Hāyâ* fixes a state in time: *hāyîṯî ʿim-mōšeh* "I was with Moses"; *ʾehyeh ʿimmāḵ* "I will be with you".

**2. Becoming: *hāyâ lə-*.** With *lə-* + noun it means "become": *wayhî hāʾāḏām lənep̄eš ḥayyâ* "the man became a living being".

**3. Possession: *hāyâ lə-* + person.** The verbless *lî* "to me" = "I have" (lesson 2.7) becomes past or future with *hāyâ*: *wayhî lip̄ninnâ yəlāḏîm* "Peninnah had children".

**4. Existence: "there was".** *Wayhî rāʿāḇ bāʾāreṣ* "there was a famine in the land". The present uses *yēš* "there is" (lesson 2.7).

**5. Happening: *wayhî* + time phrase.** Narratives and new scenes open with *wayhî* "and it happened" + a time expression, and then the main event: *wayhî ʾaḥar haddəḇārîm hāʾēlleh* (lesson 4.6). Gesenius calls this a "loose connexion" with what precedes (§111f–g). In translation, *wayhî* is usually left out: "after these things, ...".

**6. *Wəhāyâ* for the future** opens a predicted scene, as *wayhî* opens a past one (lesson 4.5; Gesenius §112y).

**7. With a participle** for continuous past action: *hāyâ rōʿeh* "he used to tend" (lesson 4.9). With *lə-* + infinitive, "be about to" (lesson 4.10).

### Agreement

*Hāyâ* agrees with its subject: *hāyəṯâ hāʾāreṣ* (f.); *wayyihyû šənêhem* (pl.). But *wayhî* of use 5 is impersonal, "it happened", and stays 3SG.M whatever follows.

### Building a clause

"And Joseph was in his master's house":

1. A past state in a narrative chain: wayyiqtol of *hāyâ*.
2. Subject Joseph, 3SG.M: *wayhî*.
3. Place: *bəḇêṯ ʾăḏōnāyw*.
4. *wayhî yôsēp̄ bəḇêṯ ʾăḏōnāyw*. In the present, outside a story, the verbless *yôsēp̄ bəḇêṯ ʾăḏōnāyw* "Joseph is in his master's house" would do.

## Where it applies

- **Being in the past or future,** where the time matters: *hāyîṯî*, *ʾehyeh*.
- **Becoming, with *lə-*:** *wayhî hāʾāḏām lənep̄eš ḥayyâ*; *lihyôṯ ləḵā lēʾlōhîm*.
- **Having, with *lə-* + person, in past or future:** *wayhî lip̄ninnâ yəlāḏîm*.
- **"There was / will be":** *wayhî rāʿāḇ*.
- **Opening a scene:** *wayhî* + time phrase, and *wəhāyâ* in prediction (Gesenius §111f–g, §112y); lesson 5.10.
- **Wishes and commands:** *yəhî ʾôr*, *wehyēh bərāḵâ*.
- **With participles and infinitives** for "used to", "was about to" (lessons 4.9, 4.10).

## Watch out

**Do not put *hāyâ* in a present description.** "The LORD is good" is *ṭôḇ YHWH*. *\*YHWH hāyâ ṭôḇ* means "the LORD was (or became) good" (lesson 2.1).

**Do not translate every *wayhî*.** "And it came to pass that after these things God tested Abraham" is the King James rendering; modern English drops *wayhî*: "Some time later, God tested Abraham."

**Do not make *wayhî* agree with a following subject when it opens a scene.** In *wayhî ʾaḥar haddəḇārîm hāʾēlleh* the verb is impersonal. Even in *wayhî lip̄ninnâ yəlāḏîm* the singular stands before a plural.

**Do not read *hāyâ lə-* as "be to".** *Hāyâ lə-* + thing is "become"; + person, "belong to, have". *Wayhî lip̄ninnâ yəlāḏîm* is not "there were children to Peninnah" in English, but "Peninnah had children".

**Do not use *hāyâ* for "there is" in the present.** That is *yēš*, and "there is not" is *ʾên* (lesson 2.7).

**Do not look for a present participle "being".** *Hōweh* hardly occurs; the verbless clause does its work.

**Do not confuse *yəhî* (jussive) and *yihyeh* (yiqtol).** *Yəhî ʾôr* "let there be light"; *yihyeh ʾôr* would be "there will be light".

## Connections

Arabic has the same split: *kāna* "he was" fixes a state in the past (*kāna zaydun marīḍan* "Zayd was ill"), but the present needs no verb (*zaydun marīḍun* "Zayd is ill"). *Kāna* + imperfect gives "used to", as Hebrew *hāyâ* + participle does. Spanish *ser*/*estar* and French *être* have no equivalent of the Hebrew wayhî that opens a scene; English "it came to pass" was a literal rendering of it.

## Exercises

1. Give the qatal 3SG.F, the yiqtol 3SG.M, the jussive and the wayyiqtol of *hāyâ*.
2. Build "The king was in the city" as part of a story.
3. Build "The king is in the city" (now).
4. Build "And the boy became a man" with *hāyâ lə-*.
5. Build "The king had a son" (past).
6. Translate *kaʾăšer hāyîṯî ʿim-mōšeh ʾehyeh ʿimmāḵ*.
7. A student writes *\*hammeleḵ hāyâ ṭôḇ* for "the king is good". What does it mean, and what is the right form?
8. Why is *wayhî* singular in *wayhî lip̄ninnâ yəlāḏîm*?
9. What is the difference between *yəhî ʾôr* and *yihyeh ʾôr*?

## Worked answers

1. *hāyəṯâ*, *yihyeh*, *yəhî*, *wayhî*. A third-*h* verb with the short jussive *yəhî* and wayyiqtol *wayhî* (lessons 4.16, 4.18).
2. *wayhî hammeleḵ bāʿîr*. A past state in the chain: wayyiqtol.
3. *hammeleḵ bāʿîr*. The present needs no verb (lesson 2.1).
4. *wayhî hannaʿar ləʾîš*. *Hāyâ lə-* "become".
5. *wayhî lammeleḵ bēn* (or *hāyâ lammeleḵ bēn*). *Hāyâ lə-* + person for possession in the past.
6. "As I was with Moses, so I will be with you." Qatal for the past, yiqtol for the future (Gesenius §106c).
7. "The king was (or became) good." For "the king is good" Hebrew uses a verbless clause: *hammeleḵ ṭôḇ* or *ṭôḇ hammeleḵ* (lesson 2.1).
8. When *wayhî* comes first, before its subject, it often stays 3SG.M; the verb opens the clause before the subject is named.
9. *Yəhî ʾôr* is the jussive, a command or wish: "let there be light". *Yihyeh ʾôr* is the yiqtol: "there will be light".
