## The idea

"He sent me", "I will serve you", "he loved her": Hebrew can say "me", "you", "her" in two ways. Lesson 3.9 taught the first: the object marker with a suffix, *ʾōṯî*, *ʾōṯəḵā*, *ʾōṯāh*, placed after the verb. The second is to fix the same suffix directly onto the verb: *šəlāḥanî* "he sent me", *ʾeʿĕḇāḏəḵā* "I will serve you", *wayyeʾĕhāḇehā* "and he loved her". Since lesson 4.12 you have met forms like *wayyišlāḥēhû* "and he sent him". This lesson explains them.

Gesenius notes that the suffixes on verbs "express the accusative of the personal pronoun", and that they are clearly connected with the independent pronouns (§58a–b). They are, in fact, nearly the same suffixes as on nouns (lesson 3.7): *-nî* "me" (noun *-î* "my"), *-ḵā* "you", *-hû*/*-ô* "him", *-hā*/*-āh* "her", *-nû* "us", *-ḵem* "you (pl.)", *-hem*/*-ām* "them". The differences are small and systematic.

Both ways are normal prose. The suffix on the verb is more common; *ʾēṯ* + suffix is used when the pronoun is stressed or contrasted, or when it stands first: *lōʾ ʾōṯəḵā māʾāsû kî-ʾōṯî māʾăsû* "it is not *you* they have rejected, but *me*" (1 Samuel 8:7). Gesenius lists the suffixes as accusatives; when the pronoun must stand on its own, Hebrew uses *ʾēṯ* (lesson 3.9).

## How it is built

### The suffixes and the joining vowel

A suffix joins the verb in one of three ways (Gesenius §58a, e–f):

- **after a vowel**, directly: *-nî*, *-ḵā*, *-hû*, *-hā*, *-nû*, *-ḵem*, *-m*. So *šāmartî* + *-ḵā* → *šəmartîḵā* "I guarded you" (by pattern; compare *ûšəmartîḵā* below); *yaʿazḇû* "they will leave" + *-nî* → *yaʿazḇûnî* (by pattern).
- **after a qatal ending in a consonant**, with *a*: *-anî*, *-āhû* (usually contracted to *-ô*), *-āh*, *-ānû*, *-ām*. So *šālaḥ* + *-nî* → *šəlāḥanî* "he sent me"; *bārāʾ* + *-ām* → *bərāʾām* "he created them".
- **after a yiqtol, imperative, or infinitive ending in a consonant**, with *ē*: *-ēnî*, *-ēhû*, *-ehā*, *-ēnû*, *-ēm*. So *yahărōḡ* + *-nî* → *yaharḡēnî* "he will kill me"; *šəmaʿ* + *-nî* → *šəmāʿēnî* "hear me!".

With *-ḵā* and *-ḵem* the joining vowel is only a shewa: *ʾeʿĕḇāḏəḵā* "I will serve you" (Gesenius §58f).

### The stem changes

The suffix moves the stress towards the end of the word, and the vowels before it reduce or lengthen as in nouns (Gesenius §59c):

- the first *ā* of the qatal, now two places from the stress, reduces: *šālaḥ* → *šəlāḥ-*;
- the second vowel, which had reduced in *šāmərû*, reappears, lengthened in the open syllable: *šāmərû* → *šəmārûhû* (by pattern);
- in the yiqtol the stem vowel reduces before the joining vowel: *yahărōḡ* → *yaharḡēnî*; before *-ḵā* it stays as *ā*: *ʾeʿĕḇōḏ* → *ʾeʿĕḇāḏəḵā*.

### Special forms

- **3SG.F qatal** takes its old ending *-aṯ* before a suffix (Gesenius §59a): *ʾāḵəlâ* "she ate" → *ʾăḵālāṯəhû* "she ate him".
- **2SG.F qatal** recovers its old *-tî* (Gesenius §59a), so it looks like the 1SG: context decides.
- **Energic *-nn-*.** The yiqtol sometimes adds *n* before the suffix, doubled: *yittənennâ* "let him give it" (*-ennâ* for *-ehā*), *-ennû* "him". This *n* is an old "energetic" ending, the Arabic *-anna* (Gesenius §48b, §58i).

### Infinitives and participles

These are nouns, so they normally take noun suffixes, which can be subject or object (Gesenius §61a): *ləšomrô* "to guard him", *mōṣəʾî* "whoever finds me" (literally "my finder").

### Building a form

"And I will guard you", future, in a chain:

1. Weqatal 1SG: *wəšāmartî*.
2. "You", masculine: after the vowel *-î*, the suffix joins directly: *-ḵā*.
3. The stress moves to the suffix: the first *ā* reduces: *ûšəmartîḵā* (*wə-* becomes *û-* before shewa, lesson 2.3) (Genesis 28:15).

## Where it applies

- **On every finite form:** qatal (*šəlāḥanî*), yiqtol (*yaharḡēnî*), wayyiqtol (*wayyeʾĕhāḇehā*), weqatal (*ûšəmartîḵā*), imperative (*šəmāʿēnî*), in all stems: piel *ʾăšallēḥăḵā* "I will let you go" (Genesis 32:27), hiphil *wayyanniḥēhû* (lesson 4.10).
- **On infinitives and participles,** as noun suffixes, subject or object: *ləšomrô* "to guard him" (1 Samuel 19:11), *mōṣəʾî* (Gesenius §61a).
- **When to use *ʾēṯ* + suffix instead:** when the pronoun is stressed, contrasted or fronted (*lōʾ ʾōṯəḵā ... kî-ʾōṯî*), when there are two objects, or simply by style; both are frequent.
- **Only for the direct object.** "To me", "for him" are prepositions + suffix: *lî*, *lô* (lesson 3.9).
- **On *hinnēh*, *ʿôḏ*, *ʾên*, *yēš*:** *hinnēnî* "here I am", *ʾênennû* "he is not": the same suffixes, often with energic *-nn-* (lessons 5.1, 5.10).

## Watch out

**Do not read *-nî* as "my".** On a verb it is "me": *šəlāḥanî* "he sent me". "My" is *-î* on a noun.

**Do not mix up *-nû* "us" and *-nû* "we".** *šāmarnû* "we guarded" has *-nû* as subject; *šəmārānû* "he guarded us" has the joining *ā* before *-nû*, object.

**Do not keep the first *ā* of the qatal.** *\*šālāḥanî* is wrong; the stress has moved and the vowel reduces: *šəlāḥanî*.

**Do not put an object suffix on a verb and also use *ʾēṯ* for the same object.** *\*šəlāḥanî ʾōṯî* says "me" twice.

**Do not take the 3SG.F *-āṯ-* for a 2SG.M or a noun.** *ʾăḵālāṯəhû* is "she ate him", the old *-at* of the feminine (Gesenius §59a).

**Do not read energic *-ennû* as "us".** *Yittənennû* is "he will give him (it)"; "us" would be *-ēnû* with no doubled *n*. Check the doubling.

**Do not confuse a participle with a noun suffix and a finite verb.** *Mōṣəʾî* is "my finder" = "whoever finds me", not "he found me" (which is *məṣāʾanî*).

## Connections

Arabic attaches the same suffixes to verbs: *ḍarabanī* "he struck me", *yaḍribuhu* "he strikes him", *ḍarabahā* "he struck her", *ḍarabtuka* "I struck you". Gesenius takes the joining *a* after the qatal to be the remains of the old final vowel that the Arabic verb still has, Hebrew *qəṭālanî* beside Arabic *qatalanī*; he says the joining vowels are "of various origin", and gives no such match for the *ē* after the yiqtol (§58f, note). The energic *-nn-* of *yittənennû* matches the Arabic energetic *yaktubanna* (Gesenius §48b).

## Exercises

1. Add "me" to *šālaḥ* (he sent) and to *yišlaḥ* (he will send).
2. Build "he created them" and "they created him" from *bārāʾ*.
3. Build "I will not leave you" (m.) from *ʿāzaḇ*, and explain the vowel before *-ḵā*.
4. Translate *ḥayyâ rāʿâ ʾăḵālāṯəhû* and explain *-āṯ-*.
5. Build "hear us!" from *šəmaʿ*.
6. Why does 1 Samuel 8:7 use *ʾōṯəḵā* and *ʾōṯî* instead of suffixes on the verb?
7. A student writes *\*šāmarnû* for "he guarded us". What does his form mean, and what is the right one?
8. Take apart *yaharḡēnî*.
9. Translate *kol-mōṣəʾî* and say why the suffix is "me" though it looks like "my".

## Worked answers

1. *šəlāḥanî*; *yišlāḥēnî* (the form Gesenius gives, §65b). After the qatal the joining vowel is *a*, after the yiqtol *ē*; the stem vowels shift with the stress (Gesenius §58f, §59c).
2. *bərāʾām*; *bərāʾûhû*. Qatal + *-ām*; qatal 3PL ends in a vowel, so *-hû* joins directly.
3. *lōʾ ʾeʿĕzāḇəḵā* (Genesis 28:15). The yiqtol stem vowel *ō* becomes *ā* in the open syllable before the shewa of *-ḵā* (Gesenius §58f).
4. "A wild animal has devoured him." The 3SG.F qatal uses its old ending *-at*, here *-āṯ*, before a suffix (Gesenius §59a).
5. *šəmāʿēnû*. Imperative + *-ēnû*; the *a* lengthens in the open syllable (compare *šəmāʿēnî*).
6. The pronouns are contrasted, "not you ... but me", and put first for emphasis; a suffix on the verb cannot stand first, so *ʾēṯ* + suffix is used.
7. *Šāmarnû* is "we guarded". "He guarded us" is *šəmārānû*: qatal 3SG.M + joining *ā* + *-nû*.
8. *yaharḡē-nî*: yiqtol 3SG.M of *hāraḡ* "kill", joining vowel *ē*, suffix *-nî* "me": "he will kill me".
9. "Whoever finds me." *Mōṣēʾ* is a participle, a noun in form, so it takes noun suffixes; "my finder" is the one who finds me (Gesenius §61a, §116g).
