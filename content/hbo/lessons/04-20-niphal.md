## The idea

Lesson 4.1 introduced the seven stems. This lesson builds the first derived stem in full, the **niphal**, named after *nip̄ʿal*. Its mark is a prefix *n*: *nip̄qaḏ* "it was missed", *wayyiqqāḇēr* "and he was buried", *yillāḥēm* "he will fight".

Gesenius compares its meaning to the Greek middle voice (§51c). Its uses are:

- **reflexive**, doing to oneself: *nišmar* "take care (guard oneself)", *nistar* "hide (oneself)";
- **reciprocal**, doing to each other: *nilḥam* "fight", *nišbaʿ* "swear (an oath)";
- **feelings and inner states**: *niḥam* "regret, be sorry";
- **passive**, the commonest in prose: *nôlaḏ* "be born", *niqbar* "be buried", *niqrāʾ* "be called".

Gesenius insists that the older grammarians "were decidedly wrong" to treat the niphal as no more than the passive of the qal (§51h); the passive use is real and frequent, but it grew out of the reflexive. For a reader the practical rule is: translate the niphal as passive or reflexive, and check the dictionary, because some niphals are ordinary active verbs in English: *nilḥam* "fight", *nišbaʿ* "swear".

In the gloss the stem is NIPH: `fight.NIPH.YQTL.3SG.M`.

## How it is built

The *n* appears in two ways (Gesenius §51a–b):

- **Qatal and participle: prefix *ni-*.** *nišmar* "he took care", *nišməʾû*; participle *nišmār*, with *ā*. The qatal takes the ordinary qatal endings: *nišmartā*, *nišmartî*.
- **Yiqtol, imperative, infinitive: the *n* is assimilated**, leaving a dagesh in the first root letter, and the stem has *ā* and *ē*: *yiššāmēr* (from *yinšāmēr*) "he will take care"; imperative and infinitive *hiššāmēr*, with an added *hi-*. The wayyiqtol shortens *ē* to *e* in the last syllable when the stress moves back: *wayyinnāḥem* "and he regretted".

So: *n* + root = qatal/participle; prefix + doubled first letter + *ā* ... *ē* = yiqtol. Lesson 4.3 warned that *nišmōr* "we will guard" and *nišmar* "he took care" look alike; the stem vowels separate them.

**With a first guttural**, which cannot double, the prefix vowel lengthens (Gesenius §51b): *wāʾēḥāḇēʾ* "and I hid" (from *ḥ-b-ʾ*); qatal *neʿĕzaḇ*, with *e* and a hatef.

**Weak verbs** combine this with their own rules (lesson 4.24): *nôlaḏ* "he was born", *niḡlâ* "he revealed himself", *nāḵôn* "established", *niṣṣāḇ* "standing".

### Building a form

"And he was buried in the city of David":

1. Passive of *qāḇar* "bury": niphal.
2. Wayyiqtol 3SG.M: prefix *yi-* + the *n* assimilated to *q* (*yiqq-*) + *ā* ... *ē*: *yiqqāḇēr*.
3. With *wa-*: *wayyiqqāḇēr bəʿîr dāwiḏ* (1 Kings 2:10).

## Where it applies

- **Passive of the qal:** *wayyiqqāḇēr*, *yiqqārēʾ*, *nip̄tāḥû*, *hibbārəʾām* (Gesenius §51f).
- **Reflexive:** *hiššāmer* "take care", *wāʾēḥāḇēʾ* "I hid", *nistar* "hide oneself" (§51c).
- **Reciprocal:** *nilḥam* "fight", *nišbaʿ* "swear", *nôʿaṣ* "consult together" (§51d).
- **Feelings:** *niḥam* "regret, be comforted" (§51c).
- **"To be done", "-able":** the participle *neḥmāḏ* "desirable", *nôrāʾ* "fearsome" (lesson 3.10; Gesenius §116e).
- **Passive of piel or hiphil** where the qal is not used: *nikbaḏ* "be honoured" (Gesenius §51f; lesson 4.1).
- **Verbs with no qal:** *nilḥam*, *nišbaʿ*, *niḥam*. They are cited by the niphal.

## Watch out

**Do not always translate the niphal as passive.** *Yillāḥēm lāḵem* is "he will fight for you", not "he will be fought". Look up the verb.

**Do not confuse the niphal qatal with the qal yiqtol 1PL.** *Nišmar* (niphal, "he took care") and *nišmōr* (qal, "we will guard") differ in the stem vowel.

**Do not confuse the niphal yiqtol with a first-*n* qal.** *Yiqqāḇēr* has the doubling of the niphal *n*; the root is *q-b-r*. A qal of first-*n* *n-q-b-*... would have *ō* or *a* in the stem; the niphal has *ā* ... *ē*.

**Do not drop the *hi-* of the imperative and infinitive.** *\*šāmēr* for "take care!" is wrong; the form is *hiššāmēr* (here *hiššāmer*).

**Do not use the niphal for the passive of the piel by default.** That is usually the pual (lesson 4.21).

**Do not add a by-phrase by habit.** Hebrew rarely names the agent of a passive; when it does, it uses *lə-* or *min*. Spanish *por*, French *par* have no regular equivalent.

## Connections

Arabic Form VII, *infaʿala*, is the same n-stem: *inkasara* "it broke", *infataḥa* "it opened", reflexive and passive. Gesenius points to it as the counterpart of the niphal (§51a, h). Hebrew *nip̄tāḥû* "they were opened" and Arabic *infataḥa* "it opened" are the same root and stem. Spanish *se* in *se abrieron las ventanas* covers the same ground as the niphal: one form for "opened (by itself)" and "were opened".

## Exercises

1. Build the niphal qatal 3SG.M and yiqtol 3SG.M of *kāraṯ* "cut".
2. Build "and he was buried" and "and they were buried".
3. Translate *YHWH yillāḥēm lāḵem* and say why the niphal is not passive here.
4. Give the niphal imperative of *šāmar* and translate it.
5. What does the doubled letter in *yiqqārēʾ* represent?
6. Why does *wāʾēḥāḇēʾ* have *ē* in the prefix?
7. A student reads *nišmar* as "we will guard". Correct him.
8. Translate *neḥmāḏ ləmarʾeh* and explain the participle.
9. Give two possible translations of *wəniḇrəḵû ḇəḵā* and say what each assumes.

## Worked answers

1. *niḵraṯ*; *yikkārēṯ*. Prefix *ni-* in the qatal; in the yiqtol the *n* assimilates to *k*, with *ā* ... *ē* (Gesenius §51a–b). Both are attested (*yikkārēṯ* 22×).
2. *wayyiqqāḇēr*; *wayyiqqāḇərû* (by pattern). The plural ending reduces the *ē*.
3. "The LORD will fight for you." *Nilḥam* is a reciprocal niphal whose English equivalent is active (Gesenius §51d).
4. *hiššāmēr* (*hiššāmer lə-ḵā*): "take care, be on your guard". Reflexive (§51c).
5. The *n* of the niphal, assimilated to the first root letter *q* (Gesenius §51a).
6. The first root letter *ḥ* cannot be doubled, so instead of doubling the vowel of the prefix lengthens to *ē* (§51b).
7. *Nišmar* is the niphal qatal "he took care, was guarded"; "we will guard" is the qal *nišmōr*, with *ō*.
8. "Pleasant to look at." The niphal participle can mean "desirable, to be desired" (Gesenius §116e).
9. "They shall be blessed in you" (passive niphal) or "they shall bless themselves by you", that is, use Abram's name in blessings (reflexive niphal). Both uses exist; the verse is debated.
