## The idea

*Dibbēr* "he spoke", *biqqēš* "he sought", *ṣiwwâ* "he commanded", *wayḇāreḵ* "and he blessed", *wayqaddēš* "and he made holy": many of the commonest verbs are **piels**. The piel's mark is the doubled middle root letter, with *i* ... *ē* in the qatal: *qiṭṭēl*. Its passive, the **pual**, doubles the same letter with *u* ... *a*: *quṭṭal* (Gesenius §52a–b).

What does the doubling add? Gesenius's general formula is "to busy oneself eagerly with the action" (§52f), and he lists several results:

- **repeated or extended action:** *šāḇar* "break", piel *šibbar* "smash"; *qāḇar* "bury (one)", piel "bury (many)"; *ṣāḥaq* "laugh", piel "joke, play" (§52f);
- **making a state into an action (factitive):** *gāḏal* "be great", piel *giddēl* "make great"; *qāḏaš* "be holy", piel *qiddēš* "make holy"; *lāmaḏ* "learn", piel *limmaḏ* "teach" (§52g);
- **declaring:** *ṣiddēq* "declare righteous" (§52g);
- **verbs made from nouns (denominatives):** *kihēn* "act as priest", from *kōhēn* (§52h);
- **verbs with no qal**, where the piel is simply the verb: *dibbēr*, *biqqēš*, *ṣiwwâ*, *bēraḵ*.

How to label the piel is debated (lesson 7.1). Gesenius's word is "intensive"; many modern grammars stress the factitive sense. In practice, learn each piel from the dictionary: the doubled letter tells you the stem, BDB tells you the meaning.

In the gloss: PIEL, PUAL: `speak.PIEL.QTL.3SG.M`, `bless.PUAL.PTCP.SG.M`.

## How it is built

### Piel

- **qatal:** *qiṭṭēl*, but most piels have *a* in the second syllable before consonant endings and often in the 3SG.M too: *dibbēr*, *dibbartî*; *šibbar* (Gesenius §52a);
- **yiqtol:** *yəqaṭṭēl* "he will ...": prefix with shewa, *a*, doubled letter, *ē*: *yəḏabbēr*; 1SG *ʾăḏabbēr*, with hatef under ʾ;
- **wayyiqtol:** *wayḏabbēr* (the *y* with shewa does not double after *wa-*, lesson 4.4); *wayšallaḥ* "and he sent away";
- **imperative and infinitive construct:** *qaṭṭēl*: *dabbēr* "speak!", *ləḏabbēr* "to speak";
- **participle:** *məqaṭṭēl*: *məḏabbēr* "speaking", *məḇaqqēš* "seeking".

The prefix vowel of the yiqtol, shewa, is the quickest sign of a piel (Gesenius §52d): *yəḇaqqēš* against qal *yišmōr*.

### Pual

- **qatal:** *quṭṭal*: *puqqaḏ* "was counted" (lesson 4.1);
- **yiqtol:** *yəquṭṭal*;
- **participle:** *məquṭṭāl*: *məḇōrāḵ* "blessed".

Gesenius notes that many "puals" without a matching piel, such as *yullaḏ* "was born" and *luqqaḥ* "was taken", are really remnants of an old qal passive (§52e; lesson 7.2). The guide glosses them as OSHB tags them, PUAL, and says so in the note.

### Guttural and *r* as middle letter

The middle letter cannot double if it is a guttural or *r* (lesson 4.11). Before *ʾ* and *r* the first vowel lengthens: *bērēḵ* "he blessed" (*b-r-k*), *wayḇāreḵ*, *məḇōrāḵ* with *ō* in the pual. Before *h*, *ḥ*, *ʿ* it usually stays short: *niḥam* "he comforted" (Gesenius §64d).

### Building a form

"I will make your name great":

1. Make *g-d-l* "great" into an action: piel.
2. 1SG yiqtol, with the cohortative *-â* of resolve (lesson 4.8): *ʾăḡaddəlâ*.
3. After *wə-*: *waʾăḡaddəlâ šəmeḵā* (Genesis 12:2).

## Where it applies

- **Verbs that exist mainly in the piel:** *dibbēr*, *biqqēš*, *ṣiwwâ*, *bēraḵ*, *šērēṯ* "serve", *kipper* "make atonement", *millēʾ* "fill", *mihar* "hurry".
- **A qal and a piel with different meanings:** *šālaḥ* "send" / *šillaḥ* "let go"; *lāmaḏ* "learn" / *limmaḏ* "teach"; *qāḏaš* "be holy" / *qiddēš* "make holy"; *šāḇar* "break" / *šibbar* "smash" (Gesenius §52f–g).
- **Declaring or treating as:** *ṭimmēʾ* "declare unclean", *ṭihar* "declare clean" (the priest in Leviticus 13).
- **The pual** for the passive of a piel: *məḇōrāḵ*, *puqqaḏ*, *məquddāš* "consecrated" (lesson 4.1).
- **The hithpael** is built on the piel: *hiṯqaddēš* "make oneself holy" (lesson 4.23).

## Watch out

**Do not read *yə-* as a qal prefix.** Qal yiqtol has *yi-* (*yišmōr*); piel has *yə-* (*yəḏabbēr*). With *wa-*: *wayḏabbēr*, not *\*wayyiḏbōr*.

**Do not look for a qal of every piel.** *\*dāḇar* "he spoke" does not occur (lesson 4.1).

**Do not double a guttural or *r*.** *\*birrēḵ* is wrong: *bērēḵ*, *wayḇāreḵ*.

**Do not translate every piel as "intensive".** *Šillaḥ* is not "send hard" but "let go"; *qiddēš* is "make holy". Use BDB.

**Do not confuse the pual and the niphal.** Both can be passive, but the pual has the doubled middle letter and *u*: *yullaḏ*; the niphal has the prefix *n* or the doubled first letter: *nôlaḏ*, *yiwwālēḏ*.

**Do not confuse the piel infinitive with the imperative.** Both are *dabbēr*. After *lə-* it is the infinitive, *ləḏabbēr*.

## Connections

Arabic Form II, *faʿʿala*, doubles the middle letter in the same way and often has the same factitive sense: *ʿalima* "know", *ʿallama* "teach", exactly Hebrew *lāmaḏ* / *limmaḏ*; *kabura* "be big", *kabbara* "make big", like *gāḏal* / *giddēl*. Gesenius compares the piel to the Arabic second conjugation (§52a). The Arabic passive *kubbira* matches the pual *quṭṭal*.

## Worked answers

1. *biqqēš*, *yəḇaqqēš*, *wayḇaqqēš*, *məḇaqqēš* (Gesenius §52a).
2. *limmaḏ ʾeṯ-hāʿām*. Factitive piel: "make learn" = "teach".
3. Qal *šālaḥ* "send"; piel *šillaḥ* "send away, let go" (Genesis 8:8, 32:27).
4. The piel doubles the middle letter, but *r* cannot double, so the first vowel lengthens (Gesenius §64d).
5. *wayḇāreḵ ʾōṯām* (Genesis 1:22).
6. *wayḏabbēr*. Piel prefix *yə-*, doubled *b*, *a* ... *ē*; the *y* with shewa does not double after *wa-*.
7. "Whoever you bless is blessed." *Təḇārēḵ*: piel yiqtol 2SG.M; *məḇōrāḵ*: pual participle.
8. That many supposed puals with no matching piel, such as *yullaḏ* and *luqqaḥ*, are really passives of the qal (§52e).
9. The prefix vowel: shewa in the piel (*yəḏabbēr*), *i* (or *a*, *e* with gutturals) in the qal (*yišmōr*); and the piel's doubled middle letter.
