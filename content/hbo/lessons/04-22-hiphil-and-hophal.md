## The idea

The **hiphil** (*hip̄ʿîl*) is the stem of making something happen. Its marks are a prefix *h-* in the qatal, and the long vowel *î* after the second root letter: *himṭîr* "he sent rain", *hišlîḵ* "he threw", *hiqrîḇ* "he brought near". You have met it as *wayyaḇdēl* "and he separated" (lesson 2.1) and *wayyaḵbēḏ* "and he made heavy" (lesson 4.1). Its passive, the **hophal**, has *o* or *u* in the prefix: *mušlāḵ* "thrown", *wayyuggaḏ* "and it was told".

The basic meaning is **causative**: the hiphil makes the subject cause someone else to do, or to be, what the qal says (Gesenius §53c):

- qal *qāraḇ* "come near" → hiphil *hiqrîḇ* "bring near, offer";
- qal *mālaḵ* "be king" → hiphil *himlîḵ* "make king";
- qal *kāḇēḏ* "be heavy" → hiphil *hiḵbîḏ* "make heavy".

But not every hiphil has a causative English equivalent. Some describe entering a state or acting on one's own: *hiqrîḇ* can also mean "draw near" (Exodus 14:10), and Gesenius notes such "internal" hiphils (§53d). Some come from nouns: *himṭîr* "send rain", from *māṭār*. And some have no qal and must be learned as they are: *hišlîḵ* "throw", *hišmîḏ* "destroy", *hišḥîṯ* "destroy", *hiškîm* "rise early".

In the gloss: HIPH and HOPH: `throw.HIPH.3PL.M`, `throw.HOPH.PTCP.SG.M`.

## How it is built

### Hiphil

- **qatal:** *hiqṭîl*; before consonant endings the *î* becomes *a*: *hiqṭaltî*, *hišlaḵtā* (Gesenius §53a). 3PL keeps *î*: *hišlîḵû*.
- **yiqtol:** *yaqṭîl*, with *a* in the prefix (the *h* has dropped between the prefix and the root): *yaqrîḇ*, *tašḥîṯ*, *ʾašḥîṯ*.
- **jussive and wayyiqtol:** the *î* shortens to *ē* (Gesenius §53n): *yap̄qēḏ* "let him appoint", *wayyaḇdēl* "and he separated", *wayyaškēm*. With endings the *î* returns: *wayyašliḵû*.
- **imperative:** *haqṭēl*, plural *haqṭîlû*: *hašlîḵû* "throw!".
- **infinitive construct:** *haqṭîl*, *ləhaqṭîl*: *ləhaqrîḇ* "to offer".
- **participle:** *maqṭîl*: *mašḥîṯ* "destroying".

The *a* of the prefix and the *î* or *ē* in the stem are the hiphil's signs; *hi-*, *ya-*, *ma-*, *ha-* are its prefixes.

### Hophal

- **qatal:** *hoqṭal* or *huqṭal* (*o*, *u* both occur): *hop̄qaḏ* (lesson 4.1); *huggaḏ* "it was told" (first *n*).
- **yiqtol:** *yoqṭal*, wayyiqtol *wayyuggaḏ*.
- **participle:** *moqṭāl*, *muqṭāl*: *mušlāḵ* "thrown".

Gesenius explains the *o*/*u* as the "obscure vowels" that mark the passives (§39e; lesson 4.1). The hophal has no imperative to speak of (Gesenius §46a, note).

### Building a form

"And they threw him into the pit":

1. *Š-l-k* "throw" exists in the hiphil: *hišlîḵ*.
2. Wayyiqtol 3PL.M: prefix *ya-*, stem *-šlîḵ-*, ending *-û*: *yašlîḵû*; the *î* is kept before the ending, here written defective: *wayyašliḵû*.
3. Object *ʾōṯô*, destination with the directional ending *habbōrâ* (lesson 5.2).
4. *wayyašliḵû ʾōṯô habbōrâ* (Genesis 37:24).

## Where it applies

- **Causative of the qal:** *hiqrîḇ* "bring near", *himlîḵ* "make king", *hiḵbîḏ* "make heavy", *hiḡdîl* "make great", *hôṣîʾ* "bring out" (lesson 4.24).
- **"Internal" or intransitive hiphil:** *hiqrîḇ* "draw near" (Exodus 14:10). Gesenius calls such forms "inwardly transitive" hiphils, which often express obtaining a quality or entering a state (§53d).
- **Denominatives:** *himṭîr* "send rain" (*māṭār*); *heʾĕzîn* "listen" (*ʾōzen* "ear").
- **Hiphil-only verbs:** *hišlîḵ*, *hiḇdîl*, *hišmîḏ*, *hišḥîṯ*, *hiškîm*, *higgîḏ*.
- **Hophal:** passive of the hiphil: *huggaḏ*, *mušlāḵ*, *hop̄qaḏ*.
- **Weak verbs in the hiphil** have special shapes: *hēqîm* (hollow), *hôšîaʿ* (first *y*), *higgîḏ* (first *n*), *heʿĕlâ* (third *h*). Lesson 4.24.

## Watch out

**Do not confuse the hiphil yiqtol *ya-* with the qal *yi-*.** *Yaqrîḇ* "he will offer" (hiphil); *yiqraḇ* "he will draw near" (qal).

**Do not keep *î* in the wayyiqtol and jussive.** *\*wayyaḇdîl* is wrong; the form is *wayyaḇdēl*. But with an ending the *î* stays: *wayyašliḵû*.

**Do not read the *h* of *himṭîr* as the article or the question *hă-*.** The hiphil *hi-* has *ḥireq*; the article has *pataḥ* + doubling, the question *hă-* a hatef.

**Do not assume the hiphil adds "cause" in English.** *Hišlîḵ* is "throw", *hiškîm* "rise early". The label is a tendency (lesson 4.1).

**Do not use the hophal as the passive of the qal.** That is usually the niphal (lesson 4.20). The hophal answers to the hiphil only.

**Do not mistake the hophal *u* for the pual.** *Mušlāḵ* has a prefix *mu-* and no doubled middle letter; the pual has the doubling, *məḇōrāḵ*.

## Connections

Arabic Form IV, *ʾafʿala*, is the causative: *ʾaḫraja* "bring out" from *ḫaraja* "go out", *ʾadḫala* "bring in". Its prefix *ʾa-* corresponds to Hebrew *ha-*, and in the prefix conjugation both lose it: Arabic *yuḫriju*, Hebrew *yôṣîʾ*. Gesenius compares the hiphil to the Arabic fourth conjugation and its passive (§53a).

## Worked answers

1. *hiqrîḇ*, *yaqrîḇ*, *wayyaqrēḇ*, *maqrîḇ*. Prefixes *hi-*, *ya-*, *ma-*; *î* shortened to *ē* in the wayyiqtol (Gesenius §53n). *Wayyaqrēḇ* is attested 19×.
2. *himlîḵ ʾeṯ-dāwiḏ*. Causative of *mālaḵ*.
3. The wayyiqtol shortens *î* to *ē* when the last syllable is closed and unstressed; with the ending *-û* the syllable is open and the *î* returns (Gesenius §53n).
4. "For the LORD God had not sent rain on the earth." A denominative hiphil, from *māṭār* "rain".
5. *Yaqrîḇ* is hiphil, "he will bring near, offer"; *yiqraḇ* is qal, "he will draw near".
6. *mušlāḵ* "thrown" (2 Samuel 20:21).
7. "It was told to Abraham": hophal of *n-g-d*, the passive of *higgîḏ* "tell".
8. *wayyaḇdēl*. In the wayyiqtol the hiphil *î* becomes *ē* (Gesenius §53n).
9. The hiphil yiqtol would be *yap̄qîḏ* with *î*; *yap̄qēḏ* has the shortened *ē* of the jussive (Gesenius §48g), and the context is advice.
