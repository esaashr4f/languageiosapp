## The idea

*Hiṯhallēḵ* "walk about, live", *hiṯpallēl* "pray", *hiṯqaddēš* "make oneself holy", *hiṯnabbēʾ* "prophesy": the **hithpael** is the piel with a prefix *hiṯ-*. Gesenius explains that, like the *n* of the niphal, the *t* of this prefix "has also a reflexive force" (§54a). The stem keeps the doubled middle letter of the piel.

Its uses (Gesenius §54e–f):

- **reflexive of the piel:** *qiddēš* "make holy" → *hiṯqaddēš* "make oneself holy";
- **acting a part, showing oneself as:** *hiṯnabbēʾ* "behave as a prophet, prophesy (in ecstasy)"; *hiṯḥakkēm* "show oneself wise";
- **reciprocal:** *hiṯrāʾâ* "look at one another";
- **doing for oneself, in one's own interest:** *hiṯhallēḵ* "walk about", *hiṯpallēl* "pray, intercede".

In the gloss: HITP: `walk.HITP.QTL.3SG.M`.

## How it is built

### The forms

- **qatal:** *hiṯqaṭṭēl*, often *hiṯqaṭṭal* (*a* before consonant endings and in pause): *hiṯhallēḵ*, *hiṯqaddāšû*;
- **yiqtol:** *yiṯqaṭṭēl*: *yiṯhallēḵ*, wayyiqtol *wayyiṯhallēḵ*, *wattiṯpallēl*;
- **imperative and infinitive:** *hiṯqaṭṭēl*: *hiṯqaddəšû* "make yourselves holy!", *ləhiṯqaddēš*;
- **participle:** *miṯqaṭṭēl*: *miṯhallēḵ* "walking about".

So the prefixes are *hiṯ-* (qatal, imperative, infinitive), *yiṯ-*/*tiṯ-* (yiqtol), *miṯ-* (participle), and the middle letter is doubled.

### The *t* meets the first letter

Gesenius describes two adjustments (§54b–c):

- **Metathesis with *s*, *ś*, *š*, *ṣ*:** the *t* and the sibilant change places: *\*hiṯšammēr* → *hištammēr* "take heed"; with *ṣ* the *t* also becomes *ṭ*: *niṣṭaddāq* "we will clear ourselves", from *ṣ-d-q*.
- **Assimilation to *d*, *ṭ*, *t*** (and sometimes *n*, *k*): the *t* is absorbed and the first letter doubled: *hiṭṭahărû* "purify yourselves", from *ṭ-h-r*; *hinnabbēʾ* beside *hiṯnabbēʾ*.

### *Hištaḥăwâ* "bow down"

The commonest verb that looks like a hithpael is *wayyištaḥû* "and he bowed down", plural *wayyištaḥăwû*. Traditionally it was taken as a hithpael-type form of *š-ḥ-h*, with metathesis; many grammars now analyse it as a rare stem with prefix *ši-* of a root *ḥ-w-h*, and OSHB tags it so. The guide glosses it HITP for simplicity and notes the debate.

### Building a form

"Noah walked with God":

1. "Walk about, live in a certain way": hithpael of *h-l-k*.
2. Qatal 3SG.M: *hiṯ-* + *hallēḵ*: *hiṯhallēḵ*; before maqqef *hiṯhalleḵ-*.
3. With the object of companionship first: *ʾeṯ-hāʾĕlōhîm hiṯhalleḵ-nōaḥ* (Genesis 6:9).

## Where it applies

- **Reflexive:** *hiṯqaddēš*, *hiṭṭahēr* "purify oneself", *hištammēr* "be on one's guard".
- **Showing or making oneself:** *hiṯnabbēʾ*, *hiṯḥakkēm*, *hiṯgaddēl* "make oneself great" (Gesenius §54e).
- **Reciprocal:** *tiṯrāʾû* (§54f).
- **For oneself:** *hiṯhallēḵ*, *hiṯpallēl*, *hiṯyaṣṣēḇ* "station oneself".
- **Mourning and feelings:** *hiṯʾabbēl* "mourn" (1 Samuel 15:35), *wayyiṯʿaṣṣēḇ* "he was grieved" (Genesis 6:6).
- **Rare passives:** the hothpaal *hoṯpāqəḏû* "they were mustered" (Gesenius §54l; compare lesson 4.1).

## Watch out

**Do not read *hiṯ-* as the hiphil.** The hiphil has *hi-* + root with *î*: *hišlîḵ*. The hithpael has *hiṯ-* + piel stem with a doubled middle letter: *hiṯhallēḵ*.

**Do not forget metathesis.** *\*hiṯšammēr* is wrong: *hištammēr*. *\*hiṯṣaddēq* is wrong: *hiṣṭaddēq*.

**Do not look for *t* in *hiṭṭahărû*.** It is there, assimilated to *ṭ*.

**Do not translate every hithpael as reflexive.** *Hiṯpallēl* "pray" and *hiṯhallēḵ* "walk about" are not "pray oneself" or "walk oneself".

**Do not confuse *wayyištaḥû* with *š-ḥ-t* "destroy".** The *t* belongs to the stem prefix; the verb means "bow down".

## Connections

Arabic Form V, *tafaʿʿala*, is the same shape: a *t-* prefix on the doubled stem, *taʿallama* "learn" (reflexive of *ʿallama* "teach"), *takallama* "speak". Arabic Form VIII, *iftaʿala*, puts the *t* after the first letter, and shows the same metathesis and assimilation as Hebrew: *iṣṭabara* "be patient" from *ṣ-b-r*, like Hebrew *hiṣṭaddēq*.

## Exercises

1. Give the hithpael qatal, yiqtol and participle of *qāḏaš*.
2. Build "and he walked about in the garden".
3. Why is it *hištammēr* and not *\*hiṯšammēr*?
4. What root lies behind *hiṭṭahărû*?
5. Translate *lāmmâ tiṯrāʾû* and say which use of the hithpael it shows.
6. How do you tell *hiṯhallēḵ* from a hiphil?
7. Translate *wattiṯpallēl ʿal-YHWH*.
8. Build "make yourselves holy!" (plural).
9. Why is *gəḇîaʿ* written with a furtive pataḥ?

## Worked answers

1. *hiṯqaddēš*, *yiṯqaddēš*, *miṯqaddēš*. The prefix *hiṯ-*/*yiṯ-*/*miṯ-* on the piel stem *qaddēš* (Gesenius §54a).
2. *wayyiṯhallēḵ baggān*. Wayyiqtol of the hithpael: *wa-* + *yiṯhallēḵ*.
3. When the stem begins with a sibilant, the *t* and the sibilant change places (Gesenius §54b).
4. *ṭ-h-r* "be clean". The *t* of *hiṯ-* is assimilated to *ṭ* (Gesenius §54c).
5. "Why do you keep looking at one another?" Reciprocal (Gesenius §54f).
6. The hithpael has *hiṯ-* and a doubled middle letter; the hiphil has *hi-* + the root with *î* after the second letter.
7. "She prayed to the LORD." Hithpael wayyiqtol 3SG.F of *p-l-l*.
8. *hiṯqaddəšû* (Joshua 3:5 has the pausal *hiṯqaddāšû*).
9. The word ends in a guttural *ʿ* after a long vowel *î*; the pataḥ is pronounced before it (lesson 1.4).
