## The idea

*Hôṣēʾṯîḵā* "I brought you out", *wayyāḇēʾ* "and he brought", *wayyôšaʿ* "and he saved", *wayyaḵ* "and he struck", *nāḵôn* "it is established": the commonest forms of the derived stems come from weak roots, and they can look nothing like *hiqṭîl* or *niqṭal*. This lesson puts lessons 4.13 to 4.17 together with lessons 4.20 to 4.23.

No new rule is needed. Each weak class keeps its own behaviour in every stem (Gesenius §§66–75):

- **First *n*** assimilates whenever it closes a syllable: hiphil *higgîḏ*, *hibbîṭ*; niphal *niṣṣāḇ*.
- **First *w*** comes back as *ô* (niphal, hiphil) or *û* (hophal): *nôlaḏ*, *hôṣîʾ*, *hôšîaʿ*, *hûšaḇ* (Gesenius §69i).
- **Hollow roots** have a long vowel in the prefix and no middle consonant: hiphil *hēqîm*, *hēḇîʾ*, *hēšîḇ*, *hēmîṯ*; niphal *nāḵôn* (Gesenius §72d).
- **Third *h*** has its fixed endings and short forms in every stem: hiphil *heʿĕlâ*, wayyiqtol *wayyaʿal*; *hikkâ* "strike", wayyiqtol *wayyaḵ*.
- **Doubled roots** shorten in the same way: hiphil *hēsēḇ* "turn".

So when you meet a strange derived form, identify the stem from the prefix and vowels, then restore the weak letter.

## How it is built

### The hiphil of weak roots

- **First *n*:** *n-g-d* → *higgîḏ* "tell"; *n-b-ṭ* → *hibbîṭ* "look"; *n-k-h* → *hikkâ* "strike", wayyiqtol *wayyaḵ* (first *n* and third *h* both); *n-ṣ-l* → *hiṣṣîl* "rescue".
- **First *w/y*:** *y-ṣ-ʾ* → *hôṣîʾ* "bring out", wayyiqtol *wayyôṣēʾ*; *y-š-ʿ* → *hôšîaʿ* "save", *wayyôšaʿ*; *y-l-d* → *hôlîḏ* "father"; *y-s-p* → *hôsîp̄* "add, do again".
- **Hollow:** *q-w-m* → *hēqîm* "set up", *wahăqimōṯî* "and I will establish"; *b-w-ʾ* → *hēḇîʾ* "bring", wayyiqtol *wayyāḇēʾ*; *š-w-b* → *hēšîḇ* "bring back"; *m-w-t* → *hēmîṯ* "kill", infinitive *hāmîṯ*; *s-w-r* → *hēsîr* "remove".
- **Third *h*:** *ʿ-l-h* → *heʿĕlâ* "bring up, offer", wayyiqtol *wayyaʿal*; *r-b-h* → *hirbâ* "multiply".

### The niphal and hophal of weak roots

- **First *w*:** *nôlaḏ* "was born" (niphal qatal), yiqtol *yiwwālēḏ*, infinitive *hiwwāleḏ* (lesson 4.14); hophal *hûlleḏeṯ*.
- **Hollow:** *nāḵôn* "established, certain" (*k-w-n*); hophal *hûqam*.
- **First *n*:** niphal *niṣṣāḇ* "standing"; hophal *huggaḏ* (lesson 4.22).

### Finding the root

Ask four questions of a derived form:

1. **Prefix *hô-*, *yô-*, *nô-*?** First *w*: *hôṣîʾ* → *y-ṣ-ʾ*.
2. **Prefix *hē-*, *yā-* with *î* and only two consonants?** Hollow: *hēqîm* → *q-w-m*.
3. **Doubled first visible letter after *hi-*, *ya-*, *ni-*?** First *n*: *higgîḏ* → *n-g-d*.
4. **Ending *-â*, *-eh*, or a short form with two consonants?** Third *h*: *wayyaʿal* → *ʿ-l-h*. Both 3 and 4 together: *wayyaḵ* → *n-k-h*.

## Where it applies

- **The commonest hiphils of all are weak:** *hikkâ* "strike" (*wayyaḵ*), *hôṣîʾ*, *hēḇîʾ*, *hēšîḇ*, *higgîḏ*, *hiṣṣîl*, *hôšîaʿ*, *hēqîm*, *hēmîṯ*, *hēsîr*, *heʿĕlâ*, *hôlîḏ*, *hôsîp̄*.
- **Common weak niphals:** *nôlaḏ*, *nāḵôn*, *niṣṣāḇ*, *niḡlâ*, *nirʾâ* "appear" (lesson 4.13).
- **Weak piels** keep the middle letter: *ṣiwwâ* (*ṣ-w-h*), *qiyyam* (late, Gesenius §72m).
- **Hollow verbs** use the polel instead of the piel (*qômēm*; lesson 7.2).

## Watch out

**Do not take *hô-* for the article plus a word.** *Hôṣîʾ* is one word, a hiphil of *y-ṣ-ʾ*.

**Do not read *wayyaḵ* as from *\*y-k-h* or *\*k-k-h*.** It is *n-k-h*: first *n* assimilated, third *h* cut off.

**Do not confuse *wayyāḇēʾ* "he brought" with *wayyāḇōʾ* "he came".** Hiphil *ē*, qal *ō*.

**Do not confuse *wayyāšeḇ* "he brought back" (hiphil of *š-w-b*) with *wayyāšoḇ* "he returned" (qal).** The vowel after the root's first letter tells you.

**Do not treat *nāḵôn* as a qal participle.** It is a niphal of *k-w-n*; the qal of this root hardly occurs.

**Do not look for a *t* in *hôlîḏ* "he fathered".** *Hôlîḏ* is the hiphil of *y-l-d*; the long genealogies use it: *wayyôleḏ* "and he fathered".

## Connections

Arabic shows the same mechanics in Form IV of weak roots: *ʾaqāma* "set up" (Hebrew *hēqîm*), *ʾawlada* "beget" (Hebrew *hôlîḏ*, with the old *w* still visible), *ʾaʿṭā* "give". The Hebrew *ô* of *hôlîḏ* is the contracted *aw* that Arabic keeps (Gesenius §69i).

## Exercises

1. Give the root and stem of *hôṣēʾṯîḵā*, *wayyāḇēʾ*, *higgîḏ*, *nāḵôn*.
2. Build "and he brought him out" (wayyiqtol + *ʾōṯô*).
3. Build "and he struck the man".
4. Why does *hôšîaʿ* begin with *hô-*?
5. What is the difference between *wayyāḇēʾ* and *wayyāḇōʾ*?
6. Build "I will establish my covenant" as a weqatal.
7. Translate *mî higgîḏ ləḵā* and find the root.
8. A student derives *wayyaḵ* from *k-k-h*. Correct him.
9. Translate *halləḇen mēʾâ-šānâ yiwwālēḏ*.

## Worked answers

1. *y-ṣ-ʾ*, hiphil (with suffix); *b-w-ʾ*, hiphil; *n-g-d*, hiphil; *k-w-n*, niphal participle.
2. *wayyôṣēʾ ʾōṯô* (Genesis 15:5).
3. *wayyaḵ ʾeṯ-hāʾîš*. Hiphil wayyiqtol of *n-k-h*.
4. The root is *y-š-ʿ*, originally *w-š-ʿ*; in the hiphil *haw-* contracts to *hô-* (Gesenius §69i).
5. *Wayyāḇēʾ* is hiphil, "he brought"; *wayyāḇōʾ* is qal, "he came".
6. *wahăqimōṯî ʾeṯ-bərîṯî* (Genesis 9:11).
7. "Who told you?" *n-g-d*: the hiphil *higgîḏ* with the *n* assimilated.
8. The root is *n-k-h*. The doubled *k* that should mark the lost *n* cannot stand at the end of the word, and the final *-eh* has been cut off in the short form (Gesenius §66d, §75).
9. "Can a son be born to a man a hundred years old?" Niphal yiqtol of *y-l-d*.
