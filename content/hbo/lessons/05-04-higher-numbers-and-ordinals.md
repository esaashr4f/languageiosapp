## The idea

Lesson 5.3 gave one to ten. Everything above ten is built from those words, by three simple devices:

- **Eleven to nineteen** put a unit before a special word for ten: *šənêm ʿāśār* "twelve" with masculine nouns, *šətêm ʿeśrēh* with feminine ones. The units keep the reversed gender of lesson 5.3 (Gesenius §97d–e).
- **The tens** are the plurals of the units: *šəlōšîm* "thirty" is the plural of "three", *ʾarbāʿîm* "forty" the plural of "four". Only "twenty", *ʿeśrîm*, is the plural of "ten". The tens have one form for both genders and no construct (§97f).
- **Hundred and thousand** are ordinary nouns: *mēʾâ* "hundred" (feminine), *ʾelep̄* "thousand" (masculine). They are counted like other nouns: *šēš mēʾôṯ* "six hundreds", *šəlōšeṯ ʾălāp̄îm* "three thousands". Two hundred and two thousand are duals: *māʾṯayim*, *ʾalpayim* (§97g).

Then there is a rule that looks odd from English but follows from what a number is. After three to ten, the noun is plural. After the tens, and after eleven and above with a group of common nouns, it is usually **singular**: *ʾarbāʿîm yôm* "forty days", *šətêm ʿeśrēh šānâ* "twelve years". Gesenius lists the nouns that do this: *yôm* "day", *šānâ* "year", *ʾîš* "man", *nep̄eš* "person", *ʾelep̄* "thousand", and a few others "frequently used with numerals" (§134e–g). He compares English "a four-year-old", "ten pound". The singular is the unit of counting, not a plural set.

**Ordinals**, the words "second", "third" and so on, exist only up to ten. They are adjectives in *-î*, built from the units, usually with an extra *î* inside: *šēnî* "second", *šəlîšî* "third", *rəḇîʿî* "fourth" (§98a). "First" is *riʾšôn*, from *rōʾš* "head" (lesson 4.18). Above ten, the cardinal does the job: "the seventeenth day" is "day seventeen" (§134o).

## How it is built

**A number from eleven to nineteen:**

1. Find the noun's gender.
2. Masculine noun: unit in its *-â* form + *ʿāśār*: *šəlōšâ ʿāśār* "thirteen". Feminine noun: short unit + *ʿeśrēh*: *šəlōš ʿeśrēh*.
3. Eleven and twelve are special: *ʾaḥaḏ ʿāśār*, *ʾaḥaṯ ʿeśrēh* (also *ʿaštê ʿāśār*); *šənêm ʿāśār*, *šətêm ʿeśrēh*. Gesenius gives two explanations of *šənêm* and prefers the second: a contracted absolute form, not a reading correction (§97d).

**A compound number such as 130 or 175:**

1. Join the parts with *wə-*: *šəlōšîm ûməʾaṯ* "thirty and a hundred".
2. Order varies. Older prose often puts the larger number first; some books put the units first (Gesenius §134i).
3. The counted noun is often repeated for each part, singular after tens and hundreds, plural after units: *mēʾâ šānâ wəʿeśrîm šānâ wəšeḇaʿ šānîm* "a hundred years and twenty years and seven years", that is, 127 years (§134h).

**An age:** "X is a son of N years": *ben-tišʿîm šānâ* "ninety years old", literally "a son of ninety year". For a woman, *baṯ*.

**A date:**

1. The year: *bišnaṯ* "in the year of" + number, or the number + *šānâ* with *bə-*.
2. The month: an ordinal with the article, *baḥōḏeš haššēnî* "in the second month", or alone, *bāriʾšôn* "in the first (month)" (§134p).
3. The day of the month: a **cardinal** + *laḥōḏeš* "of the month", with *bə-*: *bəʾeḥāḏ laḥōḏeš* "on the first of the month", *bəšiḇʿâ-ʿāśār yôm laḥōḏeš* "on the seventeenth day of the month" (§134p).

### Building a phrase

"Abram was ninety-nine years old":

1. "Was": *wayhî*, opening the scene (lesson 4.18).
2. "Ninety": *tišʿîm*, the plural of nine; after a ten, *šānâ* singular: *tišʿîm šānâ*.
3. "And nine": *šānâ* is feminine, so the short form *tēšaʿ*, and after a unit the noun is plural: *wəṯēšaʿ šānîm*.
4. "Son of": *ben-*, in construct before the whole number.
5. *Wayhî ʾaḇrām ben-tišʿîm šānâ wəṯēšaʿ šānîm* (Genesis 17:1).

## Where it applies

- **Teens:** examples 3–4; in dates, *bəšiḇʿâ-ʿāśār yôm laḥōḏeš* "on the seventeenth day of the month" (Genesis 7:11, 8:4).
- **Tens with a singular noun:** *ʾarbāʿîm yôm* (example 5), *šəlōšîm šānâ*.
- **Tens with a plural noun:** with nouns outside Gesenius's list, such as *bānîm* "sons" and *ʿārîm* "cities" (§134e), and when the number follows the noun, mostly in late books.
- **Hundreds and thousands:** *mēʾâ*, *māʾṯayim*, *šēš mēʾôṯ*, *ʾelep̄*, *ʾalpayim*, *šəlōšeṯ ʾălāp̄îm* (examples 6–7, 9; lesson 3.13). "Ten thousand" is *rəḇāḇâ*.
- **Compound numbers, with the noun repeated:** example 7.
- **Ages:** *ben-* or *baṯ-* + number (example 8); in a question, *hăḇaṯ-tišʿîm šānâ* "(is she) ninety years old?" (Genesis 17:17).
- **Ordinals one to ten, as adjectives:** *yôm šēnî*, *bayyôm haššəḇîʿî* (examples 1–2), *haššēnîṯ* with a feminine noun (lesson 5.3, example 1).
- **Ordinals above ten:** the cardinal (example 3; Gesenius §134o).
- **Dates:** day by cardinal, month and year by ordinal (examples 10–11).
- **Months alone:** *bāriʾšôn*, *baššēnî* "in the first, in the second (month)" (example 11).
- **Leaving out the unit:** *ʾelep̄ kesep̄* "a thousand (shekels) of silver" (example 12; §134n).
- **"Two by two", distributives:** *šənayim šənayim* (Genesis 7:9; §134q).
- **"A second time":** *šēnîṯ*, the feminine ordinal as an adverb (Gesenius §100f).

## Watch out

**Do not put the noun in the plural after a ten by analogy with English "forty days".** *\*ʾarbāʿîm yāmîm* is wrong: WLC has *ʾarbāʿîm yôm* all seventeen times the phrase occurs. The rule is the opposite of three to ten, which take the plural.

**Do not apply the reversed gender to the tens.** *ʿEśrîm*, *šəlōšîm* have one form for all nouns. Only the units, including the units inside 11–19, reverse.

**Do not look for "eleventh", "twelfth" as separate words.** There are none: "the twelfth year" is *šətêm-ʿeśrēh šānâ* or *bišnaṯ šətêm ʿeśrēh*. Ordinals stop at ten.

**Do not use an ordinal for the day of the month.** *\*bayyôm hāriʾšôn laḥōḏeš* is not the classical idiom: *bəʾeḥāḏ laḥōḏeš* "on (day) one of the month". For the tenth Gesenius notes a special form, always *beʿāśôr laḥōḏeš* "on the tenth of the month" (§134p).

**Do not read *ben-* in an age as "son".** *Ben-tišʿîm šānâ* is "ninety years old"; nobody has a son. The construct "son of" means "belonging to the class of" (lesson 3.6).

**Do not drop the article on one half of a definite ordinal phrase.** *\*bayyôm šəḇîʿî* is wrong for "on the seventh day": *bayyôm haššəḇîʿî*. Ordinals follow the adjective rules (lesson 3.11). Genesis 1 shows the indefinite pattern for days two to five (*yôm šēnî* "a second day"), and the article only on the ordinal for the sixth and seventh (*yôm haššiššî*); translations differ on how much to read into this.

**Do not read *šənêm ʿāśār* as a construct "two of ten".** It is the absolute "twelve", a contracted dual (Gesenius §97d); *šənê* would be the construct.

## Connections

Arabic builds its tens the same way, as plurals of the units (*thalāthūna* "thirty" from *thalātha*), and it too puts a singular noun after eleven and above: *arbaʿūna yawman* "forty days". Arabic goes further and puts that singular in the accusative, which is the case Hebrew has lost. Spanish *cuarenta días*, French *quarante jours* use the plural, as English does.

## Worked answers

1. *Šənêm ʿāśār bānîm*; *šətêm ʿeśrēh bānôṯ*. Masculine *bēn*: *šənêm* + *ʿāśār*; feminine *baṯ*: *šətêm* + *ʿeśrēh* (Gesenius §97e). After 11–19 these nouns are plural, since they are not on the list of nouns that stay singular (§134f). Constructed by rule; *šənêm ʿāśār* is attested in Genesis 35:22.
2. After the tens, *yôm* is one of the nouns used in the singular (Gesenius §134e). The singular is the unit counted.
3. "Adam lived a hundred and thirty years." Thirty first, then *məʾaṯ*, the construct of "hundred", with *wə-*, then singular *šānâ*.
4. *Śārâ baṯ-tišʿîm šānâ*. "Daughter of ninety year": *baṯ* in construct, the ten with a singular noun (Genesis 17:17 has *hăḇaṯ-tišʿîm šānâ* "is she ninety years old?" of Sarah).
5. "On the first of the second month." *ʾEḥāḏ* "one" is a cardinal, used for the day; *haššēnî* is an ordinal, used for the month (Gesenius §134p).
6. Each part of a compound number may have the noun: singular after the hundred and the ten, plural after the unit seven, which needs a plural noun (§134h; lesson 5.3).
7. "A second day"; "on the seventh day". An ordinal is an adjective; with a definite noun it takes the article too (lesson 3.11).
8. *Bəšiḇʿâ-ʿāśār yôm laḥōḏeš* (Genesis 7:11). *Yôm* is masculine, so the unit is *šiḇʿâ* + *ʿāśār*; the noun stays singular; the day of the month is a cardinal.
9. "A thousand (shekels) of silver." The unit of weight is omitted after the number (Gesenius §134n).
10. There is no ordinal "eleventh". Use the cardinal: *bəʾaḥaḏ ʿāśār yôm* "on day eleven" (compare Deuteronomy 1:3, *bəʿaštê-ʿāśār ḥōḏeš* "in the eleventh month", with the cardinal). Constructed by rule.
