## The idea

English has "taller" and "tallest", Spanish *más alto* and *el más alto*. Hebrew has neither. Gesenius puts it flatly: "Hebrew possesses no special forms either for the comparative or superlative of the adjective" (§133a). Instead it uses the plain adjective and lets a preposition or the article do the work.

**"More than" is *min* "from".** *Gāḇōah mikkol-hāʿām* is "tall from all the people", that is, "taller than all the people". Gesenius explains the image: "tall away from all the people", standing out beyond them (§133a). The quality separates one thing from the others. You have seen this already: *ṣaddîq ʾattâ mimmennî* "you are more in the right than I" (lesson 2.5) and *qāṭōntî mikkōl* "I am too small for all" (lesson 4.2).

**"Too ... to" is the same *min*.** If a quality separates something from a goal, it is "too much" for it: *gāḏôl ʿăwōnî minnəśōʾ* "my punishment is great away from bearing", "too great to bear" (§133c). Only the context tells "more than" from "too ... for".

**"The most" has no form at all.** A definite adjective is enough: *haqqāṭān* "the small one" is "the youngest" when the family is in view (§133f–g). So *haggāḏôl* can be "the great", "the greater" or "the greatest". The article picks out the one who has the quality; the number of people compared decides the English.

**Likeness is *kə-*** and its longer form *kəmô*: *kāmôḵā* "like you".

## How it is built

**"X is more Y than Z":**

1. Put the quality as an adjective (or a stative verb): *ṭôḇ* "good", *gāḇōah* "tall", *ṣāḏəqâ* "she is in the right".
2. Put the standard after *min*: *mikkol-hāʿām*, *mēʾărî* "than a lion", *mimmennî* "than I" (lesson 3.9 for the suffixed forms).
3. The verbless clause needs no "is" (lesson 2.1): *ṭôḇ ... mēʿăśārâ bānîm* "better than ten sons".

**With a verb of action**, *min* means "more than" the other person: *wayyeʾĕhaḇ ... ʾeṯ-rāḥēl millēʾâ* "he loved Rachel more than Leah" (Gesenius §133b).

**"Too Y to do V":** adjective + *min* + infinitive: *gāḏôl ... minnəśōʾ*, *qāṭōn mēhāḵîl* "too small to hold" (§133c).

**The superlative:**

1. The adjective with the article, when the group is clear: *haqqāṭān* "the youngest".
2. "Than all": *gāḇōah mikkol-hāʿām* "taller than all", a superlative by meaning.
3. A noun in construct before its own plural: *ʿeḇeḏ ʿăḇāḏîm* "slave of slaves", the lowest slave; *qōḏeš haqqŏḏāšîm* "the holy of holies" (§133i; lesson 3.4).
4. Or *məʾōḏ* "very" (lesson 5.1) for a high degree without comparison.

### Building a sentence

"There was no man among the Israelites better than he":

1. "There was no man": *ʾên ʾîš* (lesson 2.7).
2. "Among the Israelites": *min* "from" picks out the group: *mibbənê yiśrāʾēl*.
3. "Better": *ṭôḇ*.
4. "Than he": *min* + 3SG.M suffix: *mimmennû*.
5. *Wəʾên ʾîš mibbənê yiśrāʾēl ṭôḇ mimmennû* (1 Samuel 9:2). Note the two different *min*: "from among" and "than".

## Where it applies

- **Adjective + *min*, "more ... than":** examples 1–2, 5.
- **Stative verb + *min*:** *ṣāḏəqâ mimmennî* (example 4), *wayyiḡbah mikkol-hāʿām* "he was taller than all the people" (1 Samuel 10:23; Gesenius §133b).
- **Action verb + *min*, "more than":** examples 3, 7; *ʾāhaḇ ʾeṯ-yôsēp̄ mikkol-bānāyw* "he loved Joseph more than all his sons" (Genesis 37:3).
- **Choosing one over another:** *bāḥar ... min* "choose ... rather than" (Gesenius §133b).
- **"Too ... for / to":** adjective or verb + *min* + noun or infinitive (example 6; lesson 4.2 *qāṭōntî mikkōl*); *hayippālēʾ mêYHWH dāḇār* "is anything too wonderful for the LORD?" (Genesis 18:14).
- **"Enough", "too much":** *raḇ-lāḵem* "you have had enough" (Deuteronomy 1:6; Gesenius §133c).
- **Comparative of two:** the adjective with the article, *haqqāṭōn*, *haggāḏôl* (example 8); *hammāʾôr haggāḏōl* "the greater light" (Genesis 1:16; lesson 3.11).
- **Superlative in a group:** *haqqāṭān*, *haggəḏōlîm* (example 9).
- **Superlative with a genitive or suffix:** *qəṭōn bānāyw* "the youngest of his sons" (2 Chronicles 21:17, late; Gesenius §133g).
- **Superlative by repetition in construct:** *ʿeḇeḏ ʿăḇāḏîm* (example 10), *qōḏeš haqqŏḏāšîm* (lesson 3.4), *šîr haššîrîm* "the finest song" (Song of Songs 1:1).
- **Equality, "as ... as", "like":** *kāmôḵā* (example 11), *kāmōhû* "like him" (1 Samuel 10:24), *kə-* before a noun (lesson 2.5).
- **High degree without comparison:** *ṭôḇ məʾōḏ* (lesson 5.1), *ʿaḏ-məʾōḏ* "exceedingly".

## Watch out

**Do not look for a comparative ending.** *\*gəḇōhēr*, or anything like Spanish *más*, does not exist. Write the plain adjective and *min*: *gāḇōah mikkol-hāʿām*.

**Do not translate every *min* after an adjective as "than".** *Gāḏôl ʿăwōnî minnəśōʾ* is "too great to bear", not "greater than bearing". With an infinitive, *min* usually means "too ... to" (Gesenius §133c).

**Do not read *haggāḏôl* always as "the great".** In a pair it is "the elder" or "the greater"; in a family it may be "the eldest". *Hammāʾôr haggāḏōl* "the greater light" compares two lights (Genesis 1:16).

**Do not use the article with an adjective that follows *min* to make it "the most".** *\*ṭôḇ mēhaṭṭôḇ* is not Hebrew. The superlative comes from the article on the adjective itself, or from "than all", or from the construct of a noun with its own plural.

**Do not confuse "than" with "from among".** In *ʾên ʾîš mibbənê yiśrāʾēl ṭôḇ mimmennû* the first *min* picks out the group ("among the Israelites") and the second is the comparison ("than he"). English uses two different words; Hebrew uses one.

**Do not translate *ʾên ... kāmôḵā* as "there is no one like you" only.** It often means "you are the most ... of all". Read it as a superlative where the context praises.

**Do not confuse *ʿeḇeḏ ʿăḇāḏîm* with a plain construct chain "servant of servants".** It is a fixed way of saying the extreme: the lowest slave. *Šîr haššîrîm* is "the song above all songs", not "a song made of songs".

## Connections

Arabic has a special comparative form, *ʾafʿal*: *ʾakbar* "greater", from *kabīr* "great". Hebrew has nothing like it. But the Arabic standard of comparison takes *min* "from", exactly as in Hebrew: *ʾakbar min* "greater than", Hebrew *gāḏôl min*. Spanish *de* in *más de diez* "more than ten" has a similar origin in "from".

## Exercises

1. Translate *mah-mmāṯôq middəḇaš* and say what *min* means here.
2. Say "David was taller than Saul" with *gāḇōah* (verbless clause).
3. Translate *wayyeʾĕhaḇ ʾeṯ-rāḥēl millēʾâ*. Why is there no adjective?
4. *Gāḏôl ʿăwōnî minnəśōʾ*: why is this "too great to bear" and not "greater than bearing"?
5. How does Hebrew say "the youngest" in a family? Give the verse.
6. Translate *ʿeḇeḏ ʿăḇāḏîm* and name the construction.
7. In *ʾên ʾîš mibbənê yiśrāʾēl ṭôḇ mimmennû*, explain each *min*.
8. Correct *\*hayyôṯēr ṭôḇ* for "the best", modelled on Modern Hebrew or on Spanish *el más bueno*.
9. Translate *ʾên-nāḇôn wəḥāḵām kāmôḵā*. Is it a comparison of equality or a superlative?
10. Say "the LORD is greater than all the gods" with *gāḏôl*, *min* and *kol*.

## Worked answers

1. "What is sweeter than honey?" *Min* "from" gives the standard of comparison (Gesenius §133a).
2. *Dāwiḏ gāḇōah miššāʾûl*. A verbless clause; *min* before the name, with its *n* assimilated (constructed by rule).
3. "He loved Rachel more than Leah." With an action verb, *min* compares the objects of the verb directly; no adjective is needed (Gesenius §133b).
4. With an infinitive after *min*, the quality removes the subject from the goal: "too great to bear" (Gesenius §133c). A comparison between a punishment and an act of bearing would make no sense.
5. With the article: *haqqāṭān* "the small one", which in a family of eight means "the youngest" (1 Samuel 17:14; Gesenius §133g).
6. "The lowest of slaves." A noun in construct before its own plural, a periphrasis for the superlative (Gesenius §133i).
7. *Mibbənê yiśrāʾēl* "from among the Israelites" picks out the group; *mimmennû* "than he" is the comparison. One preposition, two jobs.
8. *Haṭṭôḇ*, the adjective with the article, when the group is clear; or *ṭôḇ mikkōl* "better than all". Biblical Hebrew has no word for "more" in comparison (Gesenius §133a, g).
9. "There is no one as discerning and wise as you." Formally equality (*kəmô*), but in praise it works as a superlative: you are the wisest.
10. *Gāḏôl YHWH mikkol-hāʾĕlōhîm*: adjective first, as in a verbless clause, then *min* + *kol* + the definite noun. Jethro says exactly this, after *kî* "that" (Exodus 18:11).
