## The idea

*Kōl* is one of the commonest words in the Bible, and you have glossed it "all" since lesson 1.3. This lesson shows that it means "all", "the whole", "every", "any" or, with a negative, "no", and that one rule decides which.

The rule follows from what *kōl* is. It is not an adjective but a **noun** meaning "the totality, the whole" (Gesenius §127b). Before another noun it stands in construct, *kol-* (with qameṣ ḥaṭuf, lesson 1.10), and the following noun is its genitive: *kol-hāʾāreṣ* is "the totality of the earth". Now apply lesson 3.6: a construct chain is definite if its last member is definite.

- **Before a definite noun** (with the article, a name or a suffix), *kol-* means the whole of a known thing: *kol-hāʾāreṣ* "the whole earth", *kol-ʿaḇdê p̄arʿōh* "all Pharaoh's servants". Gesenius compares French *tous les hommes*, *toute la ville* (§127b).
- **Before an indefinite noun**, *kol-* means "every", "any", "every kind of": *kol-ʿēṣ* "every tree", *kol-məlāʾḵâ* "any work". Gesenius compares French *tout homme*, *à tout prix* (§127b).

So the article, not a different word, separates "all the" from "every". English has two words where Hebrew has one, and reads the second from the noun.

**With a negative, *kol* + indefinite noun gives "no ... at all":** *lōʾ-ṯaʿăśeh ḵol-məlāʾḵâ* "you shall not do any work", "you shall do no work". Hebrew has no word for "nothing" or "no one" of the English kind. It negates the verb and uses *kol*, *ʾîš* "anyone" or *məʾûmâ* "anything" (lesson 4.1).

**Each** is *ʾîš* "man" used for "each one", with a singular suffix on what belongs to each: *ʾîš ʾamtaḥtô* "each his sack". For women, *ʾiššâ*.

The other quantifiers are ordinary words: *rabbîm* "many", *məʿaṭ* "a few, a little" (lesson 5.5), *šəʾār* and *yeṯer* "the rest", *ḥăṣî* "half", and *min* "some of" (lesson 2.5).

## How it is built

1. **Decide whether you mean a known whole or any member.** "All the people (here)" is a known whole; "every person" is any member.
2. **For a known whole, make the noun definite:** *kol-hāʿām* "all the people", *kol-yəmê ḥayyêḵā* "all the days of your life" (definite by the suffix).
3. **For "every", leave it indefinite**, usually in the singular: *kol-ʾîš* "every man, everyone", *kol-bāśār* "every creature".
4. **For "none", negate the verb and keep *kol* + indefinite:** *lōʾ ... kol-*.
5. **For "all of them", put a suffix on *kol*:** the base is *kull-* with the doubling of *l* (root k-l-l): *kullô* "all of him, all of it", *kullām* "all of them", *kullānû* "all of us".
6. **Agreement:** a verb after *kol* + a collective noun is often plural: *kol-hāʾāreṣ bāʾû* "the whole world came".

*Kōl* alone, as an absolute noun, means "everything", "all": *bakkōl* "in everything".

### Building a sentence

"You shall not do any work":

1. "Do": yiqtol 2SG.M of *ʿāśâ*: *taʿăśeh* (lesson 4.16).
2. Prohibition: *lōʾ* + yiqtol (lesson 4.8): *lōʾ-ṯaʿăśeh*.
3. "Any work": indefinite *məlāʾḵâ* after *kol-*: *ḵol-məlāʾḵâ*.
4. *Lōʾ-ṯaʿăśeh ḵol-məlāʾḵâ* (Exodus 20:10). The negative + *kol* gives "no work at all".

## Where it applies

- **"All the", "the whole":** *kol* + definite noun (examples 1–2, 9); *kol-hayyôm* "all day" (lesson 5.1).
- **"Every", "any", "every kind of":** *kol* + indefinite noun (example 3); *kol-ʿēṣ* "every tree" (lesson 4.20); *kol-dāḇār* "anything" (Gesenius §127b).
- **"Everyone":** *kol-ʾîš* (example 5).
- **"Everything":** *kōl*, *hakkōl*, *bakkōl* (example 7).
- **"None", "no ... at all":** negative + *kol* + indefinite (example 4); *lōʾ ... ʾîš* "no one" (example 5); *lōʾ ... məʾûmâ* "nothing" (Genesis 22:12; lesson 4.1); *ʾên* + noun "there is no" (lesson 2.7).
- **"Each":** *ʾîš*, with a singular suffix (example 6); *ʾîš ʾel-ʾāḥîw* "each to his brother", "to one another" (Genesis 37:19); for women *ʾiššâ*.
- **"All of us / them / it":** *kullānû*, *kullām*, *kullô*, *kullāh* (examples 8–9). These also stand after a noun for emphasis.
- **The whole of a body part:** *kol-rōʾš* "the whole head", not "every head" (Gesenius §127c).
- **"Some":** *min* + noun (example 10).
- **"Many", "few":** *rabbîm* (example 11), *məʿaṭ* (lesson 5.5); *ʾên mispār* "without number, countless".
- **"The rest":** *šəʾār*, *yeṯer*, *šəʾērîṯ* (lesson 4.9 for *yeṯer*).
- **"Half":** *ḥăṣî*, in construct before what it divides: *ḥăṣî šēḇeṭ* "half a tribe".
- **"One ... the other", "each other":** *ʾeḥāḏ ... ʾeḥāḏ* (example 12); *ʾîš ... rēʿēhû* "each ... his fellow" (lesson 3.7).

## Watch out

**Do not read *kol* + definite noun as "every".** *Kol-hāʾāreṣ* is "the whole earth", not "every land". *Kol-ʾereṣ* without the article would be "every land, any land".

**Do not put the article on *kol* itself to make "all the".** *\*hakkol-hāʿām* is wrong. The article goes on the following noun: *kol-hāʿām*. *Hakkōl* exists only alone, "the whole, everything".

**Do not translate *lōʾ ... kol* as "not all".** *Lōʾ-ṯaʿăśeh ḵol-məlāʾḵâ* does not mean "you shall not do all the work" (some is allowed); it means "you shall do no work". Hebrew has no separate word for "none".

**Do not look for a word "nobody" or "nothing".** *\*ʾên-ʾîš ʿāmaḏ* is not how prose says "nobody stood". Negate the verb: *wəlōʾ-ʿāmaḏ ʾîš* (example 5).

**Do not use a plural suffix with distributive *ʾîš*.** *\*wayyip̄təḥû ʾîš ʾamtəḥōṯām* is wrong; each has his own sack: *ʾîš ʾamtaḥtô*.

**Do not expect the verb after *kol* to agree in form only.** *Kol-hāʾāreṣ bāʾû* has a plural verb because the meaning is "all the people"; *wayyiḡwaʿ kol-bāśār* has a singular verb. Both are normal.

**Do not pronounce *kol-* as *kāl-*.** Before maqqef the vowel is qameṣ ḥaṭuf, *o* (lesson 1.10); alone it is *kōl*.

## Connections

Arabic *kull* is the same word and works the same way: *kull al-nās* "all the people" before a definite noun, *kull insān* "every person" before an indefinite one, and *kulluhum* "all of them" with a suffix. French draws the same line with the article, *tous les hommes* against *tout homme*, as Gesenius noted (§127b).

## Exercises

1. Translate *kol-hāʿām* and *kol-ʿam*, and explain the difference.
2. Say "you shall eat no bread" with *lōʾ*, *tōʾḵal*, *kol* and *leḥem*.
3. Translate *wayyip̄təḥû ʾîš ʾamtaḥtô*. Why is the suffix singular?
4. Say "all of them" and "all of us".
5. Translate *wəlōʾ-ʿāmaḏ ʾîš ʾittô* and say what word English uses that Hebrew lacks.
6. Why is the verb plural in *kol-hāʾāreṣ bāʾû*?
7. Translate *wayyāḇēʾ qayin mippərî hāʾăḏāmâ*. Where is "some"?
8. A student writes *\*hakkol-hammeleḵ* for "the whole king". What is wrong, and what would *kol-hammeleḵ* mean?
9. Translate *waYHWH bēraḵ ʾeṯ-ʾaḇrāhām bakkōl*.
10. Why does *kol* take the construct form *kol-* before a noun?

## Worked answers

1. "All the people" (definite: the known whole); "every people, every nation" (indefinite: any member). The article on the following noun decides (Gesenius §127b).
2. *Lōʾ-ṯōʾḵal kol-leḥem*, constructed by rule: negative + *kol* + indefinite noun. Exodus 12:20 has the same pattern with the object fronted: *kol-maḥmeṣeṯ lōʾ ṯōʾḵēlû* "you shall eat nothing leavened".
3. "And each opened his sack." *ʾÎš* "each" is singular, and the suffix refers to it, one man and his own sack.
4. *Kullām*, *kullānû*: *kol* with suffixes, on the base *kull-*.
5. "And no one stayed with him." English uses "no one"; Hebrew negates the verb and keeps *ʾîš* "anyone".
6. "The whole earth" means its people; the verb agrees with the sense, a plural (Genesis 41:57).
7. "Cain brought some of the fruit of the ground." "Some" is *min* "from" before the noun (the partitive; lesson 2.5).
8. *Kol* never takes the article before a noun; the article goes on the noun: *kol-hammeleḵ*. Gesenius notes that before a definite noun *kol* means "the whole" (§127b), but a person is not normally spoken of as "the whole king"; "all the kings" is *kol-hammələḵîm*.
9. "And the LORD had blessed Abraham in everything." *Kōl* alone, with the article and *bə-*, is "everything".
10. *Kōl* is a noun, "totality"; before another noun it stands in construct, and its genitive decides whether it means "all the" or "every" (Gesenius §127b; lesson 3.5).
