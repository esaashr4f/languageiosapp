## The idea

Modern Georgian is written in an alphabet called **Mkhedruli** (*mxedruli*). The name comes from *mxedari* "horseman, knight", and Wiktionary notes it was coined in contrast to the older church script; lesson 1-8 tells that story. Mkhedruli has 33 letters. Hewitt calls the script "fully phonemic": each letter stands for one sound and each sound has one letter. There are no silent letters, no letters with two values, and no spelling rules of the English or French kind. Once you know the letters, you can say any word you read and write any word you hear.

Two more facts make the script easy to start.

It is written from left to right, like English, with spaces between words.

It has no capital letters. Aronson stresses this: every letter has only one form. Sentences and names begin with the same letters as everything else.

What takes practice is that many letters are round and look alike. The key to telling them apart is their **height**. Imagine the two lines of a school exercise book. Aronson sorts the letters into four groups:

- letters that sit between the two lines;
- letters that rise above the upper line;
- letters that hang below the lower line;
- letters that do both.

The same round shape can be two different letters depending on where it sits. Always read a letter together with its position.

This lesson teaches twelve letters: the five vowels and seven consonants you already know how to say. With them you can read real words at once. Lessons 1-6 and 1-7 add the rest.

## How it is built

Here are the twelve letters, each with its sound, its position and its name. Letter shapes vary between typefaces, so learn each from the examples in your own app as well as from these descriptions.

**The vowels**

- ა *a*: a small hook sitting between the lines. Name: *an*.
- ე *e*: an arch whose right side drops below the lower line and curls back. Name: *en*.
- ი *i*: a plain arch, like a Latin n, between the lines. Name: *in*.
- ო *o*: a rounded arch with its left side curled in, between the lines. Name: *on*.
- უ *u*: like ე with a second arch attached on its left; hangs below the line. Name: *uni*.

**The consonants**

- მ *m*: a round bowl with a stem rising above the line on the right. Name: *man*.
- ნ *n*: a round bowl with a stem rising above the line on the left, like a 6. Name: *nar*.
- ლ *l*: a wide letter of small arches whose left end sweeps below the line. Name: *las*.
- რ *r*: like ო, but its curl rises above the upper line. Name: *rae*.
- ვ *v*: a 3 that hangs below the line. Name: *vin*.
- ს *s*: a U whose left arm rises above the line. Name: *san*.
- ჰ *h*: a 3 shape that rises above the line. Name: *hae*.

The letter names come from Wiktionary, which gives each with its IPA. They are useful labels when you talk about spelling. Most of them end in *-n*.

To read a word, take three steps.

1. Find each letter's height first, then its shape.
2. Say each letter's sound in turn. Nothing is silent.
3. Count the vowels to know the syllables, as in lesson 1-1.

To write a word, reverse it: say the word slowly, write one letter for each sound, and check the heights.

## Watch out

**ი is not n.** The arch ი looks like a Latin n and is the vowel *i*. The Georgian *n* is ნ, the bowl with a tall stem.

**ო and რ.** They differ only in height: ო stays between the lines, რ climbs above. In fast handwriting, check the top.

**ვ and ჰ.** Both are 3-like. ვ hangs below the line; ჰ rises above it.

**No capitals, so no help with names.** *nino* (a woman's name) is written with the same letters as any other word. Aronson warns that you must learn to recognise names from context.

## Connections

Korean Hangul and Georgian Mkhedruli are both alphabets where letter and sound match almost perfectly, so Korean readers know the feeling: a week of effort, then you can sound out anything. Unlike Hangul, Mkhedruli writes letters in a row, not in syllable blocks. Arabic readers will find it easier than Arabic script: no joining forms, and vowels are always written.

## Exercises

1. Read aloud and transliterate: ლომი, ნავი, მელა, ერი.
2. Write in Mkhedruli: *mama*, *ena*, *suli*, *salami*.
3. Sort these letters by height into the four groups you know so far: ა ე ი ო უ მ ნ ლ რ ვ ს ჰ.
4. A learner reads ნისლი as "insli". Which letter has he misread, and how?
5. How do you tell ო from რ, and ვ from ჰ?
6. Spell *marili* aloud using the letter names.
7. How many syllables has მარილი, and how can you tell from the letters alone?

## Worked answers

1. *lomi* "lion", *navi* "boat", *mela* "fox", *eri* "nation".
2. მამა, ენა, სული, სალამი.
3. Between the lines: ა ი ო. Above: მ ნ რ ს ჰ. Below: ე უ ლ ვ. None of these twelve letters goes both above and below; you meet those in the next lessons.
4. He has read the first letter, ნ (*n*), as if it were ი (*i*), and the ი as *n*: he has taken the Latin look-alike for the sound. ნ is the bowl with a tall stem; ი is the plain arch.
5. ო stays between the lines and რ rises above. ვ hangs below the line and ჰ rises above it.
6. *man*, *an*, *rae*, *in*, *las*, *in*.
7. Three: it has three vowel letters, ა, ი and ი, and every vowel letter is one syllable.
