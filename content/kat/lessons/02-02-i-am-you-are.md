## The idea

Lesson 2-1 had one "is": *aris*, or its short form *-a*. That covers "this", "Tbilisi", "Father": anyone or anything you talk about. To say "I am a student" or "you are tired" you need other forms, because a Georgian verb changes with its subject.

Grammar sorts the people in a conversation into three **persons**. The **first person** is the speaker: "I", and "we" for a group that includes the speaker. The **second person** is the one spoken to: "you". The **third person** is everyone and everything else: "he", "she", "it", "they". Each person is **singular** (one) or **plural** (more than one). That gives six forms, and Georgian "be" has a different form for each:

- *var* "I am", *xar* "you are", *aris* "he, she, it is"
- *vart* "we are", *xart* "you are" (more than one), *arian* "they are"

The glosses label these by person and number: `be.1SG` is "be, first person singular", `be.3PL` "be, third person plural". From this lesson on, labels 1, 2, 3, SG and PL are used without warning.

The six forms are not a random list. Bolkvadze and Kiziria cut them into pieces: *v-ar*, *x-ar*, *ar-i-s*, *v-ar-t*, *x-ar-t*, *ar-i-an*. The root is *ar-*. In front of it, *v-* marks the first person and *x-* the second. Behind it, *-t* marks the plural of the first and second persons. The third person has suffixes instead: *-s* for one, *-an* for more than one.

Two of these pieces will follow you through the whole language. The *v-* of *var* is the first-person marker of almost every Georgian verb: lesson 2-5 shows it on "I write". The *-s* of *aris* is the commonest third-person ending. The *x-* is different: Bolkvadze and Kiziria explain that the second person has no marker in modern Georgian, and that *x-* is an old one, kept only in "you are" and in two forms of the verb "come". So *xar* is a fossil, and the only place you will see it regularly.

This guide does not cut the copula into pieces in its glosses, because the pieces do not repeat in a regular pattern elsewhere: *var* is glossed as one piece, `be.1SG`.

### The pronouns

The **personal pronouns** are the words for "I", "you", "he" and the rest. Georgian has six:

- მე *me* "I", შენ *šen* "you" (one person, familiar)
- ის *is* "he, she, it", also "that"
- ჩვენ *čven* "we", თქვენ *tkven* "you" (more than one, or polite), ისინი *isini* "they", also "those"

They carry no gender. Bolkvadze and Kiziria stress that none of the Georgian pronouns marks it, and Wiktionary glosses *is* as "he, she, it (no gender differentiation)". *Is ekimia* is "he is a doctor" or "she is a doctor", and only the context says which. There is a second word for "he, she, it", იგი *igi*, with the same meaning; this guide uses *is*.

*Čven* is any "we": you and I, he and I, they and I. Bolkvadze and Kiziria list every combination it can cover. Some languages have two words for "we", one including the listener and one excluding; Georgian has one.

*Tkven* has two uses. It is "you" for several people, and it is the polite "you" for one person, like French *vous*. The verb is plural in both: *tkven xart*. The predicate is not: to one person you say *tkven masc̣avlebeli xart* "you are a teacher", with "teacher" singular, because there is one teacher.

The pronouns are glossed by person and number alone: *me* `1SG`, *tkven* `2PL`.

## How it is built

1. Decide who the subject is. That fixes the person and number.
2. Pick the form of "be": *var*, *xar*, *aris*, *vart*, *xart* or *arian*.
3. Put the pronoun first, the predicate in the nominative after it, and "be" last.

"I am a doctor": the subject is the speaker, first person singular, so *var*. "Doctor" is the predicate, nominative *ekimi*. Result: *me ekimi var*.

"You (polite) are a teacher": *tkven*, so the plural *xart*; one teacher, so *masc̣avlebeli*: *tkven masc̣avlebeli xart*.

The short form *-a* exists only for the third person singular. For every other person, write the full form.

The pronoun is often left out, because *var* already says "I": *ekimi var* "I am a doctor" is the ordinary way to say it. Lesson 2-9 explains when the pronoun is kept.

## Where it applies

- **Every subject of "be"**: identity (*me ekimi var*), description (*nino bednieria*), place (*čven ak vart*), as in lesson 2-1, now in all six persons.
- **Pronouns as subjects of any verb.** The same six pronouns are the subjects of "write", "see" and the rest in lesson 2-5. In the present they stay as they are.
- **Polite address.** *Tkven* with a plural verb, to one person you do not know well, to an elder, in shops and offices. A title can be added: *baṭoni giorgi ekimia* "Mr Giorgi is a doctor". Bolkvadze and Kiziria also describe special polite verbs that replace "be" in very courteous speech; part 7 covers them.
- **"That" and "those".** *Is* and *isini* also point at things away from the speaker: *is lamazi kalakia*. Lesson 3-9 sets them beside *es* "this" and a third word, *eg*.
- **Names and nouns are third person.** *Giorgi*, *mama*, *es saxli* all take *aris* or *-a*; a plural subject of people takes *arian*.
- **Left out.** The pronoun is usually dropped when the verb shows the person: *ekimi var*, *sṭudenṭi xar*. Lesson 2-9.
- **The pronouns in other jobs.** *Me*, *šen*, *čven*, *tkven* keep the same shape when they are objects, but *is* changes (*mas*, lesson 2-6). Lesson 3-8 gives every form.

## Watch out

**Do not use *-a* outside the third person singular.** *\*Me ekimia* puts "is" with "I". Say *me ekimi var*. For "they", *\*isini ekimebia* is wrong for people; say *isini ekimebi arian*.

**Make the verb match the subject.** *\*Šen sṭudenṭi var* pairs "you" with "am". The pronoun and the verb must agree: *šen sṭudenṭi xar*.

**Polite *tkven* is still plural for the verb.** *\*Tkven masc̣avlebeli xar* mixes polite address with a familiar verb. Say *tkven masc̣avlebeli xart*. Do the opposite with the noun: *tkven masc̣avleblebi xart* means "you are teachers", several of them.

**The third person plural does not end in -t.** *\*Arit* or *\*arist* is modelled on *vart*, *xart*. The third person has its own ending: *arian*.

**No *-s* on the other persons.** *\*Vars* adds the third-person *-s* to "I am". The *-s* of *aris* means "he, she, it": *var* has none.

**Do not translate *is* as "he" by default.** English and Spanish force a choice. Georgian does not make it, so a translation has to take it from the context. If the gender matters, Georgian uses a noun: *kali* "woman", *ḳaci* "man".

**No *c'est* versus *il est*.** French distinguishes "c'est un médecin" from "il est médecin". Georgian has one pattern: *is ekimia*, *es ekimia*.

**Do not confuse *is* and *es*.** *Es* is "this", near the speaker; *is* is "that", or "he, she, it". *Es saxlia* is "this is a house"; *is saxlia* is "that is a house" or "it is a house".

## Connections

French *tu* and *vous* match *šen* and *tkven* exactly, including the plural verb with a singular *vous*: *vous êtes professeur*, *tkven masc̣avlebeli xart*. Spanish leaves out *yo* and *tú* the way Georgian leaves out *me* and *šen*. Japanese and Korean have no person marking on the verb at all, so they need context where Georgian has *var* and *xar*.

## Exercises

1. Say "I am a teacher" with and without the pronoun.
2. Say "You are a driver" to a friend, and then politely to a stranger.
3. Say "They are here" and "We are there".
4. Take apart *čven kartvelebi vart* piece by piece.
5. Correct *\*Šen ekimia* and *\*Isini ak arit*.
6. Translate *Is mc̣eralia* in two ways, and say why there are two.
7. Which piece of *vart* says "first person", and which says "plural"? Which piece of *aris* says "third person singular"?
8. Say "Mr Giorgi is a painter, Ms Nino is a writer".

## Worked answers

1. The speaker is first person singular, so *var*. "Teacher" is the predicate, nominative *masc̣avlebeli*. With the pronoun: *me masc̣avlebeli var*. Without: *masc̣avlebeli var*. Both are correct; the second is the usual one.
2. To a friend: *šen*, so *xar*: *šen mʒɣoli xar*. To a stranger: *tkven*, so the plural verb *xart*, with the noun singular because there is one driver: *tkven mʒɣoli xart*.
3. "They" is third person plural: *isini ak arian*. "We" is first person plural: *čven ik vart*.
4. *čven* "we" (1PL). *kartvel-eb-i*: stem "Georgian", plural *-eb-*, nominative *-i*. *vart*: "be", first person plural. "We are Georgians."
5. *-a* is only for the third person singular, and "you" is second person: *šen ekimi xar*. The third person plural ends in *-an*, not *-t*: *isini ak arian*.
6. "He is a writer" and "she is a writer". *Is* has no gender, and neither does the verb, so English has to pick one from the context. It could also be "that is a writer".
7. In *vart*, *v-* marks the first person and *-t* the plural. In *aris*, the final *-s* marks the third person singular, the ending you will meet on most verbs.
8. Each name is third person singular, so *-a* or *aris*. *Baṭoni giorgi mxaṭvaria, kalbaṭoni nino mc̣eralia*.
