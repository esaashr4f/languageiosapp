## The idea

Lesson 2-2 cut "be" into pieces: *v-ar* "I am", *ar-i-s* "he is", *v-ar-t* "we are". Most of those pieces were the beginning of something bigger. Almost every Georgian verb marks its subject with the same kit: a prefix *v-* for "I" and "we", a suffix *-s* for "he, she, it", a suffix *-t* for the plural of "we" and "you", and a suffix for "they". These are the **subject markers**: pieces of the verb that agree with its subject.

Here is "write" in the present, from Wiktionary's table:

- ვწერ *vc̣er* "I write", წერ *c̣er* "you write", წერს *c̣ers* "he, she writes"
- ვწერთ *vc̣ert* "we write", წერთ *c̣ert* "you write" (plural or polite), წერენ *c̣eren* "they write"

Lay it beside *var*, *xar*, *aris*, *vart*, *xart*, *arian* and the system shows. The first person has *v-* in front. The plural of the first and second persons adds *-t*. The third person has suffixes: *-s* for one, *-en* for several. Bolkvadze and Kiziria set out exactly this: first and second persons are marked with prefixes, the third with suffixes, and only the first and second have a separate plural marker, *-t*.

The second person has no marker at all. Bolkvadze and Kiziria say the modern language has none, and that the old one, *x-*, survives only in *xar* "you are" and in two forms of "come". So *c̣er* "you write" is the bare stem. You know who is meant because nothing is added: no *v-*, so not "I"; no *-s*, so not "he".

Because the verb carries the person, the pronoun is often left out: *vc̣er* alone is "I write". Lesson 2-9 deals with when to keep it.

The **present** is the tense of what happens now or what happens regularly. Bolkvadze and Kiziria give it both readings: *vc̣er* is "I write" (in general, often) and "I am writing" (right now). Georgian has no separate progressive like English "am writing" or Spanish *estoy escribiendo*. Context decides.

In the present, the subject of every verb is in the nominative, the same case you have used since lesson 2-1. Bolkvadze and Kiziria list this as the first job of the nominative: the subject of all verbs in the present and future group of tenses. Later tenses change this (part 4); in part 2 you use only the present, so the subject is always nominative.

### Pieces inside the stem

Some verbs have more inside them than a bare root. *Xaṭavs* "draws" is *xaṭ-av-s*: root *xaṭ-* "draw", then *-av-*, then *-s*. *Ašenebs* "builds" is *a-šen-eb-s*: a vowel *a-*, root *šen-*, then *-eb-*, then *-s*. The piece after the root (*-av-*, *-eb-*, *-ob-*, *-ulob-*) is the **thematic suffix**: it belongs to the present and future, and drops away in some other tenses, such as the simple past (*daxaṭa* "drew", *aašena* "built"). The vowel before the root is the **version vowel**. Both have their own lessons (4-2 and 4-21). Until then the glosses label them TS and NV, and you can treat everything between *v-* and the person suffix as the stem. The subject markers attach to it in exactly the same way.

## How it is built

1. Pick the verb and find its stem. The vocabulary gives the "he, she" form, as Wiktionary does: *c̣ers*. Remove *-s*: the stem is *c̣er-*. For *xaṭavs* it is *xaṭav-*; for *ašenebs*, *ašeneb-*.
2. Decide the subject. That fixes the markers.
3. First person: put *v-* in front. Second: add nothing in front. Third: add nothing in front.
4. Singular: first and second take no suffix; third takes *-s*.
5. Plural: first and second take *-t*; third takes *-en*.

"We draw": first person plural, so *v-* + *xaṭav* + *-t* = *vxaṭavt*. "They build": third person plural, so *ašeneb* + *-en* = *ašeneben*. "You read" to one friend: *ḳitxulob*, the bare stem.

The *v-* is pronounced as written before a vowel (*vašeneb*). Before a voiceless consonant it sounds like [f], as lesson 1-3 explained for *bavšvi*: *vc̣er* [ftsʼer], *vxaṭav* [fxaṭav]. It is always written *v*.

The verb usually comes last, after the subject and any other words: *nino nela c̣ers* "Nino writes slowly". Lesson 2-9 shows how freely the order can change.

## Where it applies

- **Every person.** The six forms cover "I", "you", "he, she, it", "we", "you all" or polite "you", and "they".
- **Every verb you meet in the present.** *C̣ers*, *xaṭavs*, *ašenebs*, *ḳitxulobs*, *xedavs*, *č̣ams*, *svams*, *mušaobs* and the rest all take *v-*, *-s*, *-t* and *-en* in the present. (A few verbs met later, such as those ending in *-is*, have *-an* for "they" instead of *-en*: Bolkvadze and Kiziria list *-en* and *-an* among the third person plural endings.) Some usually have an object (*write*, *build*, *see*); some usually do not (*work*, *play*). In the present they conjugate alike. Part 4 shows where they part ways.
- **Right now.** With *axla* "now", or from the situation: *axla vč̣am*.
- **Habits and repeated actions.** With *xširad* "often", *q̇oveltvis* "always", *zogǯer* "sometimes": *xširad vxaṭav*.
- **General truths.** What someone or something does by nature: Bolkvadze and Kiziria's example is a fish swimming.
- **With a noun subject.** Any noun is third person: *giorgi ḳitxulobs*, *bavšvebi tamašoben*.
- **With people in the plural, -en; after numbers and quantifiers, -s.** As with "be" in lesson 2-4: *bavšvebi tamašoben* but *xuti sṭudenṭi sc̣avlobs*.
- **Polite tkven.** The verb takes *-t*: *tkven kartulad laṗaraḳobt*.
- **Not yet covered.** An object ("I write a letter"): next lesson. Objects "me" and "you" on the verb: lesson 4-3. Past and future: part 4.

## Watch out

**One subject, one set of markers.** *\*Vc̣ers* puts the first-person *v-* and the third-person *-s* on one verb. "I write" is *vc̣er*.

**No -s on "you".** *\*Šen c̣ers* gives "you" a third-person ending. The second person is bare: *šen c̣er*.

**-t is for "we" and "you", never "they".** *\*Isini c̣ert* borrows the plural of the other persons. The third person plural is *c̣eren*. And the reverse: *\*čven c̣eren* is wrong for "we write"; say *vc̣ert*.

**No "be" for "am writing".** English "I am writing" and Spanish *estoy escribiendo* tempt you to add the copula: *\*var vc̣er* or *\*vc̣er var*. The plain present already means "I am writing".

**The bare stem is not a command.** *C̣er* is "you write", a statement. In Spanish *escribe* is both a statement and a command; Georgian commands are built differently (lesson 4-17).

**Keep the v- in writing.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that speakers often drop *v-* before *u*, but that written Georgian requires it. Write it every time, even where you hear [f] or nothing.

**Do not put the thematic suffix after the person suffix.** *\*Xaṭsav* or *\*ašensebs* put *-s* in the middle. The person suffix is always last: *xaṭav-s*, *ašeneb-s*.

**After a number, no -en.** *\*Xuti sṭudenṭi sc̣avloben* makes the verb plural for five people. The noun is singular, so the verb is too: *sc̣avlobs*.

## Connections

Spanish works the same way: *escribo*, *escribes*, *escribe* carry the subject, so *yo* and *tú* are optional. The difference is where the marker sits: Spanish puts every person at the end, Georgian puts "I" and "we" in front. Korean and Japanese verbs carry no person at all, so the Georgian verb holds more than theirs do even in this simplest form.

## Exercises

1. Conjugate *xedavs* "sees" in all six persons of the present.
2. Say "We are reading now" and "They often read".
3. Take apart *vḳitxulobt* and *laṗaraḳoben*, piece by piece.
4. Correct *\*Šen vc̣ers* and *\*Isini mušaobt*.
5. Say "Nino speaks Georgian well" and "I speak Georgian badly".
6. Say "Ten children are playing together". Which ending does the verb take, and why?
7. Translate *Giorgi q̇oveltvis nela č̣ams* and say whether it describes now or a habit.
8. Say "You are building" to a friend, then to an older stranger.

## Worked answers

1. Stem *xedav-*. *vxedav*, *xedav*, *xedavs*, *vxedavt*, *xedavt*, *xedaven*: *v-* for the first person, nothing for the second, *-s* and *-en* for the third, *-t* for the first and second plural.
2. "We": first person plural, *v-* and *-t*: *axla vḳitxulobt*. "They": *-en*: *isini xširad ḳitxuloben*. The adverb goes before the verb.
3. *v-ḳitx-ulob-t*: first person *v-*, root "read", thematic suffix *-ulob*, plural *-t*: "we read". *laṗaraḳ-ob-en*: root "speak", thematic suffix *-ob*, third person plural *-en*: "they speak".
4. *Šen* is second person, which takes no marker: *šen c̣er*. *-t* is only for "we" and "you"; "they" takes *-en*: *isini mušaoben*.
5. *Nino kartulad ḳargad laṗaraḳobs*: third person singular, *-s*. *Kartulad cudad vlaṗaraḳob*: first person, *v-*. The pronoun *me* can be added but is not needed.
6. *Ati bavšvi ertad tamašobs*. After a number the noun is singular, and the verb agrees with a singular noun, so *-s*, not *-en*.
7. "Giorgi always eats slowly." *Q̇oveltvis* "always" makes it a habit, not something happening now.
8. To a friend, *šen*, the bare stem: *šen ašeneb*. To a stranger, polite *tkven* with *-t*: *tkven ašenebt*.
