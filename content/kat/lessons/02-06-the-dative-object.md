## The idea

Lesson 2-5 gave you verbs with a subject only: "I write", "the children play". Most verbs also have something they act on: you write a letter, see a dog, open a window. The thing acted on is the **direct object**. In English you find it by position: "the child sees the dog" and "the dog sees the child" differ only in word order. Georgian marks it with a case instead.

In the present, the direct object is in the **dative**, a case whose ending is -ს *-s*: *ʒaɣli* "dog", *ʒaɣls* "dog" as an object. Bolkvadze and Kiziria list this among the dative's jobs: the direct object of a transitive verb in the present and future group of tenses. So *bavšvi ʒaɣls xedavs* is "the child sees the dog", and *ʒaɣli bavšvs xedavs* is "the dog sees the child". The endings say who does what.

The dative is one of the seven cases. The English name comes from Latin, "the giving case", and the Georgian name მიცემითი *micemiti* means the same, because in many languages it marks the person something is given to. Georgian uses it for that too (lesson 4-20), and for more: the object in the present, the person who feels or has something (lesson 4-18), and the time when something happens. *Dɣes* "today", which you learnt as an adverb, is the dative of *dɣe* "day": "on the day".

Why the dative, and not a special accusative as in Latin? The sources consulted do not give a historical reason, and this guide will not invent one. What they show is the system: the case of the object depends on the tense group. In the present group the object is dative; in the past (the aorist, lesson 4-12) the same object goes into the nominative and the subject changes instead. Bolkvadze and Kiziria's case list states both. So "dative" here is not "the object case"; it is the case the object takes in the present.

## How it is built

**The dative ending.** Add *-s* to the stem. It is the same for consonant and vowel stems, and none of them truncates:

- consonant stems: *saxl-i* → *saxl-s*, *c̣eril-i* → *c̣eril-s*
- vowel stems: *deda* → *deda-s*, *xe* → *xe-s*, *ezo* → *ezo-s*, *nino* → *nino-s*
- plurals: *-eb-* then *-s*: *saxl-eb-s*, *ded-eb-s*

Nouns that syncopate keep their full stem in the dative: *megobari* → *megobars* (Wiktionary). The shortening of lesson 2-4 happens in the plural (and in cases of part 3), not in the singular dative.

**Pronouns.** *Is* "he, she, it, that" has the dative მას *mas*; *isini* "they" has მათ *mat*; *es* "this" has ამას *amas*; *eseni* "these" has ამათ *amat* (Wiktionary). *Me*, *šen*, *čven* and *tkven* keep the same form, but "sees me" and "sees you" also need a marker on the verb, taught in lesson 4-3. Until then, the objects in this guide are third person.

**The verb.** A third-person object is not marked on the verb at all. The verb agrees with its subject, as in lesson 2-5, and nothing on it shows the object.

**The sentence.** The usual order is subject, object, verb:

1. I want to say "Mother is opening the window".
2. The verb is *xsnis* "opens", present. Its subject is *deda*, third person singular, so *xsnis*.
3. The subject is in the nominative: *deda*.
4. The object is *panǯara*; in the present it goes into the dative: *panǯaras*.
5. Subject, object, verb: *deda panǯaras xsnis*.

**A number or an adjective before the object.** A number or adjective that ends in a consonant drops its *-i* before a dative noun: *ori* → *or c̣igns* "two books", *didi* → *did saxls* "a big house". One that ends in a vowel does not change: *ṗaṭara saxls*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria give this pattern for numerals and adjectives; lesson 3-10 covers adjectives in full. The noun after a number is still singular.

## Where it applies

- **The direct object of a verb in the present.** Things, people, animals, languages: *c̣erils vc̣er*, *ninos xedavs*, *kartuls vsc̣avlobt*.
- **Every noun type.** Consonant stems, vowel stems and plurals all add *-s*: *saxls*, *dedas*, *saxlebs*.
- **Third-person pronouns.** *mas*, *mat*, *amas*, *amat*: *mas vxedav* "I see him, her, it".
- **With a number or adjective before it.** The number or adjective loses its *-i* if it ends in a consonant: *or c̣igns*, *did saxls*. Lesson 3-10.
- **Time.** A dative can say when: *dɣes* "today", *dilas* "in the morning", *saɣamos* "in the evening". Wiktionary lists these as adverbs and as datives of *dɣe*, *dila* and *saɣamo*. Part 5 has days and months.
- **In the future too.** The future belongs to the same group as the present (lesson 4-6), so its object is also dative.
- **Other jobs, later.** The person to whom (lesson 4-20); the person who loves, has or wants (lesson 4-18); the doer in the perfect (lesson 4-22); the base of the postpositions "in", "on", "at" (lesson 3-2).

## Watch out

**Do not leave the object in the nominative.** *\*C̣erili vc̣er* treats the object like a name. In the present it must be dative: *c̣erils vc̣er*. The nominative object belongs to the aorist (lesson 4-12), where the subject changes instead. Do not carry either pattern into the other tense group.

**Do not trust word order to show the object.** In English, "the dog sees the child" reverses "the child sees the dog". In Georgian, *ʒaɣls bavšvi xedavs* still means "the child sees the dog": the endings decide, not the order. Read the endings first.

**Do not add a preposition for a person.** Spanish puts *a* before a human object: *veo a Nino*. Georgian uses the plain dative for people and things alike: *ninos vxedav*.

**Do not use *is* as an object.** *\*Is vxedav* uses the nominative. Say *mas vxedav*.

**Do not try "sees me" yet.** *\*Is me xedavs* looks like a direct copy of *mas vxedav*, but a first- or second-person object needs its own marker on the verb. The correct sentence is *is me mxedavs*; lesson 4-3 explains *m-*.

**Do not decline the number or adjective in full.** *\*Ori c̣igns* and *\*didi saxls* keep the nominative *-i*. Before a dative noun they drop it: *or c̣igns*, *did saxls*.

**Do not mix the two -s endings.** *Ʒaɣls xedavs* has two: the dative on the noun, the person on the verb. They look the same and do different jobs. A verb never takes the dative, and a noun never takes the person ending.

**Do not syncopate in the singular dative.** *\*Megobrs* shortens the stem by analogy with *megobrebi*. The singular dative is *megobars*.

## Connections

Korean and Japanese also mark the object with a case piece (을/를, を) and put the verb last, so *c̣erils vc̣er* has the same shape as 手紙を書く. In Spain, many speakers use the dative *le* for a male person as a direct object (*le veo*), so Spanish too can let one case serve both as "to him" and "him"; Georgian does this for every object in the present.

## Exercises

1. Put *kalaki*, *ʒma*, *xe* and *mdinare* into the dative.
2. Say "The boy is reading a book" and "The boys are reading books".
3. Say "I see them" and "They see him".
4. Take apart *deda č̣ikebs recxavs* and translate it.
5. Correct *\*Bavšvi ʒaɣli xedavs* and *\*Me is vxedav*.
6. Say "We are building two houses" and "Father is closing the big door".
7. Who sees whom in *ninos giorgi xedavs*? Explain how you know.
8. Say "In the evening I drink juice."

## Worked answers

1. Every stem adds *-s*, with no truncation: *kalaks*, *ʒmas*, *xes*, *mdinares*.
2. Subject nominative, object dative, verb last. *Bič̣i c̣igns ḳitxulobs*. With plurals, *-eb-* comes before both endings and the verb takes *-en*: *bič̣ebi c̣ignebs ḳitxuloben*.
3. "Them" as an object is *mat*; "I" is *v-* on the verb: *mat vxedav*. "Him" is *mas*; "they" is *isini* with *-en*: *isini mas xedaven*.
4. *deda* "mother", no ending (a vowel stem in the nominative). *č̣ik-eb-s*: *č̣ika* "glass" truncated, plural *-eb-*, dative *-s*. *recx-av-s*: "wash", thematic suffix, third person singular. "Mother is washing the glasses."
5. In the present the object is dative: *bavšvi ʒaɣls xedavs*. "Him" as an object is *mas*: *me mas vxedav*.
6. *Or saxls vašenebt*: *ori* drops its *-i*, *saxli* stays singular, and *v-* ... *-t* says "we". *Mama did ḳars xuravs*: *didi* drops its *-i* before the dative *ḳars*.
7. Giorgi sees Nino. *Giorgi* has no dative ending, so it is the subject; *ninos* has *-s*, so it is the object. The order, object first, only puts weight on Nino (lesson 2-9).
8. *Saɣamos c̣vens vsvam.* *Saɣamos* is a dative of time; *c̣vens* is the dative object; *v-* marks "I".
