## Before you start

This review covers part 2: the nominative and the copula *aris* / *-a* (lesson 2-1), the six persons of "be" and the pronouns (2-2), the absence of gender and articles and the use of *erti* (2-3), the plural *-eb-* and the singular after numbers and quantifiers (2-4), the present of verbs with *v-*, *-s*, *-t* and *-en* (2-5), the dative object (2-6), *ar*, *ver*, *aɣar* and *arc* (2-7), yes-no questions and answers (2-8), and word order with dropped pronouns (2-9). It also needs the script and transliteration of part 1.

Everything stays in the present, with nominative subjects and dative objects. Work each exercise before reading its answer. Every answer names the lesson to reread if you went wrong.

## Passage

**A. Nino's family.** A constructed passage, using only words and grammar from parts 1 and 2. Mkhedruli first, then the transliteration.

1. ეს ნინოა. ნინო ქართველია, მასწავლებელი.
   *es ninoa. nino kartvelia, masc̣avlebeli.*
2. ნინო, ქმარი და ორი შვილი ერთად ცხოვრობენ.
   *nino, kmari da ori švili ertad cxovroben.*
3. ქმარი ექიმია. ის ხშირად მუშაობს საღამოს.
   *kmari ekimia. is xširad mušaobs saɣamos.*
4. შვილები მოსწავლეები არიან. ერთი ხატავს, მეორე ლექსებს წერს.
   *švilebi mosc̣avleebi arian. erti xaṭavs, meore leksebs c̣ers.*
5. დილას ბავშვები რძეს სვამენ, ნინო ყავას სვამს.
   *dilas bavšvebi rʒes svamen, nino q̇avas svams.*
6. დღეს წვიმს და ცივა. ბავშვები არ თამაშობენ, წიგნებს კითხულობენ.
   *dɣes c̣vims da civa. bavšvebi ar tamašoben, c̣ignebs ḳitxuloben.*
7. — ნინო, ჩაის სვამ? — არა, მადლობა. ყავას ვსვამ.
   *— nino, čais svam? — ara, madloba. q̇avas vsvam.*
8. ნინო დაღლილია, მაგრამ ბედნიერი.
   *nino daɣlilia, magram bednieri.*

**B. A proverb.** Attested: Wiktionary, entry ძაღლი ყეფს ქარავანი მიდის, which translates it "the dogs bark, but the caravan goes on".

ძაღლი ყეფს ქარავანი მიდის.
*ʒaɣli q̇eps karavani midis.*

New words, glossed here only: *q̇eps* "barks", the third person singular present of ყეფს, a regular verb like *c̣ers*; *karavani* "caravan" (nominative); *midis* "goes", an irregular motion verb taught in lesson 4-11. You need only know that *midis* is third person singular present.

## Worked answers

1. "This is Nino. Nino is Georgian, a teacher." *Ninoa*: the name plus the short copula (lesson 2-1). Two predicates share one copula: *kartvelia, masc̣avlebeli*. "Nino, her husband and two children live together." English adds "her"; Georgian leaves it to the context (lesson 2-3).
2. "The husband is a doctor. He often works in the evening." *Saɣamos* is a dative of time (lesson 2-6); *mušaobs* has *-s* for "he" (lesson 2-5). "The children are pupils. One draws, the other writes poems."
3. "In the morning the children drink milk; Nino drinks coffee." "Today it is raining and it is cold. The children are not playing; they are reading books." The second clause has no pronoun: *-en* and the context give "they" (lesson 2-9).
4. "'Nino, are you drinking tea?' 'No, thanks. I'm drinking coffee.'" The question is the statement *čais svam* said with a rise on the verb (lesson 2-8). "Nino is tired, but happy." *Magram* "but" (lesson 2-7); the second predicate shares the copula.
5. Word for word: "dog barks caravan goes". Naturally: "The dogs bark, but the caravan goes on." *Ʒaɣli* and *karavani* are nominative subjects of present verbs (lessons 2-1, 2-5); each verb has the third-person *-s*.
6. The subject is three people joined by *da*: Nino, the husband and the two children. Several subjects joined by "and" take a plural verb when they are people (lesson 2-3), and plural people take *-en* (lesson 2-5). The singular *švili* after *ori* is a rule about the noun phrase only (lesson 2-4).
7. *Švilebi* stands alone, so it takes the plural *-eb-*, and as people it takes the plural *arian*; the predicate *mosc̣avleebi* is plural too, keeping its *-e* (lesson 2-4). After the number *ori*, the noun stays singular (lesson 2-4).
8. *Erti ... meore* is "one ... the other" (lesson 2-3). *Leksebs* is *leks-eb-s*: plural, then the dative, because it is the object of *c̣ers* in the present (lessons 2-4, 2-6).
9. Both are objects of *svamen* and *svams* in the present, so dative (lesson 2-6); both are vowel stems and add *-s* directly. *Dilas* is the dative of *dila* used for time, like *dɣes* (lesson 2-6).
10. Weather verbs have no subject, so Georgian has nothing to translate "it" (lesson 2-9). *Ar* negates the verb and must stand directly before it, here *tamašoben* (lesson 2-7).
11. In the question, *svam* has no *v-* and no *-s*, so it is "you" (lesson 2-5), and the name *nino* says which you. In the answer, *v-* in *vsvam* says "I" (lesson 2-5). The objects *čais* and *q̇avas* are dative (lesson 2-6). *Ara* is the one-word "no" (lesson 2-8).
12. Georgian has no articles, so *ʒaɣli* can be "a dog", "the dog" or dogs in general (lesson 2-3). The proverb means any dog, so English uses a plural; the Georgian says it with the singular, and the verb *q̇eps* agrees with that singular (lessons 2-4, 2-5).
13. *Nino* ends in a vowel, so it has no nominative ending and the copula adds one *-a* (lesson 2-1). *Kartveli* is a consonant stem with the nominative *-i*; the *-a* follows the *-i*, which stays (lesson 2-1).
14. *Giorgi ekimi ar aris, mxaṭvaria.* The negated copula needs the full *aris* (lesson 2-7); the second clause drops the subject and uses the short copula (lessons 2-1, 2-9).
15. *Kartuls vsc̣avlobt, magram ǯer ver vc̣ert.* *V-* ... *-t* for "we" (lesson 2-5); *kartuls* is the dative object (lesson 2-6); *ver* for inability and *ǯer* for "yet" (lesson 2-7).
16. *Sami sṭudenṭi gazetebs ḳitxulobs.* After a number the noun is singular, and so is the verb (lesson 2-4); the object is the dative plural *gazet-eb-s* (lessons 2-4, 2-6).
17. Polite *tkven*: *ɣvinos svamt?* (lesson 2-2, 2-5). Answer: *ara, gmadlobt, ɣvinos aɣar vsvam.* *Aɣar* says you used to (lesson 2-7); the answer is singular, because you speak for yourself (lesson 2-8). *Ɣvino* is a vowel stem: dative *ɣvinos*.
18. *Eseni suratebia. Ʒalian lamazia.* Pictures are things, so the copula stays singular in both sentences (lesson 2-4); the second drops the subject (lesson 2-9).
19. *Bavšvebi mɣerian, xom?* or *xom mɣerian bavšvebi?* *Xom* asks for agreement (lesson 2-8); *mɣerian* is the "they" form of *mɣeris*, with *-an* (lesson 2-7).
20. After a number, the noun is singular and so is the verb: *ak ori bavšvia* or *ori bavšvi ak aris* (lesson 2-4).
21. The object must be dative in the present, and "I" needs *v-*: *me c̣igns vḳitxulob*, or without the pronoun, *c̣igns vḳitxulob* (lessons 2-5, 2-6, 2-9).
22. The short copula cannot be negated. Use the full form: *es saxli ar aris* or *es ar aris saxli* (lesson 2-7).
23. *-t* belongs to "we" and "you"; "they" takes *-en*: *isini c̣erilebs c̣eren* (lesson 2-5). The order SOV is the neutral one, though *c̣eren c̣erilebs* is also possible (lesson 2-9).
24. Adjectives have no feminine: *lamazi*, not *lamaza* (lesson 2-3). With two predicates the copula goes on the last one, and the first keeps its nominative *-i*: *deida lamazi da bednieria* (lesson 2-1).
