## The idea

English puts "in", "on" and "at" before the noun. Georgian puts them after it, and joins them to it: *saxl-ši* "in the house", *magida-ze* "on the table". A word of this kind is a **postposition** (lesson 3-1). You already know the idea from Japanese and Korean, where 家に and 집에 put the "in" after "house".

The Georgian postpositions are not added to the bare stem. Each one is added to a particular case form. Four of the commonest are built on the dative: -ში *-ši* "in", -ზე *-ze* "on", -თან *-tan* "at, near, with" and -ვით *-vit* "like". So the dative, which you know as the case of the object, has a second life as the base of the place words.

Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe what happens at the join. The postposition does not change the stem of the noun, but it may change the case ending: keep it, shorten it, or drop it altogether. That is why the dative *-s* is visible in some of these forms and not in others. Once you know the four rules below, every form is predictable.

Why does the dative lose its *-s*? The sources consulted describe the loss but do not give its history, and this guide will not guess. What matters is that the dative is still there underneath: it is the reason the stem keeps its full shape. *Megobari* "friend" loses a vowel in its genitive *megobris* (lesson 3-6), but not in its dative *megobars*, and so not in *megobarši*, *megobarze*, *megobartan*.

## How it is built

Start from the dative: *saxl-s*, *deda-s*, *ezo-s*, *megobar-s*. Then:

**-ში "in".** Drop the *-s* and add *-ši*: *saxlši*, *dedaši*, *ezoši*, *tbilisši*. The same for every stem type. Bolkvadze and Kiziria add one detail: a stem that ends in *-š* doubles it, as in *ḳomš-ši* "in a quince".

**-ზე "on".** Drop the *-s* and add *-ze*: *saxlze*, *magidaze*, *ezoze*. A stem ending in *-z* doubles it.

**-თან "at, near, with".** Here the stem type matters. A consonant stem drops the *-s*: *megobartan*, *saxltan*. A vowel stem keeps it: *dedastan*, *magidastan*, *ezostan*. A stem in *-t* doubles the *t*: *bilettan* "by the ticket".

**-ვით "like".** This one is built differently. A consonant stem adds it to the nominative, *-i* included: *čiṭivit* "like a bird", *megobarivit*. A vowel stem adds it to the long dative *-sa*: *dedasavit*, *zɣvasavit*. Wiktionary lists *-vit* among the dative postpositions; Bolkvadze and Kiziria place the consonant-stem form on the nominative and note that a dative form for consonant stems, such as *mṭersavit* "like an enemy", is going out of use.

**The plural.** The plural stem ends in a consonant, *-eb-*, so it follows the consonant rules: *saxlebši*, *dedebze*, *megobrebtan*, *dedebivit*. Remember that *megobari* syncopates in the plural, *megobrebi* (lesson 2-4).

**In a sentence.** The noun with its postposition sits where any adverb of place sits, usually before the verb:

1. I want to say "The children are playing in the yard".
2. The verb is *tamašoben*, "they play".
3. "In the yard": *ezo* is a vowel stem; dative *ezos*; drop *-s*, add *-ši*: *ezoši*.
4. *Bavšvebi ezoši tamašoben.*

With "be", the short copula attaches to the end: *c̣igni magidazea* "the book is on the table", *nino saxlšia* "Nino is at home".

**Two nouns joined by *da*.** When two nouns share one postposition, only the last one carries it, and the first stands in the long dative *-sa*: *mtasa da barši* "in the mountains and the lowlands". Bolkvadze and Kiziria give this as the standard form.

**Glossing.** In this lesson the lost *-s* is shown with a full stop: *saxl-ši* `house.DAT-in`. From lesson 3-3 on, the gloss is plain `house-in`.

## Where it applies

**-ში *-ši***

- **Inside something:** *ǯibeši* "in the pocket", *kalakši*, *saxlši*, *oǯaxši* "in the family".
- **In a city, country or region:** *tbilisši*, *sakartveloši*, *sopelši* "in the village; in the countryside".
- **Into, with verbs of putting:** *čantaši debs* "puts it in the bag". With verbs of going (lesson 4-11) it is "to": *sḳolaši* "to school".
- **At an institution:** *sḳolaši*, *universiṭeṭši*, *samsaxurši* "at work", *biblioteḳaši*. English says "at school"; Georgian thinks of it as "in".
- **Later uses:** time, "in two hours" (lesson 5-5).

**-ზე *-ze***

- **On a surface:** *magidaze*, *ḳedelze* "on the wall", *iaṭaḳze* "on the floor", *sac̣olze* "on the bed".
- **About, with verbs of speaking, thinking, reading and writing:** *pilmze laṗaraḳoben*, *ʒmaze pikrobs* "she thinks about her brother", *ṗoliṭiḳaze c̣ers* "she writes about politics", *pexburtze laṗaraḳoben* "they talk about football".
- **At an event:** *ḳoncerṭze* "at the concert". Bolkvadze and Kiziria translate *ḳoncerṭze* as both "at the concert" and "about the concert"; the verb decides.
- **Later uses:** clock time, "at two o'clock" (lesson 5-5); "than" in comparisons (lesson 5-2).

**-თან *-tan***

- **Near, next to:** *sadgurtan* "by the station", *panǯarastan* "at the window"; with *axlos*: *zɣvastan axlos* "near the sea".
- **At someone's place:** *megobartan*, *dedastan*. It is the Georgian answer to French *chez*.
- **With a person you talk or play with:** *dedastan laṗaraḳobs*, *ʒaɣltan tamašobs*.
- **Together with:** *-tan ertad*: *megobartan ertad cxovrobs* "she lives with a friend".

**-ვით *-vit***

- **Like, as:** *čiṭivit mɣeris*, *dedasavit lamazia*. It compares a person or thing with another: "as beautiful as her mother" is literally "mother-like beautiful".

## Watch out

**Do not keep the -s before -ši or -ze.** *\*Saxlsši* and *\*magidasze* add the postposition to the full dative. The *-s* drops: *saxlši*, *magidaze*.

**Do not drop the -s of a vowel stem before -tan.** *\*Dedatan* follows the consonant-stem rule. A vowel stem keeps it: *dedastan*. And the opposite mistake: *\*megobarstan* keeps an *-s* that consonant stems lose. Say *megobartan*.

**Do not build -vit on the dative of a consonant stem.** *\*Čiṭsavit* treats *čiṭi* like a vowel stem. Say *čiṭivit*. Vowel stems need the *-sa*: *\*dedavit* is wrong; say *dedasavit*.

**Do not put the postposition first.** *\*Ši sasṭumro* copies English. Postpositions follow: *sasṭumroši*.

**Do not syncopate before a dative postposition.** *\*Megobrtan* and *\*sopltan* borrow the short stem of the plural or genitive. The dative keeps the full stem: *megobartan*, *sopelši*, *ḳedelze*.

**Do not use -ze for "at school".** English "at" suggests *-ze* or *-tan*, but an institution you are inside takes *-ši*: *sḳolaši*, *universiṭeṭši*. *Sḳolastan* means "next to the school", outside it.

**Do not use -ši for "about".** French *penser à* and Spanish *pensar en* lead to *\*ʒmaši pikrobs*. Georgian thinks and talks "on" a topic: *ʒmaze pikrobs*.

**Do not use -tan for an instrument.** "With" a pen is not *\*ḳalamtan c̣ers*. *-Tan* is "with" a person or animal beside you; the tool takes the instrumental (lesson 3-4).

**Do not repeat the postposition on every noun in a pair.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria call *mtaši da barši* substandard, though it is heard in speech. The standard form puts the first noun in the long dative: *mtasa da barši*.

**Do not forget the copula after a place.** "Nino is at home" is not *\*nino saxlši*: a Georgian sentence needs its verb. Say *nino saxlšia* or *nino saxlši aris*.

## Connections

Japanese に and Korean 에 cover both "in" and "to", as *-ši* does with "be" and with verbs of movement. French *chez* is the closest match for *-tan* with a person: *megobartan* is *chez un ami*. Spanish *como* corresponds to *-vit*, but it comes before the noun and is a separate word.

## Exercises

1. Make *-ši*, *-ze* and *-tan* forms of *q̇uti*, *ḳarada* and *taro*.
2. Say "The cat is on the bed" and "The cat is in the box".
3. Say "We live near the station."
4. Say "Giorgi is at Nino's" and "Giorgi lives with Nino."
5. Translate *mama samzareuloši q̇avas svams* and explain the form *samzareuloši*.
6. Correct *\*Bavšvebi ezosši tamašoben* and *\*Dedatan vlaṗaraḳob*.
7. Say "The children sing like birds."
8. Why is it *megobarze* but *megobristvis* (lesson 3-1)?
9. Say "There are a lot of books in the library and in the shop", using the standard form for two nouns.

## Worked answers

1. *Q̇uti* is a consonant stem: *q̇utši*, *q̇utze*, *q̇uttan*, the last with a double *t* because the stem ends in *-t*. *Ḳarada* is a vowel stem: *ḳaradaši*, *ḳaradaze*, *ḳaradastan*, with the *-s* kept before *-tan*. *Taro* also: *taroši*, *taroze*, *tarostan*.
2. *Ḳaṭa sac̣olzea* and *ḳaṭa q̇utšia*. Each postposition replaces the dative *-s*; the short copula follows. You can also write *sac̣olze aris*.
3. *Sadgurtan axlos vcxovrobt.* *Sadguri* is a consonant stem, so *-tan* replaces the *-s*; *v-* ... *-t* is "we".
4. *Giorgi ninostan aris*: *nino* is a vowel stem, so it keeps the *-s*. "Lives with" is "lives together with": *giorgi ninostan ertad cxovrobs*.
5. "Father is drinking coffee in the kitchen." *Samzareulo* is an *-o* stem; its dative *samzareulos* loses the *-s* before *-ši*.
6. Drop the dative *-s* before *-ši*: *bavšvebi ezoši tamašoben*. A vowel stem keeps it before *-tan*: *dedastan vlaṗaraḳob* "I am talking with Mother".
7. *Bavšvebi čiṭebivit mɣerian.* The plural stem ends in a consonant, so *-vit* follows *-i*; *mɣerian* is the "they" form.
8. *-Ze* is built on the dative, which keeps the full stem *megobar-*. *-Tvis* is built on the genitive, and the genitive of *megobari* syncopates: *megobris* (lesson 3-6).
9. *Biblioteḳasa da maɣaziaši bevri c̣ignia.* Only *maɣazia* carries *-ši*; *biblioteḳa* stands in the long dative *-sa*. The verb is singular because the books are things and follow *bevri*.
