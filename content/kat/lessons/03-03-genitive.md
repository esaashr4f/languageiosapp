## The idea

English has two ways to link one noun to another: "the teacher's book" and "the book of the teacher". Spanish and French have one, *de*, placed between them. Georgian has a case: the **genitive**, in -ის *-is*, put on the first noun. *Masc̣avleblis c̣igni* is "the teacher's book".

The order is always the same: the genitive first, the noun it describes second. This is the order of English "the teacher's book", of Japanese 先生の本 and Korean 선생님의 책, and it is the order Georgian uses for every describing word: numbers, adjectives and pointing words all come before their noun (lessons 2-4, 3-9, 3-10). The genitive is one more describing word. If you think "a teacher-ish book", you have the logic.

Bolkvadze and Kiziria list three main meanings. **Ownership** or belonging: *megobris saxli* "a friend's house", *c̣ignis avṭori* "the author of the book". **Material**: *kvis saxli* "a stone house", a house of stone. **Function**, what a thing is for: *čais č̣ika* "a tea glass", a glass for tea. English uses a bare noun for the last two ("stone house", "tea glass"); Georgian uses the genitive for all three.

Like the dative, the genitive is also a base for postpositions. Three of them are glued on: -თვის *-tvis* "for", -გან *-gan* "from, out of" and -კენ *-ḳen* "towards". Several others are separate words written after the genitive, such as *šesaxeb* "about" and *gareše* "without"; lesson 5-1 teaches them.

## How it is built

The genitive ending is *-is*. How it joins the stem depends on the stem type. The rules here are enough to form any genitive; lesson 3-5 goes over the vowel stems again with all their cases, and lesson 3-6 explains the vowel loss.

**Consonant stems** add *-is*: *saxl-is*, *ḳac-is*, *masc̣avlebl-is*, *tbilis-is*.

**Stems in -a** lose the *-a*, then add *-is*: *deda* → *ded-is*, *mankana* → *mankan-is*, *ʒma* → *ʒm-is*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria call this truncation (lesson 2-4) and note that it happens in the genitive and instrumental singular.

**Native stems in -e** do the same: *xe* → *x-is*, *meuɣle* → *meuɣl-is*.

**Stems in -o, -u, -i**, and borrowed stems in *-e*, keep their vowel and add only *-s*: *ezo-s*, *nino-s*, *sakartvelo-s*, *čai-s*, *ḳape-s*. This genitive looks exactly like the dative. Bolkvadze and Kiziria point out that context decides, and that a longer form, *ezosi*, can make the genitive clear.

**Syncopating stems** lose the vowel of their last syllable: *megobari* → *megobr-is*, *pinǯani* → *pinǯn-is*, *panǯara* → *panǯr-is*. The vocabulary notes give these; lesson 3-6 gives the rule.

**The plural** is always *-eb-is*: *saxlebis*, *dedebis*, *megobrebis*.

**The postpositions** follow the *-is* and keep it: *saxl-is-tvis*, *megobr-is-gan*, *zɣv-is-ḳen*. On an *-o* stem they follow the *-s*: *ezo-s-tvis*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria note an optional extending vowel *-a* (*saxlisatvis*), which you will meet in writing.

**In a sentence.** Build from the noun outwards.

1. I want to say "I study at Tbilisi University".
2. The main noun is *universiṭeṭi*, and "at" it is *universiṭeṭši* (lesson 3-2).
3. "Tbilisi" describes it, so it goes first, in the genitive: *tbilisis*.
4. The genitive does not change when the main noun takes a case or a postposition: *tbilisis universiṭeṭši*.
5. *Tbilisis universiṭeṭši vsc̣avlob.*

Chains are built the same way, each genitive describing the noun after it: *megobris ʒmis saxli* "the house of my friend's brother", literally "friend's brother's house". Read them from the right: house, whose? the brother's; which brother? the friend's.

## Where it applies

**The bare genitive**

- **Owner or person related:** *megobris saxli*, *ninos c̣igni*, *giorgis ʒma* "Giorgi's brother".
- **Part of a whole:** *saxlis saxuravi* "the roof of the house", *kalakis cenṭri* "the city centre", *ṭq̇is nac̣ili* "part of the forest".
- **Material:** *kvis saxli*, *xis magida* "a wooden table", *rḳinis ḳari* "an iron door", *okros saati* "a gold watch".
- **Function or contents type:** *čais č̣ika*, *q̇avis pinǯani* "a coffee cup", *ɣvinis botli* "a wine bottle" (on *ɣvinis*, see lesson 3-5).
- **Institutions and names:** *tbilisis universiṭeṭi*, *sakartvelos isṭoria* "the history of Georgia", *sakartvelos ruḳa* "a map of Georgia".
- **Author, maker, colour, and other qualities expressed by a noun:** *c̣ignis avṭori*, *zɣvis peri* "the colour of the sea".
- **Later uses:** with a masdar ("the writing of the letter", lesson 5-8), with times of day (lesson 5-5), and as the base of separate postpositions such as *šesaxeb* "about" (lesson 5-1).

**-თვის *-tvis* "for"**

- **For a person:** *bavšvebistvis*, *meuɣlistvis* "for the husband, for the wife".
- **For a purpose or use:** *oǯaxistvis*, *sḳolistvis* "for school".

**-გან *-gan* "from, out of"**

- **From a person:** *megobrisgan viɣeb*. For "from a place" Georgian uses *-dan* instead (lesson 3-4).
- **Material, "out of":** *xisgan*, *kvisgan*, *kaɣaldisgan* "out of paper".
- **Cause:** Bolkvadze and Kiziria list *xmaurisgan* "because of the noise" among its uses.

**-კენ *-ḳen* "towards"**

- **Direction**, with verbs of movement (lesson 4-11): *zɣvisḳen*, *kalakis cenṭrisḳen* "towards the city centre". It names the direction, not the goal: "towards the sea", not "to the sea".

## Watch out

**Do not put the genitive after its noun.** Spanish *la casa de mi amigo* puts the owner last, so *\*saxli megobris* looks natural. In modern Georgian the genitive comes first: *megobris saxli*. The order *saxli megobrisa*, with the long form, exists in older and poetic writing (Hewitt notes the long forms in this position); do not use it in ordinary speech.

**Do not make the genitive agree with its noun.** In *tbilisis universiṭeṭši* the genitive stays *tbilisis*, and in *kvis saxlebi* it stays singular. *\*Tbilisši universiṭeṭši* and *\*kvebis saxlebi* copy the case or number of the main noun. Only the main noun changes.

**Do not use the genitive for "a glass of water".** *C̣q̇lis č̣ika* means "a water glass", a glass meant for water, full or empty. For a quantity, Georgian puts the measure word before the substance, with the substance taking the case: *erti č̣ika c̣q̇ali* "a glass of water"; as an object, *erti č̣ika c̣q̇als vsvam*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria have the same pattern with "two glasses of tea".

**Do not keep the -a of a truncating stem.** *\*Dedais* and *\*mankanais* keep a vowel that falls. Say *dedis*, *mankanis*. But do not truncate an *-o* stem: *\*ezis* is wrong; say *ezos*.

**Own relatives may not truncate.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that when you mean your own mother, or the mother of the person you are talking to, *deda* behaves like a name and keeps its *-a*: *dedas otaxi* "Mum's room", but *dedis* for a mother in general or someone else's. The same holds for *mama*, *babua*, *bebia*, *biʒa*. Both are correct; they mean different things.

**Do not use -gan for "from a place".** *\*Tbilisisgan* sounds as if Tbilisi were a person giving you something. Places take *-dan*: *tbilisidan* (lesson 3-4).

**Do not use -ḳen for "to" a goal you reach.** *Zɣvisḳen* is "towards the sea", in that direction. Arriving somewhere uses *-ši* or *-tan* with a verb of movement (lesson 4-11).

**Do not drop the -is before -tvis.** *\*Bavšvtvis* attaches *-tvis* to the stem. It needs the genitive: *bavšvistvis*.

## Connections

Japanese の and Korean 의 also come between the two nouns with the describer first, and Japanese 石の家 "stone house" uses の for material exactly as *kvis saxli* does. French and Spanish *de* covers ownership and material (*la maison de pierre*, *la casa de piedra*), but puts the describer second. Arabic's construct state, *bayt al-muʿallim* "the teacher's house", also puts the owner second, in the genitive: the mirror image of Georgian.

## Exercises

1. Give the genitive of *kalaki*, *magida*, *ṭq̇e*, *meṭro* and *pankari*.
2. Say "the colour of the wine" and "a wine bottle". Why do both use the genitive?
3. Say "Giorgi's cousin lives in the city centre."
4. Translate *es sakartvelos ruḳaa* and explain *sakartvelos*.
5. Say "The gift is for the teacher" and "I get a gift from the teacher."
6. Correct *\*Saxli megobris didia* and *\*Bavšvtvis*.
7. Say "I am drinking a glass of milk" and "This is a milk glass."
8. Take apart *direkṭoris meuɣlistvis* and translate it.

## Worked answers

1. *Kalakis*: consonant stem, *-is*. *Magidis*: *-a* truncates. *Ṭq̇is*: a native *-e* stem truncates too. *Meṭros*: an *-o* stem keeps its vowel and adds *-s*. *Pankris*: *pankari* syncopates, as its vocabulary note said in lesson 2-4.
2. *Ɣvinis peri* and *ɣvinis botli*. The first is a quality of the wine, the second a bottle for wine (function). Georgian uses the genitive for both. *Ɣvino* is irregular: Bolkvadze and Kiziria give the genitive *ɣvinis*, with the *-o* lost, while Wiktionary's table gives both *ɣvinos* and *ɣvinis*. Lesson 3-5 returns to it.
3. *Giorgis biʒašvili kalakis cenṭrši cxovrobs.* *Giorgis* and *kalakis* are genitives before their nouns; *cenṭrši* is *cenṭri* with *-ši*. The verb agrees with *biʒašvili*.
4. "This is a map of Georgia." *Sakartvelo* is an *-o* stem, so its genitive is *sakartvelos*, identical to the dative. It stands before *ruḳa*, which shows it describes the map; the copula *-a* follows *ruḳa*.
5. *Sačukari masc̣avleblistvis aris*: *-tvis* on the genitive *masc̣avleblis*. *Sačukars masc̣avleblisgan viɣeb*: the gift is the object, dative; *-gan* for the person it comes from.
6. The genitive comes first: *megobris saxli didia*. *-Tvis* needs the genitive: *bavšvistvis*.
7. *Erti č̣ika rʒes vsvam*: measure word first, substance in the object case, no genitive. *Es rʒis č̣ikaa*: the genitive gives the function. *Rʒe* is a native *-e* stem, so *rʒ-is*.
8. *direkṭor-is* "director's", *meuɣl-is-tvis* "for the spouse": *meuɣle* truncates before *-is*, then *-tvis*. "For the director's husband" or "for the director's wife": Georgian does not say which.
