## The idea

Two cases remain that have meanings of their own. The **instrumental**, in -ით *-it*, answers "with what?" or "by what means?": *ḳalmit c̣ers* "writes with a pen", *avṭobusit* "by bus". The **adverbial**, in -ად *-ad*, answers "as what?" or "in what way?": *mzareulad mušaobs* "works as a cook", *lamazad xaṭavs* "draws beautifully".

You have already used the adverbial without knowing it. *Ḳargad*, *cudad*, *kartulad*, *ertad* and *xširad* are adjectives and numbers in the adverbial case: "in a good way", "in the Georgian way", "as one". Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe the adverbial as the case that turns nouns, adjectives, numbers and pronouns into adverbs, and as the case of change: "turned into a town", "appointed as director". Both uses share one idea: the noun names a role or a manner, not a participant.

Each of the two cases is also the base of one postposition, and here the join changes the sound of the ending. Bolkvadze and Kiziria explain both.

- -დან *-dan* "from" sits on the instrumental. It goes back to *-it* + *-gan*, and the two pieces merged: *c̣ign-it-gan* became *c̣ign-i-dan*. So in *saxlidan* "from the house", the *-i-* is what is left of the instrumental.
- -მდე *-mde* "up to, until" sits on the adverbial, which loses its *-d*: *saxl-ad-mde* became *saxl-a-mde*. So in *saxlamde* "as far as the house", the *-a-* is what is left of the adverbial.

That is why "from" and "up to" look as if they had a vowel before them: the vowel is the trace of a case.

## How it is built

**The instrumental.**

- Consonant stems add *-it*: *saxlit*, *ṭeleponit*, *pexit* "on foot".
- Stems in *-a* and native stems in *-e* truncate, as in the genitive: *dana* → *danit* "with a knife", *ɣame* → *ɣamit* "at night".
- Stems in *-o*, *-u*, *-i* keep their vowel and add *-t*, or *-ti* in a fuller form: *meṭrot*, *ṭaksit*; Wiktionary gives *ezot(i)*.
- Syncopating stems syncopate: *ḳalami* → *ḳalmit*, *pankari* → *pankrit*, *čangali* → *čanglit* "with a fork".

**The adverbial.**

- Consonant stems add *-ad*: *saxlad*, *mzareulad*, *lamazad*.
- Vowel stems add *-d* and do not truncate: *deda* → *dedad*, *xe* → *xed*, *ezo* → *ezod*.
- Syncopating stems syncopate: *tarǯimani* → *tarǯimnad* "as a translator", *sačukari* → *sačukrad* "as a gift".

**-დან "from".** Take the instrumental and replace its *-t* with *-dan*:

- *saxlit* → *saxlidan*, *tbilisit* → *tbilisidan*, *megobrit* → *megobridan*
- *sḳolit* → *sḳolidan*, *xit* → *xidan*
- *ezot* → *ezodan*, *saɣamot* → *saɣamodan*: on these stems no *-i-* is left.

**-მდე "up to".** Take the adverbial and drop its *-d*:

- *saxlad* → *saxlamde*, *sadgurad* → *sadguramde*, *megobrad* → *megobramde*
- *sḳolad* → *sḳolamde*, *xed* → *xemde*, *saɣamod* → *saɣamomde*.

An older form, *-mdis*, means the same; Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that *-mde* is the usual modern one.

**The plural** adds the same pieces after *-eb-*: *saxlebit*, *saxlebad*, *saxlebidan*, *saxlebamde*.

**In a sentence.** "I am in the library from morning till evening":

1. The verb is *var*, with a place: *biblioteḳaši var*.
2. "From the morning": *dila*, instrumental *dilit*, so *dilidan*.
3. "Till the evening": *saɣamo*, adverbial *saɣamod*, so *saɣamomde*.
4. Time phrases come first: *dilidan saɣamomde biblioteḳaši var*.

## Where it applies

**The instrumental *-it***

- **Tool or instrument:** *ḳalmit*, *pankrit*, *danit*, *čanglit*, *ḳovzit* "with a spoon", *xelit* "by hand".
- **Means of transport and communication:** *avṭobusit*, *maṭareblit*, *tvitmprinavit*, *ṭaksit*, *pexit* "on foot" (lesson 4-11 for the verbs), *ṭeleponit*, *posṭit* "by post".
- **Times of day as adverbs:** *dilit* "in the morning", *ɣamit* "at night", and with a genitive inside, *dɣisit* "by day". Bolkvadze and Kiziria list these as adverbs made from the instrumental. *Dilit* and *dilas* both mean "in the morning".
- **Manner, with some nouns:** *siamovnebit* "with pleasure", the usual reply to an invitation.
- **Later uses:** with a masdar for the way something is done (lesson 5-8); in *-urt* "together with", a formal suffix on the instrumental (*meuɣliturt*), which you will mostly read rather than say.

**The adverbial *-ad***

- **Manner, from adjectives:** *lamazad*, *ḳargad*, *cudad*, *sc̣rapad*. Most adverbs of manner are made this way (lesson 5-1).
- **A language:** *kartulad* "in Georgian", *inglisurad* "in English" (lesson 5-10 for *-uri*).
- **A role, "as":** *mzareulad*, *tarǯimnad*, *masc̣avleblad mušaobs* "works as a teacher".
- **"As a ...", with nouns of function:** *sačukrad*, and *sṭumrad* "as a guest, on a visit", which Bolkvadze and Kiziria use with "come".
- **Change, "into":** with verbs of becoming and turning (lessons 4-10 and 4-14).
- **Some fixed adverbs:** *ertad* "together", *ṗirvelad* "for the first time" (Wiktionary, under *-ad*).

**-დან *-dan***

- **From a place:** *tbilisidan*, *sḳolidan*, *ǯibidan* "out of the pocket".
- **Origin:** *tbilisidan var* "I am from Tbilisi".
- **Since a time:** *dilidan*, *zapxulidan* "since the summer", *bavšvobidan* "since childhood".

**-მდე *-mde***

- **As far as a place:** *sadguramde*, *zɣvamde* "as far as the sea".
- **Until a time:** *saɣamomde*, *šemodgomamde* "until the autumn".
- **About, up to, with numbers:** Bolkvadze and Kiziria give *atamde* "up to ten, about ten". Numbers are lesson 5-3.

## Watch out

**Do not use -tan for a tool.** English, Spanish and French all say "with" for both a companion and a tool. Georgian splits them. *\*Ḳalamtan c̣ers* means "writes beside the pen". The tool is instrumental: *ḳalmit c̣ers*. The companion is *-tan* (*ertad*): *megobartan ertad c̣ers*.

**Do not use -ši for transport.** *\*Avṭobusši midis* is "goes into the bus". "By bus" is *avṭobusit*.

**Do not use -gan for a place.** *\*Tbilisisgan var* treats Tbilisi as a person. "From" a place is *-dan*: *tbilisidan var*. *-Gan* is "from" a person (lesson 3-3).

**Do not add -dan to the genitive or the bare stem.** *\*Tbilisisdan* and *\*tbilisdan* both miss the instrumental trace. Say *tbilisidan*. For a vowel stem in *-a*, the *-a* truncates as in the instrumental: *sḳolidan*, not *\*sḳoladan*.

**Do not keep the -d before -mde.** *\*Sadguradmde* stacks the full adverbial. It loses its *-d*: *sadguramde*.

**Do not truncate in the adverbial.** After *dedit* and *xit* it is tempting to write *\*xad* for "as a tree". The adverbial of an ordinary vowel stem does not truncate: it adds *-d* to the full stem: *xed*, *dedad*, *sḳolad*.

**Do not translate "as" with -vit.** *-Vit* compares (lesson 3-2): *masc̣avlebelivit* "like a teacher", someone who is not one. *Masc̣avleblad mušaobs* says what the person's role actually is.

**Do not use the instrumental for "in" a language.** *\*Kartulit laṗaraḳobs* follows the tool logic, "with Georgian". Languages take the adverbial: *kartulad laṗaraḳobs*.

## Connections

Japanese で covers both the tool (ペンで書く) and the means of transport (バスで), exactly the range of *-it*; Korean uses (으)로 for both, and also for "as" a role (교사로 일하다), the range of *-ad*. Spanish *desde ... hasta* and French *de ... à* match *-dan ... -mde* in meaning; in Georgian both sit after the noun.

## Exercises

1. Give the instrumental and adverbial of *mankana*, *ezo*, *čangali* and *ṭeleponi*.
2. Make *-dan* and *-mde* forms of *sḳola*, *ṭq̇e*, *bazari* and *saxli*.
3. Say "We eat with a spoon" and "She writes with a pencil."
4. Say "Nino works as a translator."
5. Say "Giorgi is from Georgia; he lives far from Tbilisi."
6. Correct *\*Ḳalamtan c̣ers*, *\*Sḳoladan* and *\*Kartulit laṗaraḳobs*.
7. Translate *gazapxulidan šemodgomamde soplad cxovroben* and explain each form. (*Soplad* is the adverbial of *sopeli*: "in the countryside".)
8. Explain why *saxlidan* has an *-i-* and *saxlamde* an *-a-*.

## Worked answers

1. *Mankana* truncates in the instrumental, not in the adverbial: *mankanit*, *mankanad*. *Ezo* keeps its vowel: *ezot* (or *ezoti*), *ezod*. *Čangali* syncopates in both: *čanglit*, *čanglad*. *Ṭeleponi* is a plain consonant stem: *ṭeleponit*, *ṭeleponad*.
2. *Sḳolidan* (instrumental *sḳolit*), *sḳolamde* (adverbial *sḳolad*). *Ṭq̇idan* (*ṭq̇it*), *ṭq̇emde* (*ṭq̇ed*). *Bazari* syncopates (vocabulary note, lesson 3-2): *bazridan*, *bazramde*. *Saxlidan*, *saxlamde*.
3. *Ḳovzit vč̣amt*: tool in the instrumental, *v-* ... *-t* for "we". *Pankrit c̣ers*: *pankari* syncopates.
4. *Nino tarǯimnad mušaobs.* A role takes the adverbial, and *tarǯimani* syncopates.
5. *Giorgi sakartvelodan aris; tbilisidan šors cxovrobs.* *Sakartvelo* keeps its *-o*, so *sakartvelodan*; *šors* takes *-dan* on its noun, as *axlos* takes *-tan*.
6. A tool is instrumental: *ḳalmit c̣ers*. An *-a* stem truncates before *-dan*: *sḳolidan*. A language takes the adverbial: *kartulad laṗaraḳobs*.
7. "From spring to autumn they live in the countryside." *Gazapxul-i-dan*: instrumental trace plus "from". *Šemodgoma-mde*: a vowel stem takes *-mde* directly. *Sopl-ad*: an adverbial of place, with the syncopated stem. *Cxovr-ob-en*: "they live".
8. *-Dan* grew out of the instrumental *-it* plus *-gan*, and *-mde* out of the adverbial *-ad* plus *-mde* with the *-d* lost. The vowels are what remain of those case endings (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
