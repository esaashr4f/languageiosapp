## The idea

The case endings of lesson 3-1 come in two shapes. Some begin with a consonant: ergative *-m(a)*, dative *-s*, adverbial *-(a)d*, vocative *-v*. Others begin with a vowel: genitive *-is*, instrumental *-it*, and the nominative *-i*. A consonant stem such as *saxl-* takes all of them without trouble. A **vowel stem**, a noun whose stem ends in a vowel, such as *deda* or *ezo*, meets a problem with the second group: *\*deda-is* would put two vowels side by side.

Georgian solves this in one of two ways, and which way a noun takes is what you need to know about it.

- **The stem gives way.** The noun drops its last vowel before *-is* and *-it*: *deda* → *ded-is*, *ded-it*; *xe* → *x-is*, *x-it*. This is truncation (lesson 2-4).
- **The ending gives way.** The noun keeps its vowel, and the ending loses its own: *ezo* → *ezo-s*, *ezo-t*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria call these nouns non-truncating.

Before a consonant ending, nothing happens to any vowel stem: *dedam*, *dedas*, *dedad*; *ezom*, *ezos*, *ezod*. And in the nominative, every vowel stem has no ending at all.

This is a description of the pattern, not its history: the sources consulted say which nouns do which, but not why the language split them this way. What they do say is that the split mostly follows the final vowel. Native nouns in *-a* and *-e* truncate; nouns in *-o*, *-u* and *-i* do not. Then there are named exceptions, and a few nouns on which the sources disagree. This lesson sorts them.

## How it is built

Ask two questions about a vowel stem: which vowel does it end in, and is it a name?

**1. Common nouns in -a: truncate in the genitive and instrumental, and in every plural form.**

- *deda*: *dedam*, *dedas*, *dedis*, *dedit*, *dedad*, *dedav*; plural *dedebi*.
- One-syllable nouns too: *ʒma* → *ʒmis*, *kva* → *kvis*, *mta* → *mtis*.
- So do place names in *-a*, Georgian or foreign: *mcxeta* → *mcxetis*, *iṭalia* → *iṭaliis*, *ameriḳa* → *ameriḳis* (Wiktionary; Bolkvadze and Kiziria list many more).
- Because *-dan* is built on the instrumental, it truncates too: *sḳolidan*, *iṭaliidan*. Because *-mde* is built on the adverbial, it does not: *sḳolamde*.

**2. Native nouns in -e: truncate in the genitive and instrumental only.**

- *xe*: *xem*, *xes*, *xis*, *xit*, *xed*, *xev*; plural *xeebi*, with no truncation.
- The same for *ɣame*, *rʒe*, *ṭq̇e*, *mtvare* "moon", *ḳlde* "cliff", *saxe* "face".
- Family names in *-ʒe*, the commonest Georgian surname ending, follow this pattern: Bolkvadze and Kiziria give the genitive of *ḳaranaʒe* as *ḳaranaʒis*.

**3. Nouns in -o, -u and -i, and borrowed nouns in -e: never truncate.** The genitive is *-s* and the instrumental *-t*, with an optional *-i* (*-si*, *-ti*):

- *ezo*: *ezom*, *ezos*, *ezos(i)*, *ezot(i)*, *ezod*, *ezov*.
- The same for *ḳino* "cinema", *radio*, *lobio* "beans", *sakartvelo*, *čai*, *ṭaksi*, *ḳape*.
- The genitive and dative look the same. Position decides: before a noun, *ḳinos* is genitive ("of the cinema"); as an object, dative. The long genitive *ezosi* is unambiguous.

**4. Given names: never truncate, whatever the vowel.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria are explicit: *nino*, *giorgi* and names in *-a* alike keep their vowel. *Ia* (a woman's name) has the genitive *ias*, although the flower *ia* is a common noun in *-a*. The same goes for **your own relatives**: when you mean your mother, or the mother of the person you are talking to, *deda* is treated as a name: *dedas otaxi* "Mum's room". When you mean a mother in general, it truncates: *dedis*.

**5. Then the plural.** *-a* stems truncate before *-eb-* (*dedebi*, *mtebi*). *-e*, *-o* and *-u* stems keep their vowel (*xeebi*, *ezoebi*). Borrowed *-i* stems vary: Wiktionary gives *ṭaksebi* and *ṭramvaebi*.

**Building a form.** "Mtskheta's history":

1. *Mcxeta* is a place name in *-a*. Rule 1: truncate before *-is*.
2. *Mcxet-is isṭoria.*

"The cinema ticket":

1. *Ḳino* ends in *-o*. Rule 3: keep the vowel, genitive *-s*.
2. *Ḳinos bileti.*

## Where it applies

- **Truncating -a nouns:** most common nouns in *-a*: *deda*, *mama*, *ʒma*, *mta*, *magida*, *sḳola*, *mankana*, *ena*, *biblioteḳa*, *isṭoria*; relatives such as *mamida*, *ṗaṗa*, *biʒa*, *bebia*, *babua* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria list the blood relations as truncating); and place names: *mcxeta*, *iṭalia*, *germania*, *ameriḳa*, *iaṗonia*, *ḳorea*.
- **Truncating -e nouns:** native nouns such as *xe*, *dɣe*, *ɣame*, *rʒe*, *ṭq̇e*, *ǯibe*, *mze*, *mtvare*, *ḳlde*, *saxe*, *mdinare*, *meuɣle*, and surnames in *-ʒe*. (*Zɣva* "sea" looks similar but ends in *-a*: *zɣvis*.)
- **Non-truncating -o and -u nouns:** *ezo*, *saɣamo*, *ɣvino* in Wiktionary (see Watch out), *meṭro*, *ḳino*, *radio*, *lobio*, *okro*, *dro* "time", *sakartvelo*, *samzareulo*, *sasṭumro*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria say *-o* nouns never truncate, with one exception, *ɣvino*.
- **Non-truncating -i nouns:** all borrowed: *čai*, *ṭaksi*, *ṭramvai* "tram".
- **Borrowed -e nouns:** never truncate: *ḳape* → *ḳapes*, *ḳapet*.
- **Given names:** *nino*, *giorgi*, *ia*: never truncate, have no vocative ending (lesson 3-7) and, unlike surnames, are not normally plural.
- **Nouns in -a that are diminutives**, such as *gogona* "little girl": see Watch out.
- **Not here:** nouns that truncate and also lose an inner vowel, such as *panǯara* → *panǯris*, *kveq̇ana* → *kveq̇nis*: lesson 3-6.

## Watch out

**Do not truncate a given name.** *\*Is c̣igni* for "Ia's book" treats the name as the flower. Names keep their vowel: *ias c̣igni*, *ninos c̣igni*.

**Do not truncate an -o noun.** *\*Ezis*, *\*ḳinis* and *\*radiit* copy the *deda* pattern. Say *ezos*, *ḳinos*, *radiot*.

**Do not keep the -a before -is or -it.** *\*Mcxetais*, *\*sḳolait* and *\*iṭaliaidan* stack two vowels. Say *mcxetis*, *sḳolit*, *iṭaliidan*.

**Do not truncate an -e noun in the plural.** *\*Xebi* and *\*ḳldebi* drop a vowel that only goes in the genitive and instrumental singular. Say *xeebi*, *ḳldeebi*.

**Do not truncate in the adverbial.** *\*Dedd* or *\*xd* has no vowel left to carry the *-d*. The adverbial never truncates: *dedad*, *xed*.

**Do not read every *-os* as a dative.** In *ḳinos bileti*, *ḳinos* is genitive. Look at what follows: a noun after it makes it genitive; a verb after it usually makes it an object.

**Your own mother is a name.** *Dedis otaxi* is "the mother's room", someone's mother. For your own, Bolkvadze and Kiziria give *dedas*: *dedas otaxi*. The same for *mama*, *babua*, *bebia*, *ṗaṗa*.

**Sources disagree on a few nouns.** For *ɣvino* "wine", Bolkvadze and Kiziria give a truncating genitive *ɣvinis* and instrumental *ɣvinit*, but Wiktionary's table has *ɣvinos*, *ɣvinot*. For *gogona* "little girl", Wiktionary truncates (*gogonis*), while Bolkvadze and Kiziria say diminutives in *-a* keep their vowel in the singular (*gogonas*) and note that many such nouns are used both ways. For *dro* "time", Bolkvadze and Kiziria give two genitives, *dros* and *drois*. These forms are labelled uncertain in this guide; you will meet both.

## Connections

Spanish solves the same clash with the opposite tool: it adds a consonant or chooses a different ending (*café* → *cafés*, not *\*cafes*). Japanese and Korean avoid it by choosing the particle by the noun's last sound: Korean 가 after a vowel and 이 after a consonant, 를 and 을, are the same idea as Georgian *-m* after a vowel and *-ma* after a consonant.

## Exercises

1. Give the genitive and instrumental of *ena*, *mze*, *radio*, *ṭaksi* and *iaṗonia*.
2. Give the plural nominative of *ḳlde*, *mamida* and *ḳino*.
3. Say "Nino's brother lives in Japan" and "Nino's brother is from Japan."
4. Say "This is the colour of the moon" and "This is the history of Korea."
5. Correct *\*Es ninois saxlia*, *\*Ezis ḳari* and *\*Xebi didia*.
6. Translate *mamidas* in two ways and explain how you would tell them apart.
7. Why is it *sḳolidan* but *sḳolamde*?
8. Say "Ia's grandad is at the cinema."

## Worked answers

1. *Ena* is a common noun in *-a*: *enis*, *enit*. *Mze* is a native *-e* noun: *mzis*, *mzit*. *Radio* keeps its vowel: *radios*, *radiot*. *Ṭaksi* is a borrowed *-i* noun: *ṭaksis*, *ṭaksit*. *Iaṗonia* is a place name in *-a*: *iaṗoniis*, *iaṗoniit*.
2. *Ḳldeebi*: an *-e* noun keeps its vowel before *-eb-*. *Mamidebi*: an *-a* noun truncates in every plural form. *Ḳinoebi*: an *-o* noun keeps its vowel.
3. *Ninos ʒma iaṗoniaši cxovrobs*: *-ši* replaces the dative *-s* and the *-a* stays. *Ninos ʒma iaṗoniidan aris*: *-dan* is built on the instrumental, so the *-a* truncates. *Ninos* is genitive: a given name never truncates.
4. *Es mtvaris peria*: *mtvare* truncates in the genitive. *Es ḳoreis isṭoriaa*: *ḳorea* is a place name in *-a*, so *ḳore-is*. *Isṭoria* ends in *-a*, so the short copula gives *isṭoriaa*.
5. A name keeps its vowel and takes *-s*: *es ninos saxlia*. An *-o* noun does not truncate: *ezos ḳari*. An *-e* noun keeps its vowel in the plural: *xeebi didia* (singular verb for things, lesson 2-4).
6. *Mamidas* is the dative of *mamida* ("the aunt", as an object, or with *-ši*, *-ze* removed) and could be the genitive only if you mean your own aunt, treated like a name. As a common noun, the genitive is *mamidis*. Position decides: before a noun, it is genitive; as an object before a verb, dative.
7. *-Dan* is built on the instrumental *sḳolit*, which truncates. *-Mde* is built on the adverbial *sḳolad*, which does not. The postposition inherits the stem of its case.
8. *Ias ṗaṗa ḳinošia* (or *ḳinoši aris*). *Ias* keeps its vowel, being a name; *ḳino* is an *-o* noun, and *-ši* replaces its dative *-s*.
