## The idea

Since lesson 2-4 you have met nouns that lose a vowel: *megobari* "friend", plural *megobrebi*; *pankari* "pencil", instrumental *pankrit*; *masc̣avlebeli* "teacher", genitive *masc̣avleblis*. This is **syncope**: the loss of the vowel in the last syllable of a stem when certain endings are added. This lesson gives the rule, the places it applies, and the exceptions.

Syncope is not random. Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe it in two parts, one about the shape of the noun and one about the case.

**The shape.** Syncope happens in consonant stems whose last syllable is one of the vowels *a*, *e*, *o* followed by one of the consonants *l*, *m*, *n*, *r*: *-al*, *-am*, *-an*, *-ar*, *-el*, *-em*, *-en*, *-er*, *-ol*, *-on*, *-or*. So *megobar-*, *ḳalam-*, *mezobel-*, *irem-* "deer", *saṗon-* "soap", *potol-* "leaf".

**The case.** The vowel goes in three singular cases, the genitive, the instrumental and the adverbial, and in every plural form. It stays in the nominative, ergative, dative and vocative singular:

- *megobari*, *megobarma*, *megobars*, *megobaro*: full stem.
- *megobris*, *megobrit*, *megobrad*, *megobrebi*: short stem.

The postpositions follow their case. Those on the dative keep the full stem (*megobarši*, *megobartan*, lesson 3-2). Those on the genitive, instrumental and adverbial take the short one: *megobristvis*, *megobridan*, *megobramde*.

Why these cases and not the others? The sources consulted do not give a settled answer. Bolkvadze and Kiziria offer a tentative one: it is possible that syncope depends on stress and on the number of syllables. Their evidence is *ḳari* "door", which never syncopates, against the compounds *cisḳari* "dawn" and *č̣išḳari* "gate", which do (*č̣išḳris*): in the longer word the stress falls on the first syllable, and the syllable *-ar* "loses its defence". Treat this as a hypothesis, not a rule you can apply.

## How it is built

**Step 1: is it a consonant stem with a/e/o + l/m/n/r in its last syllable?** If not, it does not syncopate. *Saxli*, *c̣igni*, *bavšvi*, *tbilisi* never do.

**Step 2: if it is, is the form one of the syncope places?** Genitive, instrumental, adverbial, any plural, and the postpositions built on them. If yes, drop the vowel:

1. "For the guest": *sṭumari*, last syllable *-mar*, a syncope shape.
2. *-Tvis* is on the genitive, a syncope place: *sṭumr-is-tvis*.

3. "In winter": *zamtari*, last syllable *-tar*.
4. *-Ši* is on the dative, not a syncope place: *zamtarši*.

**Step 3: check the exceptions.** Many nouns with the right shape do not syncopate, and these must be learnt. Bolkvadze and Kiziria list, among others:

- most one-syllable stems: *kali* "woman" → *kalis*, *bari* → *baris*, *xeli* → *xelis*; but *c̣eli* "year" → *c̣lis* is an exception to the exception.
- many other nouns, native and borrowed: *saxeli* → *saxelis*, *cxeni* → *cxenis*, *c̣veni* "juice" → *c̣venis* (so Wiktionary too; Hewitt, quoting Vogt, has *c̣vnis*), *gemi* "ship" → *gemis*, *sḳami*, *avṭori*, *c̣erili* (*-il* is not a syncope shape anyway).
- Wiktionary gives *cxoveli* "animal" → *cxovelis* and *anbani* → *anbanis*, although *ɣrubeli* "cloud" → *ɣrublis* and *mezobeli* → *mezoblis*. The shape alone does not decide.

**Two variations on the rule.**

- **-o- becomes -v-.** In a few nouns with *-or* or *-on* the *o* does not vanish but turns into *v*: *mindori* "field" → *mindvris*, *niori* "garlic" → *nivris*, *mac̣oni* "yoghurt" → *mac̣vnis* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria; Wiktionary has the same for *mindori* and *niori*).
- **-av- syncopates or vanishes.** Stems in *-av* can also syncopate: *ḳaravi* "tent" → *ḳarvis*, *nagavi* "rubbish" → *nagvis*. In *ambavi* "news, story" the whole *-av-* goes: *ambis*, plural *ambebi*.

**Truncation and syncope together.** A handful of *-a* nouns both truncate and syncopate: *panǯara* → *panǯris*, *kveq̇ana* → *kveq̇nis*, *karxana* "factory" → *karxnis*. Their plurals are short too: *panǯrebi*, *kveq̇nebi*.

**When you cannot predict, check.** The plural is the fastest test. Wiktionary's headword line gives it, and this guide's vocabulary notes give the genitive or plural of every noun that syncopates. If the plural is short, the genitive, instrumental and adverbial are short too.

## Where it applies

- **Genitive singular:** *megobris*, *sṭumris*, *c̣q̇lis*, *mṭris*, *q̇urʒnis* "of grapes".
- **Instrumental singular:** *ḳalmit*, *šakrit*, *saṗnit* "with soap".
- **Adverbial singular:** *mxaṭvrad*, *tarǯimnad*, *soplad*.
- **Every plural form:** *megobrebi*, *megobrebma*, *megobrebs*, *megobrebis* ... *potlebi* "leaves", *beč̣debi* "rings", *irmebi* "deer".
- **Postpositions on those cases:** *megobristvis*, *megobrisgan*, *megobrisḳen*, *megobridan*, *megobramde*.
- **Place names and surnames too:** Bolkvadze and Kiziria give the river *mṭḳvari* → *mṭḳvris* and the surname *c̣ereteli* → *c̣eretlis*. Names in *-isi* and *-eti* never syncopate: *tbilisis*.
- **Some words with a different meaning:** *tvali* "eye" keeps its vowel (*tvalis*), but *tvali* "gem, wheel" syncopates (*tvlis*, plural *tvlebi*); Wiktionary lists both plurals.
- **Not in:** nominative, ergative, dative and vocative singular; dative postpositions (*-ši*, *-ze*, *-tan*); *-vit* on a consonant stem, which is built on the nominative (*megobarivit*).

## Watch out

**Do not syncopate in the dative.** *\*Megobrs* and *\*sṭumrs* carry the short stem into a case that keeps the long one. Say *megobars*, *sṭumars*. The same before *-ši*, *-ze*, *-tan*: *sopelši*, not *\*soplši*.

**Do not syncopate in the ergative.** *\*Megobrma* looks consistent with *megobrebma*, but the singular ergative keeps the full stem: *megobarma* (Wiktionary; Bolkvadze and Kiziria's table for *aṭami* "peach" has *aṭamma*).

**Do not syncopate by shape alone.** *Cxeni*, *saxeli*, *kali*, *xeli*, *peri*, *gemi* have the shape but keep their vowel: *cxenis*, not *\*cxnis*; *saxelis*, not *\*saxlis*, which is the genitive of *saxli* "house". When a noun is new to you, check its plural before you shorten it.

**Do not forget syncope in the plural.** *\*Megobarebi*, *\*sopelebi* and *\*ḳalamebi* keep a vowel that every plural form drops. Say *megobrebi*, *soplebi*, *ḳalmebi*.

**Do not drop the whole syllable.** *\*Mindris* or *\*nirs* go too far. *Mindori* and *niori* keep a *v*: *mindvris*, *nivris*. Only *ambavi* loses a whole syllable: *ambis*.

**Do not forget truncation as well.** *\*Kveq̇anis* truncates but does not syncopate, and *\*kveq̇nais* does the opposite. Both happen: *kveq̇nis*.

**Some nouns vary, and the sources differ on others.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria accept both *limonis* and *limnis* for *limoni* "lemon" (Wiktionary has only *limonis*), and both *ɣmertis* and *ɣvtis* for *ɣmerti* "God" (Wiktionary gives *ɣvtis*, a special shortened stem). For *mxare* "side, region" they give a syncopated *mxris*, while Wiktionary has *mxaris*. For clothing words, *ṭansacmeli* "clothes" and *pexsacmeli* "shoes", their singular and plural patterns differ between the two sources. These forms are labelled uncertain here.

## Connections

Spanish has a sound alternation of the same family in verbs: *poder*, *puedo*, where a vowel changes with stress. Turkish (in this app) has syncope much like Georgian's: *burun* "nose", *burnu* "his nose", where a vowel before a vowel-initial suffix drops in a listed set of words; like Georgian, it must be learnt word by word.

## Exercises

1. Which of these syncopate in the genitive: *mezobeli*, *saxeli*, *ḳedeli*, *xeli*, *sṭumari*, *bari*? Give the genitives.
2. Give the dative, genitive and plural of *potoli* and *iremi*.
3. Say "for the guests" and "at the guests' place".
4. Say "Grandad is reading a fairy tale for the children."
5. Correct *\*Megobrs vxedav*, *\*Saṗonit* and *\*Ambavebi*.
6. Say "Giorgi is ten years old." (Use *ati*.)
7. Why does *zamtari* give *zamtarši* but *zamtramde*?
8. Translate the proverb in the examples word for word, and name the case of each noun.

## Worked answers

1. *Mezobeli* → *mezoblis* and *ḳedeli* → *ḳedlis* syncopate (their vocabulary notes gave the plurals). *Sṭumari* → *sṭumris*. *Saxeli* → *saxelis*, *xeli* → *xelis* and *bari* → *baris* keep their vowel, though they have the shape: they are among the exceptions.
2. *Potols*, *potlis*, *potlebi*. *Irems*, *irmis*, *irmebi*. The dative keeps the full stem; the genitive and every plural form lose the vowel.
3. "For the guests": plural, then genitive, then *-tvis*: *sṭumrebistvis*. "At the guests' place": plural dative base, *-tan*: *sṭumrebtan*. Both are short, because the plural always syncopates.
4. *Ṗaṗa bavšvebistvis zɣaṗars ḳitxulobs.* The object *zɣaṗars* is dative, a full-stem case. *Ṗaṗa* is the subject in the nominative.
5. The dative keeps the full stem: *megobars vxedav*. The instrumental syncopates: *saṗnit*. *Ambavi* loses the whole *-av-* in the plural: *ambebi*.
6. *Giorgi ati c̣lis aris*, or with the short copula, as Bolkvadze and Kiziria write it, *ati c̣lisaa*. The age is a genitive: "of ten years". *Ati* keeps its *-i* before the genitive noun.
7. *-Ši* is built on the dative *zamtars*, a full-stem case. *-Mde* is built on the adverbial *zamtrad*, a syncope place.
8. "My enemy's enemy my friend-is." *Mṭris*: genitive, short stem. *Mṭeri*: nominative, full stem, the subject. *Megobaria*: nominative *megobari*, full stem, plus the copula *-a*.
