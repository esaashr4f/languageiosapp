## The idea

When you speak to someone directly, calling them by name or title, Georgian can put the word in a case of its own: the **vocative**. *Megobari* is "a friend"; *megobaro!* is "friend!", said to the friend. English has no such form; Latin had one (*Marce!*), and Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe the Georgian vocative as the case used when addressing a person.

The vocative is the odd one out among the seven cases. It is not part of the sentence's machinery: no verb chooses it, no postposition attaches to it (Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that postpositions never go on the vocative), and the sentence would be complete without it. It stands outside the clause, usually at the start, separated by a comma.

What makes it worth a lesson is that the vocative is not used everywhere you might expect. Common nouns take it: *megobaro*, *baṭono* "sir", *bavšvebo* "children". Given names do not. Bolkvadze and Kiziria are explicit that given names take no vocative ending, whether they end in a vowel or a consonant: you call *nino!*, *giorgi!*, and for a name like *daviti*, the bare stem *davit!*. *Deda* and *mama*, when you speak to your own mother and father, behave like names too: *deda!*, *mama!*.

## How it is built

**Consonant stems: add -o.** *Megobar-o*, *baṭon-o* "sir", *kalbaṭon-o* "madam", *bič̣-o* "boy!", *ḳac-o* "man!". There is no syncope in the vocative: *megobaro*, *sṭumaro*.

**Vowel stems: add -v, or nothing.** *Gogo* → *gogo!* or *gogov!*; *daiḳo* "little sister" → *daiḳov*; *ḳolega* "colleague" → *ḳolegav*. Wiktionary's tables give the *-v* forms (*dedav*, *ezov*); Bolkvadze and Kiziria give the bare form as normal for many nouns and the *-v* as an option. A vowel stem never truncates in the vocative.

**The plural: -eb-o.** Every plural vocative ends the same way: *megobrebo*, *bavšvebo*, *kalbaṭonebo da baṭonebo* "ladies and gentlemen". Syncope happens here, as in every plural (lesson 3-6).

**Names: no ending.**

- Vowel-stem given names stay as they are: *nino*, *giorgi*, *ia*.
- Consonant-stem given names drop the nominative *-i* and add nothing: *daviti* → *davit!*, *tamari* → *tamar!*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria's table gives the bare stem as the vocative of such names.
- Surnames are different: those in *-švili* take *-o* (*mč̣edlišvilo*), and those in *-ʒe* take an optional *-v* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
- Place names are rarely addressed. Bolkvadze and Kiziria give no vocative ending for vowel-stem names in *-i*, *-o*, *-u* (*ṭaiṭi*, *ṗeru*), an optional one for names in *-a*, and *-o* for consonant stems (*tbiliso!*).

**Words that describe the addressee take -o too.** An adjective before a noun in the vocative takes *-o* if it is a consonant stem: *ʒvirpaso megobaro* "dear friend", *saq̇varelo daiḳo* "dear little sister". It stays singular before a plural noun: *ʒvirpaso megobrebo* "dear friends". Lesson 3-10 shows that this is the general rule for adjectives before nouns.

**Titles with names.** In formal speech Georgians address a man as *baṭono* and a woman as *kalbaṭono*, followed by the given name, not the surname: *baṭono giorgi*, *kalbaṭono nino* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria). Only the title takes the vocative ending.

**Building an address.**

1. Whom are you calling? A group of friends, whom you like.
2. *Megobari* is a consonant stem; plural *megobrebi*; vocative *megobrebo*.
3. "Dear": *ʒvirpasi*, a consonant stem; before the noun, singular, with *-o*: *ʒvirpaso*.
4. *Ʒvirpaso megobrebo!*

## Where it applies

- **Calling or addressing a person by a common noun:** *megobaro*, *bič̣o* "boy!", *ḳolegav*, *ṗropesoro* "professor", *opicianṭo* "waiter!", *mgzavrebo* "passengers".
- **Titles:** *baṭono*, *kalbaṭono*, alone ("Sir!", "Madam!") or before a first name.
- **Groups:** *bavšvebo*, *megobrebo*, *kalbaṭonebo da baṭonebo*, *xalxo* "people!".
- **Terms of endearment:** *saq̇varelo* "dear", *genacvale* "dear" (a fixed word, not a vocative form, that Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe as softening a request or reply), *daiḳo* "little sister".
- **With describing words:** adjectives and possessives before the noun take *-o* too: *ʒvirpaso megobaro*; *čemo* "my" in lesson 3-8.
- **Animals and things, in speech or poetry:** any noun can be addressed: *ʒaɣlo!* "dog!", *mtvarev!* "moon!".
- **As an interjection:** *ḳaco* "man!, come on!", especially after *ḳi* and *ara*; colloquial.
- **Prayers and set phrases:** *ɣmerto čemo* "my God". Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that *dedao* is used only for a nun or the Virgin Mary, and *mamao* only for a priest.
- **Not with given names, vowel-stem place names in *-i*, *-o* or *-u*,** or your own *deda* and *mama*: these stay bare.

## Watch out

**Do not add -o to a given name.** By analogy with *megobaro*, *\*ninoo* or *\*davito* look regular, and Latin *Marce* would lead you there. Georgian given names take no ending: *nino!*, *davit!*.

**Do not keep the -i of a consonant-stem name.** *\*Daviti, ak var!* uses the nominative. The vocative is the bare stem: *davit, ak var!* Vowel-stem names have no *-i* to drop: *giorgi!* is both nominative and vocative.

**Do not use the surname with baṭono.** English "Mr Beridze" suggests *\*baṭono beriʒe*. Georgian uses the first name: *baṭono giorgi*.

**Do not call your mother *dedao*.** *Dedao* is how you address a nun or the Mother of God, and *mamao* a priest. Call your parents *deda!* and *mama!*.

**Do not put the vocative inside the clause.** *\*Ak megobaro var* breaks the sentence. The vocative stands at the edge, with commas: *megobaro, ak var!*

**Do not syncopate in the singular vocative.** *\*Megobro* and *\*sṭumro* shorten the stem where it stays full. Say *megobaro*, *sṭumaro*. In the plural it does syncopate: *megobrebo*.

**Do not make the adjective plural.** *\*Ʒvirpasebo megobrebo* copies the noun's plural. The adjective before a noun stays singular: *ʒvirpaso megobrebo*.

**Do not use the vocative as a subject.** "Friend, you are here" is *megobaro, ak xar*: the verb *xar* already says "you". In *\*megobaro ak aris*, "friend" is addressed but the verb talks about someone else.

**Do not overuse *ḳaco*.** It is warm but very colloquial. To a stranger or an older person, use *baṭono* or *kalbaṭono*.

## Connections

Arabic has a vocative particle, *yā*, before the noun (*yā ṣadīqī* "O my friend"); Georgian marks the noun itself, as Latin did. Korean has a vocative ending too, 아/야 after a given name (민수야!), used among friends; Georgian does the opposite and leaves the given name bare. French and Spanish have no vocative form, but Spanish *hombre* used as an interjection (*¡no, hombre!*) is a close match for *ḳaco*.

## Exercises

1. Give the vocative singular and plural of *sṭumari*, *mezobeli*, *gamq̇idveli* and *mosc̣avle*.
2. Call out to: Tamar; Davit; Nino; your mother.
3. Address a teacher formally by her first name, Nino, and tell her the children are at school.
4. Say "Dear colleagues!"
5. Correct *\*Ninoo, ak xar?* and *\*Baṭonebi da kalbaṭonebi!*
6. Why is it *ʒvirpaso megobrebo* and not *ʒvirpasebo megobrebo*?
7. Take apart *ṗaṭara gogov* and translate it. Why has *ṗaṭara* no ending?
8. When would a Georgian say *mamao*?

## Worked answers

1. *Sṭumaro*, *sṭumrebo*: full stem in the singular, syncope in the plural. *Mezobelo*, *mezoblebo*: the same. *Gamq̇idvelo*, *gamq̇idvelebo*: this noun does not syncopate (Wiktionary). *Mosc̣avlev* (or *mosc̣avle*), *mosc̣avleebo*: a vowel stem adds *-v* or nothing.
2. *Tamar!* and *davit!*: consonant-stem given names lose the *-i* and take no ending. *Nino!*: a vowel-stem name, unchanged. *Deda!*: your own mother is called like a name.
3. *Kalbaṭono nino, bavšvebi sḳolaši arian.* The title takes the vocative; the name stays bare; *sḳolaši* with *-ši* (lesson 3-2); *arian* because children are people.
4. *Ʒvirpaso ḳolegebo!* *Ḳolega* truncates in the plural (*ḳolegebi*), and the plural vocative is *-eb-o*. The adjective takes *-o* and stays singular.
5. A given name takes no ending: *nino, ak xar?* A call needs the vocative: *kalbaṭonebo da baṭonebo!*
6. An adjective before a noun stays singular (lesson 2-4 for numbers, lesson 3-10 for adjectives), and in the vocative it takes only *-o*. The plural is shown once, on the noun.
7. *ṗaṭara gogo-v*: "little girl!", with the vowel-stem vocative *-v*. *Ṗaṭara* ends in a vowel, and Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that a vowel-stem adjective before a noun stays unchanged in every case.
8. When addressing a priest (Bolkvadze and Kiziria). To his own father he says *mama!*.
