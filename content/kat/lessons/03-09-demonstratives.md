## The idea

English points with two words, "this" and "that". Georgian has three, and the third is keyed to the person you are talking to:

- ეს *es* "this": near me, the speaker.
- ეგ *eg* "that": near you, the listener.
- ის *is* "that": away from both of us, over there.

Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe the three this way: *es saxli* is a house near the speaker, *eg saxli* a house near the person spoken to, *is saxli* a house further off. If you know Japanese or Korean, you already have the system: これ・それ・あれ and 이・그・저 divide space the same way. Spanish *este*, *ese*, *aquel* is close too, though *ese* is not strictly "near you".

These words are both **demonstratives**, words that point (with a noun: "this house"), and pronouns (alone: "this one", "he"). Lesson 3-8 used *is* as "he, she, it"; it is the same word.

The second thing to learn is how they change. Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that these words have two stems, one for the nominative and one for every other case. Before a noun, the second stem appears bare, with no case ending: ამ *am*, მაგ *mag*, იმ *im*. This guide calls it the **oblique** form, glossed `OBL`: the form a pointing word takes in every case but the nominative. The noun after it carries the case:

- *es saxli* "this house", but *am saxls* (dative), *am saxlši*, *am saxlis*, *am saxlit*.

So a demonstrative before a noun behaves like an adjective (lesson 3-10) with an extra twist: it has a different stem outside the nominative. The case information is on the noun, and once is enough.

## How it is built

**Before a noun.** Pick the degree (near me, near you, far), then the case:

- nominative: *es*, *eg*, *is*
- every other case: *am*, *mag*, *im*

The pointing word does not change for the plural: *es saxlebi* "these houses", *am saxlebši* "in these houses" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria). Do not use *eseni* before a noun.

**Alone, as a pronoun.** Then it takes full case endings on its second stem (Wiktionary; Bolkvadze and Kiziria):

- *es*: ergative *aman*, dative *amas*, genitive *amis-* (before a postposition), instrumental *amit*, adverbial *amad*; plural *eseni*, oblique *amat*.
- *eg*: *magan*, *magas*, *magis-*, *magit*, *magad*; plural *egeni*, oblique *magat*.
- *is*: *(i)man*, *(i)mas*, *(i)mis-*, *(i)mit*, *imad*; plural *isini*, oblique *(i)mat*. The forms with *i-* are "that one" with emphasis; the short ones are "he, she, it".

With postpositions: *amaze* "about this", *amaši* "in this", *amastan* "next to this", *amistvis* "for this", *amisgan* "from this".

**The family of pointing words.** The same three-way split runs through place and manner words:

- *ak* "here", *mand* "there, where you are", *ik* "there, over there"
- *akedan* "from here", *ikidan* "from there"; *akamde* "up to here, until now"
- *aseti* "such, like this", *iseti* "such, like that"; *ase* "like this, so", *ise* "like that"
- *amdeni* "this much, this many", *imdeni* "that much" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

*Aseti* and *iseti* decline like adjectives before a noun: *aset amindši* "in weather like this".

**Building a phrase.** "I live in that house over there":

1. Far from both of us: *is*.
2. "In the house": *saxlši*, built on the dative, so not nominative: oblique *im*.
3. *Im saxlši vcxovrob.*

## Where it applies

- **Pointing at things and people, with a noun:** *es ḳaci*, *eg ḳaci*, *is ḳaci*; oblique *am*, *mag*, *im* in every other case.
- **Pointing in time:** *am dilas* "this morning", *im dɣes* "that day"; *es zamtari* "this winter".
- **Referring back in a conversation:** *is* and *im* for what was mentioned; *es* and *am* for what is about to be said or has just been said.
- **Standing for a noun:** *es čemia* "this is mine", *amas vxedav* "I see this", *magas ḳitxulob?* "are you reading that (thing of yours)?".
- **As "he, she, it, they":** *is*, *mas*, *isini*, *mat* (lesson 3-8). *Igi* means the same as *is*; Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that it belongs mostly to older and formal writing.
- **With a possessive or adjective too:** *es čemi c̣igni* "this book of mine", *am did saxlši* "in this big house" (lesson 3-10).
- **Place and manner:** *ak*, *mand*, *ik*; *ase*, *ise*; *aseti*, *iseti*.
- **"The same":** *igive*, which Bolkvadze and Kiziria give with an oblique stem *imave*: *imave saxlši* "in the same house".

## Watch out

**Do not use es in every case.** By analogy with English "this", *\*es saxlši* or *\*es ḳacs* keep the nominative. Outside the nominative it is *am*: *am saxlši*, *am ḳacs*.

**Do not decline the oblique form.** *\*Amas saxls* and *\*amis saxlis* add the endings of the standalone pronoun. Before a noun the form is bare: *am saxls*, *am saxlis*. The full forms are only for a pointing word used alone.

**Do not use eseni before a noun.** English "these books" leads to *\*eseni c̣ignebi*. Before a noun, *es*: *es c̣ignebi*. *Eseni* stands alone: *eseni čemia* "these are mine".

**Do not use is for something near the listener.** "That book you have" is *eg c̣igni*, oblique *mag c̣igns*. *Is c̣igni* sounds as if the book were somewhere else.

**Do not mistake the personal and pointing uses of is.** *Is ak cxovrobs* is "he lives here"; *is ḳaci ak cxovrobs* is "that man lives here". Bolkvadze and Kiziria draw the same line: with a noun after it, *is* points.

**Do not translate *ak* and *ik* with -ši.** "Here" and "there" are adverbs already: *\*akši* does not exist. Say *ak var*. For "from here", *akedan*; for "up to here", *akamde*.

**Do not put the case on the pointing word and leave the noun bare.** *\*Amas saxli* puts the dative on the wrong word. The noun takes it: *am saxls*.

**Mind the person of mand.** *Mand* is "there, by you", as *eg* is "that, by you". On the phone, "what's the weather like there?" asks about the listener's place: *mand*, not *ik*.

## Connections

Japanese これ・それ・あれ and Korean 이・그・저 are the closest match: three degrees, the middle one tied to the listener. Spanish *este*, *ese*, *aquel* and the adverbs *aquí*, *ahí*, *allí* map neatly onto *es*, *eg*, *is* and *ak*, *mand*, *ik*. Classical Arabic has a two-way system (*hādhā*, *dhālika*), like English.

## Exercises

1. Say "this man", "for this man", "in that (far) city" and "that (by you) dog".
2. Say "These children are in that house."
3. Say "Is that photo (by you) yours?" and answer "Yes, this photo is mine."
4. Say "I am thinking about that" (something far off or mentioned before).
5. Correct *\*Es kalakši vcxovrob*, *\*Eseni c̣ignebi axalia* and *\*Amas ḳacs vxedav*.
6. Take apart *am bavšvebis deda* and explain why *am* is oblique.
7. Say "The station is far from here."
8. Translate *mand ra aris?* and say where the thing asked about is.

## Worked answers

1. *Es ḳaci*: nominative. *Am ḳacistvis*: the noun is in the genitive with *-tvis*, so the oblique *am*. *Im kalakši*: oblique of *is*, noun with *-ši*. *Eg ʒaɣli*: nominative, near the listener.
2. *Es bavšvebi im saxlši arian.* *Es* in the nominative stays *es* before a plural noun; *im* before *saxlši*; *arian* because children are people.
3. *Eg poṭo šenia?* *Ki, es poṭo čemia.* The question points at the photo near the listener, the answer at the same photo, now near the speaker who is talking about it: *eg* becomes *es*.
4. *Imaze vpikrob* (or *maze*, *masze*). The standalone dative *imas* with *-ze* in place of *-s*.
5. Outside the nominative, the oblique: *am kalakši vcxovrob*. Before a noun, *es*, not *eseni*: *es c̣ignebi axalia*. Before a noun, the bare oblique: *am ḳacs vxedav*.
6. *Am* "this" (oblique), *bavšv-eb-is* "of the children", *deda* "mother": "the mother of these children". *Am* goes with *bavšvebis*, which is genitive; the case of *deda* does not matter.
7. *Sadguri akedan šors aris.* *Šors* "far" takes a *-dan* phrase (lesson 3-4), and "from here" is the single word *akedan*.
8. "What's there (by you)?" The thing is near the listener: *mand* is the "you" member of *ak*, *mand*, *ik*. *Ra* "what" is lesson 3-11 in full.
