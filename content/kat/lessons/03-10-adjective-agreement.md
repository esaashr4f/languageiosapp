## The idea

A Georgian adjective comes before its noun: *didi saxli* "a big house", *ʒveli kalaki* "an old town". That much you have known since lesson 2-1. This lesson answers the question you met in lesson 2-6, when *didi* became *did* before *saxls*: what happens to an adjective when the noun changes case?

It agrees, but only partly. Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe it exactly: before a noun, a consonant-stem adjective has a full case ending in three cases only, the nominative, ergative and vocative. In the genitive and instrumental the ending is "only partially present", reduced to *-i*. In the dative and adverbial the adjective is a bare stem. A vowel-stem adjective, such as *ṗaṭara* "small", does not change at all.

Why only partly? Because in Georgian the case is shown once per phrase, on the noun at the end. Everything before the noun (pointing word, possessive, number, adjective) is a describing word, and describing words carry little or no case. You saw this with *am*, the oblique *es*, in lesson 3-9: *am saxlši*, never *\*amas saxlši*. Adjectives have kept a little more, but the principle is the same. The sources do not give the history of these reduced forms, and this guide does not guess at it; it describes them.

Number works the same way. An adjective before a plural noun stays singular: *didi saxlebi* "big houses". Bolkvadze and Kiziria state the rule: the modern norm is a singular adjective before the noun, whatever the noun's number. You already apply it to numbers (*ori saxli*, lesson 2-4).

## How it is built

**Consonant-stem adjectives** (*didi*, *ʒveli*, *axali*, *lamazi*, *c̣iteli* "red"). Before a noun:

- nominative *-i*: *didi saxli*
- ergative *-ma*: *didma saxlma* (lesson 4-12)
- dative: bare stem: *did saxls*
- genitive: *-i*: *didi saxlis*
- instrumental: *-i*: *didi saxlit*
- adverbial: bare stem: *did saxlad*
- vocative *-o*: *dido*; with a noun that can be addressed, *ʒvirpaso megobaro* (lesson 3-7).

Wiktionary's adjective tables print exactly these attributive forms: for *axali*, "nominative, genitive, instrumental *axali*; ergative *axalma*; dative, adverbial *axal*; vocative *axalo*".

**Vowel-stem adjectives** (*ṗaṭara* "small", *mc̣vane* "green", *axalgazrda* "young", *sufta* "clean", *savse* "full", *saintereso* "interesting"). Before a noun they never change: *ṗaṭara saxli*, *ṗaṭara saxls*, *ṗaṭara saxlis*, *ṗaṭara saxlši*.

**With postpositions.** The adjective takes the form of the case under the postposition:

- on the dative (*-ši*, *-ze*, *-tan*): bare stem: *did saxlši*, *axal magidaze*
- on the genitive (*-tvis*, *-gan*, *-ḳen*): *-i*: *didi saxlistvis*
- on the instrumental (*-dan*): *-i*: *didi kalakidan*
- on the adverbial (*-mde*): bare stem: *did kalakamde*.

**Several adjectives.** Each one takes the form: *did tetr saxlši* "in a big white house".

**No syncope before a noun.** A syncopating adjective such as *c̣iteli* keeps its full stem before a noun, because its genitive and instrumental forms there are *c̣iteli*: *c̣iteli pankrit* "with a red pencil".

**Glossing.** The bare stem is left unglossed (lesson 2-6: *did saxls*). The *-i* before a genitive or instrumental noun is what remains of those case endings, so this guide does not gloss it as nominative: it writes the adjective unsegmented, as `big`.

**Adjectives without a noun.** When the adjective stands for the noun ("the big one"), it is declined in full, like a noun of its stem type: *dids*, *didis*, *didit*; syncope included (*c̣itlis*). And as a predicate it is a nominative with the copula: *saxli didia*.

**Building a phrase.** "We live in a big old house":

1. The noun: *saxli*; "in" it: *saxlši*, a dative-based form.
2. Both adjectives take the dative form, the bare stem: *did*, *ʒvel*.
3. *Did ʒvel saxlši vcxovrobt.*

## Where it applies

- **Every adjective before a noun, in every case:** *axali saxli*, *axal saxls*, *axali saxlis*, *axali saxlit*, *axal saxlad*.
- **Before plural nouns:** singular adjective: *axali saxlebi*, *axal saxlebši*.
- **Under postpositions:** the form of the case underneath: *axal saxlši*, *axali saxlistvis*, *axali saxlidan*, *axal saxlamde*.
- **Words that behave like adjectives:** numbers (*or saxls*, *ori saxlis*; lesson 5-3), *aseti* and *iseti* (lesson 3-9), quantifiers such as *bevri* (*bevr c̣igns*). Possessives are the exception: they keep *-s* in the dative and adverbial (*čems saxls*, lesson 3-8).
- **Several adjectives in a row:** each in the same form: *lamaz did saxlši*.
- **Adjective plus genitive:** the adjective goes with the noun right after it. *Ʒveli kalakis cenṭri* is "the centre of the old town"; *kalakis ʒveli cenṭri* is "the old centre of the town".
- **Standing alone:** full declension, like a noun: *dids*, *didši*, *c̣itlis*.
- **As a predicate:** nominative plus "be": *saxli didia*, *es rtulia*.
- **After the noun:** possible in poetry and older styles, with fuller agreement (Bolkvadze and Kiziria; lesson 3-12 for the old plural, where agreement is complete).

## Watch out

**Do not give the adjective the noun's full ending in the dative.** *\*Dids saxls* and *\*axals c̣igns* copy the noun. Before a dative noun the adjective is bare: *did saxls*, *axal c̣igns*.

**Do not drop the -i before a genitive or instrumental noun.** Having learnt *did saxls*, it is tempting to write *\*did saxlis*. In the genitive and instrumental the *-i* stays: *didi saxlis*, *didi saxlit*.

**Do not put the adjective in the plural.** Spanish *casas grandes* and French *grandes maisons* mark the plural twice. *\*Didebi saxlebi* does the same. Georgian marks it once: *didi saxlebi*.

**Do not change a vowel-stem adjective.** *\*Ṗaṭaras saxls* and *\*mc̣vanem* treat *ṗaṭara* and *mc̣vane* like nouns. Before a noun they are frozen: *ṗaṭara saxls*.

**Do not syncopate an adjective before a noun.** *\*C̣itli pankrit* borrows the standalone genitive. Before a noun: *c̣iteli pankrit*. Alone it does syncopate: *c̣itlit* "with the red one".

**Do not put the adjective after the noun.** French *une maison blanche* and Spanish *una casa blanca* lead to *\*saxli tetri*. In ordinary Georgian, adjective first: *tetri saxli*. The reversed order belongs to poetry and older texts.

**Do not confuse the bare adjective with a predicate.** *Did saxls vxedav* is "I see a big house". "I see that the house is big" needs a separate clause (lesson 6-2); "the house is big" is *saxli didia*.

**Do not treat a possessive like an adjective in the dative.** *\*Čem saxls* follows the adjective rule. Possessives keep *-s*: *čems saxls* (lesson 3-8).

## Connections

Japanese and Korean adjectives also come first and never take case: 大きい家に, 큰 집에. Georgian is halfway between them and Latin, where adjective and noun agree fully. Spanish and French agree in number and gender but not in case; Georgian agrees partly in case and never in number.

## Exercises

1. Put *axali c̣igni* into the dative, genitive and instrumental.
2. Say "in a small room", "for a young teacher" and "from a big city".
3. Say "I see the tall mountains."
4. Say "Nino is reading an interesting book."
5. Correct *\*Dids saxlši vcxovrob*, *\*Lamazebi q̇vavilebi* and *\*did kalakis cenṭri*.
6. Take apart *didi tetri saxlistvis* and translate it.
7. Translate *es kaba ʒviria, is ki iapi* and say why *ʒviri* has the shape it has.
8. "The red one is mine": say it with *c̣iteli* standing alone, then say "with the red one".

## Worked answers

1. Dative *axal c̣igns*: the adjective is a bare stem. Genitive *axali c̣ignis* and instrumental *axali c̣ignit*: the adjective keeps *-i*.
2. *Ṗaṭara otaxši*: a vowel-stem adjective never changes. *Axalgazrda masc̣avleblistvis*: *axalgazrda* is a vowel stem too; *masc̣avlebeli* syncopates in the genitive. *Didi kalakidan*: *-dan* is on the instrumental, so *didi* keeps *-i*.
3. *Maɣal mtebs vxedav.* The plural object is dative; the adjective is singular and bare.
4. *Nino saintereso c̣igns ḳitxulobs.* *Saintereso* ends in a vowel and does not change.
5. A dative-based form takes the bare adjective: *did saxlši vcxovrob*. The adjective is singular before a plural noun: *lamazi q̇vavilebi*. Before a genitive noun the adjective keeps *-i*: *didi kalakis cenṭri*.
6. *didi* "big", *tetri* "white" (both keeping *-i* before a genitive), *saxl-is-tvis* "house, genitive, for": "for a big white house".
7. "This dress is expensive, and that one cheap." *Ʒviri* is a predicate, nominative with the copula *-a*; *iapi* shares the copula. Neither stands before a noun, so both are full nominatives.
8. *C̣iteli čemia*: alone, as the subject, the full nominative. *C̣itlit*: alone, the instrumental is declined like a noun, and *c̣iteli* syncopates there (Bolkvadze and Kiziria's table for *c̣iteli*).
