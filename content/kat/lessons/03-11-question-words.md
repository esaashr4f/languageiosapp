## The idea

Lesson 2-8 made yes-no questions out of statements. This lesson adds the other kind: questions with a **question word**, such as "who", "what", "where". Georgian builds them the way Japanese and Korean do: the question word stands where the answer would stand, in the same case, and nothing else changes. "Nino sees *Giorgi*" (*giorgis xedavs*) becomes "Whom does Nino see?" (*vis xedavs?*): *vis* is dative because *giorgis* is. Bolkvadze and Kiziria put it this way: the case of *vin* or *vis* is the case of the noun it stands for in the answer.

There is one rule of position. Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that the question word usually opens the sentence and that the verb follows it directly: *sad cxovrob?* "where do you live?", *ras aḳeteb?* "what are you doing?". They place the sentence stress on the first syllable of the question word. Hewitt describes the pitch differently, with a high fall on the verb's stressed syllable; listen for both.

The second half of the lesson is the words for "someone", "nobody" and the like. Here too Georgian is systematic. Bolkvadze and Kiziria show that they are made from the question words with small additions:

- *-me*: *vinme* "anyone, someone", *rame* "anything, something", *sadme* "anywhere, somewhere".
- *-ɣac*: *viɣac* "someone", *raɣac* "something", *sadɣac* "somewhere".
- a negative in front: *aravin* "nobody", *araperi* "nothing", *arsad* "nowhere", *arasdros* "never".

So one question word gives you a whole family. Once you know *vin*, *ra* and *sad*, you can build more than a dozen words.

## How it is built

### The question words

- **ვინ *vin* "who"**: for people. Nominative and ergative *vin*, dative and genitive *vis* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria). With postpositions: *vistan* "with whom, at whose place", *visze* "about whom", *vistvis* "for whom", *visgan* "from whom". *Visi* "whose" is an adjective-like word before a noun: *visi c̣igni?* "whose book?".
- **რა *ra* "what"**: for things and animals. Ergative *ram*, dative *ras*, genitive *ris*, instrumental *rit*, adverbial *rad*. With postpositions: *raze* "about what", *raši* "in what", *ristvis* "what for", *risgan* "out of what", *ridan* "from what". Before a noun, *ra* does not change: *ra c̣igni?*, *ra c̣igns?* "what book?".
- **სად *sad* "where"**, **საიდან *saidan* "where from"**, **სადამდე *sadamde* "how far"**.
- **როდის *rodis* "when"**, **რატომ *raṭom* "why"**, **როგორ *rogor* "how"**.
- **რამდენი *ramdeni* "how many, how much"**: with a singular noun, like any quantifier (lesson 2-4): *ramdeni bavšvi?*.
- **რომელი *romeli* "which"** and **როგორი *rogori* "what kind of"**: like adjectives before a noun (lesson 3-10): *romeli saxli?*, *romel saxlši?* "in which house?".

**Building a question.**

1. Start from the answer: *nino biblioteḳaši mušaobs* "Nino works in the library".
2. Replace the part you are asking about: the place, *biblioteḳaši*, becomes *sad*.
3. Move the question word to the front, before the verb: *sad mušaobs nino?*

### The indefinites

- **-მე *-me*** is the open "any": you do not know or care whether such a person or thing exists. It is typical of questions and requests: *vinme ak aris?* "is anyone here?"; *rame* "anything".
- **-ღაც *-ɣac*** is the specific "some": there is such a person or thing, but you cannot or do not name it: *viɣac ezošia* "someone is in the yard".

Bolkvadze and Kiziria illustrate the pair with a question and its answer: the question asks with *vinme* whether anyone has come in; the answer says with *viɣac ḳaci* that some man has. The *-me* asks; the *-ɣac* reports.

**The negatives.** *Aravin* "nobody", *araperi* "nothing", *arsad* "nowhere", *arasdros* "never". With inability, *ver-*: *veravin* "nobody (can)", *veraperi* "nothing (can)". Bolkvadze and Kiziria give both *aravin ar ...* and *aravin ...* without a second *ar*: Georgian allows a double negative, and in everyday speech it is common. *Araperi* is a consonant stem that syncopates: dative *arapers*, genitive *arapris*. *Aravin* has the dative and genitive *aravis*.

**Everything, everywhere.** *Q̇velaperi* "everything", *q̇velgan* "everywhere"; *q̇vela* "all, everybody" you know from lesson 2-4.

## Where it applies

**Question words**

- **Who, whom, whose:** *vin* as subject; *vis* as object and with postpositions; *visi* "whose": *visi c̣ignia?* "whose book is it?".
- **What:** *ra* as subject or with "be" (*es ra aris?*); *ras* as object; *rit* "with what" (*rit c̣er?* "what are you writing with?"); *raze* "about what" (*raze laṗaraḳobt?*).
- **Where, where from, how far:** *sad*, *saidan*, *sadamde*.
- **When, why, how:** *rodis*, *raṭom*, *rogor*. *Rogor xar?* "how are you?" is the everyday greeting.
- **How many, which, what kind:** *ramdeni*, *romeli*, *rogori*, each before a singular noun.
- **What for:** *ristvis*, the genitive of *ra* with *-tvis*.
- **In exclamations:** Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that *rogori* also exclaims: *rogori ḳargi amindia!* "what lovely weather!".
- **Several at once:** *vin da vin* "who (all)", *ra da ra* "what (all)", for a list in the answer.

**Indefinites and negatives**

- **In questions and requests:** *-me*: *vinme*, *rame*, *sadme*, *odesme* "ever".
- **In statements about someone or something real but unnamed:** *-ɣac*: *viɣac*, *raɣac*, *sadɣac*.
- **Before a noun:** *viɣac ḳaci* "some man", *raɣac c̣igni* "some book".
- **Negatives:** *aravin*, *araperi*, *arsad*, *arasdros*, with or without *ar* on the verb; *veravin*, *veraperi* with *ver*.
- **Everything:** *q̇velaperi*, *q̇velgan*, *q̇vela*.

## Watch out

**Do not leave the question word in its English case.** "Who" is not always *vin*. As the object of a present verb it is dative: *\*Vin xedavs nino?* means "who sees Nino?". For "whom does Nino see?", *vis xedavs nino?*

**Do not separate the question word from the verb.** Spanish *¿Dónde trabaja Nino?* puts the subject after the verb, and Georgian likes this too. What it avoids is *\*sad nino mušaobs?* with the subject wedged between, in a neutral question; say *sad mušaobs nino?* Bolkvadze and Kiziria's rule is that the question word is followed by the verb.

**Do not make the verb plural for vin.** "Who (all) is coming?" may expect several people, but *vin* takes a singular verb (Bolkvadze and Kiziria): *vin laṗaraḳobs?*

**Do not put the noun after ramdeni in the plural.** *\*Ramdeni bavšvebi* copies English "how many children". Say *ramdeni bavšvi*.

**Do not use -ɣac in an open question.** *Viɣac ak aris?* asks whether a particular unnamed person is here; to ask "is anybody here?", use *vinme ak aris?*

**Do not use -me when you are reporting a real person.** *Vinme ezošia* sounds like "anyone at all is in the yard". If you saw someone, say *viɣac ezošia*.

**Do not drop ar with ver-words in the wrong way.** *Veravin* goes with *ver*, not *ar*: *veravin ver c̣ers* "nobody can write (it)", or *veravin c̣ers*. *\*Veravin ar c̣ers* mixes the two kinds of "not".

**Do not translate "nothing" with ar alone.** *\*Ar vxedav* is "I don't see (it)". "I see nothing" needs *araperi*: *arapers vxedav*, or *arapers ar vxedav*. The object case of *araperi* is the dative *arapers*.

**Do not confuse romeli and rogori.** *Romeli?* asks "which one, of a known set?"; *rogori?* asks "what is it like?": *rogori saxlia?* "what kind of house is it?".

## Connections

Japanese builds the same families from question words: 誰 "who", 誰か "someone", 誰も "nobody"; 何, 何か, 何も. Korean does it with 누구, 누군가, 아무도. Georgian *-me* and *-ɣac* split the "some" more finely than Japanese か does, rather as Spanish *alguien* and Russian *кто-нибудь* against *кто-то*. Spanish also allows the double negative of *aravin ar*: *no viene nadie*.

## Exercises

1. Ask "Who is this woman?", "Where is the station?" and "When is the concert?"
2. Ask "Whom is Giorgi talking with?" and "What are they talking about?"
3. Ask "How many books are on the table?" and "Which book is yours?"
4. Turn into questions about the underlined part (here in capitals): *nino BIBLIOTEḲAŠI mušaobs*; *bavšvebi BURTIT tamašoben*; *GIORGI ḳitxulobs*.
5. Say "Someone is at the door" and "Is anyone at home?"
6. Say "There is nothing in the box" and "Nobody lives here."
7. Correct *\*Vin xedav?* (meaning "whom do you see?"), *\*Ramdeni bavšvebi ak arian?* and *\*Sad nino cxovrobs?* (as a neutral question).
8. Explain the difference between *rame* and *raɣac*.

## Worked answers

1. *Vin aris es kali?* *Sad aris sadguri?* *Rodis aris ḳoncerṭi?* In each, the question word opens and *aris* follows it.
2. *Vistan laṗaraḳobs giorgi?*: the answer would be *megobartan*, so the question word takes *-tan* on its dative *vis*. *Raze laṗaraḳoben?*: the answer would be *pilmze*; *ra* takes *-ze*.
3. *Magidaze ramdeni c̣ignia?* (or *ramdeni c̣ignia magidaze?*): singular noun and verb after *ramdeni*. *Romeli c̣ignia šeni?*: *romeli* before the noun, the copula on *c̣igni*.
4. *Sad mušaobs nino?* *Rit tamašoben bavšvebi?*: the instrumental *burtit* becomes *rit*. *Vin ḳitxulobs?*: the nominative subject becomes *vin*.
5. *Viɣac ḳartan aris*: a real but unnamed person, so *-ɣac*; *ḳari* is a consonant stem, so *-tan* replaces the dative *-s*. *Vinme saxlšia?*: an open question, so *-me*.
6. *Q̇utši araperi ar aris* (or *araperia*). *Ak aravin (ar) cxovrobs.* Both negatives can take *ar* on the verb or not.
7. "Whom" as the object is dative: *vis xedav?* After *ramdeni* the noun is singular, and so is the verb: *ramdeni bavšvia ak?* The question word wants the verb right after it: *sad cxovrobs nino?*
8. *Rame* is open: "anything at all, if there is something". *Raɣac* is specific: "there is something, I am not saying or do not know what". You ask with *rame* and report with *raɣac*.
