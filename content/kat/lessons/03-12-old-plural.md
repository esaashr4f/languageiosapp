## The idea

Georgian has a second plural. Beside *ḳacebi* "men" there is *ḳacni*; beside *ḳacebis* "of the men" there is *ḳacta*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria call it the **archaic plural** and say it is still used in modern Georgian; Hewitt describes it as surviving in higher, archaising styles and in some set expressions. This guide calls it the **old plural**. You will rarely need to say it, but you will read it constantly: in titles, official names, the press, the law, and every older text in part 8.

The most famous example is in the name of a document you will read in lesson 8-2. The Georgian title of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights is *adamianta uplebebis saq̇oveltao deḳlaracia*. *Adamianta* is the old plural of *adamiani* "human being": "of humans". The very next word, *uplebebis* "of rights", uses the ordinary plural. Modern formal Georgian mixes the two freely.

The old plural is built differently from the *-eb-* plural, and the difference is the reason it is easy to recognise. Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe two markers:

- **-ნ- *-n-*** marks the plural in the nominative and vocative, with the ordinary case ending after it: *ḳac-n-i*, *ḳac-n-o*.
- **-თა *-ta*** marks the plural and the case at once in the ergative, dative and genitive: *ḳac-ta*. One ending for three cases.

There is no old-plural instrumental or adverbial: Bolkvadze and Kiziria's tables and Wiktionary's leave those cells empty. For them, use *-eb-*.

You have met both markers already, without being told. The pronoun *isini* "they" is, in Bolkvadze and Kiziria's analysis, *isi-n-i*: the *-n-* plural (also *ese-n-i*, *ege-n-i*). And *mat* "them" contains the *-t* plural: they identify the *-t* of *mati* "their" as a plural marker. The pronouns kept the old plural when the nouns moved on to *-eb-*.

## How it is built

**Start from the full stem.** The old plural does not truncate and does not syncopate: *deda* → *dedani*, *dedata* (Wiktionary); *megobari* → *megobarni*, *megobarta*; Bolkvadze and Kiziria's *aṭami* "peach" has *aṭamni*, *aṭamta*, against the ordinary *aṭmebi*. So the old plural often looks longer, and closer to the dictionary form, than the *-eb-* plural.

**Add the ending.**

- nominative *-ni*: *ḳacni*, *dedani*, *xeni*, *uplebani* "rights"
- ergative, dative, genitive *-ta* (Wiktionary prints *-t(a)*: the *-a* may be left off): *ḳacta*, *dedata*, *uplebata*
- vocative *-no*: *ḳacno*, *megobarno*.

**Words before the noun agree fully.** This is the other mark of the old plural. With *-eb-* an adjective stays singular (lesson 3-10); with the old plural it may take the old plural too. Bolkvadze and Kiziria give *maɣalni mtani* "high mountains" and say that with the archaic plural agreement is always complete in case and number; their table for *didi sopeli* gives *didni sopelni*, with no syncope in the old plural, and *didta sopelta* in the other cases. They also give *didi sopelni*, with the adjective singular: both are found.

**Postpositions.** The separate postpositions of lesson 5-1 follow *-ta* as they follow a genitive or dative: *erta šoris* "between nations, among nations" (UDHR, Preamble).

**Reading a form.** When you meet a noun in *-ni* or *-ta*:

1. Take off *-ni* or *-ta*. *Uplebata* → *upleba-*.
2. Is what is left a noun stem? *Upleba* "right": yes.
3. *-ni* means nominative plural (or the subject of a present verb); *-ta* means ergative, dative or genitive plural, and the sentence tells you which. Before another noun, *-ta* is almost always genitive.

## Where it applies

- **Titles and official names:** the UDHR's *adamianta uplebebis ... deḳlaracia*; names of organisations, laws and books often use *-ta*.
- **Legal and formal prose:** the UDHR uses *uplebata* "of rights" and *tavisuplebata* "of freedoms" again and again, alongside *uplebebis*.
- **Speeches and letters:** the vocative *-no*: *megobarno*, *kartvelno* "Georgians!".
- **Poetry and older literature:** the readings of lessons 8-4 to 8-7 use the old plural freely, with full agreement.
- **Set expressions:** phrases fixed in the old form (Hewitt's "set expressions").
- **Pronouns, always:** *isini*, *eseni*, *egeni* (*-n-*) and *mat*, *amat*, *magat* (*-t*) are the old plural in everyday use.
- **Not in the instrumental or adverbial:** there are no such forms; use *-eb-it*, *-eb-ad*.
- **Not in everyday speech:** *ḳacni* for "men" in conversation sounds bookish. Use *ḳacebi*.

## Watch out

**Do not syncopate or truncate the old plural.** By analogy with *megobrebi* and *dedebi* you might write *\*megobrni* or *\*dedni*. The old plural keeps the full stem: *megobarni*, *dedani*.

**Do not read -ta as a dative or genitive singular.** *Erta* is not "of a nation": the singular genitive is *eris*. *-Ta* is always plural.

**Do not confuse -ta with the instrumental.** *Saxlta* (old plural genitive) and *saxlit* (instrumental singular) look alike in transliteration. The instrumental has *-it*; the old plural *-ta* (or *-t*) straight after the stem.

**Do not confuse -ni with the nominative of a stem in n.** *Ḳani* "skin" and *cxeni* "horse" end in *-ni* because their stems end in *-n*. Check whether removing *-ni* leaves a real noun: *cxe-* does not, so *cxeni* is singular. Its old plural is *cxenni*.

**Do not build an old-plural instrumental.** *\*Ḳactit* does not exist. Use *ḳacebit*.

**Do not mix the two plurals inside one word.** *\*Ḳacebni* and *\*ḳacebta* stack both markers. A word takes one plural or the other; only different words in a phrase may mix (*adamianta uplebebis*).

**Do not use the old plural to sound polite.** It sounds formal or literary, not courteous. Politeness comes from *tkven*, *baṭono* and the polite verbs of lesson 7-6.

## Connections

English has fossils of an old plural too (*oxen*, *children*), but in English each word has only one plural; Georgian keeps both side by side for every noun, with a difference of style. Full agreement in the old plural is closer to Latin or Spanish (*altos montes*) than modern Georgian's singular adjective is.

## Exercises

1. Give the old plural nominative and genitive of *sṭudenṭi*, *ena* and *mezobeli*.
2. Translate *dedata da švilta upleba* and say which words are old plurals. (Treat *upleba* as "right".)
3. Read *uplebani* and *uplebata*: what case and number is each?
4. Rewrite *bavšvebis uplebebi* in the formal style with both nouns in the old plural.
5. Correct *\*megobrni*, *\*dedni* and *\*ḳactit*.
6. Is *cxeni* a plural? How do you know?
7. Why does *isini* end in *-ni*?
8. Give the everyday equivalent of *ʒvirpaso megobarno* and *adamianta uplebebi*.

## Worked answers

1. *Sṭudenṭni*, *sṭudenṭta*. *Enani*, *enata*: no truncation. *Mezobelni*, *mezobelta*: no syncope (compare *mezoblebi*).
2. "The right of mothers and children." *Dedata* and *švilta* are old plural genitives; *upleba* is an ordinary singular nominative.
3. *Uplebani*: nominative plural. *Uplebata*: ergative, dative or genitive plural; before a noun it would be genitive.
4. *Bavšvta uplebani*. *Bavšvta* is the old genitive plural; *uplebani* the old nominative plural, from the full stem *upleba*.
5. The old plural keeps the full stem: *megobarni*, *dedani*. There is no old-plural instrumental: *ḳacebit*.
6. No. Take off *-ni* and you get *cxe-*, which is not a noun. *Cxeni* "horse" is a singular whose stem ends in *-n*. Its *-eb-* plural is *cxenebi*.
7. Bolkvadze and Kiziria analyse it as *isi-n-i*: the old plural marker *-n-* plus the nominative *-i*. The pronouns kept the old plural.
8. *Ʒvirpaso megobrebo* (lesson 3-7) and *adamianebis uplebebi* (or *adamianis uplebebi* "human rights", with the singular genitive).
