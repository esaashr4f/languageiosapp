## Before you start

This review covers part 3: the seven cases as one system (lesson 3-1); the postpositions on the dative, *-ši*, *-ze*, *-tan*, *-vit* (3-2); the genitive with *-tvis*, *-gan*, *-ḳen* (3-3); the instrumental and adverbial with *-dan* and *-mde* (3-4); vowel stems and truncation (3-5); syncope (3-6); the vocative (3-7); pronouns and possessives (3-8); the pointing words *es*, *eg*, *is* and their oblique forms (3-9); adjectives before nouns (3-10); question words and indefinites (3-11); and the old plural (3-12). It also needs part 2: present verbs, nominative subjects, dative objects, negation and word order.

Verbs stay in the present. Work each exercise before reading its answer; every answer names the lesson to reread.

## Passage

**A. Giorgi's family.** Constructed, using only words and grammar from parts 1 to 3. Mkhedruli first, then the transliteration.

1. ჩემი სახელი გიორგია. მცხეთაში ვცხოვრობ, თბილისთან ახლოს.
   *čemi saxeli giorgia. mcxetaši vcxovrob, tbilistan axlos.*
2. ჩვენი სახლი ძველია, მაგრამ ლამაზი. ეზოში მაღალი ხეებია.
   *čveni saxli ʒvelia, magram lamazi. ezoši maɣali xeebia.*
3. მამაჩემი ექიმად მუშაობს, დედაჩემი კი სოფლის სკოლაში მასწავლებელია.
   *mamačemi ekimad mušaobs, dedačemi ḳi soplis sḳolaši masc̣avlebelia.*
4. ჩემი ძმა თბილისის უნივერსიტეტში სწავლობს და დილიდან საღამომდე ბიბლიოთეკაშია.
   *čemi ʒma tbilisis universiṭeṭši sc̣avlobs da dilidan saɣamomde biblioteḳašia.*
5. იქ თავის მეგობართან ერთად ცხოვრობს, პატარა ბინაში.
   *ik tavis megobartan ertad cxovrobs, ṗaṭara binaši.*
6. საღამოს ტელეფონით ვლაპარაკობთ ფილმებსა და წიგნებზე.
   *saɣamos ṭeleponit vlaṗaraḳobt pilmebsa da c̣ignebze.*
7. — გიორგი, როგორ ხარ? — კარგად, მადლობა. შენ?
   *— giorgi, rogor xar? — ḳargad, madloba. šen?*
8. ზაფხულში მთაში ვართ, ბებიასა და ბაბუასთან.
   *zapxulši mtaši vart, bebiasa da babuastan.*
9. იქ არავინ არ ლაპარაკობს ფეხბურთზე: ყველა ამინდზე ლაპარაკობს!
   *ik aravin ar laṗaraḳobs pexburtze: q̇vela amindze laṗaraḳobs!*

**B. A proverb.** Attested: Wiktionary, entry დრო ყველაფრის მკურნალია, which translates it "time heals all wounds" and literally "time is a healer of everything".

დრო ყველაფრის მკურნალია.
*dro q̇velapris mḳurnalia.*

New word, glossed here only: *mḳurnali* "healer", a consonant stem in the nominative.

## Exercises

1. Translate sentences 1 and 2 of passage A.
2. Translate sentences 3 and 4.
3. Translate sentences 5 and 6.
4. Translate sentences 7 to 9.
5. Translate passage B word for word and then naturally.
6. In sentence 1, explain *tbilistan axlos*: which case, which postposition, and why *axlos* needs it.
7. In sentence 2, why is it *maɣali xeebia* with a singular adjective and a singular copula?
8. In sentence 3, explain *ekimad* and *soplis*. Why is *soplis* shorter than *sopeli*?
9. In sentence 4, explain *dilidan* and *saɣamomde*, including the vowels before *-dan* and *-mde*.
10. In sentence 5, why *tavis* and not *mis*? Why *ṗaṭara binaši* and not *\*ṗaṭaras binaši*?
11. In sentence 6, why does only *c̣ignebze* carry the postposition, and what is *pilmebsa*?
12. In sentence 7, why does *giorgi* have no vocative ending?
13. In sentence 8, explain *bebiasa da babuastan*. Why does *babua* keep its *-a* before *-tan*?
14. In sentence 9, explain *aravin ar*. Could *ar* be left out?
15. In passage B, explain *q̇velapris*: its stem, its case and its vowel loss.
16. Say "This is my friend's book; that (far) book is mine."
17. Say "Whom do you live with, and where?" to a friend, then politely to a stranger.
18. Say "In this big city there are a lot of old houses."
19. Say "Someone is talking about you (one person) on the phone."
20. Say "Dear colleagues, thank you!" first in everyday style, then with the old plural.
21. Correct *\*Es kalakši cxovrobs čem megobari*.
22. Correct *\*Ninoo, ra ḳaradaši aris?* (meaning "Nino, what is in the wardrobe?").
23. Correct *\*Bavšvebi c̣itlebi pankrebit xaṭaven*.
24. Correct *\*Giorgi mis ʒmastan cxovrobs* (meaning his own brother) and *\*Me tbilisisgan var*.

## Worked answers

1. "My name is Giorgi. I live in Mtskheta, near Tbilisi." *Čemi* "my" before *saxeli* (lesson 3-8); *giorgia* is the name with the short copula. *Mcxetaši*: *mcxeta* + *-ši*, the *-a* kept before a dative postposition (lessons 3-2, 3-5). "Our house is old, but beautiful. In the yard there are tall trees." *Čveni* (lesson 3-8); one copula serves two predicates, as in the part 2 review (lesson 2-10).
2. "My father works as a doctor, and my mother is a teacher at the village school." *Mamačemi*, *dedačemi* are the fused possessive forms (lesson 3-8); *ḳi* "and, but" contrasts the two. "My brother studies at Tbilisi University and is in the library from morning till evening."
3. "He lives there together with his (own) friend, in a small flat." "In the evening we talk on the phone about films and books." *Ṭeleponit* is the instrumental of means (lesson 3-4); *-ze* "about" (lesson 3-2).
4. "'Giorgi, how are you?' 'Fine, thanks. And you?'" "In summer we are in the mountains, at Grandma and Grandpa's." "There nobody talks about football: everyone talks about the weather!"
5. Word for word: "time everything-of healer-is". Naturally: "Time heals all wounds." *Dro* is the subject; *q̇velapris* the genitive that describes *mḳurnali*, standing before it (lesson 3-3); *mḳurnalia* is *mḳurnali* + the copula *-a*.
6. *Tbilisi* is a consonant stem; its dative *tbiliss* loses *-s* before *-tan* (lesson 3-2). *Axlos* "near" names nearness; *-tan* names what you are near. The pair is the usual way to say "close to" (lesson 3-2).
7. The adjective before a plural noun stays singular, and in the nominative it has its *-i* (lesson 3-10). The copula is singular because trees are things (lesson 2-4).
8. *Ekimad* is the adverbial of role, "as a doctor" (lesson 3-4). *Soplis* is the genitive of *sopeli* "village", describing *sḳolaši*: "the village's school" (lesson 3-3). *Sopeli* has the syncope shape *-el*, and the genitive is a syncope case (lesson 3-6).
9. *Dil-i-dan*: *-dan* is built on the instrumental *dilit*, whose *-it* leaves *-i-* (lesson 3-4); *dila* truncates as in the instrumental (lesson 3-5). *Saɣamo-mde*: *-mde* is built on the adverbial *saɣamod*, which loses its *-d* (lesson 3-4). An *-o* stem never truncates (lesson 3-5).
10. The owner is the subject of the clause, the brother himself, so the reflexive possessive *tavisi* (lesson 3-8); before the dative-based *megobartan* it is *tavis*. *Ṗaṭara* is a vowel-stem adjective and never changes before a noun (lesson 3-10).
11. When two nouns share a postposition, only the last carries it, and the first takes the long dative *-sa* (lesson 3-2): *pilm-eb-sa* is plural plus dative in its long form (lesson 3-1).
12. Given names take no vocative ending (lesson 3-7). *Giorgi* is said bare.
13. Again two nouns share *-tan*: *bebia* stands in the long dative *bebiasa*, and *babua* takes the postposition. A vowel stem keeps its *-s* before *-tan*, so *babuastan*, and it never truncates before a dative postposition (lessons 3-2, 3-5).
14. *Aravin* "nobody" with *ar* on the verb is a double negative, common and correct; Bolkvadze and Kiziria give it beside the form without *ar*, so *ik aravin laṗaraḳobs pexburtze* is also right (lesson 3-11).
15. *Q̇velaperi* "everything" (lesson 3-11) is a consonant stem ending in *-er*; in the genitive it syncopates: *q̇velapr-is* (lesson 3-6). The genitive comes before the noun it describes (lesson 3-3).
16. *Es čemi megobris c̣ignia; is c̣igni čemia.* Before a genitive noun, *čemi* (lesson 3-8); *is* for something far (lesson 3-9); *čemia* is the possessive as a predicate (lesson 3-8).
17. To a friend: *vistan cxovrob da sad?* To a stranger, with *tkven* and its plural verb: *vistan cxovrobt da sad?* *Vis* + *-tan*, as the answer would be *megobartan* (lesson 3-11).
18. *Am did kalakši bevri ʒveli saxlia.* *Am*, oblique of *es*, and *did*, bare adjective, before the dative-based *kalakši* (lessons 3-9, 3-10); *bevri* takes a singular noun and verb (lesson 2-4).
19. *Viɣac šenze laṗaraḳobs ṭeleponit.* A real but unnamed person: *viɣac* (lesson 3-11). "About you": *šen-* + *-ze* (lesson 3-8). The phone is instrumental (lesson 3-4).
20. *Ʒvirpaso ḳolegebo, gmadlobt!* (lesson 3-7). With the old plural, which keeps the full stem: *ḳolega-n-o*, so *ʒvirpaso ḳolegano, gmadlobt!* (lesson 3-12).
21. Outside the nominative, *es* becomes *am*; a possessive keeps *-s* only before a dative noun, and *megobari* here is the nominative subject, so *čemi*: *čemi megobari am kalakši cxovrobs* (lessons 3-8, 3-9; word order, lesson 2-9).
22. A given name takes no vocative ending: *nino*. The question word comes first and the verb right after it: *nino, ra aris ḳaradaši?* (lessons 3-7, 3-11).
23. Before a noun an adjective is singular and, before an instrumental noun, keeps *-i* without syncope: *c̣iteli*. The children use pencils: *bavšvebi c̣iteli pankrebit xaṭaven* (lessons 3-6, 3-10).
24. For the subject's own brother, *tavisi*: *giorgi tavis ʒmastan cxovrobs* (lesson 3-8). "From" a place is *-dan*: *me tbilisidan var*; *-gan* is "from" a person (lessons 3-3, 3-4).
