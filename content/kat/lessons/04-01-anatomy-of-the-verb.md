## The idea

Part 3 was about nouns. Part 4 is about the verb, and the verb is where Georgian keeps most of its grammar. Lesson 0-2 called it the sentence: one verb can name the doer, the person acted on, the direction, whom it is done for, and when.

It can carry all of that because a Georgian verb is built in a fixed order. Think of a row of boxes. The root sits in the middle. A few boxes stand in front of it and a few behind. Each box holds one kind of meaning, and each kind of meaning always goes in the same box. This row of boxes is the **verb template**: the fixed order of slots in which the pieces of a verb are placed.

You already know four of the boxes. In part 2 you met the subject prefix *v-* ("I", "we"), the root (*c̣er-* "write"), the thematic suffix (*-av-* in *xaṭavs*) and the person suffixes (*-s*, *-en*, and *-t* for the plural). In *ašenebs* "builds" you also met a vowel in front of the root, *a-*. This lesson adds the remaining boxes and shows the whole row once, so that every later lesson in part 4 is "fill this box".

Why learn the frame before the forms? Because Georgian verbs look frightening as wholes and simple as pieces. A long form like *davxaṭavdit* "we would draw" is five familiar pieces in a row, and the row never changes order. Once you know which box comes where, you can take any verb apart from the outside in, and build one from the inside out.

## How it is built

Here are the slots, from left to right. Bolkvadze and Kiziria fix the two ends: preverbs come in front of every other part of the verb, and the first- and second-person prefixes are preceded only by preverbs. The order of the rest is the order you see in every form in Wiktionary's tables.

1. **Preverb.** A prefix like *da-*, *ga-*, *mi-*, *mo-*. It adds a direction ("out", "down", "away", "here") and, on most verbs, turns the present into the future. Lesson 4-6.
2. **Person prefix.** One slot, holding at most one marker: the subject *v-* or an object marker ("me", "you", "us"). Lessons 4-3 and 4-4.
3. **Version vowel.** One vowel, *a-*, *i-*, *u-* or *e-*, saying whom the action is for or which way it is turned. Lesson 4-21; it also marks some intransitive verbs (lesson 4-10).
4. **Root.** The core meaning: *c̣er* "write", *xaṭ* "draw", *šen* "build".
5. **Stem suffix.** On some verbs a suffix that changes the voice: *-d-* for some "happening" verbs (lesson 4-10), *-in-* for "make someone do" (part 7).
6. **Thematic suffix.** *-eb*, *-ob*, *-av*, *-am*, *-i*, or none. It builds the present and future stem and disappears in some other tenses. Lesson 4-2.
7. **Tense marker.** For example *-d-* plus a vowel in "was doing" (lesson 4-5), or *-e* in the simple past (lesson 4-12).
8. **Person suffix.** *-s*, *-en*, *-a* and others: the subject in the third person, sometimes fused with the tense ending.
9. **Plural -t.** Last of all: *-t* for "we" or "you" plural.

No verb fills every slot. Most forms have four or five pieces. What matters is that the pieces never swap places: the preverb is always first, the plural *-t* always last, the root always between the prefixes and the suffixes.

**Only two person slots.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria point out that a Georgian verb can show several persons, but it has room for only two person markers: one in front of the stem, one behind it. A verb may concern three people (I write a letter to a friend) and still show only two of them. Lesson 4-4 shows what happens when two persons compete for the one front slot.

**Taking a form apart.** Work from the edges inwards:

1. Is there a preverb at the very front? *Da-*, *ga-*, *mo-* and the rest are a small, closed set (lesson 4-6).
2. Next: a person prefix? *V-* means "I" or "we".
3. Next: a single vowel before the root? That is the version vowel.
4. From the other end: is the last letter *-t*? Then the subject is "we" or "you" plural.
5. Before that: a person suffix such as *-s* or *-en*.
6. Before that: a tense marker, then a thematic suffix.
7. What is left in the middle is the root.

Try it on *vašenebt*. *V-* "I/we"; *a-* a version vowel; *šen* the root; *-eb* the thematic suffix; *-t* plural. So: "we build".

**Building a form.** Go the other way: start at the root and add outwards. "They are building": root *šen*; the vowel *a-* that this verb always has in front of it; the thematic suffix *-eb*; third person plural *-en*: *a-šen-eb-en*.

## Where it applies

- **Every verb.** The template is the same for transitive verbs (*c̣ers*), for verbs of activity (*mušaobs*), for verbs of feeling (lesson 4-18) and for the verbs that change their stem (lesson 4-25). Some slots are empty; the order holds.
- **Every tense.** The present uses slots 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 9. The future adds slot 1 (lesson 4-6). The past tenses change slot 7 and often empty slot 6 (lessons 4-5, 4-12).
- **The copula.** *V-ar-t* "we are" is prefix, root, plural; *ar-i-s* has a vowel and the suffix. "Be" is irregular (lesson 4-25) but still fits the frame.
- **Reading a dictionary.** Dictionaries list a verb under one form, but you find the root by stripping slots (lesson 4-26).
- **Words made from verbs.** The verbal noun and the participles use the same root and sometimes the same preverb (*c̣era* "writing", *daɣlili* "tired"). Their own endings come later.

## Watch out

**Do not put the person prefix in front of the preverb.** In English and Spanish nothing comes before the subject, so *\*vdaxaṭav* feels natural. Bolkvadze and Kiziria are explicit: the person prefix stands after the preverb. "I will draw" is *da-v-xaṭav* (lesson 4-6).

**Do not put -t inside the word.** *\*Vxaṭtav* or *\*vxaṭavtdi* break the rule that the plural *-t* is the very last piece. It is *vxaṭavt*, *vxaṭavdit*.

**The thematic suffix is not the person.** In *xaṭavs* both *-av* and *-s* follow the root, so it is tempting to treat *-avs* as one ending. Only *-s* is the person. In other tenses *-av* can vanish while the person suffix stays (lesson 4-12).

**Not every first letter is a prefix.** In *vašeneb* the *a-* is not part of the root: the verbal noun is *šeneba*, without it. But in *ḳitxulobs* and *mušaobs* nothing stands before the root, and the first letter is the root's own. You cannot always tell from one form. The verbal noun in the vocabulary notes is the quickest check: *amzadebs*, masdar *mzadeba*; *alagebs*, masdar *lageba*.

**Only one person prefix.** Spanish *me lo das* stacks two clitics in front of the verb. Georgian has one front slot. *\*Vmxaṭav* for "I draw me" or *\*vgxaṭav* for "I draw you" put two prefixes in it, and Georgian forbids it (lesson 4-4).

**The root does not always look the same.** Some roots lose or change a vowel between tenses (*č̣r-i-s* "cuts", but past *gač̣ra* and *gavč̣eri*, Wiktionary), and a few verbs replace the root entirely (lesson 4-25). The slot stays; its filling changes.

## Connections

Japanese builds verbs in a fixed order too: *tabe-sase-rare-nakat-ta* is root, causative, passive, negative, past, always in that sequence. Georgian does the same thing, but on both sides of the root, and it puts the people into the frame as well, which Japanese never does. Korean is like Japanese here. Spanish keeps everything after the root (*escrib-ía-mos*), so the Georgian habit of putting "I" in front is the main thing to get used to.

## Exercises

1. Take apart *vamzadebt* and name the slot of each piece.
2. Take apart *alageben* and *ṭovebs* the same way.
3. Build "I am sewing" from the root *ḳer* and the thematic suffix *-av*.
4. Build "we are baking" from *acxobs*.
5. In *davxaṭavdit*, which piece is in slot 1, and which in slot 9?
6. Correct *\*vdaxaṭav* and say which rule it breaks.
7. Correct *\*vxaṭtav* for "we draw".
8. Why can *vmxaṭav* not mean "I draw me"?

## Worked answers

1. *v-a-mzad-eb-t*: *v-* person prefix (slot 2), *a-* version vowel (3), *mzad* root (4), *-eb* thematic suffix (6), *-t* plural (9). "We prepare."
2. *a-lag-eb-en*: version vowel, root *lag*, thematic suffix, third person plural *-en* (slot 8): "they tidy". *ṭov-eb-s*: root, thematic suffix, third person singular *-s*: "he/she leaves (it)".
3. Root *ḳer*, then *-av*; "I" is *v-* in slot 2: *vḳerav*.
4. Strip *-s* from *acxobs* to get the stem *acxob-* (version vowel *a-*, root *cx*, thematic suffix *-ob*). "We": *v-* in front, *-t* at the end: *vacxobt*.
5. Slot 1 holds the preverb *da-*; slot 9 holds the plural *-t*.
6. The person prefix must follow the preverb, never precede it: *davxaṭav* "I will draw".
7. The plural *-t* is always the last piece: *vxaṭavt*.
8. Slot 2 has room for one marker only. *V-* and *m-* would both need it. Lesson 4-4 shows how Georgian handles two persons that both want the front slot.
