## The idea

Since part 2 the glosses have marked a piece after the root as TS and told you to wait. This is the lesson. In *xaṭ-av-s* "draws", *a-šen-eb-s* "builds", *muša-ob-s* "works", *xsn-i-s* "opens", the piece between the root and the person suffix is the **thematic suffix**: a suffix that, added to the root, makes the stem of the present and the future.

Why does Georgian need such a thing? Because the verb has more than one stem. The present and future are built on one stem; the simple past (lesson 4-12) is built on another. The thematic suffix is what marks the first one. Compare, from Wiktionary:

- *v-xaṭ-av* "I draw" but *da-v-xaṭ-e* "I drew"
- *v-a-šen-eb* "I build" but *a-v-a-šen-e* "I built"
- *v-zrd-i* "I raise" but *ga-v-zard-e* "I raised"

In the present each has a thematic suffix; in the past it is gone. Bolkvadze and Kiziria call these suffixes "present/future stem formants" for exactly this reason, and they split transitive verbs into **two-stem verbs**, which have a thematic suffix and lose it in the past, and **one-stem verbs**, which have none and keep the same stem throughout: *v-c̣er* "I write", *da-v-c̣er-e* "I wrote".

The sources do not say where the thematic suffixes came from, and this guide does not guess. What is clear is their job in the system: a thematic suffix says "this is the present-future stem".

## How it is built

**The inventory.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria list these thematic suffixes for transitive verbs:

- **-eb**, the commonest: *a-šen-eb-s* "builds", *a-mzad-eb-s* "prepares", *ṭov-eb-s* "leaves".
- **-ob**: *grʒn-ob-s* "feels". Many verbs of activity have it too: *muša-ob-s*, *tamaš-ob-s*, *cxovr-ob-s*. A longer form **-ulob** appears in a handful of verbs: *ḳitx-ulob-s* "reads", *q̇id-ulob-s* "buys", *ṗo-ulob-s* "finds".
- **-av**: *xaṭ-av-s* "draws", *ḳer-av-s* "sews", *ḳl-av-s* "kills".
- **-am**: *sv-am-s* "drinks".
- **-i**: *xsn-i-s* "opens", *zrd-i-s* "raises", *č̣r-i-s* "cuts", *gzavn-i-s* "sends", *q̇id-i-s* "sells".
- **-em** and **-op**: one verb each, *c-em-s* "beats, publishes" and *q̇-op-s* "divides".
- **none**: *c̣er-s* "writes", *tx-ov-s* "asks for" (here *-ov* is part of the root, and it stays in the past: *motxova*), *ḳrep-s* "picks", *c̣ams* "eats".

**Finding the stem.** Every verb in this guide is listed under its "he, she" form. Remove the person suffix *-s* and you have the present stem: *gzavni-s* → *gzavni-*; *ṭovebs* → *ṭoveb-*. Then look at its end. If it is one of the suffixes above, that is (very probably) the thematic suffix, and the root is what is left before it. If you are unsure whether the end belongs to the root, the past form in the vocabulary notes settles it: what disappears in the past was the thematic suffix. *Xaṭavs* has the past *daxaṭa*: *-av* goes, so it was a thematic suffix. *Txovs* has the past *motxova*: *-ov* stays, so it belongs to the root.

**The present is root + thematic suffix + person.** Once you have the stem, the person markers of lesson 2-5 go on it without change: *v-* in front for "I" and "we", *-s* for "he, she", *-t* for the plural of the first and second persons.

**The one difference: "they".** With every thematic suffix but one, "they" is *-en*: *xaṭaven*, *ṭoveben*, *grʒnoben*, *c̣eren*. With the suffix *-i*, "they" is *-an*: *xsnian*, *zrdian*, *gzavnian*, *q̇idian*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria state this as the only exception among transitive verbs. You met it in part 2 with *xsnis* and *mɣeris*; now you know why it happens: it goes with the *-i*.

A chain of thought for "the traders are selling fruit":

1. The verb is *q̇idis* "sells". Remove *-s*: the stem is *q̇idi-*, thematic suffix *-i*, root *q̇id*.
2. The subject is "the traders", third person plural, people: a plural verb.
3. With *-i*, "they" is *-an*: *q̇idian*.
4. The object "fruit" is in the dative: *xils*.
5. *Vač̣rebi xils q̇idian.*

**One stem now, another later.** Several verbs change their thematic suffix outside the present, not only lose it. *Ḳitxulobs* "reads" has the future *c̣aiḳitxavs*; *q̇idulobs* "buys" has *iq̇idis*; the activity verbs in *-ob* switch to *-eb*: *tamašobs* → future *itamašebs* (Wiktionary). This is why the vocabulary notes give the future and past of each verb. Lessons 4-6 and 4-15 return to it.

## Where it applies

- **All present forms of two-stem verbs.** Every person of the present carries the suffix: *vxaṭav*, *xaṭav*, *xaṭavs*, *vxaṭavt*, *xaṭavt*, *xaṭaven*.
- **The imperfect and the present subjunctive.** They are built on the same stem: *vxaṭavdi* "I was drawing" (lesson 4-5).
- **The future group.** The future is preverb + present stem, so the thematic suffix stays: *davxaṭav* "I will draw" (lesson 4-6). For verbs whose suffix changes, the new suffix is there instead: *itamašebs*.
- **Verbs without an object.** Activity verbs (*mušaobs*, *cxovrobs*, *ceḳvavs*) have thematic suffixes as well, and *ṭiris*, *mɣeris* have *-i* with "they" *-an*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe that *-i* on *ṭiris* as a present marker rather than a stem formant; this guide glosses it TS for simplicity, as it has since part 2.
- **"Happening" verbs.** Verbs like "is written", "grows up" have *-eb* in the present and future (lesson 4-10).
- **Part of the perfect.** Some verbs bring the suffix back in the perfect: *dauxaṭavs* "has drawn" keeps *-av*, *aušenebia* "has built" keeps *-eb* (Wiktionary). Lesson 4-22.
- **Not the simple past.** In the aorist the thematic suffix of most verbs is gone (lesson 4-12).

## Watch out

**Do not keep the thematic suffix in the past.** By analogy with Spanish *escrib-ía*, where the stem carries over, you might build *\*daxaṭava* for "drew". The past is *daxaṭa*: the *-av* belongs to the present-future stem only.

**Do not use -en with an -i verb.** *\*Vač̣rebi xils q̇iden* and *\*zrden* are wrong. Verbs in *-i* make "they" with *-an*: *q̇idian*, *zrdian*.

**Do not use -an with any other verb.** The reverse error, *\*xaṭavan* or *\*ṭoveban*, comes from overlearning *xsnian*. Only *-i* verbs take *-an*.

**Do not mistake part of the root for a suffix.** *Txovs* "asks for" ends in *-ov-s*, which looks like *-ob* or *-av*, but *-ov* is root: the past keeps it, *motxova*. The past form in the notes is the test.

**"Buys" and "sells" share a root.** *Q̇idulobs* "buys" and *q̇idis* "sells" differ only in the suffix. Read the whole stem: *\*vač̣ari xils q̇idulobs* means the trader is buying, not selling.

**Do not add -s after the suffix in the second person.** The bare stem is "you": *xaṭav*, *ṭoveb*, *gzavni*. *\*Šen gzavnis* is the third person.

## Connections

Spanish has nothing like a thematic suffix that comes and goes, but Latin, which you will meet in this app, does something comparable: *vinc-ō* "I conquer" has a nasal in the present stem that is gone in the perfect *vīc-ī*. Japanese verbs show one stem in many forms (*kak-u*, *kak-i-masu*); Georgian instead keeps a special stem for the present and future and drops it elsewhere.

## Exercises

1. Find the root and thematic suffix of *ṭovebs*, *ḳlavs*, *gzavnis* and *grʒnobs*.
2. Give "they" for *ḳrepen*'s verb *ḳreps*, for *zrdis* and for *amzadebs*. Which one is different, and why?
3. Say "We are looking for the station."
4. Say "The children sell flowers at the market."
5. Is *-ov* in *txovs* a thematic suffix? How can you tell?
6. Correct *\*Isini ṗurs č̣rien* and *\*Ʒmebi c̣erilebs c̣erian*.
7. Say "Grandmother is washing the dishes and I am tidying the room."
8. Translate *Gogo vašls č̣ris, bič̣i ḳi č̣urč̣els recxavs.*

## Worked answers

1. *ṭov-eb-s*: root *ṭov*, suffix *-eb*. *ḳl-av-s*: root *ḳl*, suffix *-av* (the past *moḳla* has no *-av*). *gzavn-i-s*: root *gzavn*, suffix *-i*. *grʒn-ob-s*: root *grʒn*, suffix *-ob*.
2. *ḳrepen* (no suffix, so *-en*), *zrdian*, *amzadeben*. *Zrdian* is different: *zrdis* has the suffix *-i*, and only *-i* verbs take *-an*.
3. *Sadgurs veʒebt.* "We": *v-* ... *-t* on the stem *eʒeb-*. The object is in the dative.
4. *Bavšvebi bazarši q̇vavilebs q̇idian.* People in the plural take a plural verb; *q̇idis* has *-i*, so *-an*.
5. No. In the past it stays: *motxova*. A thematic suffix would disappear, as *-av* does in *daxaṭa*. So *-ov* is part of the root and *txovs* is a one-stem verb.
6. *Č̣ris* has *-i*, so "they" is *-an*: *isini ṗurs č̣rian*. *C̣ers* has no suffix, so *-en*: *ʒmebi c̣erilebs c̣eren*.
7. *Bebia č̣urč̣els recxavs, me ḳi otaxs valageb.* *Recx-av-s* takes *-s*; *v-a-lag-eb* is "I tidy", with the version vowel *a-* after *v-*.
8. "The girl is cutting an apple, and the boy is washing the dishes." *Č̣r-i-s* and *recx-av-s* are both third person singular presents; *ḳi* sets the boy against the girl.
