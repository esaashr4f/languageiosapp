## The idea

So far every object has been "him", "her", "it" or a noun: *ʒaɣls xedavs* "he sees the dog". You cannot yet say "he sees me". Lesson 2-6 promised that this needs a marker on the verb, and this lesson provides it.

Georgian marks the subject on the verb (lesson 2-5). It also marks the object, when the object is "me", "us" or "you". The **object markers** are prefixes in slot 2 of the template, the same slot as the subject's *v-*:

- *m-* "me"
- *gv-* "us"
- *g-* "you" (one person or several)
- nothing for "him, her, it, them"

So *xedavs* "he sees (him, her, it)", *mxedavs* "he sees me", *gxedavs* "he sees you", *gvxedavs* "he sees us". Bolkvadze and Kiziria give this exact set, with the third person as zero, and show it on *xaṭavs*: *m-xaṭav-s* "he paints me", *g-xaṭav-s* "he paints you", *gv-xaṭav-s* "he paints us".

Why is the third person blank? Because the verb has room for only two person markers (lesson 4-1), and the third person is the default. A verb with no object marker is understood to have a third-person object, or none. Only "me", "us" and "you" need saying.

Notice what this does to the pronouns. *Me*, *šen*, *čven* and *tkven* do not change form for case (lesson 3-8): *me* is "I" and "me". In a sentence like *is me xedavs*, the pronoun alone could not show who sees whom. The verb does: *mxedavs*, with *m-*, can only mean "he sees me". So, just as with subjects, the pronoun is usually dropped, and *mxedavs* alone is a sentence.

## How it is built

**The basic move.** Take the present form for "he, she" and put the marker in front of the stem:

- *aɣviʒebs* "wakes (him)" → *m-aɣviʒebs* "wakes me"
- *zrdis* "raises (him)" → *g-zrdis* "raises you"
- *asc̣orebs* "corrects (him)" → *gv-asc̣orebs* "corrects us"

Everything else stays: the version vowel (*a-*), the root, the thematic suffix, the person suffix *-s*. The object marker goes in slot 2 and the subject is still shown at the end.

**With "you" as subject.** "You see me": the second person has no subject marker at all (lesson 2-5), so the front slot is free for *m-*: *m-xedav*. "You are looking for us": *gv-eʒeb*. No suffix, because "you" never has one.

**With "I" or "we" as subject.** "I see you" is *g-xedav*. The *v-* of "I" is gone. Bolkvadze and Kiziria state the rule: when a first- or second-person subject meets a first- or second-person object, only the object is marked in front. The slot holds one marker, and the object wins. Lesson 4-4 explains how you still know who is doing what, and what happens to plurals.

A chain of thought for "the teacher corrects us":

1. The verb is *asc̣orebs*, third person subject.
2. The object is "us": *gv-* in slot 2, in front of the version vowel.
3. The subject is "the teacher", third person singular, so *-s* stays at the end.
4. *Masc̣avlebeli gvasc̣orebs.*

And for "you are hiding me":

1. The verb is *malavs* "hides"; the stem is *malav-*.
2. The subject is "you" (one person): no prefix, no suffix.
3. The object is "me": *m-*.
4. *M-malav.* The verb already begins with *m*, so the result has two: *mmalav*.

**Two of the same letter.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria insist that both letters are written when the marker meets the same consonant: *m-malavs* "he hides me", *g-gzavnis* "he sends you". Do not simplify the spelling, even if the pair is hard to say.

**The pronouns, when you keep them.** The pronouns go where any object goes, before the verb: *is me mxedavs* "he sees me". Keep them for emphasis or contrast: *me ḳi ar mxedavs* "but me he does not see". In the present all four forms (*me*, *šen*, *čven*, *tkven*) are the same as the subject forms.

## Where it applies

- **Any transitive verb with "me", "us" or "you" as object.** *Mxedavs*, *gṗaṭižebs*, *gvacilebs* "sees us off", *mḳicxavs* "scolds me".
- **Third-person, second-person and first-person subjects.** With "he, she, they" the suffix stays (*mxedaven* "they see me"); with "you" there is no suffix (*mxedav*); with "I" and "we" the *v-* gives way (lesson 4-4).
- **Questions and commands to come.** *Mxedav?* "do you see me?" is the plain present with rising intonation. Commands with object markers come in lesson 4-17.
- **Later tenses.** The same markers appear in the imperfect and the future: *mxedavda* "he was seeing me", *damxaṭavs* "he will draw me" (lessons 4-5, 4-6).
- **Indirect objects too.** "He writes to me" uses the same *m-* (lesson 4-20), and "I love" uses it for the person who loves (lesson 4-18). This is why the glossary calls them object-set markers rather than "direct object" markers.
- **Not with nouns or "him".** Any noun object, and "him, her, it, them", is zero.

## Watch out

**Do not leave the marker off because the pronoun is there.** In Spanish *ve me* is impossible but *me ve* has the clitic; in English the pronoun alone is enough. Georgian needs the marker: *\*is me xedavs* reads as "he sees (it), I...", a broken sentence. Say *is me mxedavs*, or *mxedavs*.

**Do not contradict the marker with the pronoun.** Pairing *me* with *g-* is wrong: *\*is me gṗaṭižebs*. The pronoun and the marker must agree. "It's me he is inviting" is *is me mṗaṭižebs*.

**Do not add -s for "you see me".** *\*Mxedavs* is "he sees me". "You see me" has no suffix: *mxedav*.

**Do not keep v- before g-.** *\*Vgxedav* for "I see you" puts two markers in one slot. Say *gxedav* (lesson 4-4).

**Do not mark a third-person object.** There is no "him" prefix. *\*Sxedavs* or *\*hxedavs* for "he sees him" are wrong; *xedavs*. (Lesson 4-20 shows that *s-* and *h-* do exist, for a different job.)

**Do not simplify double letters.** *\*Malavs* is "he hides him"; "he hides me" is *mmalavs*. Likewise *ggzavnis* "he sends you", not *\*gzavnis*, which means "he sends it".

**Do not put the marker before a preverb.** In the future (lesson 4-6), *da-m-xaṭavs* "he will draw me": the preverb still comes first. *\*Mdaxaṭavs* is wrong for the same reason as *\*vdaxaṭav*.

## Connections

Spanish is the closest model: *me ve* "he sees me", *te ve* "he sees you", *nos ve* "he sees us", with the pronoun on the verb and the strong pronoun (*a mí*) added only for emphasis, exactly like Georgian *(me) mxedavs*. The difference is that Spanish also has *lo* for "him", while Georgian leaves the third person blank. Korean and Japanese mark no objects on the verb.

## Exercises

1. Say "He sees me", "he sees you", "he sees us".
2. Say "The photographer is drawing us" (use *xaṭavs*).
3. Say "You are looking for me" and "you are looking for us" to one friend.
4. Take apart *gvacilebs* and translate it.
5. Translate *Dedačemi q̇oveltvis mḳicxavs.*
6. Correct *\*Šen me xedavs* (meaning "you see me").
7. Correct *\*Is gzavnis* (meaning "he sends you").
8. Why can *gxedav* mean "I see you" when it has no *v-*?

## Worked answers

1. *Mxedavs*, *gxedavs*, *gvxedavs*. The subject is third person singular, so *-s* stays; the front slot holds *m-*, *g-* or *gv-*.
2. *Poṭograpi gvxaṭavs.* Object "us", *gv-*; subject one person, *-s*.
3. *Meʒeb* and *gveʒeb*. "You" has no marker, so the object prefix stands alone in front of the stem *eʒeb-*.
4. *gv-a-cil-eb-s*: "us" + version vowel + root + thematic suffix + third person singular. "He/she sees us off."
5. "My mother always scolds me." *M-ḳicx-av-s*: *m-* "me", *-s* the mother.
6. *-s* is third person. "You" has no suffix: *šen me mxedav*, and the object needs *m-*. Usually just *mxedav*.
7. *G-* is needed for "you", and since the stem begins with *g*, both letters are written: *is ggzavnis*.
8. Only first- and second-person subjects lose their prefix to an object marker, and the second person has none to lose. With no *-s*, the subject is not third person; with *g-* the object is "you"; so the subject must be "I". Lesson 4-4 works through every combination.
