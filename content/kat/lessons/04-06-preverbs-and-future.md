## The idea

Georgian has no future ending and no word for "will". It makes the future in the front of the verb, with a **preverb**: a short prefix in slot 1 that originally names a direction (up, down, in, out, away, here) and that also marks an action as complete. Bolkvadze and Kiziria give the rule in one line: a verb in the future has a preverb added to its present form. *Vc̣er* "I write" becomes *da-vc̣er* "I will write".

Why should a direction make a future? Because the preverb does two jobs, and the second is the important one here. Besides direction, it marks **perfective aspect**: the action is seen as a whole, with an end. Bolkvadze and Kiziria show this in the past: *ɣeba* "was painting" (no preverb) against *šeɣeba* "painted (and finished)". A present that is complete cannot be happening now, so a preverb on a present stem points forward: "I will write (it, to the end)". The present without a preverb is unfinished and now; with one, finished and later.

This also explains why most verbs have no preverb in the present. Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that, apart from verbs of motion and a few others, Georgian verbs carry no preverb in the present tense. On a verb of motion a preverb can stay in the present, because there it only gives direction: *miprinavs* "is flying away", *moprinavs* "is flying this way".

## How it is built

**The preverbs.** There are nine simple ones, each with a basic direction (Bolkvadze and Kiziria's list):

- *a-* up; *ča-* down, down into; *da-* down (and the commonest "completing" preverb)
- *ga-* out (from inside); *še-* in (from outside)
- *mi-* away from the speaker; *mo-* towards the speaker or listener
- *c̣a-* away, off; *gada-* over, across

Most of them combine with *mo-* to add "towards me": *amo-*, *gamo-*, *šemo-*, *čamo-*, *c̣amo-*, *gadmo-* (*gada-* + *mo-*), and the rarer *mimo-* "to and fro" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

**Which preverb does a verb take?** It is fixed for each verb, and you learn it with the verb. That is why every verb in this guide's vocabulary lists its future: *c̣ers* → *dac̣ers*, *aḳetebs* → *gaaḳetebs*, *amzadebs* → *moamzadebs*, *xsnis* → *gaxsnis*, *č̣ams* → *šeč̣ams*. Sometimes the direction is still visible (*gaxsnis* "will open (out)"); often it has faded into plain "completion" (*dac̣ers*, *gaaḳetebs*).

**The recipe.**

1. Take the present form for the person you need: *vc̣er*, *ašenebs*, *xsnian*.
2. Put the verb's preverb in front of everything, including *v-* and the object markers: *da-vc̣er*, *a-ašenebs*, *ga-xsnian*.
3. Nothing else changes: same thematic suffix, same endings, same object markers, and the same cases as the present (subject nominative, object dative).

A chain of thought for "tomorrow I will tidy the room":

1. The verb is *alagebs*; its future in the notes is *daalagebs*, so the preverb is *da-*.
2. "I": the present is *valageb*.
3. Preverb in front: *da-valageb*.
4. The object stays dative: *otaxs*.
5. *Xval otaxs davalageb.*

**When two a's meet.** If the stem begins with *a-* and the preverb ends in *-a*, both are written: *ga-aḳetebs*, *a-ašenebs*, *da-alagebs*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria give the rule with *daapase* "you valued". With *v-* between them there is nothing to merge: *gavaḳeteb*, *avašeneb*.

**Object markers.** They follow the preverb, in slot 2: *da-m-xaṭavs* "he will draw me", *ga-g-aɣviʒebs* "he will wake you".

**Preverbs that change the meaning.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria point out that a verb can take several preverbs, each giving a different sense in the future. With *c̣ers*: *dac̣ers* "will write", *čac̣ers* "will write down, register", *gadac̣ers* "will copy, rewrite", *aɣc̣ers* "will describe" (Wiktionary). With *debs* "puts": *dadebs* "will put", *gadadebs* "will put off, postpone". These are best learnt as separate verbs; this guide lists them under their future.

**Some verbs make their future another way.** Activity verbs such as *tamašobs*, *cxovrobs*, *mušaobs* have no preverb in the future. They put *i-* before the root and change the thematic suffix to *-eb*: *itamašebs*, *icxovrebs*, *imušavebs* (Wiktionary, lingua.ge). Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe this as a borrowed future with a version vowel. Lesson 4-15 is about these verbs. And a few verbs change their stem: *ḳitxulobs* → *c̣aiḳitxavs*, *q̇idulobs* → *iq̇idis*, *xedavs* → *dainaxavs*, *svams* → *dalevs* (lesson 4-25).

## Where it applies

- **Plain future.** *Xval davc̣er*, "I will write tomorrow". Bolkvadze and Kiziria add that the future can also mean "will be doing for a while", when the time span says so.
- **Promises and decisions.** *Aucileblad davc̣er* "I will definitely write".
- **Questions about the future.** *Vin gaaḳetebs?* "who will do it?"
- **With object markers.** *Damxaṭav?* "will you draw me?", *gagacileb* "I'll see you off".
- **Building other forms.** The preverb + imperfect gives the conditional (lesson 4-7). The same preverb appears in the aorist and the perfect (lessons 4-12, 4-22): *dac̣era* "wrote", *dauc̣eria* "has written".
- **Direction in the present.** On verbs of motion a preverb in the present is only direction: *mi-*, *mo-*, *da-* with "fly", "run", "swim" (lesson 4-11).
- **A few verbs keep a preverb even in the present.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria name *aɣnišnavs* "notes" and *mosc̣ons* "likes" (lesson 4-18) among them.

## Watch out

**There is no future ending.** *\*C̣erebs* or *\*vc̣erav* do not exist. The future is the present with a preverb.

**The preverb comes first.** *\*Vdac̣er* puts the person in front of it. Bolkvadze and Kiziria are explicit: *v-* follows the preverb, *davc̣er*.

**Do not choose the preverb from English.** "Make" suggests no direction at all, yet it takes *ga-* (*gaaḳetebs*); "prepare" takes *mo-*, "eat" *še-*. Guessing *\*daaḳetebs* or *\*gaamzadebs* fails. Take the preverb from the vocabulary notes.

**Do not drop one of the two a's.** *\*Gaḳetebs* and *\*ašenebs* (meaning "will build") lose the stem vowel. Write *gaaḳetebs*, *aašenebs*.

**Do not put the ergative in the future.** By analogy with the past (lesson 0-2), you may want *\*mamam saxls aašenebs*. The future belongs to the present group: subject nominative, object dative: *mama saxls aašenebs*.

**Do not add a preverb to a present that is not about motion.** *\*Axla dac̣ers* for "he is writing (it) down now" is a future. "He is writing now" is *axla c̣ers*.

**Do not form the future of activity verbs with a preverb.** *\*Gatamašobs*, *\*damušaobs* are wrong. Say *itamašebs*, *imušavebs*.

**Do not keep the present stem when the future changes it.** *\*C̣aḳitxulobs* mixes two stems; the future is *c̣aiḳitxavs*. *\*Daxedavs* is not the future of "see"; it is *dainaxavs*. The notes always give the real future.

## Connections

Russian, from which Georgian has borrowed much, does something very similar: a prefix makes a verb perfective, and a perfective present means the future (*pišu* "I write", *napišu* "I will write"). The parallel is typological; this guide does not claim the two systems are related. Spanish *escribiré* puts the future in an ending, which is the habit to unlearn here.

## Exercises

1. Put into the future: *vxaṭav*, *ašeneben*, *xsni*, *vč̣amt*.
2. Say "Tomorrow we'll finish the homework."
3. Say "Grandmother will bake a cake."
4. Say "Will you (one friend) see me off?"
5. What is the difference between *nino c̣ers* and *nino dac̣ers*?
6. Correct *\*Vgaaḳeteb* and *\*Xval mamam saxls aašenebs*.
7. Correct *\*Zeg gatamašoben* (meaning "they will play the day after tomorrow").
8. Translate *Am gegmas gadavdebt, magram aucileblad gavaḳetebt.*

## Worked answers

1. *Davxaṭav*, *aašeneben*, *gaxsni*, *ševč̣amt*. Each takes its own preverb in front of the whole present form.
2. *Xval davalebas davamtavrebt.* *Amtavrebs* has the future *daamtavrebs*; with *v-*: *da-v-amtavreb-t*. The object is dative.
3. *Bebia namcxvars gamoacxobs.* The preverb of *acxobs* is the compound *gamo-*.
4. *Gamacileb?* *Acilebs* has the future *gaacilebs*; the object "me" goes after the preverb: *ga-m-acileb*. No suffix for "you"; rising intonation.
5. *Nino c̣ers* is present, "Nino is writing / writes". *Nino dac̣ers* has a preverb, so it is the future: "Nino will write (it)".
6. The person prefix follows the preverb: *gavaḳeteb*. The future keeps the present cases: *xval mama saxls aašenebs*.
7. Activity verbs make the future with *i-* ... *-eb*, not a preverb: *zeg itamašeben*.
8. "We'll put this plan off, but we'll definitely do it." *Gada-v-deb-t* "we will postpone"; *ga-v-a-ḳet-eb-t* "we will do".
