## The idea

You now have two ingredients: the imperfect, which adds *-d-* to the present (lesson 4-5), and the preverb, which turns the present into a future (lesson 4-6). Put them together and you get the **conditional**: the form for what would happen, or would have happened, if things were different. *Davc̣er* "I will write" becomes *davc̣erdi* "I would write".

Bolkvadze and Kiziria build it in exactly that way: the conditional is the imperfect with a preverb added. So *vc̣erdi* "I was writing" + *da-* = *davc̣erdi*. Seen from the other side, it is the future with the imperfect's *-d-* endings: *davc̣er* + *-di*. Either way you meet the same form.

What does the combination mean? The preverb gives a complete action viewed from a point before it (the future's "will"); the imperfect's *-d-* moves that viewpoint into the past or into the unreal. The result is English "would": a completed action seen as possible but not happening. Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe it as an action that could have happened in the past, or might happen in the present, under certain conditions.

Typically the condition is stated or implied. It may be an "if" clause (part 6, lesson 6-9), or a "but" clause that says why the action does not happen: "I would write the letter today, but there is no time." This lesson uses the second kind, which needs no new grammar.

A word on the name. Lingua.ge labels this screeve with its Georgian school name ხოლმეობითი *xolmeobiti*, which contains *xolme* "habitually" (lesson 4-5). The name points to an older or wider use for repeated past actions. Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe only the conditional meaning, and this guide uses the imperfect (with *xolme*) for past habits.

## How it is built

**The recipe.**

1. Find the verb's future in the vocabulary notes: *dac̣ers*, *aašenebs*, *gaxsnis*, *icxovrebs*.
2. Make the future form for your person: *davc̣er*, *aašenebs*, *gaxsnian*.
3. Add the imperfect endings exactly as in lesson 4-5: *-di* for "I" and "you", *-da* for "he, she", *-dnen* for "they", with *-t* after *-di* for the plurals.

So: *davc̣erdi* "I would write", *aašenebda* "he would build", *gaxsnidnen* "they would open", *icxovrebda* "he would live".

**Glossing.** As with the future, this guide glosses the preverb PV and the *-d-* ending IMPF: *da-v-c̣er-di* `PV-1SBJ-write-IMPF`. The translation carries "would"; the two pieces together are the conditional.

**Cases.** The conditional belongs with the present and future. The subject is nominative, the object dative: *mama saxls aašenebda*.

**Object markers.** As always, after the preverb: *da-g-xaṭavdi* "I would draw you". With a "you all" object and one third-person subject, the *-a* stays before *-t*, as in the imperfect: Bolkvadze and Kiziria give *g-a-c̣q̇en-d-a-t* "it would harm you (all)". So "he would draw you all" is *dagxaṭavdat*.

**Activity verbs** build the conditional on their own future: *itamašebda* "he would play", *imušavebda* "he would work" (lingua.ge: ვიმუშავებდი).

A chain of thought for "Father would build a house, but there is no money":

1. "Would build": conditional. The future of *ašenebs* is *aašenebs*.
2. Third person singular: *aašeneb-* + *-da* = *aašenebda*.
3. Subject nominative *mama*, object dative *saxls*.
4. "There is no money": *puli ar aris* (lesson 2-1 for "there is").
5. *Mama saxls aašenebda, magram puli ar aris.*

## Where it applies

- **"Would" with a stated obstacle.** *Davc̣erdi, magram ...* The obstacle often follows *magram* "but".
- **Advice and opinion.** *Me amas ar gavaḳetebdi* "I wouldn't do that"; *me am c̣igns c̣aviḳitxavdi* "I'd read this book".
- **Polite offers.** *Siamovnebit dagxaṭavdi* "I'd gladly draw you".
- **Unreal conditions.** "If I had money, I would build a house": the conditional in the main clause, with *rom* and the imperfect or pluperfect in the "if" clause (lesson 6-9).
- **Would have.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria's definition covers past possibilities: with a past context the same form means "would have done". Lesson 6-9 shows it in full sentences.
- **Every verb that has a future.** Transitive (*davc̣erdi*), activity (*itamašebda*), and the happening verbs of lesson 4-10, which use *-od-*.

## Watch out

**Not the imperfect alone.** *Vc̣erdi* is "I was writing". Without the preverb there is no "would". *\*Am c̣erils dɣes vc̣erdi, magram ...* means "I was writing the letter today, but ...".

**Not the future alone.** *Davc̣er, magram dro ar aris* says "I will write, but there's no time", which contradicts itself. The *-di* is needed.

**Not "will" + "be".** English "would" is a separate word, and Spanish *escribiría* has its own ending. Georgian has neither: no *\*davc̣er iq̇o* or similar. The conditional is one word.

**The case stays present-style.** *\*Mamam saxli aašenebda* uses the past-tense cases of lesson 4-12. The conditional keeps nominative and dative: *mama saxls aašenebda*.

**"They" is -dnen.** *\*Gaxsnian-da* or *\*gaxsniandnen* stack the present ending onto the new one. Drop the present ending and add *-dnen*: *gaxsnidnen*.

**Do not use the conditional for past habits.** English "when we were young we would swim every day" is a habit, and Georgian uses the imperfect: *vcuravdit xolme* (lesson 4-5). *\*Gavcuravdit* (with a preverb) would be "we would swim (across)".

**Activity verbs take no preverb here either.** *\*Gatamašebda* is wrong; *itamašebda*.

## Connections

Spanish and French build their conditional from the future stem with the imperfect's endings: *escribir-ía*, *écrir-ais*. Georgian does the same thing with its own pieces: future (preverb) + imperfect ending. The logic is identical; only the position of the "future" part differs. Japanese and Korean have no single "would" form and use conditional clauses instead.

## Exercises

1. Make the conditional of *vamzadeb*, *xsnis*, *ḳrepen*, *vxaṭavt*.
2. Say "I would tidy the room, but I am busy."
3. Say "We'd gladly see you (one person) off."
4. Say "Grandmother would bake a cake, but there is no flour."
5. What is the difference between *vxaṭavdi*, *davxaṭav* and *davxaṭavdi*?
6. Correct *\*Bavšvebma ṭorṭi šeč̣amdnen* and *\*Gavaḳeteb, magram dro ar aris* (meaning "I would do it").
7. Correct *\*Zapxulši gavcuravdit xolme* (meaning "in summer we would swim, as a habit").
8. Translate *Ṭeleviziors čavrtavdi, magram šuki ar aris; santels avantebdi, magram santeli ar aris.*

## Worked answers

1. *Movamzadebdi*, *gaxsnida*, *daḳrepdnen*, *davxaṭavdit*. Each is the future + *-di*, *-da* or *-dnen*, with *-t* after *-di* for "we".
2. *Otaxs davalagebdi, magram daḳavebuli var.* *Daalagebs* + *-di*, first person *v-* after the preverb.
3. *Siamovnebit gagacilebdit.* Future *gaacilebs*; object "you" *g-* after the preverb; *-di-t* because "we" is plural.
4. *Bebia namcxvars gamoacxobda, magram pkvili ar aris.* Future *gamoacxobs* + *-da*.
5. *Vxaṭavdi* is the imperfect, "I was drawing"; *davxaṭav* is the future, "I will draw"; *davxaṭavdi* is the conditional, "I would draw".
6. The conditional keeps the present cases, and "cake" in this guide is *namcxvari*: *bavšvebi namcxvars šeč̣amdnen* "the children would eat the cake". "Would do" needs the *-di*: *gavaḳetebdi, magram dro ar aris*.
7. A past habit is the imperfect, without a preverb: *zapxulši vcuravdit xolme*.
8. "I would switch on the television, but there's no electricity; I'd light a candle, but there's no candle." *Ča-v-rt-av-di*, *a-v-ant-eb-di*: future + *-di*.
