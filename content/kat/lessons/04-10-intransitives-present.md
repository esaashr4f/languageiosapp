## The idea

Lesson 4-9 introduced class 2, the verbs of happening: *c̣erili ic̣ereba* "the letter is being written", *c̣q̇ali tbeba* "the water is warming up". This lesson shows how they are built.

A class 2 verb is **intransitive**: it has a subject and no direct object. Most class 2 verbs are made from a class 1 verb by a simple operation, which Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe in three steps: the doer is removed, the object of the class 1 verb becomes the subject, and any indirect object stays as it was. "Somebody writes a letter" becomes "a letter is written". When the doer of the original action is still understood, the result is a **passive**: a form in which the thing acted on is the subject. When there is no doer at all ("the water warms up", "the child grows"), it is simply an intransitive verb of change.

Georgian does not keep these two apart in form. Both are class 2, built the same way, and both have a nominative subject in every series. That is why the glossary has one label, PASS, for "passive or intransitive marker".

There are three ways to build a class 2 verb, and which one a verb uses is fixed:

1. **with the prefix i-**: *c̣ers* "writes" → *ic̣ereba* "is written"; *asxams* "pours" → *isxmeba* "is poured" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria);
2. **with the suffix -d-**: *ašenebs* "builds" → *šendeba* "is being built"; from adjectives, *ʒveli* "old" → *ʒveldeba* "gets old", *mc̣vane* "green" → *mc̣vandeba* "turns green". Bolkvadze and Kiziria call the meaning of the *-d-* verbs "inchoative": "to become X";
3. **with no marker**: *atbobs* "warms" → *tbeba* "warms up"; *ašrobs* "dries" → *šreba* "dries up". Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that there are rather few of these.

And some class 2 verbs are not made from anything: *ibadeba* "is born", *ḳvdeba* "dies", *brundeba* "returns", *rčeba* "stays".

## How it is built

**The common shape.** Whatever the marker, Bolkvadze and Kiziria give class 2 presents the thematic suffix *-eb* and two special endings: *-i* for "I" and "you", *-a* for "he, she". For "they" the ending is *-ian*. Wiktionary's table for *ic̣ereba*:

- *v-i-c̣er-eb-i* "I am written", *i-c̣er-eb-i* "you are written", *i-c̣er-eb-a* "it is written"
- *v-i-c̣er-eb-i-t*, *i-c̣er-eb-i-t*, *i-c̣er-eb-ian*

The subject markers are those of lesson 2-5: *v-* in front for "I" and "we", *-t* for the plurals. Only the third-person *-s* is replaced by *-a*.

This guide glosses the *-i* of "I" and "you" as SBJ (it marks a first- or second-person subject) and the *-a* as 3SG: *v-i-c̣er-eb-i* `1SBJ-PASS-write-TS-SBJ`, *i-c̣er-eb-a* `PASS-write-TS-3SG`, *i-c̣er-eb-ian* `PASS-write-TS-3PL.SBJ`.

**Where each marker goes.** The prefix *i-* sits in the vowel slot before the root (slot 3), after *v-*: *v-i-c̣er-eb-i*. The suffix *-d-* sits after the root, before *-eb* (slot 5): *šen-d-eb-a*, *v-brun-d-eb-i*. The thematic suffix of the class 1 verb is replaced by *-eb*: *xaṭ-av-s* → *i-xaṭ-eb-a* "is painted" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

**The imperfect.** Class 2 verbs add *-od-*, not *-d-*: *ic̣ereboda* "was being written", *vbrundebodi* "I was returning", *rčebodnen* "they were staying" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria; Wiktionary; lingua.ge). Gloss *-oda* as IMPF.3SG, *-odi* as IMPF, *-odnen* as IMPF.3PL.

**The future and conditional.** A preverb in front of the whole present form, as for class 1: *daic̣ereba* "will be written", *dabrundeba* "will return", *daibadeba* "will be born". Conditional: preverb + imperfect, *dabrundeboda* "would return".

A chain of thought for "the house will be built next year":

1. "Be built": something happens to the house; the doer is not named. Class 2 of *ašenebs*, with *-d-*: *šendeba*.
2. Future: the preverb of *ašenebs* is *a-*: *a-šendeba*.
3. The house is the subject: nominative, *saxli*.
4. *Saxli momaval c̣els ašendeba.*

**Plural subjects.** People in the plural take *-ian*: *bavšvebi izrdebian* "the children are growing up". Things in the plural take a singular verb, as everywhere (lesson 2-4): *saxlebi šendeba*.

**The doer.** A class 2 verb usually leaves the doer out; that is the point of it. Formal writing can name it with a genitive and the postposition *mier* "by" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria list *ḳacis mier* "by a man"); this guide leaves that to the readings in part 8.

## Where it applies

- **Passives with no named doer.** *C̣erili ic̣ereba*, *saxli šendeba*: something is being done.
- **Change of state.** *Tbeba* "warms up", *ʒveldeba* "ages", *mc̣vandeba* "turns green": the *-d-* verbs especially.
- **Coming into being and ending.** *Ibadeba* "is born", *ḳvdeba* "dies", *ic̣q̇eba* "begins". Note that "begin" has two verbs: *ic̣q̇ebs* (class 1, "begins something") and *ic̣q̇eba* (class 2, "starts").
- **Movement and position.** *Brundeba* "returns", *rčeba* "stays", and the verbs of going and coming (lesson 4-11).
- **What can be done.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that class 2 forms can also mean "can be eaten, can be drunk" (*ič̣meva*, *ismeva*). Recognise this; it needs no new rule.
- **Every screeve.** Present and imperfect here; future and conditional with a preverb; aorist in lesson 4-14; perfect in lesson 4-24. The subject is nominative in all of them.

## Watch out

**No object, so no dative.** *\*Saxls šendeba* treats the house as an object. In a class 2 verb it is the subject: *saxli šendeba*.

**"They" is -ian, not -en.** *\*Bavšvebi izrdeben* borrows the class 1 ending. Class 2 has *izrdebian*, *brundebian*.

**"He" is -a, not -s.** *\*Ic̣erebs* mixes the two classes. The class 2 third person is *ic̣ereba*. (*Ismis* is a listed exception.)

**The imperfect has -od-, not -d-.** *\*Ic̣erebda* is wrong; *ic̣ereboda*. The *-d-* of class 1 and 3 imperfects does not fit after the class 2 *-eb*.

**Do not drop the -i of "I".** *\*Vbrundeb* is a class 1 shape. Class 2 "I" ends in *-i*: *vbrundebi*.

**Do not keep the old thematic suffix.** From *xaṭavs*, the class 2 verb is *ixaṭeba*, not *\*ixaṭaveba*: Bolkvadze and Kiziria say the *-av* is dropped.

**"Die" changes for many.** *Ḳvdeba* is used for one subject. For a plural subject Bolkvadze and Kiziria give a different root: *ixoceba* "they die", *daixocnen* "they died". *\*Ḳvdebian* is avoided. Lesson 7-7 covers such verbs.

**Not every English passive is class 2.** "I was told", "she was given a book" use other constructions (lessons 4-20, 7-3). Use class 2 for "it is being done" and "it becomes".

## Connections

Spanish does much of this with *se*: *se escribe* "it is written", *se calienta* "it warms up". Georgian packs the same idea into the verb with *i-* or *-d-*. French *se* works the same way. Japanese has paired verbs that are a close match: *atatameru* "warm (something)" and *atatamaru* "warm up", like *atbobs* and *tbeba*.

## Exercises

1. Make the class 2 present (3SG) of *c̣ers* and *xaṭavs*.
2. Say "The children are coming back" and "we are coming back".
3. Say "The concert is beginning."
4. Say "The house was being built" (imperfect).
5. Say "Next year the new school will be built."
6. Correct *\*Bavšvebi izrdeben* and *\*Ic̣erebda*.
7. Correct *\*Saxls šendeba*.
8. Translate *Am kalakši bevri bavšvi ibadeba da coṭa ḳvdeba.*

## Worked answers

1. *Ic̣ereba* "is written" (prefix *i-*, *-eb*, *-a*); *ixaṭeba* "is painted" (the *-av* is replaced by *-eb*).
2. *Bavšvebi brundebian* (people in the plural, *-ian*); *vbrundebit* (*v-* ... *-i-t*).
3. *Ḳoncerṭi ic̣q̇eba.* Class 2, third person singular *-a*.
4. *Saxli šendeboda.* The class 2 imperfect has *-od-*.
5. *Momaval c̣els axali sḳola ašendeba.* Future with the preverb *a-*; the school is the nominative subject.
6. Class 2 "they" is *-ian*: *bavšvebi izrdebian*. The class 2 imperfect is *ic̣ereboda*.
7. The house is the subject of a class 2 verb: *saxli šendeba*.
8. "In this city many children are born and few die." After *bevri* and *coṭa* the noun and verb are singular (lesson 2-4), so *ibadeba* and *ḳvdeba*.
