## The idea

"Go" and "come" are among the commonest verbs in any language, and in Georgian they are one verb. The difference between them lies entirely in the preverb: *midis* "goes (there)" and *modis* "comes (here)". The root is the same; *mi-* points away from the speaker, *mo-* points towards the speaker or the person spoken to (Bolkvadze and Kiziria's definitions).

This is **deixis**: meaning that depends on where the speaker stands. "Here", "there", "come" and "go" are deictic words. Georgian builds deixis into the verb. On a verb of motion the preverb in the present only gives direction, and that direction is always measured from the speaker (lesson 4-6).

Then the other preverbs add the path: *še-* "in", *ga-* "out", *a-* "up", *ča-* "down", *gada-* "across". Each can be combined with *mo-* for "towards me": *šedis* "goes in", *šemodis* "comes in"; *gadis* "goes out", *gamodis* "comes out". So one verb, with about a dozen preverbs, covers what English does with "go in", "come out", "go up", "come down", "cross", "leave".

"Go" belongs to class 2 (lesson 4-9). Its subject is nominative in every series: in the past too, *is c̣avida* "he went", with no ergative.

It is also irregular. Its stem changes completely between screeves: *-di-* in the present, *-val-* / *-va* in the future, *-vid-* / *-ved-* in the past, *-sul-* in the perfect (Wiktionary's tables for *midis* and *modis*). Because the preverb is already present in the present tense, it cannot make the future here; the change of stem does that job instead. Lesson 4-25 returns to verbs like this.

## How it is built

**The present.** The stem is *-di-*, and the endings for "I" and "you" look like the forms of "be" (lesson 2-2). Wiktionary:

- *mi-v-di-var* "I am going", *mi-di-xar* "you are going", *mi-di-s* "he is going"
- *mi-v-di-vart* "we are going", *mi-di-xart* "you (all) are going", *mi-di-an* "they are going"

Notice that "I" has *v-* twice in effect: once as the subject prefix, once in *-var*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe this doubling for verbs that use "be" as an auxiliary.

**The imperfect** is regular on the present stem: *mivdiodi* "I was going", *midioda* "he was going", *midiodnen* "they were going".

**The future** has its own stem and its own endings, which fuse the person:

- *mi-val* "I will go", *mi-xval* "you will go", *mi-va* "he will go"
- *mi-valt*, *mi-xvalt*, *mi-vlen*

The *x-* of *mixval* is the old second-person subject marker. Bolkvadze and Kiziria name "be" (*xar*) and "come" (*moxval*, *moxvedi*) as the verbs that keep it; Wiktionary's tables show it in every form of this verb, *mixval* and *c̣axval* included. This guide does not cut the future forms further; it glosses *mi-va* as `PV-go.3SG`.

**Two futures for "go".** Wiktionary gives *midis* two futures: *miva* "will go (to a place), will get there", and *c̣ava* "will go off, will leave". *C̣a-* means "away, off" (lesson 4-6). In practice, "I'll go to Tbilisi tomorrow" is usually *xval tbilisši c̣aval*: you set off. *Mival* stresses reaching the destination. For "come" the matching pair is *mova* "will come" and *c̣amova* "will come along, will come away (with me)".

**Where to and where from.** The destination takes *-ši* "into" (*sḳolaši* "to school"), *-tan* "to (a person)" (*megobartan* "to my friend's"), or *-ze* "onto" (*ḳibeze* "up the stairs"). The starting point takes *-dan* "from" (*saidan* "where from?"). These are the postpositions of part 3; no new case is needed.

**Which way is "come"?** *Mo-* points to the speaker *or* the person addressed. So when you tell a friend you will visit, you say *mova*-type forms: *xval moval* "I'll come (to you) tomorrow". English would also say "come" here, but Spanish says *voy* "I go": Georgian follows English in this.

A chain of thought for "the children are coming out of the school":

1. "Come out": out (*ga-*) + towards the speaker (*mo-*): the preverb *gamo-*. Present stem *-di-*: *gamodis*.
2. "They": *gamodian*.
3. From the school: *sḳola* + *-dan*: *sḳolidan* (the *-a* truncates before *-dan*, lesson 3-5).
4. The subject is nominative: *bavšvebi*.
5. *Bavšvebi sḳolidan gamodian.*

## Where it applies

- **Going away and coming here.** *Midis* / *modis* for any movement from or to the speaker's position.
- **Coming to the person you talk to.** *Moval* "I'll come (to you)", *modixar?* "are you coming (here)?".
- **Paths.** *Šedis* / *šemodis* "goes / comes in", *gadis* / *gamodis* "goes / comes out", *adis* / *amodis* "goes / comes up", *čadis* / *čamodis* "goes / comes down", *gadadis* / *gadmodis* "crosses over / comes over".
- **Leaving.** *C̣ava* "will leave, go off"; for the present, *midis* serves.
- **Habits and attending.** *Dadis* "goes (regularly), attends, walks about": *sḳolaši dadis* "goes to school".
- **Figurative uses.** *Gamodis* also means "comes out" of a book or film; *čamodis* "comes down" to the capital is used of arriving from the provinces. Treat these as vocabulary.
- **Every screeve.** The verb is class 2, so the subject stays nominative in the aorist and perfect too: *mivida* "he went (there)", *misula* "he has gone" (lessons 4-14, 4-24).

## Watch out

**Do not use mo- for movement away from both of you.** *\*Nino megobartan modis* says Nino is coming towards you. If she is heading elsewhere, *midis*.

**Do not use mi- for coming to the listener.** Spanish *voy a tu casa* uses "go". Georgian uses *mo-*: *šentan moval* "I'll come to yours"; *\*šentan mival* sounds as if the speaker were somewhere else.

**Do not make a future with a preverb.** The present already has one. *\*Damidis* or *\*c̣amidis* are not words. The future is a new stem: *miva*, *c̣ava*, *mova*.

**Do not use -en or -s on the "I" and "you" forms.** *\*Mivdis* and *\*midis* (for "you go") are wrong; the present uses the "be" endings: *mivdivar*, *midixar*.

**Do not drop the x- of "you will come".** *\*Moval* for "you will come" is "I will come". "You" is *moxval*.

**No ergative.** "Go" is class 2. *\*Ninom c̣avida* is wrong; *nino c̣avida* "Nino left" (lesson 4-14).

**Do not use the dative for the destination.** Spanish *voy a la escuela* and English "to" have no direct Georgian equivalent; *\*sḳolas mivdivar* is wrong. Use *-ši*, *-tan* or *-ze*.

**Not every verb of motion works like this.** "Run", "fly", "swim" have their own roots (*prinavs*, *curavs*) and take *mi-* / *mo-* only for direction (lessons 4-5, 4-6).

## Connections

Japanese makes the same split: *iku* "go" and *kuru* "come", with *kuru* used for movement towards the speaker, and like Georgian (and English) it uses "come" for movement towards the listener's place. Korean *gada* / *oda* work the same way. Spanish *ir* / *venir* differ: Spanish says *ya voy* "I'm coming" to someone calling you, where Georgian says *movdivar*.

## Exercises

1. Say "We are going to Mtskheta" and "they are coming from Mtskheta".
2. Say "Mother is coming into the room" and "Father is going out of the house".
3. Say to a friend "I'll come to you tomorrow evening."
4. Say "When will you (several people) come?"
5. Which is right for "Nino is going down the stairs": *čadis* or *čamodis*? Can both be right?
6. Correct *\*Ninom c̣avida* and *\*Bavšvebi sḳolas midian*.
7. Correct *\*Xval damival* (meaning "I'll go tomorrow").
8. Translate *Gogo saxlidan gamodis, kučaze gadadis da maɣaziaši šedis.*

## Worked answers

1. *Mcxetaši mivdivart.* *Isini mcxetidan modian.* (*Mcxeta* truncates before *-dan*.)
2. *Deda otaxši šemodis.* *Mama saxlidan gadis.* The mother comes towards the speaker, so *šemo-*; the father leaves, so *ga-*.
3. *Xval saɣamos šentan moval.* *Mo-* because you are coming to the listener; the future stem *-val*.
4. *Rodis moxvalt?* Second person future *moxval* + *-t*.
5. *Čadis* if you are upstairs watching her go down; *čamodis* if you are downstairs and she is coming down towards you. Both are right; the choice depends on where you stand.
6. "Go" is class 2, with a nominative subject: *nino c̣avida*. The destination takes *-ši*: *bavšvebi sḳolaši midian*.
7. The future of "go" is a stem change, not a new preverb: *xval c̣aval* (or *mival*).
8. "The girl comes out of the house, crosses the street and goes into the shop." *Gamodis* (out, towards the speaker), *gadadis* (across), *šedis* (in, away from the speaker).
