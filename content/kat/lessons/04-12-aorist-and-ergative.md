## The idea

This is the lesson part 4 has been building towards. The **aorist** is the past tense for an event: "I wrote the letter", "Father built the house". It is the first screeve of series II, and with it the cases change.

In series I the subject of *c̣ers* was nominative and its object dative: *giorgi c̣erils c̣ers*. In the aorist the subject goes into the **ergative**, the case in *-ma* or *-m*, and the object goes into the nominative: *giorgim c̣erili dac̣era* "Giorgi wrote the letter". Bolkvadze and Kiziria state both halves: the ergative marks the subject of class 1 and class 3 verbs in series II; the nominative marks the direct object of class 1 verbs in series II and III.

Why should the cases swap? The sources consulted describe the system but do not give its history, and this guide does not guess. What they make clear is how the system holds together. The ergative appears only with doers (class 1 and class 3 subjects) and only in series II. The nominative is the case of whatever the verb agrees with as its "plain" participant in that series: the subject in series I, and the object of a transitive verb in series II. Lesson 4-8 called this the case pattern of the series. The verb's endings do not change their logic: *dac̣era* still agrees with Giorgi as its subject (third person singular), whatever case Giorgi is in.

The aorist has a second side, aspect. Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe it as a past action either completed or continuous, depending on whether there is a preverb. With a preverb it is complete: *davc̣ere* "I wrote (it)". Without one it is a continuing past action: *vc̣ere* "I was writing, I wrote for a while". Most of the time you need the first.

## How it is built

**The ergative.** You have seen its forms (lessons 3-1, 3-8, 3-10):

- consonant stems *-ma*: *ḳac-ma*, *bavšv-ma*, *megobar-ma* (no syncope in the ergative)
- vowel stems *-m*: *deda-m*, *nino-m*, *mama-m*, and *giorgi-m*, since the *-i* of *giorgi* belongs to the stem
- plurals *-eb-ma*: *bavšv-eb-ma*
- pronouns: *me*, *šen*, *čven*, *tkven* do not change; *is* → *man*, *isini* → *mat*, *es* → *aman*
- adjectives before the noun take the full ending: *did-ma saxl-ma*

**The aorist stem.** Start from the future, because the aorist has the same preverb: *dac̣ers*, *daxaṭavs*, *aašenebs*. Then:

1. Drop the thematic suffix (lesson 4-2): *daxaṭav-* → *daxaṭ-*, *aašeneb-* → *aašen-*.
2. Undo any vowel loss or change in the root. Bolkvadze and Kiziria list the main cases: *zrdi* → *zard-*, *xsni* → *xsen-*, *č̣ri* → *č̣er-*, *ḳreps* → *ḳrip-*.

The vocabulary notes give the third-person aorist of every verb, so in practice you read the stem off *dac̣era*, *daxaṭa*, *aašena*.

**The endings.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria give the usual set:

- *-e* for "I" and "you": *da-v-c̣er-e* "I wrote", *da-c̣er-e* "you wrote"
- *-a* for "he, she": *da-c̣er-a*
- *-es* for "they": *da-c̣er-es*
- and *-t* after *-e* for "we" and "you all": *da-v-c̣er-e-t*, *da-c̣er-e-t*

The person prefixes are the usual ones: *v-* after the preverb for "I" and "we".

This guide glosses the endings AOR, AOR.3SG and AOR.3PL: *da-v-c̣er-e* `PV-1SBJ-write-AOR`, *da-c̣er-a* `PV-write-AOR.3SG`, *da-c̣er-es* `PV-write-AOR.3PL`.

**Two variants.** Some verbs have *-i* instead of *-e* for "I" and "you": Bolkvadze and Kiziria list *movḳali* "I killed", *gavxseni* "I opened"; Wiktionary has *gavč̣eri* "I cut", *davasxi* "I poured". The third person is still *-a* and *-es*: *moḳla*, *gaxsna*, *gač̣ra*. And some verbs have *-o* instead of *-a* for "he, she": *daic̣q̇o* "he began", *aavso* "he filled", *gamoacxo* "he baked", *aanto* "he lit", *dargo* "he planted" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria; Wiktionary; lingua.ge). Their "they" is still *-es*. These are lexical: the notes tell you.

**Object markers.** The prefixes of lesson 4-3 work as before: *ga-m-aɣviʒ-a* "he woke me", *da-g-xaṭ-e* "I drew you". The object itself, if you name it, is now nominative: but *me*, *šen*, *čven*, *tkven* look the same in every case, so nothing shows.

**A chain of thought** for "Mother woke the children early":

1. The verb is *aɣviʒebs*, class 1; future *gaaɣviʒebs*, aorist *gaaɣviʒa*.
2. It happened, once, and is complete: aorist with the preverb.
3. Class 1 in series II: the subject is ergative, *dedam*; the object nominative, *bavšvebi*.
4. The verb agrees with the subject, third person singular: *gaaɣviʒa*.
5. *Dedam bavšvebi adre gaaɣviʒa.*

## Where it applies

- **A completed event in the past.** *Davc̣ere*, *aašena*, *gaq̇ida*: the default past for "did".
- **A sequence of events.** *Panǯara gaxsna da otaxi daalaga*: each step is an aorist. Background goes in the imperfect (lesson 4-5).
- **A past action over a span, seen as a whole** without a preverb: *mteli dɣe vxaṭe*.
- **Class 1 and class 3 verbs take the ergative.** Class 3 in lesson 4-15. Class 2 verbs keep a nominative subject in the aorist (lesson 4-14).
- **Commands.** The second-person aorist is also the imperative: *dac̣ere!* "write (it)!" (lesson 4-17). Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that only intonation tells *dac̣ere* "you wrote" from *dac̣ere!* "write!".
- **The optative** uses the same ergative pattern (lesson 4-16).
- **Indirect objects** stay dative in the aorist (lesson 4-20).

## Watch out

**The ergative is not "the past case".** It marks doers of class 1 and 3 verbs in series II only. *\*Ninom c̣avida* ("Nino left") is wrong: "go" is class 2 (lesson 4-11). And it never appears in the imperfect: *\*ninom c̣erda*.

**Change both cases, not one.** *\*Giorgim c̣erils dac̣era* changes the subject but keeps the series I object. *\*Giorgi c̣erili dac̣era* changes only the object. Both must move: *giorgim c̣erili dac̣era*.

**The verb agrees with the subject, not with the nominative noun.** English speakers sometimes match the verb to the nominative object. *\*Bavšvma surati daxaṭes* puts a "they" ending for one child. One child: *daxaṭa*. In *bavšvebma surati daxaṭes*, *-es* agrees with the children.

**Drop the thematic suffix.** *\*Daxaṭava*, *\*aašeneba* keep the present-stem suffix. Say *daxaṭa*, *aašena*.

**Do not syncopate the ergative.** *\*Megobrma* follows the genitive; the ergative keeps the full stem: *megobarma*.

**-e is not "he".** *\*Man dac̣ere* uses the "I/you" ending. "He wrote" is *dac̣era*; "they wrote" *dac̣eres*, not *\*dac̣eren* (the present ending).

**Use the preverb for a finished act.** *\*Gušin c̣erili vc̣ere* is "I spent time writing a letter yesterday", not "I wrote the letter". For the finished act: *davc̣ere*.

**Sources sometimes disagree on -a and -o.** For *agebs* "builds", Bolkvadze and Kiziria give the aorist *aago*, Wiktionary *aaga*. Where this guide's notes give two forms, both are in use. The same goes for "they baked": lingua.ge gives *gamoacxves*, Wiktionary *gamoacxes*; the table shows both.

## Connections

Basque, the other ergative language of Europe, marks the doer with *-k* (*gizonak* "the man (did)") in every tense. Georgian does it only in series II, which is why it is called split-ergative. Hindi-Urdu makes a similar split by tense: *maĩ ne likhā* "I wrote", with the ergative *ne* only in the perfective past. Spanish and French have nothing like it, so the only habit to build is: aorist of a doer verb, so ergative.

## Exercises

1. Put into the aorist: *nino vašls č̣ams* (aorist *šeč̣ama*).
2. Say "The children picked flowers" (aorist of *ḳreps*: *daḳripa*).
3. Say "We sent a parcel" (*gzavnis*, aorist *gagzavna*).
4. Say "Grandfather planted a vine."
5. Say "You (one person) woke me" and "I woke you".
6. Correct *\*Deda sadils moamzada*.
7. Correct *\*Bavšvma panǯaras gaxsna* and *\*Mat namcxvari šeč̣amen* (meaning "they ate the cake").
8. Translate *Gušin mamam ɣobe šeɣeba, me ḳi tepšebi gavrecxe.*

## Worked answers

1. *Ninom vašli šeč̣ama.* Subject ergative (*nino-m*), object nominative (*vašli*), aorist third person singular.
2. *Bavšvebma q̇vavilebi daḳripes.* Plural ergative *-eb-ma*; the object plural nominative; "they" *-es*, on the stem with *e* → *i*.
3. *Amanati gavgzavnet.* *Ga-v-gzavn-e-t*: preverb, *v-*, stem without *-i*, *-e*, *-t*.
4. *Babuam vazi dargo.* *Babua* is a vowel stem, so *-m*; *rgavs* is one of the verbs with *-o* for "he".
5. *Gamaɣviʒe* "you woke me" (*m-* after the preverb, *-e* for "you"). *Gagaɣviʒe* "I woke you": *g-* takes the front slot and *v-* is dropped (lesson 4-4).
6. Class 1 aorist needs an ergative subject and nominative object: *dedam sadili moamzada*.
7. *Bavšvma panǯara gaxsna*: the object must be nominative. "They ate" is *-es*; *-en* is the present ending: *mat namcxvari šeč̣ames*.
8. "Yesterday Father painted the fence, and I washed the plates." *Mamam* ergative; *ɣobe* nominative; *šeɣeba* aorist of *ɣebavs*; *gavrecxe* "I washed", object *tepšebi* nominative plural.
