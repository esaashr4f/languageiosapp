## The idea

Lesson 4-12 gave the aorist of transitive verbs, with the ergative. This lesson gives the aorist of class 2, the verbs of happening, and here the rule is the opposite: the subject stays nominative. Bolkvadze and Kiziria put it in one line: the subject of an intransitive verb never changes its case. *Nino daibada tbilisši* "Nino was born in Tbilisi"; *bavšvebi sḳolidan dabrundnen* "the children came back from school". No *-m*, no *-ma*.

That is the whole point of the class distinction of lesson 4-9. A doer (class 1, class 3) goes into the ergative in series II; an undergoer (class 2) stays in the nominative. In English both are plain subjects, so this is the one place where you have to think about the verb's class before you choose the case of the noun in front of it.

The aorist of class 2 also shows clearly something that exists in class 1 too: there are two sets of aorist endings. Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe them by marker: class 2 verbs with the prefix *i-* take *-e* in the first and second persons; class 2 verbs with *-d-* and those with no marker take *-i*. English-language grammars of Georgian (Hewitt's among them) call the first type **weak** and the second **strong**. This guide uses the terms like this: a **weak aorist** has *-e* for "I" and "you" (*davic̣ere*, *davc̣ere*); a **strong aorist** has *-i* for "I" and "you" (*davbrundi*, *gavč̣eri*), often with a change in the root vowel. In both, the third person singular ends in *-a* (sometimes *-o*).

Why does it matter which type a verb has? Because the next screeve depends on it. In the optative (lesson 4-16), weak aorists take *-o* and strong aorists take *-a*: Wiktionary gives *davc̣ero* "let me write" from weak *davc̣ere*, but *gavč̣ra* "let me cut" from strong *gavč̣eri*.

## How it is built

**Start from the future.** As with class 1, the aorist keeps the future's preverb and drops the thematic suffix *-eb*: *daic̣ereba* → *daic̣er-*; *dabrundeba* → *dabrund-*; *darčeba* → *darč-*.

**Then add the endings.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria give:

- for "I" and "you": *-e* (prefix *i-* verbs) or *-i* (*-d-* verbs and verbs with no marker)
- for "he, she": *-a* or *-o*
- for "they": *-nen*, for every class 2 verb
- *-t* after *-e* or *-i* for "we" and "you all"

So:

- weak, *i-* type: *da-v-i-c̣er-e*, *da-i-c̣er-e*, *da-i-c̣er-a*, *da-v-i-c̣er-e-t*, *da-i-c̣er-e-t*, *da-i-c̣er-nen* (Wiktionary)
- strong, *-d-* type: *da-v-brun-d-i*, *da-brun-d-i*, *da-brun-d-a*, *da-v-brun-d-i-t*, *da-brun-d-i-t*, *da-brun-d-nen* (lingua.ge)
- strong, no marker: *da-v-rč-i*, *da-rč-a*, *da-rč-nen* "I stayed, he stayed, they stayed" (lingua.ge); *ga-v-tb-i*, *ga-tb-a* "I warmed up, he warmed up" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria)

Note the difference from class 1 in "they": class 2 has *-nen* (*daic̣ernen*), class 1 has *-es* (*dac̣eres*).

**Root changes.** Several strong aorists change their root in the first and second persons, as *č̣r-* / *č̣er-* did in class 1. Lingua.ge: *dgeba* "gets up" → *avdeki* "I got up", *adga* "he got up"; *c̣veba* "lies down" → *davc̣eki* "I lay down", *dac̣va* "he lay down". "Go" and "come" have their own stems: *mivedi* "I went (there)", *mivida* "he went", *mividnen* "they went"; *movedi*, *moxvedi*, *movida* (Wiktionary). The *x-* of "you" appears again in *moxvedi* "you came".

**The -o ending.** Some class 2 verbs have *-o* in the third person: *daic̣q̇eba* "will begin" → *daic̣q̇o* "began" (Wiktionary).

**A chain of thought** for "the guests left late":

1. "Leave" is *c̣ava*, the future of *midis* (lesson 4-11): class 2.
2. Class 2, so the subject stays nominative: *sṭumrebi*.
3. Aorist of *c̣ava*: *c̣avida*; "they": *c̣avidnen* (Wiktionary).
4. *Sṭumrebi gvian c̣avidnen.*

## Where it applies

- **All class 2 verbs in the aorist:** passives (*daic̣era*), changes of state (*gatba*, *ašenda*), being born and dying (*daibada*, *moḳvda*), motion (*c̣avida*, *dabrunda*), position (*adga*, *dac̣va*).
- **Strong and weak in class 1 too.** Class 1 *davc̣ere*, *davxaṭe* are weak; *gavč̣eri*, *movḳali*, *gavxseni*, *davasxi* are strong (lesson 4-12).
- **Sequences mixing classes.** *Dedam sadili moamzada, sṭumrebi movidnen*: an ergative subject for the cook, a nominative one for the guests arriving, in the same story.
- **The optative.** The weak/strong difference decides its vowel (lesson 4-16).
- **Negation.** As always, before the verb: *ar dabrunda* "he did not come back".

## Watch out

**No ergative with class 2.** By analogy with *ninom c̣erili dac̣era* you might say *\*ninom daibada* or *\*sṭumrebma c̣avidnen*. Class 2 subjects are nominative in every series: *nino daibada*, *sṭumrebi c̣avidnen*.

**"They" is -nen, not -es.** *\*Bavšvebi dabrundes* takes the class 1 ending. Class 2 has *dabrundnen*.

**Do not mix weak and strong.** *\*Davbrunde* and *\*davrče* give strong verbs weak endings; *\*davic̣eri* the reverse. The type follows the marker: *i-* verbs weak (*davic̣ere*), *-d-* and markerless verbs strong (*davbrundi*, *davrči*). Lingua.ge and Wiktionary give the forms when in doubt.

**Do not use the present stem of "go" in the aorist.** *\*Midida* is not a word. The aorist stem is *-vid-* (*mivida*), with *-ved-* for "I", "we", "you" (*mivedi*, *mixvedi*).

**Watch the root changes.** *\*Avdgi* for "I got up" follows the stem *dg-* of *dgeba*; the real form is *avdeki*.

**Not every intransitive is class 2.** "Wake up" looks like a happening, but lingua.ge classifies *iɣviʒebs* as medial (class 3), with the ergative: *man gaiɣviʒa* "he woke up" (lesson 4-15). Check the class in the notes.

## Connections

French again gives the best model: *elle est née*, *ils sont partis*, *nous sommes revenus* use *être*, the auxiliary of undergoers, just as Georgian keeps these subjects nominative. Japanese and Korean mark every subject the same way (*ga*, *i/ga*), so nothing there prepares you for this; Basque does the same split as Georgian here, with no ergative on the subject of "arrive" or "be born".

## Exercises

1. Put into the aorist: *nino brundeba*, *bavšvebi rčebian*, *c̣q̇ali tbeba*.
2. Say "I was born in Mtskheta" and "my brother was born in Tbilisi".
3. Say "The guests came yesterday and left today."
4. Say "We stayed at home, but our friends went abroad."
5. Is *davic̣ere* weak or strong? And *davbrundi*? How can you tell?
6. Correct *\*Ninom daibada mcxetaši* and *\*Bavšvebi dabrundes*.
7. Correct *\*Gušin saxlši davrče*.
8. Translate *Bebia adre adga, dedam sauzme moamzada, bavšvebi ḳi gvian dabrundnen.*

## Worked answers

1. *Nino dabrunda*, *bavšvebi darčnen*, *c̣q̇ali gatba*. Same preverb as the future, *-eb* dropped, *-a* or *-nen*; the subjects stay nominative.
2. *(Me) mcxetaši davibade.* *Čemi ʒma tbilisši daibada.* Weak *i-* type: *-e* for "I", *-a* for "he".
3. *Sṭumrebi gušin movidnen da dɣes c̣avidnen.* Both are class 2: nominative subject, *-nen*.
4. *Čven saxlši davrčit, čveni megobrebi ḳi sazɣvargaret c̣avidnen.* *Davrčit*: strong, *-i-t*.
5. *Davic̣ere* is weak (*-e*); it has the prefix *i-*. *Davbrundi* is strong (*-i*); it has *-d-*. The marker predicts the type, as Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe.
6. "Be born" is class 2: *nino mcxetaši daibada*. Class 2 "they" is *-nen*: *bavšvebi dabrundnen*.
7. *Rčeba* has no marker, so a strong aorist: *gušin saxlši davrči*.
8. "Grandmother got up early, Mother made breakfast, and the children came back late." *Adga* and *dabrundnen* are class 2 (nominative subjects); *moamzada* is class 1 (ergative *dedam*, nominative *sauzme*).
