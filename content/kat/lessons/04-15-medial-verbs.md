## The idea

Class 3, the **medial** verbs, are verbs of activity: playing, working, living, crying, singing, dancing, travelling. Their single participant does something; it is not something that happens to it. That is why Georgian treats their subject like the subject of a transitive verb: nominative in series I, **ergative in series II**, dative in series III. Bolkvadze and Kiziria list this as the one exception to the rule that intransitive subjects never change case: the medio-active verbs "have a case-changing subject" like active verbs, "but they do not modify direct objects".

So you get *bavšvebi tamašoben* "the children are playing" but *bavšvebma itamašes* "the children played", with *-ma*, though nothing was played with. This is the pattern WALS calls active alignment: doers get the doer's case, whether or not there is an object.

Bolkvadze and Kiziria add a second, formal point: medial verbs "cannot form the future subseries independently". In the present they look like class 1 verbs with a thematic suffix (*tamaš-ob-s*, *muša-ob-s*, *cxovr-ob-s*, *ceḳv-av-s*, *ṭir-i-s*). For the future and the aorist they borrow a transitive-looking pattern with the vowel *i-* in front of the root and, in the future, the thematic suffix *-eb*: *itamašebs* "will play", *itamaša* "played". There is usually no preverb.

You have used these verbs since part 2. Everything about their present and imperfect stays as it was; this lesson adds the future, the conditional and the aorist.

## How it is built

**The future.** Put *i-* in front of the root (after *v-*), and replace the thematic suffix with *-eb*:

- *tamaš-ob-s* → *i-tamaš-eb-s* "will play"
- *cxovr-ob-s* → *i-cxovr-eb-s* "will live"
- *ṭir-i-s* → *i-ṭir-eb-s* "will cry"; *mɣer-i-s* → *i-mɣer-eb-s* "will sing"
- *ceḳv-av-s* → *i-ceḳv-eb-s* "will dance"

*Mušaobs* adds a *v*: *imušavebs* (lingua.ge), as *tevzaobs* gives *itevzavebs*. The person markers are the class 1 ones: *vitamašeb*, *itamašeb*, *itamašebs*, *vitamašebt*, *itamašebt*, *itamašeben* (Wiktionary).

**The conditional** is the future + *-di/-da/-dnen*: *itamašebda* "would play".

**The aorist.** Keep *i-*, drop the thematic suffix, and add the weak class 1 endings: *-e* for "I" and "you", *-a* for "he, she", *-es* for "they":

- *vitamaše*, *itamaše*, *itamaša*, *vitamašet*, *itamašet*, *itamašes* (Wiktionary)
- *viṭire*, *iṭira*, *iṭires*; *vimɣere*, *imɣera*, *imɣeres*; *viceḳve*, *iceḳva*, *iceḳves*
- *vimušave*, *imušava*, *imušaves* (lingua.ge); *icxovra* "lived"

The subject is ergative: *mamam imušava* "Father worked".

**Glossing.** This guide glosses the *i-* of these futures and aorists as SV, the version vowel (taught in lesson 4-21), following Bolkvadze and Kiziria, who call it a version prefix: *i-tamaš-eb-s* `SV-play-TS-3SG.SBJ`.

**Some medial verbs do take a preverb.** Lingua.ge gives *isvenebs* "rests" with the future *daisvenebs* and the aorist *daisvena*, and *iɣviʒebs* "wakes up" with *gaiɣviʒebs*, *gaiɣviʒa*. They are still class 3: *man daisvena* "he rested", *man gaiɣviʒa* "he woke up", with the ergative.

**An object is possible.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria give *man pexburti itamaša* "he played football", *man simɣera imɣera* "he sang a song": the thing played or sung stands in the nominative in the aorist, like a class 1 object. In the present it is dative: *pexburts tamašobs*.

**A chain of thought** for "yesterday we danced and sang all evening":

1. "Dance", "sing": class 3 (the notes say so; they are activities).
2. Aorist, so the subject would be ergative; *čven* has no case ending, so nothing shows.
3. First person plural, weak endings: *vi-ceḳv-e-t*, *vi-mɣer-e-t*.
4. *Gušin mteli saɣamo viceḳvet da vimɣeret.*

## Where it applies

- **Activities of people and animals:** *tamašobs*, *mušaobs*, *cxovrobs*, *sc̣avlobs*, *laṗaraḳobs*, *pikrobs*, *ṭiris*, *icinis*, *mɣeris*, *ceḳvavs*, *q̇viris*, *curavs*, *prinavs*, *nadirobs*, *tevzaobs*, *mogzaurobs*, *seirnobs*, *xumrobs*, *ocnebobs*.
- **Meals as activities:** *sauzmobs* "has breakfast", *sadilobs* "has lunch", *vaxšmobs* "has supper" (lingua.ge): *man isadila* "he had lunch".
- **Bodily activities:** *isvenebs* "rests", *iɣviʒebs* "wakes up".
- **Weather.** *Tovs* "snows" makes its future and aorist the same way, *itovebs*, *itova* (lingua.ge, which files weather verbs as a separate, personless type): there is no subject, so no case question arises.
- **Every screeve.** Present and imperfect as in part 2 and lesson 4-5; future, conditional and aorist here; the optative in lesson 4-16; the perfect, with a dative subject, in lesson 4-22.

## Watch out

**Do not keep the nominative in the aorist.** English "the children played" has no object, and class 2 verbs like "came back" keep the nominative, so *\*bavšvebi itamašes* looks right. It is not: class 3 subjects go into the ergative, *bavšvebma itamašes*.

**Do not give a medial verb a preverb future by analogy.** *\*Gatamašebs*, *\*damušaobs*, *\*gacxovrobs* follow class 1. The future is *itamašebs*, *imušavebs*, *icxovrebs*. Only a few medial verbs (*daisvenebs*, *gaiɣviʒebs*) have a preverb; the notes say which.

**Do not keep -ob in the future.** *\*Itamašobs* mixes the two stems. The future has *-eb*: *itamašebs*.

**"They" in the aorist is -es, not -nen.** *\*Bavšvebma itamašnen* uses the class 2 ending. Medial aorists end like class 1: *itamašes*, *iṭires*, *imušaves*.

**Do not confuse "get up" with "wake up".** *Adga* "got up" is class 2 (*bebia adga*); *gaiɣviʒa* "woke up" is class 3 (*bebiam gaiɣviʒa*), in lingua.ge's analysis. Learn the class with the verb.

**Do not rely on meaning alone.** Class membership has lexical exceptions. "Go", an activity in English, is class 2 (lesson 4-11). The notes give each verb's class.

## Connections

French again draws the line in the same place, from the other side: *il a joué*, *il a travaillé*, *il a vécu* take *avoir*, like the transitive *il a écrit*, while *il est arrivé* takes *être*. The Georgian ergative goes with the French *avoir* group, the Georgian nominative with the *être* group. Spanish has lost the distinction (*ha jugado*, *ha llegado*).

## Exercises

1. Put into the aorist: *nino ceḳvavs*, *bavšvebi ṭirian*, *me vmušaob*.
2. Put into the future: *čven vtamašobt*, *is mɣeris*, *isini sadiloben*.
3. Say "Last year we travelled often."
4. Say "Grandfather rested after lunch" (use *sadilis šemdeg* "after lunch" as a fixed phrase, lesson 5-1).
5. Why is it *mamam imušava* but *mama dabrunda*?
6. Correct *\*Bavšvebi itamašes* and *\*Bavšvebma itamašnen*.
7. Correct *\*Xval gavmušaob*.
8. Translate *Ninom gaiɣviʒa, isauzma da gazeti c̣aiḳitxa.*

## Worked answers

1. *Ninom iceḳva.* *Bavšvebma iṭires.* *(Me) vimušave.* Class 3: ergative subject (invisible on *me*), *i-* before the root, weak endings, *-es* for "they".
2. *Čven vitamašebt.* *Is imɣerebs.* *Isini isadileben.* *I-* and *-eb*; series I, so the subjects stay nominative.
3. *Šaršan xširad vimogzauret.* Aorist of a class 3 verb, first person plural: *v-i-* ... *-e-t*. (The imperfect *vmogzaurobdit* would stress the habit; lesson 4-5.)
4. *Babuam sadilis šemdeg daisvena.* *Isvenebs* is class 3 with the preverb *da-* in the aorist; the subject is ergative.
5. *Mušaobs* is class 3 (an activity): ergative in the aorist. *Brundeba* is class 2 (a happening): nominative in every series.
6. Class 3 needs the ergative: *bavšvebma itamašes*. And its "they" ending is *-es*, not the class 2 *-nen*: *bavšvebma itamašes*.
7. *Mušaobs* has no preverb future: *xval vimušaveb*.
8. "Nino woke up, had breakfast and read the newspaper." *Gaiɣviʒa* and *isauzma* are class 3 and *c̣aiḳitxa* class 1, so all three want an ergative subject: *ninom*. *Gazeti* is the nominative object of *c̣aiḳitxa*.
