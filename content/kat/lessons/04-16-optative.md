## The idea

The **optative** is the second screeve of series II. Where the aorist says what happened, the optative says what should, could or ought to happen: *davc̣ero* "let me write, I should write", *dac̣eros* "let him write", *davc̣erot* "let's write".

Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe it as a desired, necessary, possible or intended action in the future. Its Georgian name, მეორე კავშირებითი "second subjunctive" (lesson 4-8), places it in the family of subjunctive screeves: forms that present an action as wished or required rather than stated. It is the commonest of them by far. Spanish uses its present subjunctive for much the same work: *que lo escriba* "let him write it", *quiero que lo escribas*, *ojalá venga*.

Because it belongs to series II, the optative has the case pattern of the aorist. A class 1 or class 3 subject is ergative, a class 1 object nominative, a class 2 subject nominative: *ninom c̣erili dac̣eros* "let Nino write the letter"; *nino dabrundes* "let Nino come back".

Bolkvadze and Kiziria say the optative is "based on the aorist forms", and the base is plain to see: the same preverb, the same stem, a different vowel. *Davc̣ere* "I wrote", *davc̣ero* "let me write".

The optative rarely stands alone in a statement. It usually follows a word that sets up the wish or the need: *unda* "must, should", *neṭav* "if only, I wish", *rom* "so that", or the particle *modi* "come on, let's". Alone, with the right intonation, it gives commands and suggestions to "him", "them" and "us" (lesson 4-17 deals with "you").

## How it is built

**Start from the aorist** and replace its vowel:

1. **Weak aorists** (*-e* for "I"): the optative vowel is *-o*. *Davc̣ere* → *davc̣ero*; *davxaṭe* → *davxaṭo*; *avašene* → *avašeno*; *vitamaše* → *vitamašo*.
2. **Strong aorists** of class 1 (*-i* for "I"): the optative vowel is usually *-a*. Wiktionary: *gavč̣eri* → *gavč̣ra*; Bolkvadze and Kiziria: *gavušvi* "I released" → *gavušva*. A few take *-o* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria's exception *davasc̣ro* "let me get ahead"), and "give" takes *-e* (lesson 4-25).
3. **Class 2 verbs**: *i-* verbs take *-o*, as weak aorists do: *davic̣ero*, *daic̣eros*. Strong class 2 verbs take *-e*: *mivide* "let me go", *mividet* "let's go", *mividnen* "let them go" (Wiktionary); *moḳvde* "I should die" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

**Then add the person endings.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that the optative always has its vowel, even in the third person, where the aorist had none:

- "I" and "you": the vowel alone: *davc̣ero*, *dac̣ero*
- "he, she": vowel + *-s*: *dac̣eros*, *gač̣ras*, *dabrundes*
- "we", "you all": vowel + *-t*: *davc̣erot*, *dac̣erot*
- "they": vowel + *-n*: *dac̣eron*, *gač̣ran*; class 2 strong verbs have *-nen*: *dabrundnen*, *mividnen*

This guide glosses the vowel OPT, with the person suffix as its own piece: *da-c̣er-o-s* `PV-write-OPT-3SG.SBJ`; the fused "they" form *da-c̣er-on* `PV-write-OPT.3PL`.

**With unda "must, should".** *Unda* is an unchanging word (lesson 6-5 treats it fully). Put it before the optative for an obligation in the present or future: *unda davc̣ero* "I must write", *ninom unda dac̣eros* "Nino should write". The case is still series II: *ninom*.

**With neṭav "if only, I wish".** *Neṭav male dabrundes!* "If only he would come back soon!" Wiktionary gives *neṭa(v)* as the particle of wishes.

**"Let's".** The first person plural alone is a suggestion: *davc̣erot!* "let's write!" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria). Wiktionary describes *modi* "come on" as a particle that softens it: *modi, vitamašot!* "come on, let's play!"

**A chain of thought** for "the children should tidy their room":

1. "Should": *unda* + optative.
2. "Tidy" is *alagebs*, class 1; aorist *daalaga*, weak (*davalage*), so the optative vowel is *-o*.
3. "They": *-on*: *daalagon*.
4. Series II, class 1: subject ergative *bavšvebma*, object nominative *otaxi*.
5. *Bavšvebma tavianti otaxi unda daalagon.*

## Where it applies

- **Obligation and advice with unda.** *Unda davc̣ero* "I must write", *unda c̣avidet* "we should go". For the past ("should have") Georgian uses *unda* with the pluperfect (lesson 4-23).
- **Requests and orders to a third person.** *Ninom moamzados* "let Nino make it". Bolkvadze and Kiziria give the pattern for "nobody should ring me today".
- **Suggestions with "we".** *Davc̣erot!*, *c̣avidet!*, *modi, vitamašot!*
- **Offers and deliberation.** *Me gavč̣ra?* "shall I cut it?"; *sad c̣avidet?* "where shall we go?"
- **Wishes.** *Neṭav dabrundes!*; the toast *gaumarǯos!* "long live!, cheers!" is an optative (Wiktionary), as in Bolkvadze and Kiziria's *gaumarǯos čvens megobrobas!* "here's to our friendship!".
- **Purpose and wanting.** After *rom* "so that" (lesson 6-6) and after verbs of wanting (lesson 4-18, 6-2): *minda c̣avide* "I want to go" (preview).
- **Negative commands.** *Ar* + optative: *ar dac̣ero!* "don't write (it)!" (lesson 4-17).

## Watch out

**Do not use the present cases.** The optative is series II. *\*Nino vaxšams moamzados* keeps the present pattern. Say *ninom vaxšami moamzados*.

**Do not use -o with every verb.** Strong class 1 verbs take *-a*: *gavč̣ra*, not *\*gavč̣ro*. Strong class 2 verbs take *-e*: *dabrundes*, not *\*dabrundos*. Derive the optative from the aorist.

**Do not drop the vowel in the third person.** In the aorist, *dac̣era* has *-a*; the optative is *dac̣eros*, not *\*dac̣ers* (that is the future).

**Optative and future look alike in some persons.** *Davc̣ero* (optative) and *davc̣er* (future) differ only in the *-o*. Listen for it: *unda davc̣er* is wrong; *unda* needs the optative.

**Not for statements of fact.** English "I will write" is the future *davc̣er*, not *\*davc̣ero*. Use the optative only for what is wished, needed or proposed.

**"They" in class 2 strong verbs is -nen.** *\*Mividen* is wrong; the optative of "they go" is *mividnen*, the same as the aorist.

**Unda does not change.** *\*Undat* or *\*vunda* for "we must" are wrong (and *minda* is a different verb, "I want", lesson 4-18). The person is shown on the optative: *unda c̣avidet*.

## Connections

Spanish speakers have the closest model: the optative covers *que lo escriba*, *escribamos* "let's write", *¡ojalá vuelva!* and *debo escribir*-type obligation (with *unda*), and much of what the Spanish present subjunctive does in subordinate clauses. Arabic has a similar *let*-form: the jussive with *li-* (*li-yaktub* "let him write"). Japanese *-ou / -mashou* covers the "let's" use only.

## Exercises

1. Make the optative of *davxaṭe*, *gavč̣eri*, *davbrundi*, *vimɣere* (first person singular).
2. Say "Let Giorgi read the newspaper" (aorist *c̣aiḳitxa*).
3. Say "Let's go to the cinema!"
4. Say "You (one person) should rest."
5. Say "If only the guests would come soon!"
6. Correct *\*Giorgi gazets unda c̣aiḳitxos*.
7. Correct *\*Mat unda gač̣ron*, meaning "they should cut it", and *\*Neṭav dabrundos*.
8. Translate *Dedam tkva, rom bavšvebma saxli daalagon* (preview: *tkva* "said", lesson 4-25).

## Worked answers

1. *Davxaṭo* (weak, *-o*); *gavč̣ra* (strong class 1, *-a*); *davbrunde* (strong class 2, *-e*); *vimɣero* (a medial verb with a weak aorist, *-o*).
2. *Giorgim gazeti c̣aiḳitxos.* Series II: ergative subject, nominative object; the optative third person *-o-s*.
3. *C̣avidet ḳinoši!* or *modi, c̣avidet ḳinoši!* First person plural optative of "go off".
4. *Unda daisveno.* *Isvenebs* is class 3; its aorist *daisvena* is weak, so the optative is *daisveno*.
5. *Neṭav sṭumrebi male movidnen!* "Come" is class 2: nominative subject, and its optative "they" is *movidnen*.
6. With *unda* the verb is optative, so the cases are series II: *giorgim gazeti unda c̣aiḳitxos*.
7. *Gač̣ris* has a strong aorist (*gavč̣eri*), so the optative has *-a*: *mat unda gač̣ran*. *Dabrundeba* is strong class 2, so *-e*: *neṭav dabrundes*.
8. "Mother said that the children should tidy the house." After *rom* the optative *daalagon*; series II, so *bavšvebma* is ergative and *saxli* nominative (lesson 6-2 for clauses with *rom*).
