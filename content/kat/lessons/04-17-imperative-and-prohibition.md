## The idea

Georgian has no separate command form. To tell someone to do something, you use a form you already know: the second person of the aorist. *Dac̣ere* means "you wrote" and also "write!". Bolkvadze and Kiziria state it directly: an order to the second person is expressed with the aorist, and only intonation (or, in writing, an exclamation mark) tells the two apart.

Why the aorist? It is the series II form of a complete action, with a preverb (lesson 4-8). An order asks for a complete action: "write it (and finish)". The same logic is behind the optative, also series II, which gives the commands to "him", "them" and "us" (lesson 4-16). So the **imperative**, the mood of commands, is not a screeve of its own in Georgian: it is the aorist for "you" and the optative for everyone else. Wiktionary's tables reflect this: the imperative row repeats *dac̣ere*, *davc̣erot*, *dac̣eret*.

Forbidding, the **prohibition**, is built differently, and here Georgian makes a distinction English does not. There are two negatives for commands, *nu* and *ar*, and they go with different screeves:

- *nu* + the future (or the present): a request or plea not to do something. Bolkvadze and Kiziria say *nu* is preferred in requests: *nu dac̣er!* "(please) don't write!". With the present it means "stop doing it": *nu c̣er!* "stop writing!".
- *ar* + the optative: a firmer instruction, "you must not": *ar dac̣ero!* "don't write (it)!".

Lingua.ge calls these "prohibitive I" (*ar* + optative) and "prohibitive II" (*nu* + future), and gives both for every verb.

## How it is built

**Positive commands to "you".**

1. Find the aorist (from the notes): *dac̣era*, *daxaṭa*, *gaaḳeta*, *itamaša*, *dabrunda*.
2. Use the second person: singular *-e* or *-i* (*dac̣ere*, *gaaḳete*, *dabrundi*), plural or polite with *-t* (*dac̣eret*, *dabrundit*).
3. Keep the case pattern of series II: a class 1 object is nominative. *C̣erili dac̣ere!* "write the letter!", not *c̣erils*.

**Object markers** go after the preverb as usual: *da-m-xaṭ-e!* "draw me!", *ga-m-aɣviʒ-e!* "wake me!", *gv-acile!*... in the aorist, *ga-gv-acil-e!* "see us off!".

**Irregular commands.** The verbs of going and coming have imperatives of their own (Wiktionary): *c̣adi!* "go (away)!", *midi!* "go (there)!", *modi!* "come!", *c̣amodi!* "come along!", with plurals *c̣adit*, *midit*, *modit*. *Modi* is also the "let's" particle of lesson 4-16.

**Commands to "him", "them" and "us"** use the optative: *dac̣eros!* "let him write!", *dac̣eron!* "let them write!", *davc̣erot!* "let's write!".

**Prohibitions.**

- *Nu* + future: *nu dac̣er*, *nu dac̣ert*; to a third person *nu dac̣ers*; "let's not" *nu davc̣ert*. Wiktionary's "let's not go" is *nu c̣avalt*.
- *Nu* + present: "stop": *nu ṭiri!* "stop crying!", *nu laṗaraḳob!* "stop talking!".
- *Ar* + optative: *ar dac̣ero*, *ar dac̣erot*, *ar dac̣eros*; Wiktionary's "don't go!" is *ar c̣axvide!*.

Note that the object case follows the screeve. With *nu* + future (series I) the object is dative: *nu dac̣er c̣erils!*. With *ar* + optative (series II) it is nominative: *c̣erili ar dac̣ero!*.

**Never *ar* + aorist.** *Ar dac̣ere* is a statement, "you did not write". To forbid, change the screeve.

**Softening.** Say *gtxov* "please" to a friend, *gtxovt* to several people or politely to one. Both are forms of *txovs* "asks (someone) for": "I ask you" (Wiktionary). *Gtxovt* is lesson 4-4 in action: *g-* "you", *-t* "plural or polite you", no *v-*.

**A chain of thought** for "children, don't open the window, it's cold!":

1. A prohibition. Firm: *ar* + optative. (A plea would be *nu* + future.)
2. *Xsnis*, future *gaxsnis*, aorist *gaxsna* (strong: *gavxseni*), optative *gaxsna*-, "you all": *gaxsnat*.
3. Series II: the object is nominative, *panǯara*.
4. *Bavšvebo, panǯara ar gaxsnat, civa!*

## Where it applies

- **Orders, requests, instructions** to one person (*dac̣ere!*), to several or politely (*dac̣eret!*).
- **Recipes and instructions** use the same forms, often in the plural.
- **Commands to others** through the optative: *dac̣eros!*, *davc̣erot!* (lesson 4-16).
- **Pleas and polite refusals** with *nu* + future: *nu c̣axval*.
- **"Stop it"** with *nu* + present: *nu ṭiri*, *nu laṗaraḳob*.
- **Firm prohibitions, signs and rules** with *ar* + optative: *ar gaxsnat!*.
- **Every class.** Class 1 *dac̣ere*, class 2 *dabrundi*, *darči*, *adeki* "get up!" (lingua.ge), class 3 *itamaše*, *daisvene* "rest!".
- **Softened requests.** *Gtxov*, *gtxovt*; Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that the numeral *erti* "one" before an order softens it into a request.

## Watch out

**Do not use the present as a command.** Spanish *¡escribe!* looks like the present; in Georgian *c̣er* is only "you write". The command is *dac̣ere*.

**Do not use ar with the aorist.** *\*Ar dac̣ere!* means "you didn't write", not "don't write!". Use *ar dac̣ero* or *nu dac̣er*.

**Do not use nu with the optative or the aorist.** *\*Nu dac̣ero* and *\*nu dac̣ere* mix the two systems. *Nu* goes with the present or future: *nu dac̣er*.

**Match the object case to the screeve.** *\*Nu gaaɣviʒeb bavšvi* gives a series I verb a series II object; *\*bavšvs ar gaaɣviʒo* the reverse. Say *nu gaaɣviʒeb bavšvs* or *bavšvi ar gaaɣviʒo*.

**Do not drop the preverb.** *\*Ḳitxe!* or *\*xaṭe!* (without preverbs) are aorists of continuing action ("keep reading"); for "read it!" use *c̣aiḳitxe!*, "draw it!" *daxaṭe!*.

**Irregular imperatives.** *\*C̣avide!* is the optative "let me go". "Go!" is *c̣adi!*; "come!" *modi!*.

**Polite is plural.** To a stranger *dac̣ere* is rude; *dac̣eret*, with *gtxovt*, is the polite form.

## Connections

Spanish distinguishes the positive *¡escribe!* from the negative *¡no escribas!*, which uses the subjunctive: Georgian *ar dac̣ero*, with the optative, is the same idea. Arabic also forbids with its jussive mood (*lā taktub* "don't write"). Korean and Japanese have separate prohibitive endings (*-지 마*, *-na*), comparable to the job of *nu*.

## Exercises

1. Tell a friend: "Draw me!", "Come back!", "Rest!".
2. Say politely to a stranger: "Sit... " is not yet possible; say "Write your name here, please."
3. Say "Let them play in the yard."
4. Say to a child "Stop shouting!"
5. Say "Please don't leave the door open" is too hard; say "Please don't close the window" (*xuravs*, future *daxuravs*).
6. Say firmly: "Don't eat the cake!" (*šeč̣ams*, aorist *šeč̣ama*, weak).
7. Correct *\*Ar dac̣ere!* and *\*Nu gaxsna!*
8. Correct *\*C̣erils dac̣ere!* and *\*C̣avide!* (meaning "go!").

## Worked answers

1. *Damxaṭe!* (*da-m-xaṭ-e*, object marker after the preverb). *Dabrundi!* (strong class 2 aorist). *Daisvene!* (class 3 with a preverb, weak aorist).
2. *Ak dac̣eret tkveni saxeli, gtxovt.* Polite plural *-t*; *tkveni saxeli* is nominative because the command is series II.
3. *Ezoši itamašon!* or *bavšvebma ezoši itamašon*. The optative third person plural *-on*; ergative subject.
4. *Nu q̇viri!* *Nu* + present for "stop doing it".
5. *Nu daxurav panǯaras, gtxov.* *Nu* + future; the future is series I, so *panǯaras* is dative.
6. *Namcxvari ar šeč̣amo!* *Ar* + optative; weak aorist, so *-o*; nominative object.
7. *Ar* + aorist is a statement. Say *ar dac̣ero!* or *nu dac̣er!*. *Nu* cannot take the optative (*gaxsna*): say *nu gaxsni!* (future) or *ar gaxsna!* (optative).
8. In the imperative the object is nominative: *c̣erili dac̣ere!*. "Go!" is irregular: *c̣adi!*.
