## The idea

"I love music" in Georgian is *musiḳa miq̇vars*. Look at the pieces. *Musiḳa* is nominative. The verb ends in *-s*, the third-person subject suffix. And "I" is not *v-*: it is *m-*, the object marker "me". Taken literally, the sentence says "music is dear to me": music is the grammatical subject, and the person who loves is marked like an object.

This is the class of **indirect verbs** (class 4, lesson 4-9): verbs of feeling, wanting, having, and bodily states, in which the person who feels (the **experiencer**) is in the dative and marked on the verb with the object-set prefixes *m-*, *g-*, *gv-*, and the thing felt (the **stimulus**) is in the nominative and agrees with the subject-set suffixes. Bolkvadze and Kiziria call them verbs whose "grammatical and actual" persons do not coincide: the real subject (the one who loves) is the grammatical object, and the real object (what is loved) is the grammatical subject. Their list includes *makvs* "I have", *miq̇vars* "I love", *mʒuls* "I hate", *mšia* "I am hungry"; they count about forty such verbs of feeling.

The swap of roles between the two marker sets is called **inversion**. It is not peculiar to Georgian: Spanish *me gusta la música* has exactly this structure. "Music" is the subject (*la música gusta*, *las canciones gustan*), and the person who likes it is the indirect object *me*. Georgian *musiḳa momc̣ons* "I like music" is word for word the Spanish sentence.

In class 4 the pattern holds in every series: the experiencer is always dative. There is no ergative, because there is no doer acting on anything.

**How this guide glosses inversion.** From here on, the glosses label each marker by its set, not by its role: *m-* is always `1SG.OBJ`, the final *-s* always `3SG.SBJ`, whatever they mean in the sentence. So *m-i-q̇var-s* is glossed `1SG.OBJ-OV-love-3SG.SBJ`, and the literal line spells out the inversion: "he/she/it is loved to me". Bolkvadze and Kiziria analyse the prefixes in the same way, as object markers that refer to the real subject. The advantage is that each prefix keeps one gloss across the whole guide; the cost is that you must read the literal line to see who loves whom. The same convention applies to the perfect (lesson 4-22), where the doer of a transitive verb is marked the same way.

## How it is built

**The four verbs.** From lingua.ge (present), with the experiencer marker in front:

- "love": *miq̇vars*, *giq̇vars*, *uq̇vars*, *gviq̇vars*, *giq̇vart*, *uq̇vart*
- "have (a thing)": *makvs*, *gakvs*, *akvs*, *gvakvs*, *gakvt*, *akvt*
- "want": *minda*, *ginda*, *unda*, *gvinda*, *gindat*, *undat*
- "like": *momc̣ons*, *mogc̣ons*, *mosc̣ons*, *mogvc̣ons*, *mogc̣ont*, *mosc̣ont*

Read across: *m-* "me", *g-* "you", *gv-* "us", and for "him, her, them" a vowel *u-* (*uq̇vars*, *unda*), nothing (*akvs*), or *s-* (*mosc̣ons*). The vowel before the root is a version vowel (lesson 4-21): *i-* with "me, you, us", *u-* with "him, her". *Mosc̣ons* keeps the preverb *mo-* even in the present, one of the few verbs Bolkvadze and Kiziria list as doing so.

**The ending.** The suffix agrees with the thing: *-s* (*miq̇vars*), or *-a* in *minda*. When the experiencer is plural "you" or "they", the suffix is replaced by *-t*: *giq̇vart* "you all love", *uq̇vart* "they love". Bolkvadze and Kiziria's paradigm of *gonia* "it seems to someone" shows the same: *hgonia* "it seems to him", *hgoniat* "it seems to them". This is the one place where *-t* marks a third-person plural: here it pluralises the dative experiencer, the person who feels.

**When the thing loved is "me" or "you".** The stimulus is then first or second person, and the verb uses the endings of "be": Wiktionary gives *miq̇varxar* "I love you" and *momc̣onxar* "I like you". Read them as "you are dear to me", "you please me".

**Cases.**

1. The experiencer: dative. *Ninos* (vowel stem + *-s*), *bavšvebs*, *mas*, *mat*; *me*, *šen*, *čven*, *tkven* unchanged.
2. The thing: nominative. *Musiḳa*, *c̣igni*, *q̇ava*.
3. In every series. *Ninos musiḳa uq̇varda* "Nino loved music"; *ninos musiḳa eq̇vareba* "Nino will love music".

**Other screeves.** Lingua.ge gives imperfects with *-d-*: *miq̇varda* "I loved", *mindoda* "I wanted", *momc̣onda* "I liked". "Have" changes its stem: *mkonda* "I had", *hkonda* "he had". The future is built with *e-* ... *-eb-a*: *meq̇vareba*, *mekneba* "I will have", *mendomeba* "I will want", *momec̣oneba* "I will like". "Love", "have" and "want" have no aorist; for the past, Georgian uses the imperfect. *Mosc̣ons* has one: *momec̣ona* "I liked (it)", "it pleased me".

**A chain of thought** for "the children love ice cream":

1. "Love": class 4, *uq̇vars*. Inverted: the children are the experiencers, ice cream the thing.
2. Experiencer in the dative: *bavšvebs*.
3. Thing in the nominative: *naq̇ini*.
4. The experiencer "they" is plural: *-t* replaces *-s*: *uq̇vart*.
5. *Bavšvebs naq̇ini uq̇vart.*

## Where it applies

- **Feelings:** *uq̇vars* "loves", *mosc̣ons* "likes", *sʒuls* "hates".
- **Wanting:** *unda* "wants": with a noun (*q̇ava minda*) or with an optative verb, *minda c̣avide* "I want to go" (lesson 6-2).
- **Having:** *akvs* for things; for people and animals, *hq̇avs* (lesson 4-19).
- **Bodily states:** *šia* "is hungry", *sc̣q̇uria* "is thirsty".
- **Thinking and seeming:** *hgonia* "thinks, believes (perhaps wrongly)", "it seems to him" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria's *mgonia*).
- **Every screeve.** The experiencer stays dative throughout: imperfect *miq̇varda*, future *meq̇vareba*.
- **The perfect of class 1 and 3 verbs** uses the same inversion (lesson 4-22): *damic̣eria* "I have (apparently) written", with *m-*.

## Watch out

**Do not put the experiencer in the nominative.** English "I love" and Spanish *yo amo* push you to *\*me vuq̇var* or *\*nino uq̇vars q̇avas*. The experiencer is dative and the thing nominative: *ninos q̇ava uq̇vars*.

**Do not use v- for "I".** *\*Vq̇var*, *\*vakv* treat "I" as a subject marker. The experiencer takes *m-*: *miq̇vars*, *makvs*.

**The verb agrees with the thing, not with you.** *Me c̣ignebi makvs* "I have books": things, so a singular verb. The ending does not change because you are "I".

**Plural experiencers take -t.** For "they love" lingua.ge gives *uq̇vart*, and for "you all love" *giq̇vart*, never *\*giq̇varst*: the *-t* replaces the *-s*. This is the one case where *-t* goes with a third person.

**"I love you" needs the "be" ending.** *\*Miq̇vars šen* treats "you" as a third person. Say *miq̇varxar*.

**Do not look for an aorist of "love", "have", "want".** *\*Miq̇vara* does not exist. For the past, use the imperfect: *miq̇varda*, *mkonda*, *mindoda*.

**Do not drop mo- from "like".** *\*Mc̣ons* is not a word; the present already has the preverb: *momc̣ons*.

**Unda "must" and unda "wants" are the same word.** *Mas unda c̣avides* can mean "he must go" or "he wants to go"; context and the dative *mas* (an experiencer) point to "wants". With no dative experiencer, *unda* is "must" (lesson 4-16).

## Connections

Spanish *me gusta*, *te gusta*, *le gusta* is the model for *momc̣ons*, *mogc̣ons*, *mosc̣ons*, down to the agreement: *me gustan los libros*, with the verb agreeing with the books. Korean has dative-experiencer "have": *나에게 책이 있다* "to me a book exists", like *makvs c̣igni*. Japanese *watashi wa X ga suki da* marks the thing liked with *ga*, the subject particle, which is the Georgian idea again: the thing is the grammatical subject.

## Exercises

1. Say to a friend "Do you love chess?"
2. Say "Giorgi has a lot of money."
3. Say "What do you (polite) want?"
4. Say "My parents like this restaurant."
5. Say "We wanted coffee" (imperfect of *unda*).
6. Correct *\*Me miq̇vars xinḳals*.
7. Correct *\*Nino q̇avas uq̇vars* and *\*Vminda čai*.
8. Translate *Bavšvobaši šoḳoladi ar miq̇varda, axla ḳi ʒalian miq̇vars.*

## Worked answers

1. *Č̣adraḳi giq̇vars?* The experiencer "you" is *g-*; the game is nominative; the verb agrees with it, *-s*.
2. *Giorgis bevri puli akvs.* Experiencer dative *giorgis*; *puli* nominative; *akvs* for a thing.
3. *Ra gindat?* *G-* "you", *-t* for polite plural, replacing the *-a*.
4. *Čems mšoblebs es resṭorani mosc̣ont.* Dative plural experiencer (*čems* before a dative noun, lesson 3-8); the restaurant nominative; *-t* for the plural experiencer.
5. *Q̇ava gvindoda.* *Gv-* "us"; the imperfect *-oda* agrees with the coffee; "want" has no aorist, so the imperfect is the past.
6. The experiencer is *me* (no change) but the thing must be nominative: *me miq̇vars xinḳali*, or more naturally *xinḳali miq̇vars*.
7. *Ninos q̇ava uq̇vars*: experiencer dative, coffee nominative. "I want" has no *v-*: *čai minda*.
8. "As a child I didn't like chocolate, but now I love it very much." Imperfect *miq̇varda* for the past (no aorist), present *miq̇vars*; *šoḳoladi* is nominative in both.
