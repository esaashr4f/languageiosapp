## The idea

Lesson 4-18 gave *akvs* "has". It was used only for things, and that was deliberate. For a person or an animal, Georgian uses a different verb: *hq̇avs*. "I have a book" is *c̣igni makvs*; "I have a sister" is *da mq̇avs*; "I have a dog" is *ʒaɣli mq̇avs*.

The distinction is **animacy**: whether a noun names something alive (a person or an animal) or a thing. Bolkvadze and Kiziria tie it to a split you met in lesson 3-11: Georgian asks *vin?* "who?" about people and *ra?* "what?" about everything else, and it chooses between the two verbs of having in a similar way. *Hq̇avs* is for "humans, as well as everything animate"; *akvs* "only for inanimate objects". So animals fall with people for having, although you ask *ra?* about them.

There is one well-known exception. Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that vehicles (a car, a bicycle, a boat) take *hq̇avs*: *ʒveli mankana mq̇avs* "I have an old car". The question word stays *ra?*: *ra gq̇avs?* "what (car) have you got?"

The same split runs through another group of common verbs: carrying, bringing and taking. Bolkvadze and Kiziria give the roots *-q̇van-* for living beings and *-ṭan-* or *-ɣ-* for things: *deda bavšvs sḳolaši c̣aiq̇vans* "Mother will take the child to school", but *deda c̣igns sḳolaši c̣aiɣebs* "Mother will take the book to school". So animacy is not a quirk of one verb: it is a small system.

Why should Georgian care? The sources describe the split without explaining its origin, and this guide does not speculate. What matters for you is that the choice is automatic once you ask: is this alive?

## How it is built

**Hq̇avs** is a class 4 verb, exactly like *akvs*: the owner is dative and marked with *m-*, *g-*, *gv-*; the person or animal owned is nominative. From lingua.ge:

- *mq̇avs* "I have", *gq̇avs* "you have", *hq̇avs* "he, she has"
- *gvq̇avs* "we have", *gq̇avt* "you all have", *hq̇avt* "they have"

The *h-* of *hq̇avs* is the third-person marker of lesson 4-20, written before *q̇*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria list *h-* before *b, p, ṗ, g, k, ḳ, q̇*.

**Other screeves** (lingua.ge; Bolkvadze and Kiziria): imperfect *mq̇avda* "I had (someone)", future *meq̇oleba* "I will have", perfect *mq̇olia*. For *akvs*: imperfect *mkonda*, future *mekneba*, perfect *mkonia*. Neither has a true aorist; the imperfect serves for the past.

**Which verb?**

1. Is the thing owned a person, an animal, a bird, a fish? *Hq̇avs*.
2. Is it a car, bicycle or other vehicle? *Hq̇avs* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria's exception).
3. Anything else, including time, ideas, money, work, a cold? *Akvs*.

**Carrying, bringing, taking.** The present tense is again inverted (class 4), with *akvs* inside it; the other screeves are transitive (class 1) with a new stem. Lingua.ge and Bolkvadze and Kiziria give:

- things: *moakvs* "brings", future *moiṭans*, aorist *moiṭana*; *miakvs* "takes (there)", future *miiṭans* "will take there" or *c̣aiɣebs* "will take away", aorist *c̣aiɣo*
- people and animals: *mohq̇avs* "brings (someone)", future *moiq̇vans*, aorist *moiq̇vana*; *mihq̇avs* "takes (someone) there", future *c̣aiq̇vans* "will take away"

So "I'm bringing a book" is *c̣igni momakvs*, with *m-* for "I" and the book in the nominative, but "I'll bring the book" is *c̣igns moviṭan*, a class 1 future with the book in the dative.

**A chain of thought** for "Do you have friends here?":

1. Friends are people: *hq̇avs*.
2. Class 4: "you" is the owner, *g-*; the people owned are nominative, *megobrebi*.
3. The verb stays singular: Bolkvadze and Kiziria write *megobrebi mq̇avs* "I have friends", with a plural noun and *mq̇avs*.
4. *Ak megobrebi gq̇avs?*

## Where it applies

- **People:** family, friends, colleagues, children, guests: *švilebi hq̇avs* "he has children".
- **Animals:** pets, livestock, birds, fish: *ʒroxa hq̇avt* "they have a cow".
- **Vehicles:** car, bicycle, boat: *velosiṗedi mq̇avs*.
- **Things and abstractions:** everything else with *akvs*: *bina*, *puli*, *dro*, *idea*, *gegma*, *ṭeleponi*.
- **Bringing and taking:** *-q̇van-* for living beings and vehicles, *-ṭan-* and *-ɣ-* for things: *c̣aiq̇vans* / *c̣aiɣebs*, *moiq̇vana* / *moiṭana*.
- **Every screeve.** Imperfect *mq̇avda* / *mkonda*, future *meq̇oleba* / *mekneba*.

## Watch out

**Do not use akvs for people.** English, Spanish and French have one verb for having, so *\*da makvs* feels natural. It is wrong: *da mq̇avs*.

**Do not ask vin? about animals.** Animals take *hq̇avs*, but the question word is *ra?*: *ra gq̇avs, ʒaɣli tu ḳaṭa?* "what have you got, a dog or a cat?"

**Do not use akvs for a car.** *\*Mankana makvs* follows the "thing" rule, but Bolkvadze and Kiziria give *mankana mq̇avs*.

**Do not put the owner in the nominative.** Both verbs are class 4: *\*nino ḳaṭas hq̇avs* is the class 1 pattern. Say *ninos ḳaṭa hq̇avs*.

**The present of "bring" is inverted; the future and past are not.** *\*Me c̣igns moṭan* and *\*c̣igni moviṭan* mix the two. Present: *c̣igni momakvs* (book nominative). Future: *c̣igns moviṭan* (book dative). Aorist: *c̣igni moviṭane* (book nominative, I ergative).

**Do not use -ṭan- for people.** *\*Bavšvi moviṭane* treats the child as luggage. Say *bavšvi moviq̇vane*.

**Do not write ჰ- for "me" or "you".** The *h-* is only the third person: *mq̇avs*, *gq̇avs*, not *\*mhq̇avs*.

## Connections

Japanese makes the same cut between living and non-living with its verbs of existence: *iru* for people and animals, *aru* for things, and "I have a sister" is *ane ga iru*, "I have a car" *kuruma ga aru*. Georgian differs only in putting cars with the living. Korean *있다* covers both, and Spanish *tener* knows no such split.

## Exercises

1. Say "I have a cat and a dog" and "I have a flat and a garden".
2. Say "Do you (one person) have a bicycle?"
3. Say "Giorgi had twins" (imperfect).
4. Say "We'll have a guest tomorrow."
5. Say "I'm bringing my friend" and "I'll bring the suitcase".
6. Correct *\*Ninos ori ʒma akvs* and *\*Čven mankana gvakvs*.
7. Correct *\*Deda bavšvs sḳolaši c̣aiɣebs.*
8. Translate *Ra gq̇avs, ʒaɣli tu ḳaṭa? — Arc erti: mxolod tevzebi mq̇avs.*

## Worked answers

1. *Ḳaṭa da ʒaɣli mq̇avs.* *Bina da baɣi makvs.* Animals with *hq̇avs*, things with *akvs*; the owner is *m-* in both.
2. *Velosiṗedi gq̇avs?* A vehicle: *hq̇avs*; "you" *g-*.
3. *Giorgis ṭq̇uṗebi hq̇avda.* Dative owner; the imperfect is the past of "have".
4. *Xval sṭumari gveq̇oleba.* A person, so the future of *hq̇avs*; *gv-* "us".
5. *Megobari momq̇avs.* (Inverted present with *-q̇av-*: lingua.ge's *momq̇avs*.) *Čemodans moviṭan.* (Future of "bring a thing": class 1, *čemodans* dative.)
6. Brothers are people: *ninos ori ʒma hq̇avs*. A car is a vehicle: *čven mankana gvq̇avs*.
7. A child is alive: *-q̇van-*: *deda bavšvs sḳolaši c̣aiq̇vans*.
8. "What have you got, a dog or a cat? — Neither: I only have fish." Animals take *hq̇avs* but the question word *ra?*; fish are animals too, so *mq̇avs*.
