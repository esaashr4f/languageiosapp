## The idea

Since part 2 the glosses have labelled a vowel before the root NV, SV or OV and promised an explanation. Here it is. Slot 3 of the template (lesson 4-1) holds a single vowel, and that vowel says **whom the action is for, or whose the thing is**. This is **version**: the marking, by a vowel before the root, of the relation between the action and the people involved. Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe it as the verb indicating the "goal-specific direction" of the action.

Their key example is one verb in three forms:

- *v-a-šeneb* "I build (it)": nothing said about whom the house is for
- *v-i-šeneb* "I build it for myself"
- *v-u-šeneb* "I build it for him" (for my son, say)

The three vowels are the three versions:

- **neutral version** (NV), *a-* or no vowel: the action is not directed at anyone in particular
- **subjective version** (SV), *i-*: the action is for the subject itself, or acts on the subject's own body or belongings
- **objective version** (OV): the action is for someone else, the indirect object, or the thing belongs to them. The vowel is *u-* when that person is third person, *i-* when it is first or second person (after *m-*, *g-*, *gv-*)

So OV uses the same vowel *i-* as SV, but with a different person prefix in front of it. Bolkvadze and Kiziria give the test: a verb with two persons and *i-* is subjective (*avišene* "I built it for myself"); a verb with three persons and *i-* is objective (*amišena* "he built it for me").

Version also has a second, more mechanical side. Many verbs have a version vowel as part of their form, with no clear meaning: *ḳitxulobs* has the future *c̣a-i-ḳitxavs*, *q̇idulobs* has *i-q̇idis*, the medial verbs have *i-* in the future and aorist (lesson 4-15), and the indirect verbs have *i-*/*u-* in *miq̇vars* / *uq̇vars* (lesson 4-18). Wiktionary describes Georgian verbs "with neutral versioner", "with subjective versioner" and so on, as lexical variants. Analysts differ here: Aronson avoids the term "version" and treats these vowels as separate functions; Hewitt and Bolkvadze and Kiziria use it. This guide uses "version" for the vowel slot and its three values, and tells you when a vowel has a real meaning.

## How it is built

**Neutral.** The plain verb: *vc̣er*, *vxaṭav*, *vašeneb*, *vaḳeteb*. Some verbs have *a-*, some have nothing; Bolkvadze and Kiziria call both neutral.

**Subjective: i- for oneself.**

1. Replace or add the vowel: *a-šen-eb* → *i-šen-eb*; *ḳer-av* → *i-ḳer-av*.
2. Keep everything else: *v-i-šeneb* "I build for myself", *i-ḳeravs* "she sews for herself" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
3. Bolkvadze and Kiziria list the typical uses: acting on one's own body or clothes (*tavi daibana* "washed his hair", *prčxilebi daič̣ra* "cut his nails", *kudi daixura* "put on his hat"), and getting something for oneself (*puli isesxa* "borrowed money", *moiṗara* "stole").

**Objective: u- or i- for someone else.**

1. The beneficiary is an indirect object: dative noun, and on the verb *m-*, *g-*, *gv-* or (for the third person) nothing.
2. Third person: the vowel *u-*, and no *s-* or *h-*: *u-ḳer-av-s* "she sews it for her". Bolkvadze and Kiziria: the *u-* is not preceded by a third-person marker.
3. First or second person: *m-i-*, *g-i-*, *gv-i-*: *deda m-i-ḳer-av-s* "Mother sews it for me".
4. In series I the direct object is dative; in series II nominative: *dedam ḳaba šemiḳera* "Mother sewed a dress for me" (future *šeḳeravs*).

**Possession.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria stress that the objective version can mean "belongs to": *deda kališvils ḳabas uḳeravs* is "Mother sews the daughter's dress / sews a dress for the daughter". With intransitive verbs this is the only reading: Bolkvadze and Kiziria's *mas cxeni uḳvdeba* "his horse is dying" (lit. "the horse is dying on him").

**Verbs with a recipient.** Many verbs of "to" use *u-* / *i-* as their normal way of marking the recipient: *u-reḳ-av-s* "rings him" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria's *vureḳav* "I call him"), *mi-gzavn-i-s* "sends to me" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria). With these, *u-* replaces the *s-*/*h-* of lesson 4-20.

**A chain of thought** for "Father built a house for his son":

1. "Build" is *ašenebs*, future *aašenebs*, aorist *aašena*.
2. For someone else, a third person: objective version *u-*: *a-u-šen-a*.
3. Series II: father ergative *mamam*, house nominative *saxli*, son dative *važs*.
4. *Mamam važs saxli aušena.*

## Where it applies

- **Subjective version:** actions on one's own body, clothes and belongings (*daibana*, *gaixada* "took off (his coat)", lingua.ge's *gaixdis*); getting something for oneself (*isesxa* "borrowed", *moiṗara* "stole", *iq̇ida* "bought").
- **Objective version:** doing something for someone (*uḳeravs*, *aušena*); doing something to someone's possession (*xelebi daubana*); intransitives meaning "someone's X happened" (*mouḳvda*).
- **Recipients.** Verbs like *ureḳavs* "rings", *migzavnis* "sends me" use the objective vowel for the person addressed.
- **Lexical vowels.** Many verbs carry *i-* or *u-* by habit, with no clear "for whom": *c̣aiḳitxavs*, *iq̇idis*, the medial futures *itamašebs*; the indirect verbs *miq̇vars*, *uq̇vars*. Learn these as part of the verb.
- **Series III.** The perfect of transitive verbs uses *i-* / *u-* systematically (*damic̣eria*, *dauc̣eria*, lesson 4-22), and the pluperfect *e-* (lesson 4-23).
- **The locative version** with *a-* "on, onto" (*ac̣ers* "writes on it") is rarer and has its own lesson (7-5).

## Watch out

**Do not use a possessive where version does it.** English "the child washed his hands" invites *\*bavšvma tavisi xelebi dabana*. Georgian says *bavšvma xelebi daibana*: the *i-* already says "his own".

**i- after m-, g-, gv- is objective, not subjective.** *Miḳeravs* is "she sews for me", not "I sew for myself". "I sew for myself" is *viḳerav*.

**Do not add s- or h- before u-.** *\*Suḳeravs*, *\*hušenebs* double the third-person marking. Bolkvadze and Kiziria: *u-* takes no third-person marker.

**Do not use u- for "me" or "you".** *\*Muḳeravs* is wrong; the first and second persons take *i-*: *miḳeravs*, *giḳeravs*.

**Version is not a reflexive.** *Višeneb* means "I build (something) for myself", not "I build myself". For "myself" as the object, Georgian uses *tavi* (lesson 5-9).

**Not every i- is subjective in meaning.** In *iq̇idis* "will buy" or *itamašebs* "will play" the *i-* is part of the form. Do not translate it as "for oneself".

**Keep the beneficiary dative in the aorist.** *\*Mamam važi saxli aušena* puts two nominatives. Only the direct object changes: *mamam važs saxli aušena*.

## Connections

Spanish expresses the same three relations with pronouns: neutral *construyo una casa*, subjective *me construyo una casa*, objective *le construyo una casa*. Even the "possessive" use matches: *se lavó las manos* "he washed his hands", *le lavó las manos al niño* "she washed the child's hands", with no possessive in either. Georgian puts in a vowel what Spanish puts in a clitic. Ancient Greek's middle voice (*louomai* "I wash myself") is another parallel.

## Exercises

1. Say "I'm sewing a dress", "I'm sewing myself a dress", "I'm sewing her a dress".
2. Say "Grandfather is building us a house" (*gv-i-*).
3. Say "Mother took off her coat" and "Mother took the child's coat off" (lingua.ge: *gaixada*; objective *gauxada*).
4. Say "My neighbour borrowed money."
5. Why does *miḳeravs* mean "she sews for me" and not "I sew for myself"?
6. Correct *\*Bavšvma tavisi xelebi dabana* (meaning "washed his hands").
7. Correct *\*Mamam švils saxli sušena* (meaning "built for his son").
8. Translate *Bebiam švilišvils ṗerangi šeuḳera.*

## Worked answers

1. *Ḳabas vḳerav* (neutral). *Ḳabas viḳerav* (subjective *i-*). *Ḳabas vuḳerav* (objective *u-*, third person).
2. *Babua saxls gvišenebs.* *Gv-* "us" + *i-* (objective, first person) + the stem.
3. *Dedam ṗalṭo gaixada* (subjective: her own coat). *Dedam bavšvs ṗalṭo gauxada* (objective *u-*: the child's coat; the child dative).
4. *Čemma mezobelma puli isesxa.* Subjective *i-* for getting something for oneself; ergative subject, nominative object.
5. The *m-* is an object-set marker ("me" as indirect object) and the subject is the third-person *-s*. With *m-* before it, *i-* is objective: for me. "I sew for myself" has *v-* and *i-*: *viḳerav*.
6. The subjective version already says "his own": *bavšvma xelebi daibana*.
7. The third-person objective vowel *u-* takes no *s-*: *mamam švils saxli aušena*.
8. "Grandmother sewed a shirt for her grandchild." *Še-u-ḳer-a*: preverb, objective *u-* (the beneficiary is third person), root, aorist *-a*. (*Švilišvili* "grandchild" is glossed here only.)
