## The idea

The **perfect** is the first screeve of series III. In form it brings two surprises; in meaning, a third.

**The first surprise is inversion.** In the perfect, a transitive or medial verb marks its doer the way an indirect verb marks its experiencer (lesson 4-18): with the object-set prefixes *m-*, *g-*, *gv-* and the dative case. The thing done is nominative and agrees with the subject-set suffix. Bolkvadze and Kiziria set the three series side by side: *vc̣er* "I write (it)", *davc̣ere* "I wrote (it)", *damic̣eria* "I have written (it)". In the first two, "I" is *v-*; in the third, *m-*. So *ninos c̣erili dauc̣eria* "Nino has written the letter" has Nino in the dative and the letter in the nominative, exactly like *ninos c̣igni akvs* "Nino has a book". This is the dative subject of series III that lesson 4-8 promised.

**The second surprise is the vowel.** The perfect always has a version vowel, *i-* after *m-*, *g-*, *gv-* and *u-* for a third-person doer: *da-m-i-c̣eria*, *da-u-c̣eria*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that here the vowel "cannot express the category of version": it is part of the perfect's shape, not a "for whom". This guide still glosses it OV, by the marker-set convention of lesson 4-18.

**The third point is meaning.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria write that the perfect "often implies that the subject did not witness or was not aware of the action being completed", and that its Georgian name, თურმეობითი, comes from *turme* "apparently". That is an **evidential** meaning: the form says something about how the speaker knows. *Ninom c̣erili dac̣era* (aorist) reports an event as seen or known; *ninos c̣erili dauc̣eria* (perfect) reports it as inferred or heard: "Nino has written the letter, it seems". But the perfect is not always evidential. Bolkvadze and Kiziria show that with *ar* it can be a plain statement: *ǯer ar minaxavs* "I haven't seen it yet" simply states that it has not happened, with no hint of hearsay. And with *uḳve* "already" it often means plain English "have done". Descriptions differ on how central the evidential sense is; this guide gives both readings and lets the context decide.

## How it is built

**Start from the verb's preverb and stem.** Then:

1. Preverb (the future's): *da-*, *a-*, *ga-*, *še-*, *c̣a-*.
2. The doer's object-set prefix: *m-* "I", *g-* "you", *gv-* "we"; nothing for "he, she, they".
3. The vowel: *i-* after *m-*, *g-*, *gv-*; *u-* for the third person.
4. The stem, and an ending. Bolkvadze and Kiziria's two main types:
   - most verbs: stem + *-ia*: *da-m-i-c̣er-i-a*, *ga-m-i-ḳet-eb-i-a* "I have done", *še-m-i-č̣am-i-a* "I have eaten" (Wiktionary)
   - verbs in *-av* and *-am*: stem with the suffix + *-s*: *da-m-i-xaṭ-av-s* "I have drawn", *c̣a-m-i-ḳitx-av-s* "I have read" (Wiktionary). Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that these also have forms in *-ia* (*damixaṭia*), less often.
5. For a plural doer "you all" and "they", add *-t*: *dagic̣eriat*, *dauc̣eriat*; *dauxaṭavt* (Wiktionary).

The thematic suffix *-eb* stays when the root has a vowel (*a-u-šen-eb-i-a* "has built"); other suffixes drop or stay as the vocabulary notes show (they give the perfect of every verb).

**Medial verbs** (class 3) form the perfect the same way, usually with no preverb: *mitamašia* "I have played", *umušavia* "he has worked", *uṭiria* "he has cried" (Wiktionary, lingua.ge). Their doer is dative too: *ninos umɣeria* "Nino has sung".

**Cases.** Series III: the doer dative, the thing done nominative. Bolkvadze and Kiziria's sentence: *bavšvs saxli dauxaṭavs* "the child has painted a house". An indirect object of the other series moves into a phrase with *-tvis*: Bolkvadze and Kiziria's *ḳacs saxli aušenebia švilistvis* "the man has built a house for his son". That is the topic of lesson 7-4; until then this guide's perfects have no third participant.

**Glossing.** As the dossier fixes: *da-m-i-c̣er-i-a* `PV-1SG.OBJ-OV-write-PRF-3SG.SBJ`; for the *-av* type, *da-m-i-xaṭ-av-s* `PV-1SG.OBJ-OV-draw-TS-3SG.SBJ`, with "perfect" in the note.

**A chain of thought** for "the children have (apparently) eaten the whole cake":

1. "Eat", class 1; perfect: preverb *še-*, stem *č̣am*, ending *-ia*.
2. The doers are third person: no prefix, vowel *u-*: *še-u-č̣am-i-a*.
3. Plural doers: *-t*: *šeuč̣amiat*.
4. Series III: doers dative *bavšvebs*, cake nominative *mteli namcxvari*.
5. *Bavšvebs mteli namcxvari šeuč̣amiat!*

## Where it applies

- **Inference.** You see the result and conclude the action: *šeuč̣amiat!*.
- **Hearsay and reported history.** What you were told or read: *turme umušavia*; Bolkvadze and Kiziria's church built in the tenth century.
- **Experience.** "Have you ever...?": *odesme gimɣeria?*
- **Result and completion, often with uḳve.** *Uḳve damic̣eria* "I've already written it".
- **"Not yet".** *Ǯer ar c̣amiḳitxavs*: Bolkvadze and Kiziria contrast it with the aorist *ar vnaxe* "I didn't see it (and didn't want to)".
- **Class 1 and class 3 verbs** take the dative doer. Class 2 verbs make their perfect differently, from the participle (lesson 4-24); class 4 verbs keep their dative experiencer and have their own perfects (*hq̇varebia*, lingua.ge).
- **Story-telling about what you did not witness** strings perfects together, often with *turme* (lesson 7-8).

## Watch out

**Do not use v- in the perfect.** *\*Davc̣eria* puts the series I and II "I" marker into series III. "I have written" is *damic̣eria*.

**Do not keep the ergative.** *\*Ninom c̣erili dauc̣eria* carries the aorist case over. In series III the doer is dative: *ninos*.

**Do not use the perfect for things you saw.** English "I have written" is a plain present perfect; Georgian *damic̣eria* can carry "it seems". For an event you did and remember, the aorist is usual: *gušin davc̣ere* "I wrote it yesterday".

**Do not translate every perfect as "apparently".** With *ar*, *ǯer*, *uḳve* and *odesme* the perfect is often plain; Bolkvadze and Kiziria's *ǯer ar minaxavs* is not hearsay.

**Do not use -t for a plural object.** *Dauc̣eriat* means "they have written (it)", with a plural doer. "He has written the letters" is still *c̣erilebi dauc̣eria*.

**Do not drop the version vowel.** *\*Damc̣eria*, without *i-*, does not exist: the vowel is part of every class 1 perfect.

**No indirect object prefix in the perfect.** *\*Mic̣eria* for "he has written to me" is wrong; the perfect cannot mark a third participant on the verb (Bolkvadze and Kiziria). Lesson 7-4 shows what to do instead.

## Connections

Turkish has a very close match: its *-miş* past is used for what you infer or were told (*gelmiş* "he has apparently come"), against *-di* for what you witnessed. Bulgarian and Macedonian have a similar "renarrated" mood. English "it seems he has..." and French *il aurait* (the journalist's conditional) are lexical substitutes. Spanish has none, and Spanish *he escrito* is never evidential, so do not let it guide you.

## Exercises

1. Make the perfect "I have ..." of *dac̣ers*, *gaaḳetebs*, *šeč̣ams*, *daxaṭavs*.
2. Say "Giorgi has (apparently) built a house."
3. Say "We have already done the homework."
4. Say "Have you (one person) ever played chess?"
5. Say "Apparently they have read this book" (plural doer).
6. Correct *\*Ninom c̣erili dauc̣eria* and *\*Me davc̣eria*.
7. Correct *\*Bavšvebs namcxvari šeuč̣amia* (meaning "the children have eaten the cake").
8. Translate *Dedas sadili moumzadebia, magram čven ǯer ar šegvič̣amia.*

## Worked answers

1. *Damic̣eria*, *gamiḳetebia*, *šemič̣amia*, *damixaṭavs*: preverb, *m-*, *i-*, stem, *-ia* or (for the *-av* verb) *-av-s* (Wiktionary).
2. *Giorgis saxli aušenebia.* Dative doer, nominative house, *a-u-šen-eb-i-a*.
3. *Davaleba uḳve gagviḳetebia.* *Gv-* "we" + *i-*; *uḳve* gives the plain "already" reading.
4. *Odesme gitamašia č̣adraḳi?* A medial verb with an object; the game is nominative; *g-i-* for "you".
5. *Turme es c̣igni c̣auḳitxavt.* Series III: the book is nominative; the *-t* is for the plural doers.
6. Series III doers are dative: *ninos c̣erili dauc̣eria*. "I" is *m-* + *i-*: *(me) damic̣eria*.
7. With plural doers the sources give *-t*: *bavšvebs namcxvari šeuč̣amiat*. Without the *-t*, *šeuč̣amia* is "he/she has eaten".
8. "Mother has (apparently) made lunch, but we haven't eaten it yet." *Dedas* dative doer; *sadili* nominative; *moumzadebia* perfect of *moamzadebs*; *šegvič̣amia* has *gv-* for "we", and with *ǯer ar* it is a plain statement.
