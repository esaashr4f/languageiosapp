## The idea

Series III has two more screeves, the **pluperfect** and the **perfect subjunctive**. Both are built the same way, both keep the perfect's inversion (the doer dative, marked with *m-*, *g-*, *gv-*), and both replace the perfect's vowel *i-*/*u-* with *e-*.

The pluperfect has two quite different jobs, and the second is the one you will use most.

**1. An earlier past, often not witnessed.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe the pluperfect, like the perfect, as reporting an unwitnessed action, but one that came before another past event. Its Georgian name is მეორე თურმეობითი, the "second *turme* form". *Turme mas misamarti uḳve daec̣era* "apparently he had already written the address".

**2. A wished-for or required past action that did not happen.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria list it with *neṭav* "if only", with *unda* "should", and in "if" clauses: *neṭav damec̣era!* "if only I had written it!", *unda damec̣era* "I should have written it". This is where English uses "should have", "I wish I had", "if I had". It is the past partner of the optative: *unda davc̣ero* "I should write" (optative) against *unda damec̣era* "I should have written" (pluperfect).

Why should a series III form express what did not happen? The sources consulted state the use but not its history. What links the two jobs is distance: the pluperfect presents an action as something not directly in the speaker's experience, either because it was not seen or because it never took place.

The **perfect subjunctive** (მესამე კავშირებითი "third subjunctive") is rare. Bolkvadze and Kiziria say it "has largely fallen out of use", replaced by the optative and the pluperfect, and survives mainly in wishes, blessings, toasts and curses, and in a few clauses of denial ("as if he had written it"). You need to recognise it more than to use it.

## How it is built

**The pluperfect** (Bolkvadze and Kiziria): the aorist stem, with the vowel *e-* in front of the root and the doer marked by the object set.

1. Preverb: *da-*, *ga-*, *a-* (as in the aorist).
2. The doer's prefix: *m-*, *g-*, *gv-*, or nothing for "he, she, they".
3. The vowel *e-*.
4. The aorist stem (thematic suffix dropped): *c̣er*, *xaṭ*, *gzavn*.
5. The ending *-a* (agreeing with a third-person thing), and *-t* for a plural doer "you all", "they".

Wiktionary: *damec̣era* "I had written / should have written", *dagec̣era*, *daec̣era*, *dagvec̣era*, *dagec̣erat*, *daec̣erat*; *damexaṭa* "I had drawn"; *gamegzavna* "I had sent". Medial verbs: *metamaša* "I had played" (Wiktionary), *memušava* (lingua.ge).

Verbs whose perfect keeps *-eb* add *-in-* in the pluperfect: *amešenebina* "I had built" (Wiktionary), *gamiḳetebia* → *gameḳetebina* "I had done". Recognise the *-ina* as part of the shape.

**The perfect subjunctive**: the same, but with the optative's endings: *-o-s* for a third-person thing, *-o-t* for a plural doer. *Damec̣eros*, *dagec̣eros*, *daec̣eros*, *dagvec̣eros*, *dagec̣erot*, *daec̣erot* (Wiktionary). *-Eb* verbs: *amešenebinos*.

**Glossing.** This guide glosses the pluperfect's *e-* as PLUP and the ending as the aorist ending it is: *da-m-e-c̣er-a* `PV-1SG.OBJ-PLUP-write-AOR.3SG`. In the perfect subjunctive the same *e-* is glossed PLUP and the ending as optative: *da-m-e-c̣er-o-s* `PV-1SG.OBJ-PLUP-write-OPT-3SG.SBJ`. The literal line shows the inversion.

**Cases.** Series III: the doer dative, the thing nominative: *ninos c̣erili daec̣era*.

**A chain of thought** for "we should have sent the parcel yesterday":

1. "Should have": *unda* + pluperfect.
2. "Send": aorist *gagzavna*; pluperfect: preverb *ga-*, doer "we" *gv-*, *e-*, stem *gzavn*, *-a*: *gagvegzavna*.
3. The parcel is nominative: *amanati*.
4. *Amanati gušin unda gagvegzavna.*

## Where it applies

- **Missed obligations:** *unda damec̣era* "I should have written"; *ar unda meq̇ida* "I shouldn't have bought".
- **Probability about the past:** Bolkvadze and Kiziria's *unda aešenebinat* "they must have built it": *unda* + pluperfect can also mean "must have (probably)".
- **Wishes about the past:** *neṭav damec̣era!*
- **Unreal past conditions:** "if I had written ..., I would have ...": *rom* + pluperfect, with the conditional (lesson 6-9). Bolkvadze and Kiziria's pattern: *c̣erili rom adre damec̣era, uḳve gavgzavnidi*.
- **Earlier, unwitnessed past:** with *turme*, *uḳve*.
- **The perfect subjunctive:** blessings and toasts (*gecxovrot!*), curses, and denials after *titkos* "as if" and *ar mgonia* "I don't think". Elsewhere use the optative.

## Watch out

**Do not use the aorist for "should have".** *\*Unda davc̣ere* mixes *unda* with a statement form. *Unda* takes the optative (present or future duty) or the pluperfect (past duty): *unda damec̣era*.

**Do not use the ergative.** *\*Ninom c̣erili unda daec̣era* carries the aorist case over. The pluperfect is series III: *ninos*.

**Do not use v- for "I".** *\*Unda davec̣era* is wrong; "I" is *m-*: *damec̣era*.

**e-, not i-.** *\*Unda damic̣eria* uses the perfect. The pluperfect has *e-*: *damec̣era*.

**English "had done" is usually the aorist.** In a story told as witnessed, "she had left before I came" is normally the aorist in Georgian; the pluperfect brings in "apparently". Do not translate every "had" with it.

**Do not overuse the perfect subjunctive.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria warn that outside wishes and blessings it "sounds too affected". For "may he come", use the optative: *movides*.

**-eb verbs need -ina.** *\*Unda daelaga* drops a piece. Wiktionary: *daelagebina*.

## Connections

Spanish uses its pluperfect subjunctive for exactly these jobs: *debería haberlo escrito*, *¡ojalá lo hubiera escrito!*, *si lo hubiera escrito...*. Georgian does with one form what Spanish does with *hubiera* + participle. Japanese has a close match for the wish: *mō sukoshi hayaku kaite ireba yokatta* "it would have been good if I had written it earlier", a conditional of regret.

## Exercises

1. Make the pluperfect "I had / should have" of *daxaṭavs*, *gagzavnis*, *iq̇idis*, *itamašebs*.
2. Say "You (one person) should have rung me."
3. Say "If only we had bought tickets!"
4. Say "The children shouldn't have opened the window."
5. Say "They (apparently) had already eaten."
6. Correct *\*Unda davc̣ere c̣erili*.
7. Correct *\*Ninom unda daec̣era*.
8. Translate *Es saxli momaval c̣els unda daamtavron, šaršan ḳi unda daemtavrebinat.*

## Worked answers

1. *Damexaṭa*, *gamegzavna*, *meq̇ida*, *metamaša*: preverb (if the aorist has one), *m-*, *e-*, aorist stem, *-a*.
2. *Unda dagereḳa.* *Reḳavs*, aorist *dareḳa*; pluperfect with *g-* for the doer "you": *da-g-e-reḳ-a*. (The person rung, "me", cannot be marked here; lesson 7-4.)
3. *Neṭav biletebi gveq̇ida!* *Gv-* "we", *e-*, *q̇id*, *-a*; the tickets are nominative.
4. *Bavšvebs panǯara ar unda gaexsnat.* Plural doer *-t*; dative *bavšvebs*.
5. *Turme uḳve šeeč̣amat.* *Še-e-č̣am-a-t*: preverb, *e-*, stem, *-a*, *-t* for "they".
6. *Unda* needs the optative or the pluperfect: *c̣erili unda davc̣ero* "I should write the letter" or *c̣erili unda damec̣era* "I should have written it".
7. Series III needs a dative doer: *ninos unda daec̣era*.
8. "They must finish this house next year; they should have finished it last year." Optative *daamtavron* (series II, future duty) against pluperfect *daemtavrebinat* (series III, past duty, an *-eb* verb with *-ina* and *-t*).
