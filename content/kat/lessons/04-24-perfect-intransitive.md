## The idea

Class 2 verbs have a series III too, and it is built quite differently from the transitive perfect of lesson 4-22. There is no inversion, because class 2 has no doer to invert: the subject stays nominative, as it is in every series (lesson 4-9). Instead the perfect is made from an adjective and the verb "be".

The adjective is the **past participle**: a word made from a verb that describes the state something is in after the action: *dac̣erili* "written", *dabrunebuli* "returned", *misuli* "arrived (there)". Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe these participles as derived mostly from passive verbs, with the suffixes *-il*, *-ul* or the circumfix *m-* ... *-ar*. Put "be" after it and you have the perfect: *dac̣erila* "it has (apparently) been written" is *dac̣eril-* + *-a* "is". *Misula* "he has (apparently) gone" is *misul-* + *-a*. The literal sense is "he is gone", "it is written", much like English "he is gone" or French *il est parti*.

The meaning is the same as the transitive perfect's. Wiktionary gives *dac̣erila* as the perfect of *daic̣ereba*, and Bolkvadze and Kiziria's evidential examples include a class 2 verb: *giorgi gušin londonši gaprenila* "Giorgi has (apparently) flown to London". With *ar*, *ǯer* and *odesme* it is also a plain statement: *ǯer ar dabrunebula* "he hasn't come back yet".

The pluperfect and perfect subjunctive follow the same idea, with the past forms of "be" instead of the present: *dac̣eriliq̇o* "it had been written; it should have been written", *dac̣eriliq̇os* (perfect subjunctive).

## How it is built

**Step 1: the past participle.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria give three main formants, added after the preverb and the root:

- *-il*: *da-c̣er-il-i* "written", *da-rčen-il-i* "remaining", *ga-zrd-il-i* "grown up", *da-c̣ol-il-i* "lying down"
- *-ul*: especially after *-eb*: *da-brun-eb-ul-i* "returned", *da-bad-eb-ul-i* "born", *a-šen-eb-ul-i* "built"; also *da-ḳarg-ul-i* "lost", *ga-q̇id-ul-i* "sold"
- *m-* ... *-ar* (or *-al*) for verbs without a thematic suffix: *ga-m-tb-ar-i* "warmed", *mo-m-ḳvd-ar-i* "dead", *ga-m-šr-al-i* "dried"

The verbs of motion have *-sul-*: *mi-sul-i* "gone (there)", *mo-sul-i* "come", *c̣a-sul-i* "gone off" (Wiktionary). Which formant a verb takes is lexical; the vocabulary notes give the perfect.

**Step 2: drop the -i and add "be".**

- *da-v-c̣er-il-var* "I have been written", *da-c̣er-il-xar* "you have", *da-c̣er-il-a* "it has"
- *da-v-c̣er-il-vart*, *da-c̣er-il-xart*, *da-c̣er-il-an* (Wiktionary)

As Bolkvadze and Kiziria note, the first person has *v-* twice: once after the preverb, once in *-var* (*mo-v-sul-var* "I have come").

**The pluperfect**: the participle + the aorist of "be" (*iq̇avi*, *iq̇o*, *iq̇vnen*): *mi-v-sul-iq̇avi* "I had gone", *mi-sul-iq̇o* "he had gone", *mi-sul-iq̇avit* "we, you had gone", *mi-sul-iq̇vnen* "they had gone" (Wiktionary, *midis*). Note: Wiktionary's table for *daic̣ereba* gives its plural pluperfect forms wrongly as identical to the singular; the pattern is the one shown for *midis*, which Bolkvadze and Kiziria confirm (*dac̣q̇obil-iq̇avit*, *dac̣q̇obil-iq̇vnen*).

**The perfect subjunctive**: the participle + *iq̇o*, *iq̇os*, *iq̇ot*, *iq̇on*: *misuliq̇os* (Wiktionary).

**Glossing.** *da-c̣er-il-a* `PV-write-PTCP-be.3SG`. The motion participle *-sul-* is not cut further: *mo-v-sul-var* `PV-1SBJ-come.PTCP-be.1SG`. The pluperfect *mi-sul-iq̇o* `PV-go.PTCP-be.AOR.3SG`.

**A chain of thought** for "the guests seem to have left already":

1. "Leave" is *c̣ava*, class 2: subject nominative, *sṭumrebi*.
2. Perfect for an inference: participle *c̣asuli* + "be".
3. "They": *c̣asul-an*.
4. *Sṭumrebi uḳve c̣asulan.*

## Where it applies

- **All class 2 verbs** in series III: passives (*dac̣erila*), changes of state (*gamtbara*), motion (*misula*, *mosula*, *c̣asula*), birth and death (*dabadebula*, *momḳvdara*), position (*dac̣olila*, lingua.ge).
- **"Be" itself:** *q̇opila* "has been, apparently was"; *q̇opilxar?* "have you been?".
- **The same uses as the transitive perfect:** inference and hearsay, experience (*odesme*), "not yet" (*ǯer ar*), results with *uḳve*.
- **The pluperfect** for an earlier past, a missed obligation (*unda dabrunebuliq̇avi*) and an unreal past condition (lesson 6-9).
- **The participle on its own** is an adjective: *daḳarguli c̣igni* "a lost book", *daḳeṭili ḳari* "a locked door", *daɣlili* "tired" (lesson 2-1). Lesson 5-7 covers participles in full.

## Watch out

**No dative subject.** By analogy with lesson 4-22, *\*sṭumrebs uḳve c̣asulan* or *\*ninos dabadebula* look right. They are wrong: class 2 has a nominative subject in series III too. *Sṭumrebi c̣asulan*, *nino dabadebula*.

**Do not use the class 1 perfect shape.** *\*Daubrunebia* treats "return" as transitive. Class 2 uses the participle: *dabrunebula*.

**Keep both first-person markers.** *\*Mosulvar* lacks the *v-* after the preverb; *\*movsuli var* splits the word. Write *movsulvar*.

**The -a is "is", not the aorist ending.** *Dac̣erila* (perfect) and *dac̣era* (aorist, "he wrote") differ by the *-il-*. Read the whole form.

**The m- of m- ... -ar is not "me".** In *gamtbara* and *momḳvdara* the *m-* belongs to the participle. *Momḳvdara* is "he has died", not "he has died on me".

**Watch the pluperfect plural.** *\*Dac̣eriliq̇avi* for "we had been written" repeats Wiktionary's error. The plural "we", "you" ends in *-iq̇avit*, and "they" in *-iq̇vnen* (pattern of *misuliq̇avit*, *misuliq̇vnen*).

**Do not confuse the participle with the masdar.** *Dac̣erili* "written" is a participle; *dac̣era* "writing (it)" is the verbal noun (lesson 5-8).

## Connections

French *il est parti*, *elle est née*, *ils sont arrivés* are the exact model: participle + "be" for the perfect of undergoer verbs, against *il a écrit* for doers. Georgian uses dative inversion where French uses *avoir*, but the split runs along the same line. English "he is gone", "the letter is written" preserve the same pattern.

## Exercises

1. Make the perfect "he/she has ..." of *dabrundeba*, *daibadeba*, *darčeba*, *mova*.
2. Say "Have you (several) ever been to Mtskheta?"
3. Say "Apparently the children have gone to bed" (*dac̣veba*, participle *dac̣olili*).
4. Say "I've never come here" (use *arasdros*).
5. Say "When Nino arrived, the train had already left" (*maṭarebeli*; *c̣asula*-type forms).
6. Correct *\*Ninos dabadebula* and *\*Sṭumrebma c̣asulan*.
7. Correct *\*Giorgi ǯer ar daubrunebia*.
8. Translate *Am kalakši ǯer ar vq̇opilvar, magram čemi ʒma ak xširad mosula.*

## Worked answers

1. *Dabrunebula*, *dabadebula*, *darčenila*, *mosula*: participle (without *-i*) + *-a* (lingua.ge, Wiktionary).
2. *Odesme q̇opilxart mcxetaši?* *Q̇opil-* + *xart* "you (all) are".
3. *Bavšvebi turme dac̣olilan.* Nominative subject; *-an* for "they".
4. *Ak arasdros movsulvar.* Double *v-* marking; *arasdros* "never" (with or without *ar*, lesson 3-11).
5. *Roca nino mivida, maṭarebeli uḳve c̣asuliq̇o.* Aorist for the arrival; pluperfect *c̣asuliq̇o* for the earlier departure.
6. Class 2 has a nominative subject in every series: *nino dabadebula*, *sṭumrebi c̣asulan*.
7. "Return" is class 2: *giorgi ǯer ar dabrunebula*.
8. "I haven't been to this city yet, but my brother has often come here." *Vq̇opilvar* (perfect of "be", first person, double *v-*); *mosula* (perfect of "come"). Both plain statements of experience.
