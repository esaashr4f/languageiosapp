## The idea

Most Georgian verbs keep one root through all eleven screeves; the preverb, the thematic suffix and the endings change around it. A handful of very common verbs do not. They take a different root in different series: "say" is *amb-ob-s* in the present, *i-ṭq̇v-i-s* in the future, *tkv-a* in the aorist. Using unrelated roots in one paradigm is called **suppletion**, and the verbs that do it are suppletive. English has the same thing in *go* / *went* and *be* / *was*; Spanish in *ir* / *fue*; French in *aller* / *irai*.

Bolkvadze and Kiziria group these verbs as "irregular verbs with changing stems by tense" and add an important observation: there is a common pattern even here. The present-subseries screeves are built on the present stem, the future-subseries screeves on the future stem, and series III on the same stem as series II. So a suppletive verb has at most three stems, one per group, and once you know them, every screeve is regular. You have met two already: "go" (*-di-* / *-val-* / *-vid-* / *-sul-*, lesson 4-11) and "have" (*akv-* / *kneb-* / *kon-*, lesson 4-19).

Why do the commonest verbs behave this way? In many languages the most frequent verbs keep old or mixed forms because they are learnt early and used constantly. The sources consulted do not give the history of the Georgian stems, so this guide gives none. Learn the principal parts listed below; the rest follows from the lessons before.

## How it is built

**"Be"** (Wiktionary; Bolkvadze and Kiziria). Present *var*, *xar*, *aris*, *vart*, *xart*, *arian* (lesson 2-2); no imperfect. Future *viknebi*, *iknebi*, *ikneba*, *viknebit*, *iknebit*, *iknebian*: a class 2 future. Aorist *viq̇avi*, *iq̇avi*, *iq̇o*, *viq̇avit*, *iq̇avit*, *iq̇vnen*. Optative *viq̇o*, *iq̇o*, *iq̇os*, ... *iq̇on*. Perfect *vq̇opilvar*, *q̇opila* (lesson 4-24). The subject is always nominative.

**"Say"** (Bolkvadze and Kiziria: *ambobs* - *iṭq̇vis* - *tkva*). A class 1 verb, so the cases change by series.
- present *vambob*, *ambobs*; imperfect *ambobda*
- future *viṭq̇vi*, *iṭq̇vis*, *iṭq̇vian*; conditional *iṭq̇oda*
- aorist (strong) *vtkvi*, *tkvi*, *tkva*, *tkves*; optative *vtkva*, *tkvas*, *tkvan*
- perfect *mitkvams*, *utkvams* "has said"

**"Tell (someone)"** (lingua.ge): present *eubneba* "tells him", a class 2 verb with an indirect object (lesson 7-3); future *eṭq̇vis*; aorist *vutxari* "I told him", *utxra* "he told him"; perfect *utkvams*. The present and future treat the teller as a nominative class 2 subject; the aorist is class 1, with an ergative.

**"Give"** (Bolkvadze and Kiziria; lingua.ge): present *vaʒlev*, *aʒlevs*; future *mivcem*, *miscems*; aorist *mi-v-ec-i* "I gave", *mi-s-c-a* "he gave"; optative *mivce*, *misces* (with *-e*, Bolkvadze and Kiziria's exception); perfect *mimicia*, *miucia*. The person given to is an indirect object: dative noun, *s-* or *m-*, *g-* on the verb.

**"See"** (Bolkvadze and Kiziria; Wiktionary): present *vxedav*, *xedavs*; future *vnaxav*, *naxavs* "will see; will visit, find"; aorist *vnaxe*, *naxa*; optative *vnaxo*; perfect *minaxavs*, *unaxavs*. Besides *naxavs*, the vocabulary of lesson 2-5 gave *dainaxavs* / *dainaxa* "will catch sight of, caught sight of": Bolkvadze and Kiziria give both futures, *naxavs* for seeing or visiting and *dainaxavs* for noticing.

**"Eat" and "drink".** *Č̣ams* "eats" is regular (*šeč̣ams*, *šeč̣ama*, *šeuč̣amia*). *Svams* "drinks" has a full regular set (*šesvams*, *šesva*), but Bolkvadze and Kiziria say it is usually replaced outside the present subseries by *dalevs*: future *davlev*, aorist *davlie*, *dalia*, optative *davlio*, perfect *daulevia*. *Dalevs* has no present: it is used only with its preverb.

**"Know"** (lingua.ge; Bolkvadze and Kiziria): present *vici*, *ici*, *icis*, *vicit*, *icit*, *ician*; imperfect *vicodi*, *icoda* "knew"; future *mecodineba* "I will know", a class 4 form with a dative knower; perfect *mcodnia*, *scodnia*. No aorist: for "knew" use the imperfect.

A chain of thought for "Nino said she would give me the book":

1. "Said": aorist of "say", *tkva*; class 1, so *ninom*.
2. "Would give": conditional of "give": future *momcems* "will give me" (*mo-* for a recipient that is me, as in lesson 4-20) + *-da*: *momcemda*.
3. The book is the direct object of a series I form: dative *c̣igns*.
4. *Ninom tkva, rom c̣igns momcemda* (the clause with *rom* "that" is lesson 6-2).

## Where it applies

- **Be:** existence, location, identity, state, in every screeve; also inside other forms (the class 2 perfect, lesson 4-24).
- **Say:** reported speech (*tkva, rom ...*, lesson 6-3), with the quotative particles.
- **Tell:** with a person told, an indirect object.
- **Give:** with a recipient; *mo-* in the future and aorist when the recipient is "me" or "you" (*momca* "he gave me").
- **See:** *xedavs* "sees, can see" (present); *naxavs* "will see, will visit"; *dainaxavs* "will catch sight of".
- **Drink:** *svams* in the present group, *dalevs* elsewhere; the toast *gaumarǯos!* goes with *davliot* "let's drink".
- **Know:** *icis* "knows (a fact)"; for people, Georgian uses *icnobs* (lesson 4-3's *cnobs* family).
- **Go, come, have, bring** are the other suppletive verbs already met (lessons 4-11, 4-19).

## Watch out

**Do not build a future of "say" on the present.** *\*Iambobs*, *\*daambobs* do not exist. The future is *iṭq̇vis*.

**Do not build an aorist of "give" with ʒlev.** *\*Miaʒleva* is wrong (Bolkvadze and Kiziria say *šeaʒlia* means "offered"). "Gave" is *misca*.

**Do not use xed- outside the present group.** *\*Daxeda*, *\*mixedavs* are not "saw", "has seen". Use *naxa*, *dainaxa*, *minaxavs*.

**Do not use svams for a completed drink.** *\*Šesva* exists but sounds formal or ceremonial; in everyday speech, "he drank it" is *dalia*.

**"Know" has no aorist.** *\*Vicode* or *\*gavige* for "I knew" are wrong (*gavige* means "I found out"). Say *vicodi*.

**The future of "know" is inverted.** *\*Vicodineb* is wrong; it is *mecodineba* "it will be known to me" (lingua.ge).

**"Be" never takes the ergative.** *\*Ninom iq̇o* is wrong; *nino iq̇o saxlši*.

## Connections

Every language in this app has a few such verbs, and they are always the commonest ones: Spanish *ser* (*soy*, *fui*, *seré*), French *aller* (*vais*, *irai*, *allé*), English *go* / *went*, Japanese *iku* with honorific *irassharu*. Georgian differs in organising its suppletion by series, so that one stem serves a whole group of screeves.

## Exercises

1. Give the future and aorist (3SG) of *ambobs*, *aʒlevs*, *xedavs*, *svams*.
2. Say "Where were you yesterday?" (one person).
3. Say "What did Nino say?"
4. Say "I'll give you the money tomorrow."
5. Say "Have you (one person) seen this film?"
6. Say "We didn't know the address."
7. Correct *\*Ninom ambo simartle* and *\*Xval iambobs*.
8. Correct *\*Gušin es pilmi vxede* and *\*Vicode*.

## Worked answers

1. *Iṭq̇vis*, *tkva*; *miscems*, *misca*; *naxavs* (or *dainaxavs*), *naxa*; *dalevs*, *dalia*.
2. *Gušin sad iq̇avi?* The aorist of "be", second person singular.
3. *Ninom ra tkva?* Aorist of "say", so an ergative subject; *ra* is the nominative object.
4. *Puls xval mogcem.* Future *miscems*, with *mo-* and *g-* for a recipient "you"; *v-* gives way to *g-* (lesson 4-4). The money is dative (series I).
5. *Es pilmi ginaxavs?* Perfect with *g-* for "you"; the film nominative.
6. *Misamarti ar vicodit.* The imperfect serves as the past of "know".
7. *Ninom simartle tkva*: the aorist stem is *tkv-*. The future is *iṭq̇vis*: *xval iṭq̇vis*.
8. The aorist of "see" is *vnaxe*: *gušin es pilmi vnaxe*. "Know" has no aorist: *vicodi*.
