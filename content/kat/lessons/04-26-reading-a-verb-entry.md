## The idea

Spanish dictionaries list *escribir*, French ones *écrire*: the infinitive. Georgian has no infinitive, so dictionaries have to choose another form to list a verb under, and they do not all choose the same. The form a dictionary lists a word under is its **citation form**.

This guide has followed Wiktionary: a verb is cited by its **third person singular present**, *c̣ers* "writes", and, when a verb has no present, by its third person singular future, *dalevs* "will drink" (lesson 4-25). Wiktionary also gives the **masdar**, the verbal noun (*c̣era* "writing"), which Georgian grammars call საწყისი *sac̣q̇isi*. Lingua.ge lists verbs under their masdar with a preverb, *daxaṭva* for "draw". Traditional Georgian dictionaries often arrange verbs by root or by masdar (sources.json notes this for the large Georgian-English dictionary). So to find a verb you may need to start from any of three forms.

None of these forms tells you everything. Lesson 4-1 called the verb a row of slots; a citation form fills some of them and leaves others empty. The present has no preverb; the future has no imperfect *-d-*; the masdar has no person. That is why this guide's vocabulary notes give a small set of **principal parts**, like a Latin dictionary: present, future (which shows the preverb), aorist (which shows weak or strong, and any root change), perfect, and masdar. From those five forms, everything in part 4 lets you build all eleven screeves.

## How it is built

**From the present (3SG) you learn:**

1. The present stem: remove *-s* (or *-a* for class 2): *tesav-s*, *c̣mend-s*, *ic̣er-eb-a*.
2. The thematic suffix and so the "they" ending (lesson 4-2): *tesaven*, but *xsnian*.
3. The class, roughly: *-s* with an object is class 1; *-eb-a* is class 2; an activity in *-ob-s* is probably class 3; a form with *m-*/*u-* for the feeler is class 4.
4. The imperfect: add *-d-* (class 1, 3) or *-od-* (class 2): *tesavda*, *ic̣ereboda*.

**From the future (3SG):**

5. The preverb: *da-tesavs*, *mo-c̣mends*. Or, for a medial verb, *i-* ... *-eb* without a preverb.
6. Hence the conditional (future + *-da*).

**From the aorist (3SG and 1SG):**

7. Weak or strong (lesson 4-14): *davtese* (weak, *-e*), *gavč̣eri* (strong, *-i*).
8. Root changes: *c̣mends* → *moc̣minda*: the *e* becomes *i* (Wiktionary; Bolkvadze and Kiziria's ablaut verbs).
9. Hence the optative: weak *-o*, strong class 1 *-a*, strong class 2 *-e* (lesson 4-16).

**From the perfect (3SG):**

10. The series III shape: *dautesavs* (*-av-s* type), *mouc̣mendia* (*-ia* type), or a participle + "be" for class 2 (*dac̣erila*).
11. Hence the pluperfect (*e-* for *i-*/*u-*) and perfect subjunctive.

**From the masdar:** a noun, declined like any noun (*tesva*, *c̣menda*); it shows the root without the version vowel or thematic suffix, which helps you find the root (lesson 4-1), and it often carries the preverb in lingua.ge's listing.

**Worked example.** Wiktionary lists *tesavs* "sows", verbal nouns *tesva* and *datesva*, with the future *datesavs*, aorist *davtese*, *datesa*, optative *davteso*, perfect *dautesavs*. So:

- present *vtesav*, *tesavs*, *tesaven*; imperfect *vtesavdi*
- future *davtesav*; conditional *davtesavdi*
- aorist *davtese*, *datesa*, *dateses*; optative *davteso*, *datesos*
- perfect *damitesavs*, *dautesavs*; pluperfect *dametesa* (Wiktionary)
- class 1: subject nominative, ergative, dative by series.

## Where it applies

- **Using Wiktionary.** Look up the 3SG present (or future); the conjugation table gives every screeve, with the subject pronouns showing the series (lesson 4-8).
- **Using lingua.ge.** Look up the masdar, often with its preverb: *daxaṭva*, *dabruneba*; the page gives all screeves and the imperative.
- **Using printed dictionaries** arranged by root or masdar: strip the preverb, the version vowel and the thematic suffix to find the root (lesson 4-1).
- **Learning new verbs:** note the five principal parts, as this guide's vocabulary does.
- **Suppletive verbs** need all their stems (lesson 4-25).

## Watch out

**Do not look up a conjugated form.** *Davtese* will not be in most dictionaries; strip it to the root and find *tesavs* or *tesva*.

**Do not take the present as a guide to the preverb.** *C̣ers* gives no hint that its future is *dac̣ers* and not *\*gac̣ers*. Only the future form tells you.

**Do not take the present as a guide to the aorist.** *C̣mends* looks regular; its aorist *moc̣minda* is not predictable from it.

**Do not assume the masdar has no preverb.** Lingua.ge lists *dabruneba*, not *bruneba*; Wiktionary gives both *tesva* and *datesva*.

**Do not confuse a future citation with a present.** Wiktionary marks *dalevs* and *naxavs* as future. They cannot mean "drinks" or "sees" now.

## Connections

Latin dictionaries list four principal parts (*scrībō, scrībere, scrīpsī, scrīptum*) for the same reason: no single form predicts the rest. Georgian needs five, but the logic is identical. Arabic dictionaries go further and list verbs by root, as some Georgian ones do.

## Exercises

1. From *tesavs*, *datesavs*, *datesa*, *dautesavs*, give the imperfect 1SG, the conditional 1SG and the optative 3SG.
2. From *c̣mends*, *moc̣mends*, *moc̣minda*, *mouc̣mendia*, give "they will clean", "we cleaned", "you have cleaned".
3. Which principal part tells you that *c̣mends* changes its vowel?
4. You meet *gaatbo* in a text. Strip it down and find the citation form.
5. You meet *moumzadebia*. Name its screeve and the citation form of the verb.
6. Why does this guide cite *dalevs* as a future?
7. Correct *\*Gac̣ers* (meaning "will write").
8. Correct *\*Movc̣mende* (meaning "I cleaned").

## Worked answers

1. Imperfect *vtesavdi* (present stem + *-di*). Conditional *davtesavdi* (future + *-di*). Optative *datesos* (weak aorist, so *-o*, + *-s*).
2. *Moc̣menden* (future, *-en*). *Movc̣mindet* (aorist stem with *i*, *-e-t*). *Mogic̣mendia* (perfect with *g-* + *i-*).
3. The aorist, *moc̣minda*: the present and future have *e*.
4. *Ga-a-tb-o*: preverb *ga-*, version vowel *a-*, root *tb*, aorist *-o*. The verb is *atbobs* "warms" (lesson 4-9).
5. Perfect (*-u-* ... *-ia*, no *v-*), third person doer. The verb is *amzadebs* "prepares" (future *moamzadebs*).
6. It has no present: Bolkvadze and Kiziria say it is used only with its preverb, so its lowest form is the future.
7. The preverb of *c̣ers* is *da-*: *dac̣ers*.
8. The aorist root has *i*: *movc̣minde*.
