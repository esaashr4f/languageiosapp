## Before you start

This review covers all of part 4, with the weight on its second half: the aorist of class 2 verbs and strong and weak aorists (lesson 4-14); medial verbs (4-15); the optative (4-16); commands and prohibitions (4-17); indirect verbs and inversion (4-18); having things and people (4-19); indirect objects (4-20); version (4-21); the perfect of transitive and medial verbs (4-22); the pluperfect and perfect subjunctive (4-23); the perfect of intransitives (4-24); suppletive verbs (4-25); and principal parts (4-26). The first half (template, object markers, imperfect, future, conditional, series, classes, aorist and ergative) was reviewed in lesson 4-13 and is needed throughout.

For every verb, ask three questions in order: which class? which series? so which case for each noun? Then build the form slot by slot.

## Passage

**A friend comes back.** Constructed, using only words and grammar from parts 1 to 4. Mkhedruli first, then the transliteration.

1. გუშინ საღამოს მეგობარმა დამირეკა.
   *gušin saɣamos megobarma damireḳa.*
2. „ხვალ თბილისში ჩამოვალ“, — მითხრა.
   *„xval tbilisši čamoval“, — mitxra.*
3. ბავშვობაში ერთ სოფელში ვცხოვრობდით და ყოველდღე ერთად ვთამაშობდით.
   *bavšvobaši ert sopelši vcxovrobdit da q̇oveldɣe ertad vtamašobdit.*
4. მერე ის საზღვარგარეთ წავიდა და იქ დიდხანს იცხოვრა.
   *mere is sazɣvargaret c̣avida da ik didxans icxovra.*
5. თურმე იქ ორი წიგნი დაუწერია.
   *turme ik ori c̣igni dauc̣eria.*
6. დღეს მისი წიგნი ვიყიდე, მაგრამ ჯერ არ წამიკითხავს.
   *dɣes misi c̣igni viq̇ide, magram ǯer ar c̣amiḳitxavs.*
7. ხვალ ვახშამს მოვამზადებ და ძმას დავპატიჟებ.
   *xval vaxšams movamzadeb da ʒmas davṗaṭižeb.*
8. ძმამ ღვინო მოიტანოს, მე ღვინო არ მაქვს.
   *ʒmam ɣvino moiṭanos, me ɣvino ar makvs.*
9. ნეტავ ადრე ჩამოსულიყო!
   *neṭav adre čamosuliq̇o!*

## Exercises

1. Translate sentences 1 and 2.
2. Translate sentences 3 and 4.
3. Translate sentences 5 and 6.
4. Translate sentences 7 to 9.
5. In sentence 1, take apart *damireḳa*. Why is *megobarma* ergative?
6. In sentence 2, take apart *čamoval* and *mitxra*. Which verb does *mitxra* belong to?
7. In sentence 4, why is it *is ... c̣avida* but *icxovra* with the same subject? What case would *is* take with *icxovra* alone?
8. In sentence 5, explain *dauc̣eria*: series, case of the (unnamed) writer, and the meaning of *turme*.
9. In sentence 6, explain *viq̇ide* (class, version vowel, aorist type) and *c̣amiḳitxavs* (screeve, who has not read).
10. In sentence 8, name the screeve of *moiṭanos* and say why *ʒmam* is ergative. Why *makvs* and not *mq̇avs*?
11. In sentence 9, name the screeve of *čamosuliq̇o* and its use here.
12. Say "We played football all day" and "the children came back late".
13. Say "Let's go to the cinema!" and "Don't go (one person), stay!" (two ways for the prohibition).
14. Say "I love my brother, and my brother loves music."
15. Say "Do you (polite) have children?" and "Do you (polite) have time?"
16. Say "Grandmother sewed me a shirt."
17. Say "I should have rung Mother yesterday."
18. Say "Nino has (apparently) gone to Mtskheta."
19. Say "Who told you that?" (aorist of "tell", with *g-*).
20. Correct *\*Bavšvebi itamašes ezoši.*
21. Correct *\*Ninom daibada tbilisši.*
22. Correct *\*Me miq̇vars musiḳas.*
23. Correct *\*Ar dac̣ere es c̣erili!* (meaning "don't write this letter!").
24. Correct *\*Giorgim c̣igni dauc̣eria* and *\*Vicode*.

## Worked answers

1. "Yesterday evening a friend rang me. 'Tomorrow I'll come to Tbilisi,' he told me." *Damireḳa* is the aorist (lesson 4-12) of *ureḳavs* "rings him", with the objective vowel (lesson 4-21).
2. "As children we lived in the same village and played together every day." Two imperfects for the background and habit (lesson 4-5). "Then he went abroad and lived there a long time." *C̣avida* aorist of class 2 "go off" (lessons 4-11, 4-14); *icxovra* aorist of class 3 "live" (lesson 4-15).
3. "Apparently he has written two books there." Perfect, hearsay (lesson 4-22). "Today I bought his book, but I haven't read it yet." Aorist *viq̇ide*, then a plain negative perfect (lessons 4-12, 4-22).
4. "Tomorrow I'll make supper and invite my brother. Let my brother bring wine; I have no wine. If only he had come earlier!" Futures (lesson 4-6), optative (4-16), class 4 *makvs* (4-18), class 2 pluperfect (4-24).
5. *da-m-i-reḳ-a*: preverb *da-* (future *dareḳavs*), *m-* "to me", *i-* the objective vowel after *m-* (the third-person form is *daureḳa*), root, aorist *-a* "he". "Ring" is a class 1 verb with an indirect object; in the aorist its subject is ergative (lessons 4-12, 4-20, 4-21).
6. *Čamo-val*: preverb *čamo-* "down here", the future stem of "come", first person: "I'll come (down to Tbilisi)" (lesson 4-11). *M-i-txr-a*: "he told me", the aorist of *eubneba* "tells", with *m-* "to me" in place of the *u-* of *utxra* (lessons 4-20, 4-25).
7. The pronoun stands next to *c̣avida*, a class 2 verb, so it is nominative, *is*; it is not repeated before the second verb. With *icxovra* (class 3) on its own, the subject would be ergative: *man icxovra* (lessons 4-14, 4-15).
8. Series III, perfect (*da-u-c̣er-i-a*): the writer would be dative (*mas*); the books are nominative (*ori c̣igni*, singular after a number). *Turme* marks the information as not witnessed (lesson 4-22).
9. *V-i-q̇id-e*: class 1 (lingua.ge lists *q̇idulobs* as transitive), version vowel *i-* (lexical; lesson 4-21), weak aorist *-e*, no preverb (lesson 4-12). *C̣a-m-i-ḳitx-av-s*: perfect of the *-av* type; *m-* marks the person who has not read: "I" (lessons 4-18, 4-22).
10. Optative: *moiṭana* has a weak aorist, so *-o-s* (lesson 4-16). Series II, class 1: ergative subject, nominative object *ɣvino*. *Makvs*: wine is a thing, so *akvs*, not *hq̇avs* (lesson 4-19).
11. Pluperfect of the class 2 verb *čamodis* (participle *čamosul-* + *iq̇o*), with *neṭav*: a wish about the past that did not come true (lessons 4-23, 4-24).
12. *Mteli dɣe pexburti vitamašet.* Class 3 aorist with *i-*, *-e-t* (lesson 4-15). *Bavšvebi gvian dabrundnen.* Class 2 aorist: nominative subject, strong *-nen* (lesson 4-14).
13. *C̣avidet ḳinoši!* (optative, lesson 4-16). *Nu c̣axval, darči!* (*nu* + future, a plea) or *ar c̣axvide, darči!* (*ar* + optative, firmer) (lesson 4-17).
14. *Me čemi ʒma miq̇vars, čems ʒmas ḳi musiḳa uq̇vars.* Class 4: the lovers are dative (*me* unchanged, *čems ʒmas*), the things loved nominative (lesson 4-18).
15. *Švilebi gq̇avt?* (people: *hq̇avs*); *dro gakvt?* (a thing: *akvs*); *-t* for the polite plural (lessons 4-4, 4-19).
16. *Bebiam ṗerangi šemiḳera.* Objective version *m-i-* for "for me"; aorist of *šeḳeravs*; series II cases (lessons 4-12, 4-21).
17. *Gušin unda damereḳa.* A past obligation: *unda* + pluperfect, with *m-* for the doer "I" (lingua.ge: დამერეკა). Mother cannot be marked on a series III verb; lesson 7-4 shows how to add her (lesson 4-23).
18. *Nino mcxetaši c̣asula.* Class 2 perfect from the participle *c̣asul-*; nominative subject (lesson 4-24).
19. *Es vin gitxra?* Aorist of "tell" with *g-* "to you"; *vin* is the ergative subject (it does not change form) (lessons 3-11, 4-25).
20. Class 3 subjects are ergative in the aorist: *bavšvebma ezoši itamašes* (lesson 4-15).
21. "Be born" is class 2: no ergative. *Nino tbilisši daibada* (lesson 4-14).
22. The thing loved is nominative: *me musiḳa miq̇vars* (lesson 4-18).
23. *Ar* + aorist is a statement. Use *ar* + optative or *nu* + future: *es c̣erili ar dac̣ero!* or *nu dac̣er am c̣erils!* (lesson 4-17).
24. Series III needs a dative doer: *giorgis c̣igni dauc̣eria* (lesson 4-22). "Know" has no aorist: *vicodi* (lesson 4-25).
