## The idea

An **adverb** is a word that tells you how, where, when, how often or why something happens. It never changes its form: no case, no person, no tense. Bolkvadze and Kiziria say it can modify a verb (*gvian movida* "he came late"), a participle, an adjective (*ʒalian didi* "very big") or another adverb (*ʒalian ḳargad* "very well").

Georgian has very few adverbs that are words in their own right. Bolkvadze and Kiziria name *ak* "here", *ik* "there", *c̣in* "in front", *uḳan* "behind", *garet* "outside", *šin* "at home" and *axla* "now" as the main ones. Almost every other adverb is a noun, adjective or numeral in a case that has stopped working as a case. You already know many: *dɣes* "today" is the dative of *dɣe* "day", *ɣamit* "at night" is the instrumental of *ɣame*, *ḳargad* "well" is the adverbial of *ḳargi* "good" (lesson 3-4).

That is the logic of this lesson. Once you know the seven cases, you can read most adverbs by taking them apart. The case tells you the kind of meaning: the adverbial *-ad* gives a manner ("in a good way"), the dative often a time ("on that day"), the instrumental a route or a time span ("by night"), *-dan* a starting point.

The second idea is that the line between adverb and postposition is thin. *Uḳan* alone means "back, behind". Put a genitive in front of it and it means "behind something": *saxlis uḳan* "behind the house". Bolkvadze and Kiziria make exactly this point: an adverb after a noun works as a postposition. A small set of these separate-word postpositions take the genitive: *šesaxeb* "about", *garda* "except", *šemdeg* "after", *c̣in* "before, in front of", *uḳan* "behind", *mier* "by", and two more you will want at once, *gamo* "because of" and *gareše* "without".

## How it is built

**Adverbs of manner from adjectives.** Take the adjective, drop *-i*, add *-ad* (lesson 3-4): *mšvidi* "calm" gives *mšvidad* "calmly"; *išviati* "rare" gives *išviatad* "rarely". This is the productive pattern: almost any adjective can do it.

**Shortened -ad.** A few very common ones have lost the *-d*: *čkara* "quickly" from *čkarad*, *nela* "slowly" from *nelad*, *maɣla* "high up" from *maɣlad*, *dabla* "down below" from *dablad*, *martla* "really". Bolkvadze and Kiziria list these as "partly truncated" adverbial forms. Both *čkara* and *sc̣rapad* mean "quickly".

**Doubling.** Repeat a word to mean "little by little" or "one by one": *nel-nela* "slowly, gradually" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

**How many times.** Add *-ǯer* to a numeral: *sam-ǯer* "three times", *ramden-ǯer* "how many times?". "Once" is the exception: *ertxel*, with *-xel*, which is used only with *erti* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

**Place.** Pairs built on the same stems: *zemot* "above, upstairs" and *kvemot* "below"; *šignit* "inside" and *garet* "outside". Add *-dan* for "from": *zevidan* "from above", *šignidan* "from inside" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

**Genitive postpositions.** Put the noun in the genitive, then the postposition as a separate word:

1. I want to say "after the lesson". The noun is *gaḳvetili*.
2. *Šemdeg* "after" takes the genitive: *gaḳvetilis*.
3. *Gaḳvetilis šemdeg.*

The same steps give *omis šemdeg* "after the war", *ori c̣lis c̣in* "two years ago" (*c̣eli*, genitive *c̣lis*, lesson 3-6), *saxlis c̣in* "in front of the house", *ninos garda* "except Nino", *kalakis šesaxeb* "about the city". A vowel-stem name keeps its *-s* genitive: *nino*, genitive *ninos* (lesson 3-5).

**Glossing.** The separate postposition is a word of its own, glossed in lower case: *saxl-is uḳan* `house-GEN behind`. Contrast the suffixed postpositions of part 3, joined by a hyphen: *saxl-ši* `house-in`.

## Where it applies

- **Manner** from any adjective or participle in *-ad*: *mšvidad* "calmly", *xširad* "often", *išviatad* "rarely", *ucbad* "suddenly, straight away" (Wiktionary: straightway).
- **Manner and degree** from pronouns: *ase* "like this", *ise* "like that" (lesson 3-11).
- **Time** in the dative: *dɣes* "today", *dilas*, *saɣamos* (lesson 2-5), and in the instrumental: *ɣamit*, *dɣisit*, *dilit*. Fixed compounds: *amaɣam* "tonight", *gušinc̣in* "the day before yesterday".
- **Place** with *-dan* and *-mde*: *akedan*, *akamde* (lesson 3-11), *tavidan* "from the beginning; again" (Wiktionary).
- **Frequency** with *-ǯer*: *orǯer* "twice", *samǯer*, *ramdenǯer?* "how many times?". *Ertxel* "once" is the only *-xel* form.
- **Cause and reason**: *amiṭom* "therefore, that is why", *imiṭom* "for that reason". *Imiṭom* also opens "because" clauses (lesson 6-6).
- **Postpositions on the genitive** (each a separate word): *šemdeg* "after", *c̣in* "before, in front of, ago", *uḳan* "behind", *šesaxeb* "about", *garda* "except, besides", *mier* "by (the doer)", *gamo* "because of", *gareše* "without".
- **mier with participles**: the doer of a passive or a participle (*mamačemis mier ašenebuli*). It is formal and written.
- **Fixed expressions**: *zeṗirad* "by heart" (Wiktionary), *pexit* "on foot" (lesson 4-11).

## Watch out

**No case endings on an adverb.** By analogy with nouns, *\*axlas* or *\*akši* look possible. Adverbs do not decline: *axla*, *ak*.

**The genitive, not the dative, before these postpositions.** From lesson 3-2 you might write *\*gaḳvetils šemdeg*. *Šemdeg*, *c̣in*, *uḳan*, *šesaxeb*, *garda*, *mier*, *gamo* and *gareše* take the genitive: *gaḳvetilis šemdeg*.

**Do not glue them on.** *\*Saxlisuḳan* treats *uḳan* like *-ši*. It is a separate word: *saxlis uḳan*. Only *-tvis*, *-gan*, *-ḳen* join the genitive (lesson 3-3).

**"Before" a time is c̣in; "until" is -mde.** *Gaḳvetilis c̣in* is "before the lesson"; *gaḳvetilamde* is "up to the lesson, until the lesson". The two overlap in English but not in Georgian.

**"Ago" is a postposition, not a verb.** English and Spanish build "ago" from a verb (*hace dos años*). Georgian uses the genitive and *c̣in*: *ori c̣lis c̣in*, not *\*ori c̣eli c̣in*.

**Ertxel, not \*ertǯer.** "Once" is irregular. All other numerals take *-ǯer* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

**Do not use mier for an ordinary doer.** *\*Mamačemis mier saxli aašena* is wrong: an active verb already has its ergative subject (*mamačemma saxli aašena*). *Mier* belongs with participles and passives.

**Gamo is "because of", not "for".** *Šenis gamo* means "because of you"; "for you" is *šentvis*.

## Connections

Spanish makes manner adverbs from the feminine adjective plus *-mente*, a fossilised noun meaning "with a mind"; Georgian uses a living case, *-ad*. Japanese and Korean, like Georgian, put "after" and "before" after a modifying noun: Japanese *jugyō no ato*, "lesson's after", is the same shape as *gaḳvetilis šemdeg*.

## Exercises

1. Make adverbs from *mšvidi* "calm" and *išviati* "rare", and translate them.
2. Translate: "after the war".
3. Translate: "Behind the school there is a garden."
4. Take apart *ramdenǯer* and say what it means.
5. Translate: "Three years ago we lived in the village."
6. Is *uḳan* an adverb or a postposition in *ǯer uḳan ar dabrunebula*? Why?
7. Find and fix the error: *\*Gaḳvetils šemdeg c̣avedi.*
8. Translate: "I read a book about Tbilisi."

## Worked answers

1. Drop the *-i*, add the adverbial *-ad*: *mšvidad* "calmly", *išviatad* "rarely".
2. *Omi* "war", genitive *omis*; *šemdeg* follows: *omis šemdeg*.
3. *Sḳola*, genitive *sḳolis* (a truncating vowel stem, lesson 3-5); then *uḳan*, then "there is a garden": *Sḳolis uḳan baɣia.*
4. *Ramden-* "how many" (lesson 3-11) + *-ǯer* "times": "how many times?". Wiktionary also gives "many times".
5. "Three years" before *c̣in* is genitive: *sami c̣lis c̣in*. "We lived" is the imperfect *vcxovrobdit* (lesson 4-5). *Sami c̣lis c̣in sopelši vcxovrobdit.*
6. An adverb: no noun in the genitive comes before it. "He hasn't come back yet" (lesson 4-24).
7. *Šemdeg* takes the genitive, not the dative: *Gaḳvetilis šemdeg c̣avedi.*
8. *Tbilisi*, genitive *tbilisis*; *šesaxeb* after it; "I read" in the aorist is *c̣avikitxe* (lesson 4-12): *Tbilisis šesaxeb c̣igni c̣avikitxe.*
