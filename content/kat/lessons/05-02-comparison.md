## The idea

Georgian adjectives have no comparative ending like English *-er*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria describe the everyday system as two words placed before the plain adjective: *upro* "more" and *q̇velaze* "most". *Upro didi* is "bigger", *q̇velaze didi* "biggest". The adjective itself does not change.

The thing you compare against is marked by a postposition you already know: *-ze* "on" (lesson 3-2). Wiktionary lists "than" among the meanings of *-ze*, with the example *šenze maɣali* "taller than you". So Georgian says "tall on you", where Spanish says *más alto que tú*. Once you see that, *q̇velaze* explains itself: it is *q̇vela* "all" + *-ze*, "than all" (Wiktionary notes it is a form of *q̇vela* with *-ze*). "The biggest" is "bigger than everything".

There is also an older, one-word superlative, the circumfix *u-* ... *-es-i*: *didi* "big" gives *udidesi* "greatest", *ʒveli* "old" gives *uʒvelesi* "oldest". A **circumfix** is an affix in two parts, one before the stem and one after it. Bolkvadze and Kiziria note that it often means no more than "very": *udidesi* is "greatest" or "outstanding". It is literary and formal, and it is common in fixed phrases, so you need to recognise it even if you mostly say *q̇velaze*.

The two commonest adjectives are irregular. For *ḳargi* "good" and *cudi* "bad", the *u-* ... *-es-i* form means "better" and "worse", not "best" and "worst": *uḳetesi* "better", *uaresi* "worse" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria; Wiktionary). "Best" adds yet another circumfix, *sa-* ... *-o*: *sauḳeteso*. Irregular forms like these keep the old stems because the words are so frequent; English *good, better, best* is the same kind of survival.

## How it is built

**"X is bigger than Y."**

1. The adjective is *didi*. "More" is *upro*: *upro didi*.
2. The standard of comparison takes *-ze*. *-Ze* sits on the dative and swallows its *-s* (lesson 3-2): *kutaisi*, dative *kutaiss*, gives *kutaisze*.
3. The copula after an adjective in *-i* is the enclitic *-a* (lesson 2-1): *didia*.
4. *Tbilisi kutaisze upro didia.* "Tbilisi is bigger than Kutaisi."

When a *-ze* phrase is present, *upro* can be left out: *tbilisi kutaisze didia* says the same. Wiktionary's *šenze maɣali* "taller than you" has no *upro*.

**Pronouns as the standard.** "Than me" is *čemze*, "than you" *šenze*, "than him/her" *masze* (lesson 3-8 built *čemtan* and *masze* the same way).

**"The most."** *Q̇velaze* + the plain adjective: *q̇velaze didi kalaki* "the biggest city". In a sentence: *es q̇velaze didi kalakia*.

**The u- ... -es-i superlative.** Take the stem without *-i*, put *u-* in front and *-es-i* after: *lamaz-i* → *u-lamaz-es-i*; *ʒvel-i* → *u-ʒvel-es-i*. Some stems shrink, as Bolkvadze and Kiziria list: *mc̣are* "bitter" → *umc̣aresi*; *grʒeli* "long" → *ugrʒesi*; *ɣrma* "deep" → *uɣrmesi*. *Mdidari* "rich" syncopates: *umdidresi* (Wiktionary). The form declines like any adjective in *-i*.

**"More" and "less" as amounts.** *Meṭi* "more" and *naḳlebi* "less, fewer" (Wiktionary gives *naḳlebi* as the comparative of *coṭa*). *Meṭi dro* "more time", *naḳlebi puli* "less money". As adverbs: *meṭad*, *naḳlebad*.

**"-ish".** A third circumfix, *mo-* ... *-o*, softens colours and sizes: *tetri* "white" → *motetro* "whitish" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria; Wiktionary). It is for recognition.

**Glossing.** This guide has no abbreviation for the superlative, so the superlative stem is glossed as one piece: *udides-i* `greatest-NOM`, *uḳetes-i* `better-NOM`. *Q̇vela-ze* is cut: `all-than`. *-Ze* meaning "than" is glossed `than`.

## Where it applies

- **Adjectives in the predicate**: *tbilisi kutaisze didia*; and **before a noun**: *upro didi kalaki* "a bigger city", *q̇velaze didi kalaki*.
- **Adverbs**: *upro* and *q̇velaze* work the same way with adverbs: *upro sc̣rapad* "faster", *q̇velaze ḳargad* "best" (as in "she sings best"). *Uḳetesad* is also used for "better" as an adverb.
- **Pronoun standards**: *čemze*, *šenze*, *masze*, *čvenze*, *tkvenze*, *matze* (lesson 3-8 forms).
- **Nouns of amount**: *meṭi* and *naḳlebi*: *meṭi puli*, *naḳlebi xalxi*.
- **vidre** when the standard is not a plain noun or pronoun: an adverb (*vidre gušin* "than yesterday") or, in part 6, a whole clause.
- **The u- ... -es-i forms** in formal writing and set phrases: *uʒvelesi* "ancient", *umaɣlesi* "highest, supreme" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria: *umaɣlesi sasc̣avlebeli* "an institution of higher education"), *udidesi* "outstanding".
- **"Very"** with *u-* ... *-es-i*: often the form means "extremely", not a true superlative (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
- **mo- ... -o** for "-ish" with colours and sizes: *motetro*, *modido* "biggish".

## Watch out

**Not -ze on the nominative.** Thinking of the noun as a subject, *\*tbilisize* or *\*kutaisize* may look right. *-Ze* sits on the dative stem, whose *-s* disappears: *kutaisze*, *čemze*.

**No *\*upro uḳetesi*.** *Uḳetesi* already means "better". Adding *upro* doubles the comparison. Say *uḳetesi* or *upro ḳargi*.

**Uḳetesi is not "best".** By analogy with *udidesi* "greatest", you might read *uḳetesi* as a superlative. For *ḳargi* and *cudi* it is the comparative; "best" is *sauḳeteso*, "worst" is *q̇velaze cudi*.

**Not *\*upro uprosi*.** *Uprosi* "elder" and *umcrosi* "younger" are comparatives already. "My elder brother" is *čemi uprosi ʒma*.

**Do not reach for a conjunction.** From Spanish *más ... que*, you may want a word for "than" before the noun. With a noun or pronoun, Georgian uses *-ze*, not *vidre*: *šenze maɣali*, not *\*vidre šen maɣali*.

**q̇velaze is one word.** *\*Q̇vela ze* splits the postposition from its noun.

**Nothing extra on the adjective.** *\*Didze*, *\*didesi* are not words; only the circumfix form *udidesi* exists, and only for some adjectives.

## Connections

Spanish keeps a few old synthetic comparatives (*mejor*, *peor*, *mayor*) beside *más* + adjective; Georgian's *uḳetesi*, *uaresi*, *uprosi* are the same kind of island. Japanese and Korean also mark the standard with a postposition and need no word for "more": Japanese *anata yori takai*, Korean *neo-boda keuda* are built exactly like *šenze maɣali*.

## Exercises

1. Translate: "Nino is taller than Giorgi."
2. Translate: "This is the most beautiful city."
3. Make the u- ... -es-i forms of *lamazi* and *mdidari*.
4. What does *uḳetesi* mean, and why is it not "best"?
5. Translate: "My sister is younger than me." (*umcrosi* "younger")
6. Find and fix the error: *\*Es ɣvino upro uḳetesia.*
7. Translate: "I have more money today."
8. Take apart *q̇velaze* and explain its meaning.

## Worked answers

1. The standard *giorgi* takes *-ze* on the dative stem: *giorgize*. *Maɣali* + the enclitic: *maɣalia*. *Nino giorgize maɣalia* (or *upro maɣalia*).
2. *Q̇velaze* + *lamazi* + *kalaki*, with the enclitic copula: *Es q̇velaze lamazi kalakia.*
3. *u-lamaz-es-i* "most beautiful" (Wiktionary: *ulamazesi*); *umdidresi* "richest", with syncope of the stem (Wiktionary).
4. "Better". For *ḳargi* and *cudi* this circumfix gives the comparative; the superlative needs *sa-* ... *-o*: *sauḳeteso* (lesson 5-2, Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
5. *Umcrosi* is a comparative already, so no *upro*: *Čemi da čemze umcrosia.*
6. *Uḳetesi* already means "better": *Es ɣvino uḳetesia* (or *upro ḳargia*).
7. Possession uses *makvs* (lesson 4-19); "more money" is *meṭi puli*: *Dɣes meṭi puli makvs.*
8. *Q̇vela* "all" + *-ze* "than": "than all", hence "most". It stands before the adjective: *q̇velaze didi*.
