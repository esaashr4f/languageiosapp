## The idea

You met *erti* to *ati*, one to ten, in lesson 2-4, together with the rule that matters most: after a number the noun stays singular, and so does the verb. *Ori saxli* is "two house"; *xuti sṭudenṭi ak aris* is "five student is here". This lesson adds eleven to twenty, shows how a number behaves in each case, and adds two particles that turn a number into "all five" and "about five".

The teens are the place to start, because they are transparent once you know lesson 5-2. Bolkvadze and Kiziria give the underlying form of thirteen as *at-sam-meṭi* and gloss it *atze samit meṭi*: "by three more than ten". *Meṭi* is "more" (lesson 5-2), *-ze* marks what you compare with, and the instrumental *-it* gives the difference. So every number from eleven to nineteen is "ten, X, more", worn down by sound changes into one word: *cameṭi* 13, *totxmeṭi* 14, *txutmeṭi* 15.

The worn-down shapes need care. The *at-* of "ten" has shrunk to *t-* and merged with what follows. Bolkvadze and Kiziria show *at-sam-meṭi* becoming *t-sam-meṭi* and then *cameṭi*: *t* + *s* gives *c*, as the letters themselves suggest (ც *c* is the sound [ts]). In *čvidmeṭi* 17, *t* + *š* gives *č*. In *tvrameṭi* 18 the *t* stays, and in *cxrameṭi* 19 it has been absorbed into the *c* of *cxra*. Eleven and twelve, *tertmeṭi* and *tormeṭi*, are built the same way on *erti* and *ori*. Twenty, *oci*, is a separate root, and lesson 5-4 shows why it is the most important number in the system.

## How it is built

**The teens.** Ten-stem + unit + *-meṭi*, with the sound changes Bolkvadze and Kiziria list:

- *tertmeṭi* 11, *tormeṭi* 12, *cameṭi* 13, *totxmeṭi* 14, *txutmeṭi* 15
- *tekvsmeṭi* 16, *čvidmeṭi* 17, *tvrameṭi* 18, *cxrameṭi* 19, *oci* 20

All of them end in *-i* and decline like ordinary consonant-stem nouns (*-i*, *-ma*, *-s*, *-is* ...). Only *rva* 8 and *cxra* 9 end in a vowel.

**A number before a noun** behaves like an adjective before a noun (lesson 3-10). Bolkvadze and Kiziria's table for "five books":

1. Nominative: both have *-i*: *xuti c̣igni*.
2. Ergative: both have *-ma*: *xutma c̣ignma*.
3. Dative: the number is a bare stem: *xut c̣igns*.
4. Genitive and instrumental: the number keeps *-i*: *xuti c̣ignis*, *xuti c̣ignit*.
5. Adverbial: bare stem: *xut c̣ignad*.

*Rva* and *cxra* never change before a noun: *cxra bič̣i*, *cxra bič̣ma*, *cxra bič̣s* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

**A chain of thought** for "seventeen guests are coming":

1. "Guest" is *sṭumari*. After a number it stays singular: *čvidmeṭi sṭumari*.
2. "Come" is *modis*, class 2, nominative subject. The noun is singular, so the verb is too: *modis*.
3. *Čvidmeṭi sṭumari modis.*

Now "I am inviting seventeen guests": *ṗaṭižebs* is class 1, so in the present the object is dative. The noun gets *-s*, and the number loses its *-i*: *čvidmeṭ sṭumars vṗaṭižeb*.

**A number on its own** declines fully, like a noun: *xutma* "five (of them), ergative", *xuts*, *xutis*. *Oc-a-mde* "up to twenty" is the adverbial *ocad* with *-mde* (lesson 3-4).

**"All five", "about five".** Two particles attach to the number and make it a vowel stem that no longer changes before a noun (Bolkvadze and Kiziria):

- *-ve* "all": *samive* "all three" (Wiktionary), *ative* "all ten". *Orive* "both" (lesson 4-4) is the same word for two.
- *-ode* "about": *xutiode* "about five".

**Glossing.** *Tormeṭ-i* `twelve-NOM`; *čvidmeṭ sṭumar-s* `seventeen guest-DAT`, with the bare stem unglossed as in lesson 3-10; *sami-ve* `three-all`; *xuti-ode* `five-about`.

## Where it applies

- **Counting things and people**: always a singular noun and a singular verb (*txutmeṭi mosc̣avlea*; lesson 2-4).
- **Every case**: the number agrees like an adjective (*totxmeṭma mosc̣avlem*, *čvidmeṭ sṭumars*), and stands alone like a noun (*ocamde*).
- **Age**: number + *c̣lis* (*tertmeṭi c̣lisaa*), from lesson 3-3.
- **Prices and amounts**: *xuti lari* "five lari", *tormeṭi ḳilomeṭri*.
- **"How many?"**: *ramdeni* + singular noun; answer with the number alone.
- **"All N"** with *-ve* and **"about N"** with *-ode*.
- **Clock times and dates** use the same numbers (lesson 5-5); higher numbers (lesson 5-4) build on *oci*.
- **Frequency**: number + *-ǯer* (*tormeṭǯer* "twelve times", lesson 5-1).

## Watch out

**No plural after a number.** *\*Tormeṭi tveebi* repeats the plural, as English and Spanish do. Say *tormeṭi tve* (lesson 2-4).

**No plural verb.** *\*Čvidmeṭi sṭumari modian* counts the people twice. The verb follows the singular noun: *modis*.

**Drop -i only in the dative and adverbial.** By analogy with the dative, *\*xut c̣ignit* or *\*xut c̣ignis* look tidy, but the genitive and instrumental keep the *-i*: *xuti c̣ignis*, *xuti c̣ignit*. And in the ergative both words carry *-ma*: *xutma c̣ignma*, not *\*xuti c̣ignma*.

**Rva and cxra never change.** *\*Cxram bič̣ma* treats nine like a word in *-a* that takes *-m*. Before a noun it stays *cxra*.

**Spell the teens as single words.** *\*At sami*, *\*at-sam-meṭi* are reconstructions, not modern Georgian. Write *cameṭi*.

**Do not confuse cameṭi and cxrameṭi.** Both begin with ც. Thirteen is *cameṭi*, from *sami* "three"; nineteen is *cxrameṭi*, from *cxra* "nine".

**Erti is "one", not always "a".** *Erti* can mean "a, a certain" (lesson 2-3), but it is still a number: *erti c̣igni* is "one book" when you count.

## Connections

French keeps a trace of the same "ten and X" logic in *dix-sept*, *dix-huit*, and Spanish in *dieciséis* (*diez y seis*); Georgian goes further and says "ten, X, more", fused into one word. Japanese *jū-san* and Korean *sip-sam* "ten-three" are transparent in the same way, without the sound changes.

## Exercises

1. Write in Georgian: 13, 16, 19.
2. Take apart *totxmeṭi* using Bolkvadze and Kiziria's analysis.
3. Translate: "There are twenty children in the yard."
4. Translate: "I have twelve friends." (lesson 4-19: *mq̇avs*)
5. Put "five books" in the dative and the genitive.
6. Find and fix the error: *\*Tvrameṭi sṭudenṭi ak arian.*
7. Translate: "Nine boys came." (aorist *movida*)
8. What is the difference between *samive* and *sami*?

## Worked answers

1. *Cameṭi*, *tekvsmeṭi*, *cxrameṭi*.
2. *At-otx-meṭi*: "ten, four, more", that is, four more than ten. The *a* of *at-* has gone: *totxmeṭi*.
3. Singular noun and copula after the number: *Ezoši oci bavšvia.*
4. People are "had" with *q̇avs* (lesson 4-19); first person *mq̇avs*. The noun is singular: *Tormeṭi megobari mq̇avs.*
5. Dative: bare number, noun in *-s*: *xut c̣igns*. Genitive: number keeps *-i*: *xuti c̣ignis* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
6. After a number the verb is singular: *Tvrameṭi sṭudenṭi ak aris.*
7. *Movida* is class 2: the subject stays nominative even in the aorist (lesson 4-14). *Cxra* does not change: *Cxra bič̣i movida.*
8. *Sami* is "three"; *samive* "all three", "the three of them". *-Ve* makes it a vowel stem that does not change before a noun.
