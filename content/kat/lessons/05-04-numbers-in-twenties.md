## The idea

English counts in tens: thirty is "three tens", seventy "seven tens". Georgian counts in twenties up to a hundred. A counting system built on twenty is called **vigesimal**, from Latin *viginti* "twenty". Bolkvadze and Kiziria call it "bi-decimal": twenty, forty, sixty and eighty are the basic units, and the numbers one to nineteen are added to them.

So there is no word for thirty, fifty, seventy or ninety. Thirty is "twenty and ten", *ocdaati*. Fifty is "forty and ten", *ormocdaati*. Seventy-eight is "sixty and eighteen", *samocdatvrameṭi*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria stress the point with exactly that number: you might think of it as (7 × 10) + 8, but in Georgian it is counted as (3 × 20) + 18. This is why the teens of lesson 5-3 matter so much: every number up to ninety-nine reuses them.

The multiples of twenty are themselves built from *oci*: *or-m-oci* "two twenties" (40), *sam-oci* "three twenties" (60), *otx-m-oci* "four twenties" (80). Bolkvadze and Kiziria explain the odd *-m-*: it is what is left of the prefix *me-*. The full form was *or-me-oci*, which shrank to *ormoci*; in *sam-me-oci* the two *m*'s merged to give *samoci*.

Above a hundred, Georgian is decimal like English: *asi* 100, *orasi* 200, *atasi* 1,000, *milioni* 1,000,000.

The same *me-* turns up as the ordinal marker. An **ordinal** is a number that gives a position in a series: "third", "twentieth". Georgian makes it with the circumfix *me-* ... *-e* around the number's stem: *sami* → *mesame* "third", *oci* → *meoce* "twentieth". Only "first" is different: *ṗirveli*, which Bolkvadze and Kiziria trace not to *erti* "one" but to the noun *ṗiri* "face, person". You have already met *meore* "second; the other" (lesson 2-4).

## How it is built

**Twenty to ninety-nine.** Pick the largest multiple of twenty, then add *-da-* "and" and the rest (one to nineteen), all written as one word:

1. I want 47. The largest multiple of twenty below it is 40, *ormoci*.
2. The remainder is 7, *švidi*.
3. Drop the *-i* of *ormoci*, add *-da-*, then the remainder: *ormocdašvidi*.

More: *ocdaerti* 21, *ocdaati* 30 (Wiktionary), *ocdacameṭi* 33 (Wiktionary), *ormocdaati* 50, *samocdaekvsi* 66, *otxmocdacxrameṭi* 99 (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

**Hundreds.** *Asi* 100; the multiples join the unit's stem to *-asi*: *orasi* 200, *samasi* 300, *xutasi* 500, *cxraasi* 900 (Bolkvadze and Kiziria; Wiktionary). The multiples of a hundred are decimal: there is no "five twenties".

**Big numbers.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria's spelling rule: numbers up to a hundred and round hundreds are one word; thousands, hundreds and the rest are separate words, and every part except the last drops its *-i*: *atas cxraas samocdaekvsi* 1,966, *ori atas tormeṭi* 2,012. "Two thousand" keeps the *-i* of *ori* because *atasi* here behaves like a noun after a number: *ori atasi*.

**Ordinals.** Put *me-* before the stem and *-e* after it, replacing the *-i*:

- *me-otx-e* "fourth", *me-xut-e* "fifth", *me-at-e* "tenth", *me-oc-e* "twentieth", *me-as-e* "hundredth" (Wiktionary).
- In a compound number, *me-* goes on the part after *-da-*: *ocdameerte* "twenty-first" (Wiktionary), *ocdamečvidmeṭe* "thirty-seventh" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
- In a number with hundreds or thousands, only the last word changes: *as meerte* "hundred and first".

Ordinals end in *-e* (except *ṗirveli*), so they are vowel stems and do not change before a noun: *mesame taro*, *mesame taros* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

**Fractions and "for the nth time"** come from the ordinal. Add the adverbial *-d* for "the nth time": *meored* "for the second time". Add *-i* to that for a fraction: *mesamedi* "a third", *meatedi* "a tenth" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria; Wiktionary). "Half" is a separate word, *naxevari*.

**Writing ordinals with figures.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria: *me-* is written before the figure for round numbers and numbers to twenty (*me-6*, *me-12*, *me-100*); *-e* after the figure otherwise (*55-e*, *107-e*); "first" is *1-eli*.

**Glossing.** *Oc-da-at-i* `twenty-and-ten-NOM`; *or-m-oc-da-xut-i* is written as one piece *ormocdaxut-i* `forty.five-NOM`. Ordinals are glossed as one word: *mesame* `third`.

## Where it applies

- **All numbers from 21 to 99**: multiple of twenty + *-da-* + 1 to 19, one word.
- **Hundreds and thousands**: decimal; separate words; every part but the last without *-i* (*oras*, *atas*).
- **Years**: *atas cxraas samocdaekvsi* 1966; *ori atas ocdaekvsi* 2026. In a date the year takes the dative *c̣els* (lesson 5-5), and the last number drops its *-i* before it: *atas as ocdaert c̣els* "in the year 1121" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
- **Ordinals for position**: floors, centuries, school years, kings (*baɣraṭ mesame* "Bagrat III", Bolkvadze and Kiziria), chapters and pages.
- **Ordinals + -d** for "the nth time" (*meored*, *meated*) and **+ -di** for fractions (*mesamedi*, *meotxedi*).
- **meore** has two jobs: "second" and "the other (of two)" (lesson 2-4).
- **Phone numbers and prices** read with the same numbers: *ormocdaati lari* "fifty lari".

## Watch out

**No word for thirty, fifty, seventy, ninety.** By analogy with English, *\*samati* "three tens" or *\*xutati* look logical. They do not exist: *ocdaati*, *ormocdaati*, *samocdaati*, *otxmocdaati*.

**Ninety-five is not "nine tens and five".** Count from eighty: *otxmocdatxutmeṭi* (4 × 20 + 15).

**One word below a hundred.** *\*Oci da xuti* is how the parts are explained, not how they are written. Bolkvadze and Kiziria: numbers to a hundred are spelled together, *ocdaxuti*.

**Ordinals for compounds put me- in the middle.** *\*Meocdaerti* puts *me-* on the front. It goes after *-da-*: *ocdameerte*.

**"First" is not \*meerte on its own.** Alone it is *ṗirveli*. Only in compounds does *-meerte* appear (*ocdameerte*, *as meerte*).

**Ordinals in -e do not shrink before a noun.** After lesson 5-3 (*xut c̣igns*) you might write *\*mesam sartulze*. Ordinals in *-e* are vowel stems and stay whole in every case before a noun: *mesame sartulze*, *mesame tarom* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

**Thousands and hundreds lose -i in the middle.** *\*Orasi ocdatxutmeṭi* keeps an ending that belongs only at the end: *oras ocdatxutmeṭi*.

## Connections

French keeps a fossil of counting in twenties: *quatre-vingts* "four twenties" is 80 and *quatre-vingt-dix* "four twenties ten" is 90, exactly Georgian's *otxmoci* and *otxmocdaati*. Georgian applies the same logic consistently from 20 to 99. Basque, if you meet it in the app, also counts in twenties (*hogei* 20, *berrogei* 40).

## Exercises

1. Write in Georgian: 25, 46, 57.
2. Explain why 78 is *samocdatvrameṭi*.
3. Translate: "There are thirty-three pupils in the class."
4. Form the ordinals of *xuti*, *oci* and *ocdaerti*.
5. Translate: "We live on the fifth floor."
6. Find and fix the error: *\*Čemi ṗaṗa samati c̣lisaa.*
7. Read *atas cxraas otxmocdatormeṭi* as a figure.
8. What is *meotxedi*?

## Worked answers

1. 25 = 20 + 5: *ocdaxuti*. 46 = 40 + 6: *ormocdaekvsi*. 57 = 40 + 17: *ormocdačvidmeṭi* (all three are in Bolkvadze and Kiziria's table).
2. 78 = (3 × 20) + 18: *samoci* "sixty" + *-da-* + *tvrameṭi* "eighteen" (lesson 5-4).
3. Singular noun, singular copula: *Ḳlasši ocdacameṭi mosc̣avlea.*
4. *Mexute* "fifth", *meoce* "twentieth", *ocdameerte* "twenty-first", with *me-* after *-da-* (Wiktionary).
5. The ordinal stays unchanged before *sartulze*: *Čven mexute sartulze vcxovrobt.*
6. There is no *\*samati*; 30 is *ocdaati*: *Čemi ṗaṗa ocdaati c̣lisaa* (or, more likely for a grandfather, *otxmocdaati*, 90).
7. 1,000 + 900 + (4 × 20 + 12) = 1992.
8. *Meotxe* "fourth" + *-d* + *-i*: "a quarter, one fourth" (lesson 5-4, Bolkvadze and Kiziria's pattern *mesamedi*).
