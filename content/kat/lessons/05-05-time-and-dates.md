## The idea

Georgian has no special grammar for time. It uses the cases and postpositions you already have, each with its usual meaning. Bolkvadze and Kiziria's table of adverbial modifiers lays it out: the **dative** answers "when?" for a day or a date (*oršabats* "on Monday", *at sekṭembers* "on the tenth of September"); **-ši** "in" answers "when?" for a month and "how soon?" for a span (*aṗrilši* "in April", *or saatši* "in two hours"); **-ze** "on, at" fixes a point on the clock (*xut saatze* "at five o'clock"); the **instrumental** gives a length of time (*erti ḳvirit* "for a week"); and the genitive + *c̣in* or *šemdeg* puts things before or after (lesson 5-1).

You have been doing this since lesson 2-5: *dɣes* "today", *dilas*, *saɣamos* are datives of time. The dative is the case for a point in time you can name; *-ši* is the case for a container of time.

The names of the days tell their own story. Wiktionary derives *oršabati* "Monday" from *ori* "two" + *šabati* "Saturday", and *šabati* itself, through Old Georgian, ultimately from Hebrew *šabbat*. The week is counted from the Sabbath: Monday is the "second (day from) Sabbath", Tuesday *samšabati* the third, Wednesday *otxšabati* the fourth, Thursday *xutšabati* the fifth. Friday, *ṗarasḳevi*, is from Greek *paraskeuē* "preparation (day)", and Sunday, *ḳvira*, from Greek *kyriakē* "the Lord's (day)" (Wiktionary). *Ḳvira* also means "week" (lesson 4-5).

The clock uses one idea that differs from English: the minutes and the half hour belong to the hour that is coming, not the one that has passed. Bolkvadze and Kiziria's *xutis naxevari* "half of five" is half past four, as German *halb fünf* is. Wiktionary gives the same: *oris naxevari* "half past one".

## How it is built

**"What time is it?"** *Romeli saatia?* "Which hour is it?" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria). Answer with the number, *saati* and the copula: *sami saatia* "it is three o'clock". After the number *saati* is singular, as always (lesson 5-3).

**"At" a time.** Use *-ze* on the dative: number (bare stem before the dative) + *saatze*: *xut saatze* "at five", *rva saatze* "at eight" (*rva* does not change).

**Half past.** Genitive of the *next* hour + *naxevari*:

1. I want "half past four". The coming hour is five, *xuti*.
2. Genitive: *xutis*.
3. Add *naxevari*: *xutis naxevaria* "it is half past four".
4. "At half past four": *naxevari* takes *-ze*: *xutis naxevarze*. "After half past four": *xutis naxevris šemdeg* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria, with syncope of *naxevari*).

**Minutes.** Course materials (polyglotclub wiki, Easy Georgian) give the same pattern for minutes past: genitive of the coming hour + minutes + *c̣utia*: *samis ocdaxuti c̣utia* "it is twenty-five past two". For minutes to the hour they give the dative of the hour + *aḳlia* "is lacking": *xuts ati c̣uti aḳlia* "ten to five". These were not checked in a grammar, so the examples are marked unverified. The official style counts on a 24-hour clock: *totxmeṭi saati da ocdaxuti c̣uti* "14:25".

**Days.** Dative for "on": *oršabats*, *šabats*, *ḳvira-s*. "Today is Monday": *dɣes oršabatia*.

**Months.** *-Ši* for "in": *aṗrilši* "in April" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria). The month names are the European ones: *ianvari*, *tebervali*, *marṭi* ... *deḳemberi*.

**Dates.** Number (bare stem) + month in the dative: *at sekṭembers* "on 10 September" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria). As a subject, both are nominative: *ṗirveli ianvari* "the first of January". Note that dates use the cardinal *at*, not *meate*; only "the first" is *ṗirveli*.

**Years.** The dative *c̣els*: *atas as ocdaert c̣els* "in 1121" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria), and on its own *c̣els* "this year" (Wiktionary).

**Spans.** "For a week": instrumental, *erti ḳvirit* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria). "In a week" (from now): *-ši*, *erti ḳviraši*. "A week ago": *erti ḳviris c̣in* (lesson 5-1).

## Where it applies

- **Dative (point in time)**: days (*oršabats*), dates (*at sekṭembers*), years (*c̣els*, *atas as ocdaert c̣els*), parts of the day (*dilas*, *saɣamos*, lesson 2-5), and *dɣes*, *c̣els* "this year".
- **-ši (inside a period)**: months (*aṗrilši*), seasons (*zapxulši* "in summer"), centuries (*metormeṭe sauḳuneši*), and "in (so long) from now": *or saatši davbrundebi* "I'll be back in two hours" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
- **-ze (a point on the clock)**: *xut saatze*, *xutis naxevarze*; and the fixed *droze* "on time" (lesson 4-23).
- **Instrumental (duration)**: *erti ḳvirit*, *ori c̣lit*; and habitual parts of the day: *dilit*, *ɣamit*.
- **Genitive + c̣in, šemdeg**: before, after, ago (lesson 5-1).
- **-dan, -mde**: from, until: *dilidan saɣamomde* "from morning till evening" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
- **The genitive of time of day** before a clock time: *dilis cxra saatze* "at nine in the morning" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
- **Age**: number + *c̣lis* (lesson 5-3).

## Watch out

**Not -ze for days.** From Spanish *el lunes* or English "on Monday", *\*oršabatze* looks natural. Days take the plain dative: *oršabats*.

**Not -ši for clock times.** *Xut saatši* means "in five hours"; "at five o'clock" is *xut saatze*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria contrast exactly these.

**Half past counts forward.** *Xutis naxevari* is 4:30, not 5:30. Think of German *halb fünf*, not English "half five".

**Dates use the cardinal.** *\*Meate sekṭembers* translates "the tenth". Georgian says "ten September": *at sekṭembers*. Only the first is *ṗirveli*.

**Keep the number bare before a dative.** *\*Ati sekṭembers*, *\*xuti saatze* keep an *-i* that drops before a dative-based form (lesson 5-3): *at sekṭembers*, *xut saatze*.

**Saati stays singular.** *\*Sami saatebia* pluralises after a number: *sami saatia*.

**Months and days are not capitalised.** Georgian has no capitals (lesson 1-8): *ianvari*, *oršabati*.

**Ḳvira has two meanings.** *Ḳvira* is "Sunday" and "week". *Ḳvira-s* is "on Sunday"; *erti ḳvirit* is "for a week". Context decides.

## Connections

Spanish names the weekdays after planets and gods (*lunes*, *martes*); Georgian, like Portuguese *segunda-feira*, *terça-feira*, counts them from a holy day, and like Greek and Portuguese calls Sunday "the Lord's day" (*domingo*, *kyriakē*). Telling the half hour by the coming hour is shared with German and Russian, not with English, French or Spanish.

## Exercises

1. Translate: "On Friday I'm going to the theatre."
2. Translate: "It's eight o'clock."
3. How do you say "at half past six"?
4. Translate: "The concert begins on the fifth of May."
5. Which case or postposition goes with: a month, a day, a clock time, a duration?
6. Find and fix the error: *\*Oršabatze ar vmušaob.*
7. Translate: "I'll come back in three hours."
8. What does *xutis naxevari* mean, and why?

## Worked answers

1. A day takes the dative: *ṗarasḳevs*. "I'm going to the theatre": *teaṭrši mivdivar* (lesson 4-11). *Ṗarasḳevs teaṭrši mivdivar.*
2. Number + *saati* + copula: *rva saatia*. *Rva* is a vowel stem and does not change (lesson 5-3).
3. The coming hour is seven: genitive *švidis*, then *naxevari* with *-ze*: *švidis naxevarze*.
4. The date: bare number + month in the dative: *xut maiss*. "Begins": *ic̣q̇eba* (lesson 4-10), or *daic̣q̇eba* "will begin". *Ḳoncerṭi xut maiss ic̣q̇eba.*
5. Month: *-ši*; day: dative; clock time: *-ze*; duration: instrumental (lesson 5-5, Bolkvadze and Kiziria §16.4).
6. Days take the plain dative, not *-ze*: *Oršabats ar vmušaob.*
7. "In three hours" from now is *-ši*: *sam saatši*. "I'll come back": *davbrundebi* (lesson 4-10). *Sam saatši davbrundebi.*
8. Half past four. Half-hours and minutes are counted towards the coming hour: "half of five" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria; Wiktionary *oris naxevari* "half past one").
