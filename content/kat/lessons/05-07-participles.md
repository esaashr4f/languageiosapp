## The idea

A **participle** is an adjective or noun made from a verb. You met one kind in lesson 4-24, the past participle *dac̣erili* "written", which with "be" makes the perfect of class 2 verbs. Georgian has four main participles, and they map out every role around one action. Take "write":

- the one who does it: *mc̣erali* "writer"
- the thing it has been done to: *dac̣erili* "written"
- the thing it is still to be done to, or the thing it is done with: *dasac̣eri* "to be written", *sac̣eri* "for writing"
- the thing it has not been done to: *dauc̣ereli* "unwritten"

Bolkvadze and Kiziria explain the system through one verb, *ašenebs* "builds". Its doer is the **active participle** *mšenebeli* "builder". Its object can be described by three **passive participles**: *asašenebeli* "to be built", *ašenebuli* "built", *aušenebeli* "not yet built". The active participle names the subject of the verb; the passive ones describe its object.

Each kind has its own affix, and each affix sits in the same slots of the verb template you learnt in lesson 4-1. Bolkvadze and Kiziria's rule: if the verb has a preverb, it stays at the front; the first part of the participle's affix comes right after it; the last part comes after the thematic suffix. So *a-šen-eb-s* gives *a-m-šen-eb-el-i*, *a-sa-šen-eb-el-i*, *a-u-šen-eb-el-i*. Once you see the frame, you can take apart a participle you have never met.

Many common nouns are participles you already know. *Masc̣avlebeli* "teacher" is "one who teaches"; *gamq̇ideli* "shop assistant" is "one who sells"; *mzareuli* "cook"; *maṭarebeli* "train" is literally "carrier" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria). *Sac̣meli* "food" is "thing to be eaten"; *satamašo* "toy" is "thing for playing".

## How it is built

**1. The active participle: m- ... with a suffix.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria list the main shapes:

- *m-* ... *-el-i*: *m-ḳitxv-el-i* "reader", *m-smen-el-i* "listener", *m-ḳvl-el-i* "killer", *m-šen-eb-el-i* "builder". *-Eb* stays; a thematic *-i* drops.
- *m-* alone, keeping *-av*: *m-ḳer-av-i* "tailor, seamstress", *m-ṗar-av-i* "thief".
- *m-* ... *-al-i* or *-ar-i*: *m-c̣er-al-i* "writer", *m-xaṭv-ar-i* "painter" (lesson 2-2).

**2. The past passive participle: -il, -ul, m- ... -ar** (lesson 4-24): *da-c̣er-il-i* "written", *ga-ḳet-eb-ul-i* "made, done", *ga-m-tb-ar-i* "warmed". There is also an older *na-*: *na-c̣er-i* "something written", *na-xaṭ-i* "picture" (lesson 4-24's *naxaṭi*).

**3. The future passive participle: sa- or sa- ... -el.** It means "to be done" or "for doing":

1. I want "a letter to be written".
2. The verb is *dac̣ers*, preverb *da-*.
3. *Sa-* goes after the preverb: *da-sa-c̣er-i*.
4. *Dasac̣eri c̣erili*.

Without the preverb it names a purpose or a tool: *sa-c̣er-i ḳalami* "a pen for writing" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria; Wiktionary *sac̣eri* "for writing"). Verbs in *-eb* take *sa-* ... *-el*: *ga-sa-ḳet-eb-el-i* "to be done".

**4. The privative (negative) participle: u- ... -el, or u-.** "Not (yet) done":

- *da-u-c̣er-el-i* "unwritten" (Wiktionary *dauc̣ereli*), *ga-u-ḳet-eb-el-i* "not done", *da-u-mtavr-eb-el-i* "unfinished" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
- Verbs whose past participle is *m-* ... *-ar* take *u-* alone: *ga-u-m-tb-ar-i* "unheated".

**Using them.** All four decline like adjectives in *-i* (lesson 3-10). Before a noun they are attributes (*dasac̣eri c̣erili*); on their own they are nouns (*mḳitxveli* "a reader"). The future participle often follows "have": *bevri sakme makvs gasaḳetebeli* "I have a lot to do" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

**Glossing.** Following the dossier, the circumfix parts are glossed by type: active and past participles `PTCP` (`m-ḳitxv-el-i` `PTCP-read-PTCP-NOM`), future `FUT.PTCP` (`da-sa-c̣er-i` `PV-FUT.PTCP-write-NOM`), privative `PRIV` (`da-u-c̣er-el-i` `PV-PRIV-write-PRIV-NOM`).

## Where it applies

- **Active participles as nouns for people, animals and machines**: *mc̣erali*, *mḳitxveli*, *mšenebeli*, *mḳeravi*, *maṭarebeli* "train". Many job names are built this way (*masc̣avlebeli*, *gamq̇ideli*).
- **Active participles as adjectives**: *mḳeravi kali*; Bolkvadze and Kiziria's *mḳurnali ekimi* "family doctor".
- **Past participles** as adjectives (*daxuruli*, *daḳarguli*, lesson 4-24), as predicates of state (*banḳi daxurulia*), in the class 2 perfect (lesson 4-24), and with *mier* (lesson 5-1).
- **The na- participle** in nouns for results: *naxaṭi* "picture", *nac̣eri* "piece of writing".
- **Future participles** for "to be done" (*dasac̣eri c̣erili*), for tools and purposes (*sac̣eri ḳalami*, *sač̣reli dana*), after "have" (*sakme makvs gasaḳetebeli*), and in many everyday nouns: *sac̣meli* "food", *sasmeli* "drink" (Wiktionary: future passive participle of *svams*), *satamašo* "toy".
- **Privative participles** for "not done, un-": *dauc̣ereli*, *daumtavrebeli*, *gauḳetebeli*; also as fixed adjectives (*gauzrdeli* "rude", literally "not brought up", Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

## Watch out

**The object of a future participle is genitive.** By analogy with the verb, *\*ṗurs sač̣reli dana* gives "bread" the dative object case. A participle is a noun-like word, so its object is genitive: *ṗuris sač̣reli dana*.

**Keep the preverb in front.** *\*Sadac̣eri* puts *sa-* before the preverb. The order is preverb + *sa-*: *dasac̣eri*. The same for *u-*: *dauc̣ereli*, not *\*udac̣ereli*.

**The m- is not "me".** In *mḳitxveli* and *mc̣erali* the *m-* is part of the participle, as in lesson 4-24's *gamtbari*.

**Do not mix up the past participle and the perfect.** *Dac̣erili* is "written" (adjective); *dac̣erila* is "it has been written" (lesson 4-24). The final *-a* is the verb "be".

**Not every verb has every participle.** Bolkvadze and Kiziria list medial verbs such as *xumrobs* "jokes" that form no participle. Check each in a dictionary.

**Do not use -ul and -il at random.** *-Ul* goes with *-eb* and most *-av* verbs (*ašenebuli*, *daḳarguli*); *-il* with root verbs (*dac̣erili*). *\*Daḳargili* and *\*dac̣eruli* are wrong.

**The plural -eb- goes after the participle suffix.** *\*Mḳitxv-eb-eli* inserts it inside. Treat the participle as a finished noun: *mḳitxvelebi*.

## Connections

Spanish *-dor* (*lector*, *constructor*) and English *-er* make doer nouns as Georgian *m-* ... *-el* does, and Spanish *por hacer* "to be done" (*trabajo por hacer*) matches the future participle's "to be done". Japanese uses a plain verb before a noun for all of these (*kaku pen* "a pen for writing", *kaita tegami* "a written letter"); Georgian builds a separate word for each role.

## Exercises

1. Take apart *mšenebeli* and say what it means.
2. Make the past and future participles of *dac̣ers* and translate them.
3. Translate: "an unwritten letter".
4. Translate: "I have a letter to write." (*makvs*)
5. Find and fix the error: *\*Es ṗurs sač̣reli danaa.*
6. Which participle is *sac̣meli* "food", and what does it literally mean?
7. Translate: "The readers met the writer."
8. Explain the difference between *daxuruli* and *daxurula*.

## Worked answers

1. *M-šen-eb-el-i*: the active circumfix *m-* ... *-el* around the stem *šen-eb* of *ašenebs*: "builder" (Wiktionary; lesson 5-7).
2. Past: *dac̣erili* "written". Future: *dasac̣eri* "to be written", with *sa-* after *da-*.
3. The privative with the preverb in front: *dauc̣ereli c̣erili*.
4. "Have" + future participle: *Dasac̣eri c̣erili makvs* (or *C̣erili makvs dasac̣eri*, on Bolkvadze and Kiziria's pattern).
5. A participle's object is genitive: *Es ṗuris sač̣reli danaa.*
6. A future passive participle of "eat" (*sa-* ... *-el*): "thing to be eaten".
7. *Šexvda* "met" takes a dative object (Bolkvadze and Kiziria's sentence). Swap roles: *Mḳitxvelebi mc̣erals šexvdnen*: the plural people take a plural verb; *šexvdnen* is the 3PL aorist (lingua.ge).
8. *Daxuruli* is the past participle, "closed" (an adjective); *daxurula* adds "be" and is the perfect, "it has (apparently) been closed" (lesson 4-24).
