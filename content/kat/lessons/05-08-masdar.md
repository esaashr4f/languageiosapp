## The idea

The **masdar** is a noun made from a verb that names the action itself: *c̣era* "writing", *ḳitxva* "reading", *č̣ama* "eating". Lesson 4-26 introduced it as the form dictionaries such as lingua.ge list verbs under; Georgian grammars call it *sac̣q̇isi*, "the starting form". The English name is the Arabic grammatical term *maṣdar* "source", which fits: it is the bare action from which the forms are made.

Bolkvadze and Kiziria, who call it the gerund, state its double nature. It has properties of a verb: it is built on the verb's stem, it keeps the preverb (*dac̣era* "writing (it) down", as against *c̣era*), and so it keeps the verb's aspect. But it has no person markers and it declines like a noun. With a few exceptions it has no plural.

That noun nature decides how its arguments are marked. A verb takes subjects and objects in the nominative, ergative and dative. A noun takes other nouns in the genitive (lesson 3-3): *c̣ignis gverdi* "the book's page". The masdar is a noun, so its object is genitive: *c̣ignis ḳitxva* "the reading of the book", "reading the book". This is why Bolkvadze and Kiziria's "I want to buy a new car" is *axali mankanis q̇idva minda*, literally "of a new car the buying I want".

The masdar is also how Georgian says many things that English says with an infinitive. Georgian has no infinitive. "I want to read" is either *unda* + optative (lesson 4-16: *minda c̣aviḳitxo*) or *unda* + masdar (*ḳitxva minda* "I want reading"). "It is forbidden to stand here" is *ak dgoma aḳrʒalulia* "here standing is forbidden".

## How it is built

**From the present or future stem + -a** (Bolkvadze and Kiziria). Take the verb, strip the person markers and the version vowel, keep the preverb if there is one:

1. *C̣er-s* "writes" → stem *c̣er-* → *c̣er-a*. With the preverb: *da-c̣er-a*.
2. *A-šen-eb-s* "builds" → *šen-eb-a*; with the preverb *a-šen-eb-a*. Thematic *-eb* and *-ob* stay: *grʒnoba* "feeling".
3. A thematic *-i* drops: *xsn-i-s* "opens" → *xsn-a*; *č̣r-i-s* "cuts" → *č̣r-a*.
4. *-Av* and *-am* lose their vowel: *xur-av-s* → *xurv-a* "closing"; *cur-av-s* → *curv-a* "swimming"; *a-sx-am-s* → *sxm-a* "pouring".
5. After a consonant cluster ending in *n*, *r*, *l*, the *v* moves inside: *ḳl-av-s* → *ḳvl-a* "killing".

**Other shapes**, which you learn word by word (Bolkvadze and Kiziria list them):

- *-n-a*: *txov-n-a* "request" (*txovs*), *ʒeb-n-a* "search" (*eʒebs*).
- *-om-a*, *-ol-a*: *dg-om-a* "standing", *ǯd-om-a* "sitting", *c̣-ol-a* "lying", *brʒ-ol-a* "fighting".
- *-il-i*: *dum-il-i* "silence", *q̇vir-il-i* "shouting", *ṭq̇u-il-i* "lie" (lesson 4-4).
- *si-* ... *-il-i* / *-ul-i*: *si-c-il-i* "laughter" from *icinis*, *si-rb-il-i* "running", *si-xar-ul-i* "joy", *si-q̇var-ul-i* "love" (lesson 4-18).
- Motion: *svla* "going", *mosvla* "coming", *c̣asvla* "leaving" (Wiktionary).
- Some medial verbs are made *from* a noun, which then serves as their masdar: *laṗaraḳi* "talk" → *laṗaraḳobs* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).

**Using it.**

- **As subject or object** of another verb: *ḳitxva miq̇vars* "I love reading".
- **Its own object in the genitive**: *mankanis q̇idva*.
- **Its doer**, if named, also genitive or possessive: *čemi mosvla* "my coming".
- **With postpositions**, like any noun: *č̣amis c̣in* "before eating" (lesson 5-1), *c̣asvlamde* "before leaving", *mosvlis šemdeg* "after arriving".
- **With -isas** "while, at the time of": *misvlisas* "on arriving" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria's list of adverbs).

**Glossing.** The suffix *-a* is glossed `MSD`: *c̣er-a* `write-MSD`, *da-c̣er-a* `PV-write-MSD`. Fused shapes are glossed as one piece: *ḳitxv-a* `read-MSD`, *sicil-i* `laughter-NOM`.

## Where it applies

- **Naming an activity**: *curva*, *ḳitxva*, *sirbili*, as subject or object of another verb.
- **After "want", "love", "like"** (class 4 verbs, lesson 4-18) instead of an English infinitive: *q̇idva minda*, *ḳitxva miq̇vars*.
- **Prohibitions and notices**: *ak dgoma aḳrʒalulia* "standing here is forbidden".
- **With postpositions for time**: *č̣amis c̣in*, *č̣amis šemdeg*, *c̣asvlamde*, *misvlisas* "on arriving".
- **With -tvis "for"**: *sc̣avlistvis* "for studying".
- **Nouns that started as masdars**: *sicocxle* "life", *siq̇varuli* "love", *ṭq̇uili* "lie", *txovna* "request", *daxmareba* "help", *dabadeba* "birth" (earlier lessons). Many are now ordinary nouns, some with plurals (*brʒolebi* "battles", Wiktionary).
- **Citation**: the masdar is how lingua.ge and Georgian dictionaries list verbs (lesson 4-26).

## Watch out

**The object is genitive, not dative.** From the verb (*mankanas q̇idulobs*), *\*mankanas q̇idva minda* looks natural. The masdar is a noun: *mankanis q̇idva minda*.

**No person markers.** *\*Vc̣era* "my writing" adds the verb's *v-*. The masdar never has them; say *čemi c̣era*.

**There is no infinitive to translate "to".** *\*Minda c̣era c̣igni* copies the English order with a verb-like object. Either *c̣ignis c̣era minda* (masdar) or *minda c̣igni davc̣ero* (optative, lesson 4-16). The optative is commoner when there is a definite one-off action.

**Keep the preverb that matches the meaning.** *C̣era* is "writing" as an activity; *dac̣era* is writing something through to the end. *Mosvla* is "coming", *c̣asvla* "leaving": the preverb carries the direction (lesson 4-11).

**Do not confuse the masdar with the participle.** *Dac̣era* "the writing down" (noun of action) versus *dac̣erili* "written" (lesson 5-7). The masdar ends in *-a* (or *-il-i*), the past participle in *-il-i*, *-ul-i*.

**Do not form a masdar by rule for every verb.** *\*Icinisa* or *\*cinra* for "laughter" fails; the masdar is *sicili*. The vocabulary notes give each masdar.

**The masdar of "want" or "know" is not what you need.** Class 4 verbs have masdars too (*codna* "knowledge" from *icis*, lesson 4-25), but they name a state. "I want to know" is *minda vicode*, not *\*codna minda*.

## Connections

Arabic gives the masdar its name and its job: Arabic *qirāʾat al-kitāb* "the reading of the book" puts the object in the genitive exactly as Georgian *c̣ignis ḳitxva* does. Spanish infinitives can act as nouns (*el leer*), but keep a verb-like object (*leer el libro*); Georgian makes the object fully a noun's. Japanese *hon o yomu koto* keeps the object marker *o*; Georgian does not.

## Exercises

1. Make the masdars of *c̣ers*, *xsnis*, *xuravs*.
2. Translate: "I want to buy a book."
3. Translate: "Reading is my pleasure." (*siamovneba*)
4. Find and fix the error: *\*Mankanas q̇idva minda.*
5. Translate: "After the lesson ... " and "after eating".
6. Take apart *sicili* and name its source verb.
7. Translate: "Swimming here is forbidden."
8. Why is the object of a masdar genitive?

## Worked answers

1. Stem + *-a*: *c̣era*; thematic *-i* drops: *xsna*; *-av* loses its vowel: *xurva* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
2. Object genitive + masdar + *minda*: *C̣ignis q̇idva minda.*
3. *Ḳitxva čemi siamovnebaa* (masdar as subject, lesson 5-8).
4. The masdar's object is genitive: *Mankanis q̇idva minda.*
5. *Gaḳvetilis šemdeg* (lesson 5-1); *č̣amis šemdeg*, with the masdar *č̣ama* in the genitive.
6. *Si-c-il-i*: the circumfix *si-* ... *-il-i* on the root of *icinis* "laughs", shortened (Bolkvadze and Kiziria: *sicili* < *si-cin-il-i*).
7. *Ak curva aḳrʒalulia.* The masdar is the subject; the participle with *-a* is the predicate.
8. Because it is a noun. A noun's dependent noun is genitive (lesson 3-3); the masdar keeps the verb's meaning but takes a noun's syntax.
