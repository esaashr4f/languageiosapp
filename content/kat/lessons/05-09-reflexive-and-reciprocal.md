## The idea

A **reflexive** says that the doer acts on itself: "he washed himself". A **reciprocal** says that two or more doers act on each other: "they love each other". English uses special pronouns, *-self* and *each other*. Georgian uses two ordinary nouns.

The reflexive is *tavi* "head" (lesson 1-2). Bolkvadze and Kiziria show the two readings side by side: *man tavi daibana* "he washed his head (his hair)", but *man tavi gadairčina* "he saved himself". The head stands for the whole person, as English "a head count" or Spanish *cabeza de familia*. The same word gave the possessive *tavisi* "his own" (lesson 3-8), which Bolkvadze and Kiziria derive from the genitive of *tav-*.

The reciprocal is *ertmaneti* "each other", which is "one" twice. Wiktionary and Bolkvadze and Kiziria explain it: Old Georgian *ertmanerti*, in which the first *erti* "one" carries the old ergative ending *-man*. So it is literally "one (as doer) ... one (as object)": one does it to the other.

Both are grammatically **third-person nouns**. This is the key to using them. *Tavi* is "a head", so the verb agrees with it as with any third-person object: zero marking. "I see myself" is "I see my head": *čems tavs vxedav*, with the same *vxedav* as "I see the house". The verb never takes *m-* "me" for a reflexive. The person comes from a possessive: *čemi tavi* "myself", *šeni tavi* "yourself", *tavisi tavi* or plain *tavi* "himself, herself".

## How it is built

**The reflexive.**

1. Decide the case. *Tavi* is the object, so it takes the object's case for the series (lesson 4-9): dative in series I, nominative in series II.
2. Add the owner's possessive for first and second person: *čemi*, *šeni*, *čveni*, *tkveni*. Before a dative noun they are *čems*, *šens*, *čvens*, *tkvens* (lesson 3-8).
3. The verb agrees with a third-person object (zero).
4. Very often the verb takes the subjective version *i-* (lesson 4-21), "for oneself": *daibana*, *gadairčina*. Bolkvadze and Kiziria list "the subject acts upon itself" as the first use of the subjective version.

So "I protect myself": *icavs* "protects" already has *i-*; first person *vicav*; object in the dative: *čems tavs vicav*.

**Third person.** With a third-person doer, *tavi* alone is enough when the version vowel or the context makes it clear: *tavi moiḳla* "he killed himself" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria). To insist on "himself and no one else", use *tavisi tavi*: *tavis tavs xedavs*.

**The academic analysis.** Tamar Makharoblidze's paper on version in Georgian (Ilia State University, 2012) gives examples like *dagavic̣q̇a man šen čemi tavi* "he made you forget me (myself)", in which *čemi tavi* is a third-person nominative object. That confirms that the possessive marks the person while the verb treats *tavi* as third person.

**The reciprocal.**

1. The doers are plural, so the verb is plural (lesson 2-4).
2. *Ertmaneti* takes the object's case: dative in series I (*ertmanets xedaven* "they see each other"), nominative with *uq̇vart* (*ertmaneti uq̇vart* "they love each other", Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
3. It takes postpositions like any noun: *ertmanettan* "with each other", *ertmanetis* "each other's", *ertmanetze* "about, on each other".

Bolkvadze and Kiziria give two synonyms, *urtierti* and *ertimeore* ("one-second"), and note that *ertmaneti* is preferred in modern Georgian.

**Emphatic "himself" is not reflexive.** "The director himself came" uses *tviton* (lesson 2-9): *direkṭori tviton movida*. *Tvit* and *tavad* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria) are literary equivalents.

**Glossing.** *Tav-i* `self-NOM` when reflexive, `head-NOM` when literal; *ertmanet-s* `each.other-DAT`.

## Where it applies

- **Any transitive verb whose object is the doer**: *tavi moiḳla*, *čems tavs vicav*, *šens tavs xedav*.
- **Body-care verbs with the subjective version**, where *tavi* is literal: *tavi daibana* "washed his hair". Bolkvadze and Kiziria list *muxli iṭḳina* "hurt his knee", *ḳudi daixura* "put on his hat" as the same pattern with other nouns.
- **Feelings and states**: *tavs ḳargad grʒnobs* "feels well".
- **After postpositions**: *tavis tavze pikrobs* "thinks about himself".
- **Reciprocal objects in any series**: dative in series I (*ertmanets eʒeben*), nominative in series II (*ertmaneti naxes* "they saw each other").
- **With indirect verbs**: nominative *ertmaneti* (*ertmaneti uq̇vart*).
- **With postpositions**: *ertmanettan* "with each other", *ertmanetis* "each other's", *ertmanetze* "on, about each other".
- **Fixed expressions**: *vai čems tavs!* "woe is me!" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria, interjections).

## Watch out

**No first-person object marker.** From lesson 4-3, *\*čems tavs mxedav* looks right for "you see me". It is wrong for "I see myself": the verb treats *tavi* as third person. *Čems tavs vxedav* "I see myself".

**Version is not a reflexive.** *Viban* "I wash (something) for myself" does not mean "I wash myself" (lesson 4-21). The object must be named: *tavs viban*.

**Do not drop the possessive for first and second person.** *\*Tavs vxedav* is ambiguous and, out of context, tends to be read as "I see (his) head". Say *čems tavs*.

**Spanish se is not one word in Georgian.** *Se lava* "he washes himself" and *se aman* "they love each other" share one pronoun in Spanish. Georgian keeps them apart: *tavs ibans*, *ertmaneti uq̇vart*.

**Emphatic "himself" is tviton.** *\*Direkṭorma tavisi tavi movida* misreads "the director himself came". Use *direkṭori tviton movida*.

**The verb agrees with the doers, not with ertmaneti.** *\*Bavšvebi ertmanets eʒebs* makes the verb singular because *ertmaneti* is singular. The doers are plural people: *eʒeben*.

**Ertmaneti is not "one another" in two words.** *\*Erti meores* is possible in older or literary style (*ertimeore*), but the everyday form is one word, *ertmaneti*.

## Connections

Using "head" for "self" is widespread: Basque *bere burua* "his own head" is exactly Georgian *tavisi tavi* (a Georgian study of Basque and Kartvelian version systems, in the National Parliamentary Library's collection, compares the two). Arabic uses *nafs* "soul" (*nafsahu* "himself") the same way, and Arabic *baʿḍuhum baʿḍan* "some of them some" is built like *ertmaneti*, "one ... one". Spanish *se* and French *se* cover both meanings with one word; Georgian never does.

## Exercises

1. Translate: "I protect myself." (*vicav*)
2. Translate: "You see yourself in the mirror."
3. Why is "I see myself" *čems tavs vxedav* and not *\*čems tavs mxedav*?
4. Translate: "They see each other every day." (*xedaven*)
5. Translate: "We love each other." (*gviq̇vars*)
6. Find and fix the error: *\*Bavšvebi ertmanets eʒebs.*
7. What does *man tavi daibana* mean, and what are its two readings?
8. Translate: "The teacher himself came."

## Worked answers

1. *Tavi* is the object, dative in series I, with *čems*: *Čems tavs vicav.*
2. Second person subject has no prefix: *Sarḳeši šens tavs xedav.*
3. *Tavi* is grammatically third person, so the object marker is zero, as for any third-person object (lesson 4-3). The person is shown by *čems*.
4. Plural doers, plural verb, *ertmaneti* dative: *Ertmanets q̇oveldɣe xedaven.*
5. With the indirect verb the lovers are dative and *ertmaneti* nominative: *Čven ertmaneti gviq̇vars* (Bolkvadze and Kiziria's pattern, lesson 4-18 for *gviq̇vars*).
6. The verb follows the plural doers: *Bavšvebi ertmanets eʒeben.*
7. Literally "he washed his head", that is, his hair; *tavi* is a body part. With another verb (*gadairčina* "saved"), the same *tavi* means "himself" (Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
8. Emphatic, not reflexive: *Masc̣avlebeli tviton movida.*
