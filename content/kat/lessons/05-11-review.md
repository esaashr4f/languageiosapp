## Before you start

This review covers part 5: adverbs and the genitive postpositions (lesson 5-1); comparison (5-2); numbers to twenty and numbers before nouns (5-3); counting in twenties and ordinals (5-4); days, dates and the clock (5-5); conjunctions and the particles *-c*, *ḳi* and *xom* (5-6); the four participles (5-7); the masdar (5-8); the reflexive *tavi* and the reciprocal *ertmaneti* (5-9); and derivation (5-10). Verbs are used in the screeves of part 4, which you need throughout.

Two habits from this part are worth checking in every sentence. After a number the noun and the verb are singular, and the number loses its *-i* before a dative. And whenever a noun depends on something noun-like (a postposition such as *šemdeg*, a participle, a masdar), it goes into the genitive.

## Passage

**A weekend in Kutaisi.** Constructed, using only words and grammar from parts 1 to 5, except sentence 2, which is Bolkvadze and Kiziria's (chapter 11). Mkhedruli first, then the transliteration.

1. შაბათს, ათ მაისს, ქუთაისში წავედით.
   *šabats, at maiss, kutaisši c̣avedit.*
2. თბილისიდან ქუთაისამდე ორას ოცდათხუთმეტი კილომეტრია.
   *tbilisidan kutaisamde oras ocdatxutmeṭi ḳilomeṭria.*
3. მატარებელი დილის რვა საათზე გავიდა და ოთხის ნახევარზე ჩავედით.
   *maṭarebeli dilis rva saatze gavida da otxis naxevarze čavedit.*
4. ქუთაისი თბილისზე პატარაა, მაგრამ უფრო ძველია.
   *kutaisi tbilisze ṗaṭaraa, magram upro ʒvelia.*
5. მთელი დღე ქალაქში ვსეირნობდით.
   *mteli dɣe kalakši vseirnobdit.*
6. საღამოს ერთ პატარა რესტორანში ვიყავით.
   *saɣamos ert ṗaṭara resṭoranši viq̇avit.*
7. იქ ხინკალიც შევჭამეთ და კახური ღვინოც დავლიეთ.
   *ik xinḳalic ševč̣amet da ḳaxuri ɣvinoc davliet.*
8. ღვინო ოდნავ მწარე იყო, ხინკალი კი საუკეთესო.
   *ɣvino odnav mc̣are iq̇o, xinḳali ḳi sauḳeteso.*
9. მეორე დღეს, კვირას, სახლში დავბრუნდით.
   *meore dɣes, ḳviras, saxlši davbrundit.*
10. ქუთაისში ისევ წავალთ, ოღონდ ზაფხულში.
   *kutaisši isev c̣avalt, oɣond zapxulši.*

## Exercises

1. Translate sentences 1 to 3.
2. Translate sentences 4 to 6.
3. Translate sentences 7 to 10.
4. In sentence 1, why is it *at maiss* and not *\*ati maiss* or *\*meate maiss*? Which case is *šabats*?
5. In sentence 2, read the number as a figure and explain how it is built.
6. In sentence 3, what time is *otxis naxevarze*? Why *-ze*?
7. In sentence 4, which word is the standard of comparison, and how is it marked? Could *upro* be left out of the first half?
8. In sentence 7, what do the two *-c*'s add? Take apart *ḳaxuri*.
9. In sentence 8, what does *ḳi* do, and why is it after *xinḳali*? What kind of form is *sauḳeteso*?
10. In sentence 10, what does *oɣond* mean here, and why *zapxulši* with *-ši*?
11. Say "Three years ago I lived in Kutaisi, but now I live in Tbilisi."
12. Say "This is the biggest city in Georgia." and "Tbilisi is bigger than Batumi."
13. Say "Sixty-seven pupils are coming on the twentieth of September."
14. Say "We live on the fourth floor." and "This is my first letter."
15. Say "a letter written by my mother" and "an unwritten letter".
16. Say "I want to buy a new bag." with a masdar.
17. Say "I see myself in the mirror." and "They love each other."
18. Make the "trade" word from *baɣi* and the "quality" word from *lamazi*.
19. Find and fix the error: *\*Xuti sṭudenṭi ak arian.*
20. Find and fix the error: *\*Gaḳvetils šemdeg saxlši c̣avedi.*
21. Find and fix the error: *\*Čai an q̇ava ginda?*
22. Find and fix the error: *\*Čems tavs mxedav sarḳeši* (meant: "I see myself in the mirror").

## Worked answers

1. (1) "On Saturday, the tenth of May, we went to Kutaisi." (2) "It is two hundred and thirty-five kilometres from Tbilisi to Kutaisi." (3) "The train left at eight in the morning, and we arrived at half past three." Days and dates take the dative (lesson 5-5); *-dan* and *-mde* mark the ends of the distance (lesson 3-4).
2. (4) "Kutaisi is smaller than Tbilisi, but older." (5) "We strolled around the city all day." (6) "In the evening we were in a little restaurant." *Mteli dɣe* "the whole day" is a nominative of duration; *vseirnobdit* is the imperfect of the medial *seirnobs* (lessons 4-5, 4-15).
3. (7) "There we ate khinkali and drank Kakhetian wine too." (8) "The wine was slightly bitter, but the khinkali were the best." (9) "The next day, Sunday, we came back home." (10) "We'll go to Kutaisi again, but in summer."
4. Dates use the cardinal (only "the first" is *ṗirveli*), and before a dative noun a number drops its *-i* (lessons 5-3, 5-5; Bolkvadze and Kiziria's *at sekṭembers*). *Šabats* is the dative: "on" a day.
5. 235 = *oras* (200, without its final *-i* inside the number) + *ocdatxutmeṭi* (20 + 15). The part under a hundred is vigesimal (lesson 5-4).
6. 3:30. Half hours count towards the coming hour: "half of four" (lesson 5-5). *-Ze* marks a point on the clock, as in *rva saatze*.
7. *Tbilisze*: *-ze* on the dative stem marks the standard (lesson 5-2). Yes: with a *-ze* phrase, *upro* is optional (*šenze maɣali*); in the second half there is no *-ze* phrase, so *upro* is needed.
8. Each *-c* adds its noun to the other: "khinkali too, and wine too", a "both ... and" (lesson 5-6). *Ḳax-ur-i*: *-ur-i* for a thing from a place (Kakheti), against *ḳaxeli* for a person (lesson 5-10).
9. *Ḳi* contrasts the khinkali with the wine; it follows the contrasted word (lesson 5-6). *Sauḳeteso* is the superlative of *ḳargi*, with the circumfix *sa-* ... *-o* around *uḳetes-* (lesson 5-2).
10. "But (only), provided it is" in summer (lesson 5-6, *oɣond* "but, only"). Seasons, like months, are periods: *-ši* "in" (lesson 5-5).
11. *Sami c̣lis c̣in kutaisši vcxovrobdi, axla ḳi tbilisši vcxovrob.* Genitive + *c̣in* for "ago" (lesson 5-1); *ḳi* after *axla* for the contrast (lesson 5-6); the imperfect for a past state (lesson 4-5).
12. *Es sakartvelos q̇velaze didi kalakia* (lesson 5-2; *q̇velaze* "than all"). *Tbilisi batumze didia* or *upro didia* (*batumi*, dative *batums* → *batumze*).
13. 67 = 60 + 7, so *samocdašvidi* (lesson 5-4). *Samocdašvidi mosc̣avle oc sekṭembers modis.* Singular noun and verb after the number (lesson 5-3); bare *oc* before the dative date (lesson 5-5).
14. *Čven meotxe sartulze vcxovrobt*: the ordinal *me-otx-e* is unchanged before the noun (lesson 5-4). *Es čemi ṗirveli c̣erilia*: "first" is *ṗirveli* (lesson 5-4).
15. *Dedačemis mier dac̣erili c̣erili*: past participle with *mier* + genitive for the doer (lessons 5-1, 5-7). *Dauc̣ereli c̣erili*: the privative *u-* ... *-el* after the preverb (lesson 5-7).
16. *Axali čantis q̇idva minda.* The masdar *q̇idva* takes its object in the genitive (lesson 5-8; Bolkvadze and Kiziria's *axali mankanis q̇idva minda*). *Čanta* truncates: genitive *čantis* (lesson 3-5).
17. *Sarḳeši čems tavs vxedav*: *tavi* is a third-person object, so plain *vxedav* (lesson 5-9). *Mat ertmaneti uq̇vart*: the lovers dative, *ertmaneti* nominative with the indirect verb (lesson 5-9, Bolkvadze and Kiziria).
18. *Mebaɣe* "gardener" (*me-* ... *-e*); *silamaze* "beauty" (*si-* ... *-e*) (lesson 5-10).
19. After a number the verb is singular too: *Xuti sṭudenṭi ak aris* (lessons 2-4, 5-3).
20. *Šemdeg* takes the genitive: *Gaḳvetilis šemdeg saxlši c̣avedi* (lesson 5-1).
21. "Or" in a question is *tu*: *Čai ginda tu q̇ava?* (lesson 5-6).
22. The verb does not mark "me" for a reflexive: *Sarḳeši čems tavs vxedav* (lesson 5-9). *Mxedav* means "you see me".
