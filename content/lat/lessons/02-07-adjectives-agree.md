## The idea

An **adjective** is a word that describes a noun: "good", "long", "my". In English it never changes. In Latin it changes its ending to match the noun it describes, in three things at once: gender, number and case (ag1903 §286). This matching is called **agreement**. You met agreement in number between a subject and its verb. Here it is complete.

Why should a describing word change at all? Because in Latin an adjective is a kind of noun. Allen and Greenough say adjectives are declined like nouns and belong with them by origin (ag1903 §36). An adjective like *bonus* "good" has a full set of endings for each gender: masculine like *servus*, feminine like *puella*, neuter like *bellum* (ag1903 §110). It takes the set that fits its noun.

This is what gender was for. The noun *mūrus* shows no gender in its ending, but an adjective with it must take the masculine form: *mūrus longus*. Gender is the noun's instruction to its adjective (ag1903 §30).

The dictionary lists such an adjective by its three nominative singulars: *bonus, bona, bonum*, usually shortened to *bonus, -a, -um*.

## How it is built

Say "the good sailor is working".

1. Find the noun and its job: *nauta*, the subject, so nominative singular.
2. Look up its gender: *nauta, nautae*, m. Masculine.
3. Find the adjective: *bonus, -a, -um*.
4. Take the adjective's masculine nominative singular: *bonus*.
5. Put it beside the noun: *Nauta bonus labōrat*.

Step 2 matters. The adjective copies the noun's gender, not the sound of its ending. *Nauta* ends in *-a*, but *bona* would be feminine. Agreement is not rhyme.

The same steps work for an adjective after *sum*. "The girl is happy": *puella* is nominative feminine singular, so the adjective is too: *Puella laeta est*. An adjective used this way, to say what the subject is like, is a **predicate adjective**. One beside its noun, as in *puella laeta* "the happy girl", is **attributive** (ag1903 §285).

A few adjectives end in *-er* in the masculine nominative singular, like *puer* and *magister* (ag1903 §§111-112). The feminine form shows whether the *e* stays: *miser, misera, miserum* "wretched" keeps it; *pulcher, pulchra, pulchrum* drops it. So "a wretched girl" is *puella misera*, and "a beautiful field" is *ager pulcher*, accusative *agrum pulchrum*.

## Where it applies

- **Beside a noun** (attributive), before or after it: *nauta bonus*, *multōs amīcōs* (ag1903 §285).
- **After *sum*** (predicate), in the nominative: *Puella laeta est* (ag1903 §§284-285).
- **When the subject is in the verb.** The adjective then shows the speaker's gender: a man says *Fessus sum*, a woman *Fessa sum*, "I am tired". A group of men or a mixed group says *Fessī sumus* (ag1903 §287), a group of women *Fessae sumus*.
- **On its own, as a noun** (ag1903 §288). The masculine names people, the feminine women, the neuter things: *bonī* "good men, good people", *bona* "good things". *Rōmānī* is "the Romans".
- **Words for "my", "your", "our"**: *meus*, *tuus*, *noster*, *vester* are adjectives and agree with the thing owned (ag1903 §286): *domina mea* "my mistress", whoever is speaking.
- **Fixed phrases with a set order**: *populus Rōmānus* "the Roman people" always has the adjective second (ag1903 §598).
- **Exclamations**: nominative noun and adjective with no verb, as in *Dominus meus et Deus meus!*
- **One adjective with nouns of different genders**: the rules are in part 5 (ag1903 §287).
- **Words formed from verbs that act as adjectives**, the participles, agree in the same way. Part 4.

## Watch out

**Do not make the adjective rhyme with the noun.** *\*Nauta bona* copies the *-a* of *nauta*, which is masculine. Copy the gender: *nauta bonus*. The same holds for *poēta clārus* "a famous poet".

**Do not put a predicate adjective in the accusative.** *\*Puella laetam est* treats "happy" as an object. After *sum* it is nominative: *Puella laeta est*.

**Do not forget the neuter.** *\*Bellum longus* takes the *-us* of the most familiar form. *Bellum* is neuter: *bellum longum*, plural *bella longa*.

**Do not leave the adjective singular with a plural noun.** *\*Puerī fessus sunt* breaks agreement in number: *Puerī fessī sunt*.

**Do not keep or drop the *e* by guesswork.** *\*Pulchera* and *\*pulcherum* keep an *e* the word drops. *\*Misra* drops one it keeps. Check the feminine in the dictionary: *pulchra*, *misera*.

**Do not make "my" agree with the owner.** English "his" and "her" follow the owner. Latin possessives follow the thing owned, like French *son* and *sa*. A man speaking of his mistress says *domina mea*, not *\*domina meus*.

**Do not look for one fixed place for adjectives.** Spanish and French put most adjectives after the noun, English before. Latin uses both, putting the more important word first (ag1903 §598). Lesson 8 of this part explains.

## Connections

Spanish and French adjectives agree in gender and number, an inheritance from this system: Spanish *bueno, buena, buenos, buenas* continue *bonus, bona, bonōs, bonās*. Arabic adjectives also agree with their noun in gender, number, case and definiteness. Japanese and Korean adjectives never change to match a noun.

## Exercises

1. Say "a small boy", "a small girl" and "a small town" in the nominative.
2. Say "the poet is famous".
3. Take apart *Servōs fessōs vocat* and translate it.
4. A woman says "I am happy". A man says the same. Write both.
5. Correct *\*Nauta laeta est* and explain the error.
6. Say "we are looking at the high walls".
7. Translate *Bona et mala habet vīta*.
8. Say "our island is safe".

## Worked answers

1. *Puer* is masculine, *puella* feminine, *oppidum* neuter: *puer parvus*, *puella parva*, *oppidum parvum*.
2. *Poēta* is masculine nominative singular, so the predicate adjective is too: *Poēta clārus est*.
3. *Serv-ōs*: slaves, accusative plural. *Fess-ōs*: tired, accusative plural masculine, agreeing with *servōs*. *Voca-t*: calls, third person singular. "He calls the tired slaves."
4. The adjective shows the speaker's gender: *Laeta sum* for the woman, *Laetus sum* for the man.
5. *Nauta* is masculine, so the adjective must be masculine: *Nauta laetus est*. The error was to copy the *-a*.
6. *Mūrus* is masculine; the object is plural: *mūrōs altōs*. "We look at" is *spectāmus*: *Mūrōs altōs spectāmus*.
7. *Bona* and *mala* are neuter plural adjectives with no noun, so they mean "good things" and "bad things" (ag1903 §288). They are the object; *vīta* is the subject. "Life has good things and bad."
8. *Īnsula* is feminine nominative singular, so both adjectives are too: *Īnsula nostra tūta est*.
