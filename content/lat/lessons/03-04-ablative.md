## The idea

The genitive ties a noun to another noun. The dative names the person concerned. The **ablative** gives the circumstances of the action: where it starts from, what it is done with, where and when it happens.

As lesson 1 of this part said, the ablative is three old cases in one (ag1903 §398):

- a **"from" case**: separation, source, cause;
- a **"with" case**: the tool, the manner, the company, a quality;
- an **"in" case**: the place where and the time when.

Allen and Greenough explain that these three drifted together in meaning and that sound change then gave them the same endings. Latin kept one set of forms. The cost is ambiguity: *silvā* by itself could mean "from the forest", "with the forest" or "in the forest".

Latin solves this in two ways. Often the verb and the noun make the meaning plain: *gladiō pugnat* can only be "he fights with a sword". Where they do not, Latin adds a **preposition**, a small word placed before a noun to say how it relates to the action. *Ē silvā* is "out of the forest", *cum sociīs* "with the allies", *in silvā* "in the forest". Each preposition is tied to a case: the ones in this lesson take the ablative.

The name "ablative" is Latin too: *ablātīvus*, from *ablātus* "taken away" (Wiktionary), after its "from" meaning.

## How it is built

The endings:

- first declension: *-ā* for one, *-īs* for more than one: *puellā*, *puellīs*;
- second declension: *-ō* for one, *-īs* for more than one: *servō*, *servīs*; neuters the same: *bellō*, *bellīs*;
- third declension singular: *-e* on the stem: *rēge*, *virtūte*, *lapide*. The plural comes in lesson 7 of this part.

The plural is the dative plural again (ag1903 §38). The first-declension singular differs from the nominative only by its long vowel: *puella* has a short final *a*, *puellā* a long one (ag1903 §604).

Why is the vowel long? The old ablative ended in *-d*. Early Latin writes *praidād* for later *praedā* "booty" and *Gnaivōd* for *Gnaeō* (ag1903 §§43, 49). The *-d* dropped, and the long vowel before it remained.

To say "the allies are fighting with spears":

1. The verb: *pugnō*, third person plural: *pugnant*.
2. The subject: *sociī*.
3. The spears are the tool. A tool takes the plain ablative, with no preposition (ag1903 §409). *Tēlum*, plural: *tēlīs*.
4. *Sociī tēlīs pugnant*.

To say "we are fighting with the allies":

1. The allies are not a tool but company. Company takes *cum* with the ablative (ag1903 §413).
2. *Cum sociīs pugnāmus*.

The difference matters. *\*Sociīs pugnāmus* would mean that we use the allies as weapons.

Two of the new prepositions change shape. *Ā* and *ab* mean "from, away from". *Ā* is not used before a vowel or *h*: *ā portā* but *ab oppidō* (Wiktionary). *Ē* and *ex* mean "out of". *Ē* is likewise not used before a vowel or *h*; elsewhere both forms occur (Wiktionary).

## Where it applies

**The "from" ablative**

- **Separation and lack**, after verbs of freeing, removing and lacking: *līberō*, *careō*; and adjectives such as *vacuus* "empty of" (ag1903 §§400-401). *Servōs vinculīs līberat*. With real movement away, a preposition is usual: *ab oppidō*, *ē silvā* (ag1903 §402).
- **Source and origin**, usually with *ā*, *ab*, *ē*, *ex* or *dē*: *ē silvā*, *dē mūrō* "down from the wall" (ag1903 §403). Material: *ex aurō*, "of gold".
- **Cause**: *gaudiō rīdet*; *labōrō* with the ablative is "suffer from" (ag1903 §404).
- **The agent**, "by", with *ā* or *ab* and a passive verb (ag1903 §405). Part 4.
- **Comparison**, "than" (ag1903 §406). Part 5.

**The "with" ablative**

- **Means and tool**, no preposition: *tēlīs pugnant* (ag1903 §409).
- **Filling and abounding**: *dōnīs complent*; *plēnus* takes the ablative as well as the genitive (ag1903 §409).
- **Manner**: *cum cūrā*, or *magnā cūrā*, or *magnā cum cūrā* (ag1903 §412). Some nouns are used alone as adverbs: *silentiō* "in silence", *iūre* "rightly", *iniūriā* "wrongly" (ag1903 §412).
- **Company**: *cum sociīs* (ag1903 §413).
- **Quality**, with an adjective: *vir magnā virtūte* (ag1903 §415). Prose prefers this to the genitive of quality (lesson 2 of this part).
- **Respect**, "in respect of": *nōn rē sed nōmine* (ag1903 §418).
- **Worthiness**: *dignus* "worthy of" takes the ablative: *dignus laude*, "worthy of praise".
- **Price** (ag1903 §416) and the ablative absolute (part 6).

**The "in" ablative**

- **Place where**, with *in*: *in silvā*, *in templō* (ag1903 §421). Lesson 13 of this part shows where *in* is left out.
- **Time when**: "in the first hour", "at night" (ag1903 §423). Lesson 13 of this part.

**The prepositions with the ablative** (ag1903 §220): *ā/ab* "from, away from; by"; *ē/ex* "out of, from"; *dē* "down from; about"; *cum* "with"; *sine* "without"; *prō* "in front of; on behalf of; instead of": *prō patriā pugnant*, "they fight for their country"; *in* "in, on" (the next lesson adds its second meaning). *Sub* "under" is in the next lesson.

## Watch out

**Do not use *cum* for a tool.** Spanish *con la espada* and French *avec l'épée* tempt *\*Cum gladiō pugnat*. A tool takes the plain ablative: *Gladiō pugnat*. *Cum* is for company and manner.

**Do not leave out *cum* with people.** *\*Sociīs pugnāmus* reads as "we fight using the allies". People you act alongside need *cum*: *Cum sociīs pugnāmus* (ag1903 §413).

**Do not leave out *cum* in manner without an adjective.** *\*Cūrā labōrat* is not the classical way to say "he works carefully" (ag1903 §412). Say *Cum cūrā labōrat*, or add an adjective: *Magnā cūrā labōrat*. The adverb-like nouns *silentiō*, *iūre*, *iniūriā* are the exceptions.

**Do not give *careō* an accusative.** English "lack money" has an object. *\*Pecūniam caret* is wrong. *Careō* takes the ablative: *Pecūniā caret*.

**Do not write *ā* or *ē* before a vowel.** *\*Ā oppidō*, *\*ē undā*. Use *ab oppidō*, *ex undā*.

**Do not mistake the ablative for the nominative.** *Puella* and *puellā* differ only in vowel length. In a text without macrons, the verb's number and the other nouns decide. *Puella in silvā labōrat*: the girl works in the forest.

**Do not read every *-īs* as a dative.** *Dōnīs complent* is "they fill with gifts", ablative; *Deīs dōna dant* is "they give the gods gifts", dative. The verb tells you which.

**Do not describe with an ablative of quality alone.** *\*Vir virtūte* is not "a man of courage" (ag1903 §415). Add an adjective: *vir magnā virtūte*.

**Do not use *dē* or *ex* for "of" after a noun.** *\*Fīlia dē rēge* is "the daughter about the king". "The king's daughter" is genitive: *fīlia rēgis* (lesson 2 of this part).

## Connections

Japanese で marks both the tool and the place of an action, 刀で戦う "fight with a sword", 森で寝る "sleep in the forest", two of the ablative's three jobs in one particle. Korean splits them: 로 for the tool, 에서 for the place. Spanish *con* and French *avec* cover both the tool and company, which Latin keeps apart.

## Exercises

1. Say "the boy is fighting with a stone".
2. Say "the boys are fighting with the girls" (on the same side).
3. Take apart *Nautae ē silvā magnō clāmōre properant* and translate it.
4. Translate *Templum praedā complent*.
5. Correct *\*Cum lapide pugnat* and *\*Sociīs labōrat* ("he works with his allies").
6. Say "the town is empty of men" with *vacuus* and *vir*.
7. Why does *ā* become *ab* in *ab oppidō*?
8. Translate *Prō patriā pugnāmus, nōn prō praedā*.

## Worked answers

1. The stone is a tool, so the plain ablative, with no preposition. *Lapis, lapidis* gives the stem *lapid-* and the ablative *lapide*. *Puer lapide pugnat*.
2. The girls are company: *cum* and the ablative plural *puellīs*. *Puerī cum puellīs pugnant*.
3. *Naut-ae*: sailors, nominative plural. *Ē silv-ā*: out of the forest. *Magn-ō clāmōr-e*: with a great shout, manner with an adjective. *Propera-nt*: they hurry. "The sailors hurry out of the forest with a great shout."
4. *Templ-um*: the temple, object. *Praed-ā*: with booty, ablative after a verb of filling. "They fill the temple with booty."
5. A stone used as a weapon is a tool: *Lapide pugnat*. Allies are company: *Cum sociīs labōrat*.
6. *Vacuus* takes the ablative. *Oppidum* is neuter, so the adjective is *vacuum*. *Vir* in the ablative plural is *virīs*. *Oppidum vacuum virīs est*.
7. *Ā* is not used before a vowel or *h*, and *oppidō* begins with a vowel.
8. "We fight for our country, not for plunder." *Prō* with the ablative means "on behalf of"; both nouns are first-declension ablatives in *-ā*.
