## The idea

The third declension is really two declensions that have grown together. One is the consonant stems of the last lesson: *rēx, rēg-is*. The other is the **i-stems**: nouns whose stem once ended in the vowel *i*, such as *cīvis* "citizen", stem *cīvi-*. Allen and Greenough describe the two as having merged so thoroughly that the Romans themselves were confused by it (ag1903 §73).

For most cases the merger is complete. *Cīvis* has the same accusative, genitive, dative and ablative singular endings as *rēx*: *cīvem*, *cīvis*, *cīvī*, *cīve*. The old *i* survives in only a few places. Those places are what this lesson teaches.

The borrowing went both ways. The consonant stems took their nominative plural *-ēs* from the i-stems. Allen and Greenough explain that the old plural ending *-es* contracted with the stem vowel *i* to give *-ēs*, and that Latin then spread this *-ēs* to the consonant stems (ag1903 §77). Meanwhile many i-stems gave up their *i*-forms in the singular. Allen and Greenough note the direction of travel: nouns tended to lose their *i*-forms, adjectives to gain them (ag1903 §73).

The result is a system of tendencies, not rules. Ancient authors differ, and so do modern editions (ag1903 §§71, 73). This lesson gives the regular pattern and says where you should expect variation.

## How it is built

The i shows in four places (ag1903 §74):

1. **Genitive plural *-ium*** instead of *-um*: *cīvium*, *urbium*, *montium*. This is the most reliable sign, found in almost every i-stem.
2. **Neuter plural *-ia*** in the nominative and accusative: *maria* "seas", *animālia* "animals".
3. **Ablative singular *-ī*** instead of *-e*: always in neuters in *-e*, *-al* and *-ar* (*marī*, *animālī*), and in some masculines and feminines (*ignī*, *nāvī*, *turrī*), usually beside *-e* (ag1903 §76).
4. **Accusative *-īs* and *-im***. The accusative plural is often *-īs* beside *-ēs*: *cīvīs* or *cīvēs* (ag1903 §77). A few nouns have accusative singular *-im*: *turrim*, *vim*; a handful always do (ag1903 §75).

Everything else is as in the consonant stems: singular *-em*, *-is*, *-ī*, *-e*; plural *-ēs*, *-ibus*.

To know whether a noun is an i-stem, use three rules of thumb (ag1903 §§65, 71):

1. **Same number of syllables in nominative and genitive**, with a nominative in *-is* or *-ēs*: *cī·vis, cī·vis*; *nā·vis, nā·vis*; *nū·bēs, nū·bis*. Allen and Greenough call these "parisyllabic".
2. **A one-syllable nominative in *-s* or *-x* whose stem ends in two consonants**: *urbs, urb-is*; *ars, art-is*; *mōns, mont-is*; *nox, noct-is*; *arx, arc-is*. Several longer nouns in *-ns* or *-rs* go the same way (ag1903 §71).
3. **Neuters in *-e*, *-al*, *-ar***: *mare*, *animal* (ag1903 §§65, 68).

Rules 1 and 2 decide the genitive plural; rule 3 also brings the ablative *-ī* and the plural *-ia*.

To decline *mōns, montis* m. "mountain":

1. The stem is *mont-*: one syllable in the nominative, two consonants at the end of the stem. Rule 2: an i-stem.
2. Singular as for consonant stems: *mōns*, *montem*, *montis*, *montī*, *monte*.
3. Plural with the i: *montēs*, accusative *montēs* or *montīs*, genitive *montium*, dative and ablative *montibus*.

## Where it applies

- **Parisyllabic nouns in *-is***: *cīvis*, *hostis*, *nāvis*, *ignis*, *fīnis*, *auris*, *avis*, *classis*, *turris*, *vestis* (ag1903 §65). Genitive plural *-ium*; accusative plural *-ēs* or *-īs*; a few with ablative *-ī* or accusative *-im*.
- **Nouns in *-ēs* with genitive *-is***: *nūbēs*, *caedēs* (ag1903 §71). Genitive plural *-ium*, with exceptions such as *sēdēs* and *vātēs* (ag1903 §78).
- **One-syllable nouns whose stem ends in two consonants**: *urbs*, *gēns*, *ars*, *pars*, *mōns*, *fōns*, *mēns*, *mors*, *nox*, *arx* (ag1903 §71). Singular like consonant stems; plural with *-ium*.
- **Longer nouns in *-ns* and *-rs***, such as *cohors* "cohort" (ag1903 §71). Nouns in *-tās* waver: *cīvitātum* and *cīvitātium* both occur (lesson 7 of this part).
- **Neuters in *-e*, *-al*, *-ar***: *mare*, *animal*. These are the full i-stems: ablative *-ī*, plural *-ia*, genitive plural *-ium* (ag1903 §68). The plural-only *moenia, moenium* "city walls" belongs here.
- **The irregular *vīs* "force"**: accusative *vim*, ablative *vī*; plural *vīrēs* "strength" with genitive *vīrium* (ag1903 §79). The plural has a different meaning from the singular.
- **Third-declension adjectives** keep more i-forms than nouns, notably the ablative *-ī* (ag1903 §73). Part 5.
- **Texts and editions.** You will meet *-īs* for *-ēs* in the accusative plural in some editions and not in others, for the same author. Both are classical (ag1903 §77). Treat *-īs* after an i-stem as an accusative plural.

## Watch out

**Do not give consonant stems *-ium*.** *\*Rēgium*, *\*mīlitium*, *\*patrium*: these nouns are consonant stems. *Rēgum*, *mīlitum*, *patrum*. *Pater, patris* has two syllables in both forms, but its nominative is not in *-is*, so rule 1 does not apply.

**Do not trust the look of *-is* alone.** *Canis* and *iuvenis* are parisyllabic but have *canum*, *iuvenum* (ag1903 §78). Allen and Greenough explain that both are really *n*-stems. Note these two.

**Do not give the neuter i-stems *-a* or *-e*.** *\*Mara*, *\*animāla* and *\*in mare* (as ablative) follow *corpus* and *rēx*. Neuters in *-e*, *-al*, *-ar* have *maria*, *animālia*, *in marī*. (Allen and Greenough note that *mare* sometimes has the ablative *mare*, ag1903 §76, but *marī* is the regular form.)

**Do not read *-īs* as a dative or ablative.** In *Mīlitēs montīs occupant*, *montīs* is an accusative plural of the third declension, not the *-īs* of *puellīs* and *servīs*. The noun's declension tells you which.

**Do not use *-im* and *-ī* everywhere.** *\*Cīvim*, *\*urbī* as ablative: most i-stems have *-em* and *-e* in the singular, like *rēx*. Only a few nouns have *-im*, and the ablative *-ī* is regular only in the neuters.

**Do not take *vīs* for the second-person verb.** *Vīs* "force" looks like a verb form you will meet in part 4. Here it is a noun: *Vīs* is nominative, *vim* accusative, *vī* ablative.

**Do not translate *vīrēs* as "forces" in the military sense.** *Vīrēs* is "strength", the physical power of a person or army. "Forces", meaning troops, is usually *cōpiae* (lesson 2 of this part).

## Connections

Spanish *monte* and *muerte* come from the accusatives *montem* and *mortem* (Wiktionary). The accusative singular was the one form where i-stems and consonant stems had already merged, so the Romance languages kept no trace of the difference.

## Exercises

1. Is *hostis, hostis* an i-stem? Give the reason and the genitive plural.
2. Is *rēx, rēgis* an i-stem? Give the reason and the genitive plural.
3. Decline *mare* in the plural.
4. Take apart *Classis hostium in marī est* and translate it.
5. Say "the citizens of the cities love peace".
6. Correct *\*Animāla in montibus habitant*.
7. Why is *Ignis aurum probat* not ambiguous, although *aurum* could be nominative?
8. Translate *Vīrēs mīlitum fīnem habent*.

## Worked answers

1. Yes. *Hostis* and *hostis* have the same number of syllables, and the nominative is in *-is* (rule 1). Genitive plural *hostium*.
2. No. *Rēx* has one syllable, *rēgis* two, so rule 1 does not apply; and the stem *rēg-* ends in a single consonant, so rule 2 does not either. Genitive plural *rēgum*.
3. *Maria*, *maria*, *maria*, *marium*, *maribus*, *maribus*. As a neuter in *-e*, *mare* takes *-ia* in the nominative and accusative plural.
4. *Class-is*: the fleet, nominative singular. *Host-ium*: of the enemy, genitive plural. *In mar-ī*: on the sea, ablative in *-ī*. *Est*: is. "The enemy's fleet is at sea."
5. *Cīvēs* is the subject. "Of the cities" is the genitive plural *urbium*, an i-stem by rule 2. Peace is the object: *pācem*. *Cīvēs urbium pācem amant*.
6. *Animal* is a neuter in *-al*, so its plural is *animālia*: *Animālia in montibus habitant*, "animals live in the mountains".
7. *Ignis* is nominative and singular, and *probat* is singular; *aurum* could be nominative, but then the sentence would have two subjects and no object. The neutral order also puts the subject first.
8. *Vīr-ēs*: strength, nominative plural of *vīs*. *Mīlit-um*: of the soldiers. *Fīn-em*: an end, accusative. *Habe-nt*: have. "The soldiers' strength has a limit."
