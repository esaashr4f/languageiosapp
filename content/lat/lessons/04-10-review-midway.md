## Before you start

This review covers the first half of part 4: principal parts and the three stems (lesson 1), the present of the third and fourth conjugations (lesson 2), the infinitive with *possum*, *volō*, *nōlō*, *mālō* and *dēbeō* (lesson 3), the imperfect (lesson 4), the future (lesson 5), the perfect (lesson 6), the choice between perfect and imperfect (lesson 7), the pluperfect and future perfect (lesson 8), and commands (lesson 9). It also uses the cases, pronouns and pointing words of part 3 and the sentence basics of part 2.

Work each exercise before reading its answer. Every answer names the lesson to reread if you went wrong.

## Passage

**A. The farmer and his sons.** A constructed passage, after the old fable. It uses only words and grammar taught so far.

1. Ōlim prope silvam senex cum fīliīs in vīllā habitābat.
2. Senex agrōs semper colēbat et pecus alēbat; fīliī autem labōrāre nōlēbant et saepe dormiēbant.
3. Quondam pater fīliōs vocāvit et dīxit: "Fīliī, aurum in agrīs cēlāvī. Agrōs bene colite; aurum inveniētis."
4. Mox senex vītam relīquit.
5. Fīliī diū in agrīs labōrāvērunt, sed aurum nōn invēnērunt.
6. Agrī tamen multum frūmentum dedērunt, et fīliī magnam pecūniam habuērunt.
7. Tum fīliī sēnsērunt: "Pater nōs labōrāre docuerat. Labor erat aurum."

**B. Catullus to himself (Catullus 8, lines 3 and 9 to 14, shortened).** Attested; wording from The Latin Library, in this guide's spelling. Catullus addresses himself, then the girl who has left him. New words, glossed here: *impotēns* "weak, without self-control" (a third-declension adjective, part 5); *obdūrō* "hold out, be hard" (first conjugation); *requīrō* "look for again"; *invītus* "unwilling".

1. *Fulsēre quondam candidī tibi sōlēs.*
2. *Nunc iam illa nōn vult: tū quoque, impotēns, nōlī,*
3. *nec miser vīve, ... obdūrā.*
4. *Valē, puella. Iam Catullus obdūrat,*
5. *nec tē requīret nec rogābit invītam.*
6. *At tū dolēbis.*

## Exercises

1. Translate sentences 1 and 2 of passage A.
2. Translate sentences 3 and 4.
3. Translate sentences 5, 6 and 7.
4. Translate passage B.
5. In sentence 2, why are *colēbat*, *alēbat*, *nōlēbant* and *dormiēbant* all imperfect? Explain the vowel before *-bā-* in each.
6. In sentences 3 and 4, why do *vocāvit*, *dīxit* and *relīquit* use the perfect? Give the principal parts that build them.
7. In sentence 3, explain *colite* and *inveniētis*: mood or tense, and how each is formed.
8. In sentence 7, why is *docuerat* pluperfect?
9. In passage B, line 1, what is *fulsēre*, and how do you know it is not an infinitive?
10. In passage B, lines 2 and 3, explain *vult*, *nōlī*, *vīve* and *obdūrā*.
11. In passage B, line 5, what tense are *requīret* and *rogābit*? Why do they look different?
12. Give the present, imperfect, future and perfect, third person plural, of *dūcō*, *capiō* and *audiō*.
13. Say "the soldiers were sleeping in the camp; suddenly the enemy attacked the gate".
14. Say "we shall be able to see the sea tomorrow".
15. Say "boys, listen to your teacher and don't laugh".
16. Say "when you have read the letter, you will understand", using *sentiō* for "understand".
17. Say "the king had ruled the city for many years".
18. Say "I would rather stay at home than go to Rome", with *maneō* and *properō* for "go".
19. Correct *\*Hostēs urbem capiēbunt* ("the enemy will take the city").
20. Correct *\*Rēx populum regēbit* ("the king will rule the people") and *\*Puerī librōs legint* ("the boys are reading books").
21. Correct *\*Nōn timē, Mārce!* ("don't be afraid, Marcus").
22. Correct *\*Possumus vidēmus montem* ("we can see the mountain").
23. Correct *\*Caesar ter pugnābat* ("Caesar fought three times") and *\*Mīlitēs fugēbant*, meant as a single event, "the soldiers fled".
24. Correct *\*Cum veniēs, gaudēbō* ("when you come, I shall be glad").
25. Without macrons, *venit* can be present or perfect. Give both readings and say what decides.

## Worked answers

1. "Once upon a time an old man lived with his sons in a farmhouse near a forest. The old man always farmed the fields and raised cattle; but the sons refused to work and often slept." The imperfects set the scene and describe habits (lessons 4 and 7). *Prope* takes the accusative, *cum* the ablative (part 3, lesson 5). *Labōrāre nōlēbant*: infinitive with *nōlō* (lesson 3).
2. "One day the father called his sons and said: 'Sons, I have hidden gold in the fields. Farm the fields well; you will find gold.' Soon the old man died." *Vocāvit* and *dīxit* are events (lessons 6 and 7). *Cēlāvī* is a perfect with the "have done" sense: the gold lies hidden now (lesson 7). *Colite* is a plural command (lesson 9); *inveniētis* a future (lesson 5). *Vītam relīquit*, "left life", is a way of saying "died".
3. "The sons worked in the fields for a long time, but they did not find the gold. The fields, however, produced a great deal of grain, and the sons had plenty of money. Then the sons realised: 'Our father had taught us to work. Work was the gold.'" *Labōrāvērunt diū*: a long stretch summed up, perfect (lesson 7). *Dedērunt*: perfect of *dō, dedī* (lesson 6). *Docuerat*: pluperfect (lesson 8). *Erat*: imperfect of *sum*, a lasting state (lesson 4).
4. "Once the days shone bright for you. Now she does not want it any more: you too, weak as you are, must refuse, and do not live in misery, ... hold out. Goodbye, girl. Catullus is holding out now, and he will not come looking for you or ask you against your will. But you will be sorry." *Valē* is a farewell command (lesson 9). *Dolēbis*: second-conjugation future (lesson 5).
5. They describe the situation and the habits, not events (lessons 4 and 7). *Colēbat* and *alēbat*: third conjugation, short *e* lengthened to *ē* before *-bā-*. *Nōlēbant*: *nōlō* is regular in the imperfect. *Dormiēbant*: fourth conjugation, *iē* (lesson 4).
6. Each is a single event that moves the story on (lesson 7). *Vocō, vocāvī*; *dīcō, dīxī* (an *s*-perfect, *c* + *s* written *x*); *relinquō, relīquī* (the nasal lost, the vowel lengthened). The perfect stem comes from the third principal part (lessons 1 and 6).
7. *Colite* is the present imperative plural of *colō, colere*: the stem *cole-*, with *e* becoming *i* before *-te* (lesson 9). *Inveniētis* is the future of *inveniō*, fourth conjugation: *inveni-* + *ē* + *-tis* (lesson 5).
8. The teaching was complete before the moment when the sons realised it, a past moment. An act complete before another past act is pluperfect: *docu-era-t* (lesson 8).
9. *Fulsēre* is the perfect, third person plural, of *fulgeō*, in its *-ēre* form (= *fulsērunt*). It has the perfect stem *fuls-*; the infinitive would be *fulgēre*, on the present stem (lesson 6).
10. *Vult*: "she wants", third person singular of *volō* (lesson 3). *Nōlī*: the imperative of *nōlō*, here used on its own, "refuse!", "don't want it either!" (lesson 9). *Vīve*: imperative of *vīvō*, third conjugation, the bare stem with short *e*; with *nec* it amounts to "and do not live". A negative with the imperative is poetic; prose would say *nōlī vīvere* (lesson 9). *Obdūrā*: imperative of the first-conjugation *obdūrō*, the bare stem *obdūrā* (lesson 9).
11. Both are future (lesson 5). *Requīrō* is third conjugation, so it takes the *ē* future: *requīr-e-t*. *Rogō* is first conjugation, so it takes the *b* future: *rogā-bi-t*.
12. *Dūcunt, dūcēbant, dūcent, dūxērunt*. *Capiunt, capiēbant, capient, cēpērunt*. *Audiunt, audiēbant, audient, audīvērunt* (lessons 2, 4, 5 and 6).
13. *Mīlitēs in castrīs dormiēbant; subitō hostēs portam petīvērunt.* Background imperfect, event perfect (lesson 7). *In castrīs*: place where, ablative (part 3, lesson 5).
14. *Crās mare vidēre poterimus.* *Poterimus*: future of *possum*, *pot-* + *erimus* (lessons 3 and 5).
15. *Puerī, magistrum audīte et nōlīte rīdēre.* Plural command *audīte*; plural prohibition *nōlīte* + infinitive (lesson 9). *Audiō* takes the person heard in the accusative.
16. *Cum epistulam lēgeris, sentiēs.* The reading will be complete first, so future perfect, *lēg-eri-s*; then the future, *senti-ē-s* (lesson 8). The "when" clause is taught fully in part 6.
17. *Rēx urbem multōs annōs rēxerat.* Pluperfect of *regō, rēxī* (lesson 8). *Multōs annōs*: duration, accusative (part 3, lesson 13).
18. *Domī manēre mālō quam Rōmam properāre.* *Mālō* with two infinitives and *quam* (lesson 3). *Domī*: locative; *Rōmam*: goal, no preposition (part 3, lesson 13).
19. *Hostēs urbem capient.* *Capiō* is third conjugation, so its future has *ē*, not *b* (lesson 5).
20. *Rēx populum reget*: the third-conjugation future has no *b* (lesson 5). *Puerī librōs legunt*: the third-conjugation present third person plural ends in *-unt* (lesson 2).
21. *Nōlī timēre, Mārce!* Classical prose forbids with *nōlī* and the infinitive (lesson 9).
22. *Montem vidēre possumus.* Only the main verb carries a person ending; the second verb is an infinitive (lesson 3).
23. *Caesar ter pugnāvit*: a counted repetition is summed up in the perfect (lesson 7). *Mīlitēs fūgērunt*: a single event, perfect; the perfect stem is *fūg-*, from *fūgī* (lessons 6 and 7).
24. *Cum vēneris, gaudēbō.* The coming will be complete before the gladness, so Latin uses the future perfect where English has a present (lesson 8).
25. *Venit* (short *e*) is the present, "he comes"; *vēnit* (long *ē*) is the perfect, "he came" (lessons 1 and 6). In a text without macrons, the context decides: the other verbs around it, time words like *nunc* or *ōlim*, and whether the sentence is part of a story.
