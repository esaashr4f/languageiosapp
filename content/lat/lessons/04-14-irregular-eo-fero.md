## The idea

Two of the commonest verbs in Latin do not fit any conjugation: *eō* "go" and *ferō* "carry, bear". Both are irregular for the same deep reason as *sum*: they belong, in part, to the old verbs that joined their endings straight to the root, without a linking vowel (lesson 2 of this part). Allen and Greenough list *ferō* and *eō* among the verbs that "add some of the personal endings of the present system directly to the root" (ag1903 §197), and they give *fer-t* "he carries" as an example of such a form beside *es-t* "he is" (ag1903 §174).

The irregularity is limited. Each verb has a handful of odd forms and is otherwise regular. Learn the odd ones, and the rest follows a pattern you know.

**Ferō** is a regular third-conjugation verb in most of its forms: *ferimus*, *ferunt*, *ferēbam*, *feram*. Only the forms where the linking vowel would come before *s*, *t* or *r* lose it: *fers* (not *\*feris*), *fert* (not *\*ferit*), *fertis*, the infinitive *ferre* (not *\*ferere*), and the passive *ferris*, *fertur*. Its other two stems come from a different verb altogether. Wiktionary explains the perfect *tulī* as originally the perfect of *tollō* "lift", and *lātum* as from the same root, reduced from an older *\*tlātos*. A paradigm made from two or more roots like this is called **suppletive**: one verb fills its gaps with forms of another. English "go, went" is suppletive in the same way.

**Eō** has a root that shows up in two shapes: *e-* before a vowel (*e-ō*, *e-unt*) and *i-* or *ī-* before a consonant (*ī-s*, *i-t*, *ī-mus*, *ī-re*). Wiktionary traces *eō* to an older *\*ejō*, from the Indo-European for "goes". Why the vowel alternates in exactly this way is not explained here. Its imperfect and future use *-bā-* and *-bō* (*ībam*, *ībō*), like the first and second conjugations, which the fourth conjugation once did too (ag1903 §183).

Both verbs make many compounds with prepositions, and each compound is conjugated like its simple verb (ag1903 §200). Lesson 16 of this part explains how prefixes work in general.

## How it is built

**Ferō, ferre, tulī, lātum.**

1. Present: *ferō, fers, fert, ferimus, fertis, ferunt*. Three short forms (*fers*, *fert*, *fertis*); the others are third-conjugation.
2. Infinitive *ferre*; imperative *fer*, *ferte* (*fer* is one of the four short imperatives, lesson 9).
3. Imperfect *ferēbam*; future *feram, ferēs, feret*: regular third-conjugation.
4. Perfect system on *tul-*: *tulī, tulistī, tulit*; *tuleram*; *tulerō*.
5. Passive present: *feror, ferris, fertur, ferimur, feriminī, feruntur*; infinitive *ferrī*. Perfect passive: *lātus est*.

**Eō, īre, iī (īvī), itum.**

1. Present: *eō, īs, it, īmus, ītis, eunt*.
2. Infinitive *īre*; imperative *ī* "go!", *īte*.
3. Imperfect *ībam, ībās, ībat*; future *ībō, ībis, ībit, ībimus, ībitis, ībunt*.
4. Perfect: *iī, īstī, iit, iimus, īstis, iērunt*. The forms with *v* (*īvī*) are "very rare in the simple verb" (ag1903 §203b), and *ii* before *s* contracts to *ī*: *īstī*, *īsse* (ag1903 §203c).
5. *Eō* has no personal passive, since "go" takes no object. The impersonal *ītur* "there is going", *itum est* "people went" occurs (ag1903 §203a; part 7).

**Compounds.** Add a prefix, then conjugate as the simple verb.

- *ab-eō* "go away", *ad-eō* "go to, approach", *ex-eō* "go out", *red-eō* "go back", *per-eō* "perish", *trāns-eō* "go across", *sub-eō* "go under; undergo", *in-eō* "go into; begin".
- With *ferō*, the prefix often changes its last consonant to fit the next sound, and the perfect and supine change with it (ag1903 §200): *af-ferō, at-tulī, al-lātum* "bring to"; *au-ferō, abs-tulī, ab-lātum* "take away"; *cōn-ferō, con-tulī, col-lātum* "bring together, compare"; *dif-ferō, dis-tulī, dī-lātum* "put off; differ"; *īn-ferō, in-tulī, il-lātum* "bring in"; *of-ferō, ob-tulī, ob-lātum* "offer"; *re-ferō, ret-tulī, re-lātum* "bring back; report".
- *Tollō, tollere, sustulī, sublātum* "raise, remove" borrows *sus-tulī* and *sub-lātum*, the forms of *sufferō* (ag1903 §200, footnote).

Transitive compounds of *eō*, like *adeō* "approach" and *trānseō* "cross", have a passive in the third person: *flūmen trānsītur*, "the river is crossed" (ag1903 §203a).

Say "the soldiers are going back to the camp". *Redeō*: *red-* + *eō*. Third person plural, so *e-* before the vowel: *redeunt*. *Mīlitēs in castra redeunt.*

Say "we brought help to our allies". *Afferō*: perfect *attulī*. First person plural: *attulimus*. "To our allies" is dative. *Sociīs auxilium attulimus.*

## Where it applies

- **Going somewhere:** *eō* with *ad* or *in* + accusative, or a bare town name: *Rōmam īmus*. The compounds add direction: *abeō*, *adeō*, *exeō*, *redeō*, *trānseō*.
- **"Perish":** *pereō*, literally "go through, go to ruin".
- **Undertaking:** *ineō* "begin" (*cōnsilium inīre* "form a plan"), *subeō* "undergo, face" (*perīculum subīre*).
- **Carrying and bringing:** *ferō* and its compounds: *afferō* "bring to", *auferō* "take away", *dēferō* "carry down, report", *īnferō* "bring in" (*bellum īnferre* "make war"), *offerō* "offer", *referō* "bring back, report".
- **Bearing and enduring:** *ferō* also means "put up with": *ferēmus aequō animō*.
- **Saying:** *ferunt* "they say", "people say", and *fertur* "he is said": common in narrative.
- **Comparing and differing:** *cōnferō* "bring together, compare"; *differō* "postpone" and, intransitively, "differ".
- **Raising and removing:** *tollō*, *sustulī*, *sublātum* "lift", "do away with".
- **Impersonal passives of eō:** *ītur*, *itum est*, "people go, went" (ag1903 §203a; part 7).

## Watch out

**Do not give *ferō* a linking vowel where it has none.** *\*Feris*, *\*ferit*, *\*feritis* and *\*ferere* are wrong. The forms are *fers*, *fert*, *fertis*, *ferre*. But *ferimus* and *ferunt* do have the vowel.

**Do not build the perfect of *ferō* on *fer-*.** *\*Ferāvī*, *\*ferī*, *\*ferātus* do not exist. The perfect is *tulī*, the participle *lātus*.

**Do not use *e-* before a consonant or *ī-* before a vowel.** *\*Ēs*, *\*et*, *\*īunt* and *\*iō* are all wrong. Before a vowel: *eō*, *eunt*, *eam*. Before a consonant: *īs*, *it*, *īmus*, *īre*.

**Do not give *eō* an *ē* future.** It is not fourth conjugation: the future is *ībō*, *ībit*, *ībunt*, not *\*eam*, *\*iet*. (*Eam* exists, but as the subjunctive, lesson 17.)

**Do not lengthen the *i* of *it*.** *It* "he goes" has a short *i*, before final *t* (part 2, lesson 1); *īs* and *īmus* have a long *ī*.

**Do not read *iit* as a mistake.** *Iit* "he went" and *iērunt* "they went" are the normal perfects; *īvit* is rare (ag1903 §203b). The compounds have *abiit*, *rediit*, *periit*.

**Do not confuse *adeō* the verb with *adeō* the adverb.** *Adeō* "I approach" is a verb; *adeō* "to such a degree" is an adverb (part 5). Context and position decide.

**Do not try to make *eō* passive with a person subject.** *\*Ego īror* is not a form. Only the impersonal *ītur* exists, and the transitive compounds' third persons (*trānsītur*).

**Do not mistake *lātus* "carried" for *lātus* "wide" or *latus* "side".** Three different words: the participle of *ferō*, the adjective "broad" (part 5), and the noun *latus, lateris* "side".

## Connections

English *transfer*, *refer*, *offer*, *differ*, *confer* are compounds of *ferō*; *relation*, *translation* and *collation* come from its other stem, *lātum*. English *exit* is Latin "he goes out", and *transit*, *initial* and *perish* (through French) come from *eō*. The suppletion of *ferō, tulī, lātum* is like English "go, went", where *went* was borrowed from another verb, *wend*.

## Exercises

1. Conjugate *exeō* in the present and *referō* in the present.
2. Translate *Cōnsul ad senātum adiit et rem rettulit.*
3. Say "the enemy will take away the booty".
4. Say "we were going to Athens".
5. Take apart *abstulērunt*, *ībit* and *fertis*.
6. Correct *\*Mīlitēs in urbem īunt* and *\*Servus vīnum ferit*.
7. Translate *Rēx in proeliō periit*, and say how *periit* is formed.
8. Why are *tulī* and *lātum* so unlike *ferō*? Give Wiktionary's explanation.
9. Say "go away, Marcus, and don't come back", using *abeō* and *redeō*.

## Worked answers

1. *Exeō, exīs, exit, exīmus, exītis, exeunt*: *e-* before a vowel, *ī-* or *i-* before a consonant. *Referō, refers, refert, referimus, refertis, referunt*: like *ferō*.
2. "The consul went to the senate and reported the matter." *Adiit*: perfect of *adeō*, here with *ad*. *Rettulit*: perfect of *referō*, with the double *t* (ag1903 §200).
3. *Hostēs praedam auferent.* Future of *auferō*, like *feram*: *aufer-e-nt*.
4. *Athēnās ībāmus.* *Ībāmus*: imperfect, with the *-bā-* marker. *Athēnās*: goal, a town name without preposition (part 3, lesson 13).
5. *Abs-tul-ērunt*: perfect of *auferō*, "they took away". *Ī-bi-t*: future of *eō*, "he will go". *Fer-tis*: present of *ferō*, "you (plural) carry", no linking vowel.
6. *Mīlitēs in urbem eunt*: before the vowel of *-unt* the root is *e-*. *Servus vīnum fert*: *ferō* has no linking vowel before *-t*.
7. "The king died in the battle." *Peri-it*: the perfect stem *peri-* of *pereō* (from *periī*) with the ending *-it*; the form without *v* is the normal one (ag1903 §203b).
8. Wiktionary explains that *ferō* is suppletive: its present comes from the Indo-European root for "carry", but *tulī* was originally the perfect of *tollō*, and *lātum* comes from the same root as *tulī*, from an older *\*tlātos*.
9. *Abī, Mārce, et nōlī redīre.* *Abī*: imperative of *abeō*, like *ī*. *Nōlī redīre*: prohibition with the infinitive (lesson 9).
