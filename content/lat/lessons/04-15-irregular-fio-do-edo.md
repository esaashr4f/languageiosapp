## The idea

This lesson collects the remaining odd verbs. None is hard once you see what is odd about it, and each oddity has a reason.

**Fīō** "become, happen, be made". *Faciō* "make" has no passive of its own in the present system. Its place is taken by a different verb, *fīō*: *fit* "it is made, it happens, it becomes"; *fīunt* "they are made". In the perfect system the normal passive of *faciō* returns: *factum est* "it was made", "it happened" (ag1903 §204). So *fīō* and *factus sum* are one suppletive verb, like *ferō, tulī* (lesson 14). Wiktionary derives *fīō* from the Indo-European root *\*bʰuH-* "become", the root of *fuī*, *-bam* and *-bō*: the same root you have met three times already in this part.

**Dō** "give" is first conjugation, but its *a* is short in almost every form: *damus*, *datis*, *dabam*, *dare*, *datum*. Only *dās* and the imperative *dā* have a long *ā*. Wiktionary explains that *dō* comes from a reduplicated Indo-European present, *\*dédeh₃ti*, "gives": the reduplication was lost in the Latin present but survives in the perfect *dedī*. The short *a* reflects the old root, not the long *ā* of *amāre*.

**Edō** "eat" is regular third conjugation, but it also has short forms made straight from the root, without the linking vowel (ag1903 §201): *ēs* "you eat", *ēst* "he eats", *ēstis*, *ēsse* "to eat", beside regular *edis*, *edit*, *editis*, *edere*. They are the same kind of athematic forms as *est* "is" and *fert*.

**Defective verbs.** A **defective** verb is one that lacks some of its forms. Some Latin verbs have only a perfect system, used with a present meaning (ag1903 §205):

- *ōdī* "I hate", *ōderam* "I hated", *ōderō* "I shall hate";
- *meminī* "I remember", *memineram*, *meminerō*; imperative *mementō* "remember!";
- *coepī* "I began"; for the present, Latin uses *incipiō*.

Why would a perfect mean "I remember"? Because an old perfect described a state reached, not an event (lesson 6). Wiktionary traces *meminī* to an Indo-European perfect of the root *\*men-* "think", the root of *mēns* "mind": "I have it in mind", so "I remember". *Nōvī* "I know" works the same way.

Other verbs survive in only a few forms (ag1903 §206): *inquam* "I say", used only beside a quotation (*inquit* "he says" or "he said"); *aiō* "I say, I say yes" (*ait*, *aiunt* "they say"); *quaesō* "I beg", "please"; *queō* "I can" and *nequeō* "I cannot", conjugated like *eō*.

## How it is built

**Fīō, fierī, factus sum.**

1. Present: *fīō, fīs, fit, fīmus, fītis, fīunt*. The *ī* is long except before final *-t* (*fit*) and before *-er-* (*fierī*, *fierem*).
2. Imperfect *fīēbam*; future *fīam, fīēs, fīet*.
3. Infinitive *fierī* "to become, to be made".
4. Perfect system: *factus sum*, *factus eram*, *factus erō*, agreeing with the subject (lesson 12).

To say "is made" or "happens", use *fit*. To say "was made" or "happened" as an event, use *factum est*. With an adjective or noun after it, *fīō* means "become": *laetus fit* "he becomes happy". The adjective is nominative, as after *sum*.

**Dō, dare, dedī, datum.** Conjugate like *amō* with short *a*: *dō, dās, dat, damus, datis, dant*; *dabam*; *dabō*; *dedī*; passive *datur*, *datus est*.

**Compounds of dō.** A few are first conjugation like *dō*. Most are third conjugation, with the vowel weakened: *reddō, reddere, reddidī, redditum* "give back"; *trādō* "hand over"; *addō* "add"; *condō* "found, store, hide"; *prōdō* "betray, hand down"; *perdō* "destroy, lose"; and *crēdō*, which you know. Allen and Greenough assign these third-conjugation compounds to a different root, meaning "put" (ag1903 §209, note). *Perdō* has no classical passive (Wiktionary); *pereō* "perish" (lesson 14) covers the meaning.

**Edō, edere (ēsse), ēdī, ēsum.** Use the regular forms, and recognise the short ones: *ēst* "he eats" has a long *ē*, *est* "he is" a short one.

**Defective verbs.** Conjugate *ōdī*, *meminī* and *coepī* as perfects (lesson 6), and translate *ōdī* and *meminī* as presents. *Meminī* takes an accusative or genitive of what is remembered, or an infinitive: *mementō servāre* "remember to keep".

**Inquit.** Put it inside the quotation, after the first word or phrase: *"Venīte," inquit, "mīlitēs."* It never stands before the quotation in prose.

## Where it applies

- ***Fit* "it happens"** and ***factum est* "it happened"**: very common in narrative.
- ***Fīō* "become"** with a predicate noun or adjective: *cōnsul fit* "he becomes consul".
- ***Fīō* as the passive of *faciō*** in the present system, with or without an agent (ag1903 §204). The compounds of *faciō* in *-ficiō* have ordinary passives instead: *cōnficitur* "it is finished" (ag1903 §204a; lesson 16).
- ***Dō*** and its first-conjugation compounds, with short *a*; the third-conjugation compounds *reddō*, *trādō*, *addō*, *condō*, *prōdō*, *perdō*, *crēdō*.
- ***Edō*** "eat", in regular and short forms.
- ***Ōdī*, *meminī*, *coepī***: perfect forms only; the first two with present meaning (ag1903 §205b). *Coepī* with a passive infinitive takes a passive form: *coeptus est vocārī* "he began to be called" (ag1903 §205a).
- ***Inquam*, *inquit*, *inquiunt***: only with direct quotation (ag1903 §206b).
- ***Aiō*, *ait*, *aiunt***: "say", "say yes"; *ut aiunt* "as they say".
- ***Quaesō***: "please", added to a request: *dīc, quaesō* "tell me, please".
- ***Queō*, *nequeō***: "can", "cannot", mostly in the present, *queō* usually with a negative (ag1903 §206d). *Possum* is the ordinary verb.

## Watch out

**Do not make a passive of *faciō* in the present system.** *\*Facitur* and *\*faciuntur* are not classical. Use *fit*, *fīunt*. But the perfect passive is *factus est*, not *\*fitus est*.

**Do not lengthen the *a* of *dō*.** *\*Dāmus*, *\*dāre*, *\*dābam* follow *amāmus*, but *dō* has short *a*: *damus*, *dare*, *dabam*. Only *dās* and *dā* are long.

**Do not conjugate *reddō*, *trādō* like *dō*.** *\*Reddat* as a present indicative or *\*trādāmus* are wrong: these compounds are third conjugation, *reddit*, *trādimus*. Their perfects keep the reduplication: *reddidī*, *trādidī*.

**Do not confuse *ēst* and *est*, *ēsse* and *esse*.** "He eats" and "to eat" have a long *ē*; "he is" and "to be" a short *e*. In texts without macrons only the sense decides.

**Do not look for a present of *ōdī* or *meminī*.** *\*Ōdō* and *\*meminō* do not exist. "I hate" is *ōdī*, "I remember" *meminī*; "I hated" is *ōderam*.

**Do not use *coepī* for the present.** *\*Coepiō* is early and rare (ag1903 §205a, note). "I begin" is *incipiō*; "I began" can be *coepī* or *incēpī*.

**Do not put *inquit* before the quotation.** *\*Inquit: "Venīte"* follows English "he said: 'come'". In Latin, *inquit* comes after the first word or words: *"Venīte," inquit.* For a speech introduced in advance, use *dīcit* or *dīxit*.

**Do not read *fierī* as *ferre*.** *Fierī* "to become, to be made" (from *fīō*) and *ferre* "to carry" (from *ferō*) look alike without care.

## Connections

Spanish *dar* and French *donner* both mean "give", but only *dar* continues *dare*; *donner* comes from *dōnāre* (lesson 1 of this part; Wiktionary). English *tradition* and *treason* both come from *trādō* "hand over": the first is what is handed down, the second what is handed to the enemy. English *memento* is the imperative of *meminī*.

## Exercises

1. Conjugate *fīō* in the present and the future.
2. Translate *Hoc saepe fit.*
3. Say "the gate was made by the slaves" (event) and "the gate is being made by the slaves".
4. Give the present of *trādō*, all six persons.
5. Translate *Rōmulus Rōmam condidit, et rēx factus est.*
6. Correct *\*Domus ā servīs facitur* and *\*Dāmus tibi dōnum*.
7. Translate *Ōderam eum, nunc amō*, and explain *ōderam*.
8. Why does *meminī*, a perfect, mean "I remember"? Use Wiktionary's etymology.
9. Say "'Come here, boys,' says the teacher", placing *inquit* correctly.

## Worked answers

1. Present *fīō, fīs, fit, fīmus, fītis, fīunt*; future *fīam, fīēs, fīet, fīēmus, fīētis, fīent*. The *ī* is short in *fit*, before final *-t*.
2. "This often happens." *Fit*: third person singular of *fīō*, "it happens" (ag1903 §204).
3. *Porta ā servīs facta est*: perfect passive of *faciō*, *facta* agreeing with *porta*. *Porta ā servīs fit*: *fīō* supplies the present passive.
4. *Trādō, trādis, trādit, trādimus, trāditis, trādunt*: third conjugation (ag1903 §209, note).
5. "Romulus founded Rome and became king." *Condidit*: perfect of *condō*, with reduplication. *Factus est*: perfect of *fīō*, "became"; *rēx* is a predicate noun in the nominative.
6. *Domus ā servīs fit*: *faciō* has no present passive; *fīō* takes its place. *Damus tibi dōnum*: *dō* has short *a* except in *dās* and *dā*.
7. "I used to hate him; now I love him." *Ōderam* is the pluperfect form of *ōdī*, used with the meaning of an imperfect (ag1903 §205b).
8. *Meminī* continues an Indo-European perfect of the root *\*men-* "think", the root of *mēns* (Wiktionary). An old perfect described a state reached: "I have it in mind", which is "I remember".
9. *"Venīte hūc, puerī," inquit magister.* *Inquit* comes after the first words of the quotation, never before it (ag1903 §206b). *Hūc* "to here" is the adverb of motion towards (part 3, lesson 12).
