## Before you start

This review covers all of part 4: stems and principal parts (lesson 1); the present of the third and fourth conjugations (lesson 2); the infinitive with *possum*, *volō*, *nōlō*, *mālō* and *dēbeō* (lesson 3); the imperfect, future and perfect (lessons 4 to 6) and the choice between perfect and imperfect (lesson 7); the pluperfect and future perfect (lesson 8); commands (lesson 9); the passive (lesson 11) and the perfect passive (lesson 12); deponents (lesson 13); the irregular verbs *eō*, *ferō*, *fīō*, *dō*, *edō* and the defective verbs (lessons 14 and 15); compounds (lesson 16); and the subjunctive, its forms and its uses in main clauses (lessons 17 to 19). The midway review (lesson 10) covered the first nine lessons; this one weights the second half. It also uses the cases and pronouns of part 3.

Work each exercise before reading its answer. Every answer names the lesson to reread if you went wrong.

## Passage

**A. A letter from the camp.** A constructed passage, using only words and grammar taught so far. Marcus writes to his brother Quintus.

1. Mārcus Quīntō frātrī salūtem dīcit.
2. Ad castra nūper vēnimus; iter longum et dūrum fuit.
3. Hostēs nōs ex silvīs secūtī sunt, sed ā nostrīs fugātī sunt.
4. Multī mīlitēs vulnerātī sunt; nēmō tamen periit.
5. Crās in fīnēs hostium proficīscēmur; dux nōs hortātus est, et cūnctī pugnāre volumus.
6. Utinam tū nōbīscum essēs! Patrem et mātrem meō nōmine salūtā.
7. Nōlī timēre: mox Rōmam redībō. Valē.

**B. The standard-bearer of the Tenth Legion (Caesar, Gallic War 4.25.3-5, shortened).** Attested; wording from The Latin Library, in this guide's spelling. Caesar's ships have reached Britain, but his soldiers hesitate to jump into the deep water under enemy fire. You have met two pieces of this passage in lessons 8 and 15. New words and constructions, glossed here: *nisi* "unless" (part 6); *aquila* "eagle", the legion's standard; *hoc cum ... dīxisset* "when he had said this", a *cum* clause with the pluperfect subjunctive (part 6); *prōiciō* "throw forward" (*prō* + *iaciō*); *cohortātī inter sē* "encouraging one another" (*cohortor*, a compound of *hortor*); *ūniversī* "all together".

1. *"Dēsilīte," inquit, "mīlitēs, nisi vultis aquilam hostibus prōdere; ego certē meum reī pūblicae atque imperātōrī officium praestiterō."*
2. *Hoc cum vōce magnā dīxisset, sē ex nāvī prōiēcit atque in hostēs aquilam ferre coepit.*
3. *Tum nostrī, cohortātī inter sē, ... ūniversī ex nāvī dēsiluērunt.*

## Exercises

1. Translate sentences 1 to 4 of passage A.
2. Translate sentences 5 to 7.
3. Translate passage B.
4. In passage A, sentences 3 and 4, find every perfect passive and every deponent, and say how you can tell them apart.
5. In sentence 5, explain *proficīscēmur*, *hortātus est* and *volumus*.
6. In sentence 6, why is *essēs* imperfect subjunctive, and what would *sīs* have meant?
7. In sentence 7, explain *nōlī timēre* and *redībō*.
8. In passage B, line 1, explain *dēsilīte*, *inquit*, *vultis*, *prōdere* and *praestiterō*.
9. In passage B, line 2, explain *prōiēcit*, *ferre* and *coepit*.
10. In passage B, line 3, *cohortātī* is a perfect participle. Why can it mean "having encouraged", when *vocātī* means "having been called"?
11. Give the principal parts of *prōdō*, *afferō*, *sequor* and *fīō*, and name the type of each.
12. Make *Hostēs urbem dēlent* passive in the present, the imperfect and the perfect.
13. Say "the city will be taken by the Romans".
14. Say "the letter had been written by my father".
15. Say "let us follow the general and not be afraid".
16. Say "if only the soldiers had stayed in the camp!".
17. Say "what am I to do?" and "you would have thought them happy".
18. Say "don't do this, Marcus" in two classical ways.
19. Say "we were going to Rome; the slaves were carrying our things".
20. Say "this often happens; it happened then too", using *fīō* and *factum est*.
21. Correct *\*Dux mīlitēs hortāvit* ("the general encouraged the soldiers").
22. Correct *\*Urbs ab hostibus capiēbit* ("the city will be taken by the enemy").
23. Correct *\*Gladium ūtuntur* ("they use swords") and *\*Ego parcor* ("I am spared").
24. Correct *\*Utinam adsīs*, meant as "if only you were here (but you are not)", and *\*Nōn veniat*, meant as "let him not come".
25. Take apart *interfēcerint*, *ferrētur* and *abiissent*, and give every possible meaning.

## Worked answers

1. "Marcus greets his brother Quintus. We recently came to the camp; the journey was long and hard. The enemy followed us out of the woods, but were put to flight by our men. Many soldiers were wounded; but no one died." *Salūtem dīcit*: the letter-opening formula, "says greeting". *Fuit*: the journey summed up as a finished fact (lesson 7). *Secūtī sunt*: deponent perfect, active in meaning (lesson 13). *Fugātī sunt* and *vulnerātī sunt*: perfect passives, with *ā nostrīs* as agent (lessons 11 and 12). *Periit*: perfect of *pereō* (lesson 14).
2. "Tomorrow we shall set out into the enemy's territory; the general has encouraged us, and we all want to fight. If only you were here with us! Greet our father and mother in my name. Don't be afraid: I shall soon return to Rome. Goodbye." *Cūnctī*: "all of us", agreeing with the subject of *volumus*. *Salūtā*: singular imperative (lesson 9). *Valē*: farewell (lesson 9).
3. "'Jump down, soldiers,' he said, 'unless you want to betray the eagle to the enemy; I at any rate shall have done my duty to the republic and to my general.' When he had said this in a loud voice, he threw himself forward from the ship and began to carry the eagle against the enemy. Then our men, encouraging one another, ... jumped down from the ship all together."
4. Perfect passives: *fugātī sunt*, *vulnerātī sunt* (participle of an ordinary verb + *sum*, passive in meaning, with an agent possible). Deponent: *secūtī sunt*, from *sequor, sequī, secūtus sum*: the dictionary form in *-or* shows it is deponent, so the same build is active (lessons 12 and 13). *Periit* is neither: an active perfect.
5. *Proficīscēmur*: future of the deponent *proficīscor*, third conjugation, so the *ē* future with the passive ending *-mur*, active in meaning (lessons 5 and 13). *Hortātus est*: deponent perfect, "has encouraged" (the result still holds: lesson 7). *Volumus*: present of *volō*, with the infinitive *pugnāre* (lesson 3).
6. *Utinam* with the imperfect subjunctive expresses a wish about the present that is not true: Quintus is not there (lesson 19). *Utinam nōbīscum sīs* (present) would be a wish that might come true: "I hope you will be with us".
7. *Nōlī* + infinitive forbids one person (lesson 9). *Redībō*: future of *redeō*; *eō* and its compounds take the *b* future (lesson 14).
8. *Dēsilīte*: plural imperative of *dēsiliō*, fourth conjugation (lesson 9). *Inquit*: "he said", only beside a quotation, placed inside it (lesson 15). *Vultis*: second person plural of *volō* (lesson 3). *Prōdere*: infinitive of *prōdō*, a third-conjugation compound of *dō* (lessons 3 and 15). *Praestiterō*: future perfect of *praestō*: by the time of reckoning, he will have done his duty (lesson 8).
9. *Prōiēcit*: perfect of *prōiciō*, *prō* + *iaciō*, with *a* weakened to *i* in the present and the long *ē* of *iēcī* kept in the perfect (lessons 6 and 16). *Ferre*: infinitive of *ferō*, with no linking vowel (lesson 14). *Coepit*: "he began", a defective verb with only perfect forms, taking an infinitive (lesson 15).
10. *Cohortor* is a deponent, and a deponent's perfect participle is active: "having encouraged" (ag1903 §190b; lesson 13). *Vocō* is an ordinary verb, whose perfect participle is always passive: "having been called" (lesson 12).
11. *Prōdō, prōdere, prōdidī, prōditum*: third-conjugation compound of *dō* (lesson 15). *Afferō, afferre, attulī, allātum*: irregular, a compound of *ferō* (lesson 14). *Sequor, sequī, secūtus sum*: deponent, third conjugation (lesson 13). *Fīō, fierī, factus sum*: irregular; the passive of *faciō* (lesson 15).
12. Present: *Urbs ab hostibus dēlētur*. Imperfect: *Urbs ab hostibus dēlēbātur*. Perfect: *Urbs ab hostibus dēlēta est*, with *dēlēta* feminine to agree with *urbs* (lessons 11 and 12).
13. *Urbs ā Rōmānīs capiētur.* *Capiō* is third conjugation -iō, so the *ē* future: *capi-ē-tur* (lessons 5 and 11).
14. *Epistula ā patre meō scrīpta erat.* Pluperfect passive: participle + *erat*; *scrīpta* agrees with *epistula* (lesson 12).
15. *Ducem sequāmur nec timeāmus*, or *et nē timeāmus*. Present subjunctive of proposal: *sequāmur* is deponent (*sequ-ā-mur*), *timeāmus* second conjugation; the negative of a proposal is *nē*, joined by *nec* or *et nē* (lessons 17 and 19).
16. *Utinam mīlitēs in castrīs mānsissent!* A wish about the past that did not come true: *utinam* + pluperfect subjunctive (lessons 18 and 19).
17. *Quid faciam?*: deliberative present subjunctive (lesson 19). *Laetōs crēderēs*: potential of the past, imperfect subjunctive, second person "you" in general (lesson 19).
18. *Nōlī hoc facere, Mārce* (lesson 9), and *Nē hoc fēceris, Mārce* (lesson 18). (*Cavē hoc faciās* is a third, ag1903 §450.)
19. *Rōmam ībāmus; servī rēs nostrās ferēbant.* Imperfect of *eō* with *-bā-* (*ībāmus*); imperfect of *ferō*, regular third conjugation (*ferēbant*) (lessons 4 and 14). Both describe what was going on (lesson 7).
20. *Hoc saepe fit; tum quoque factum est.* *Fit*: present of *fīō*, the present passive of *faciō*; *factum est*: perfect passive of *faciō*, neuter to agree with *hoc* (lesson 15).
21. *Dux mīlitēs hortātus est.* *Hortor* is deponent: it has no active forms, and its perfect is participle + *sum* (lesson 13).
22. *Urbs ab hostibus capiētur.* Third-conjugation verbs have no *b* future, and the passive needs the *-tur* ending (lessons 5 and 11).
23. *Gladiīs ūtuntur*: *ūtor* takes the ablative (lesson 13). *Mihi parcitur*: *parcō* takes the dative, so its passive is impersonal (lesson 11).
24. *Utinam adessēs*: an impossible present wish needs the imperfect subjunctive (lesson 19). *Nē veniat*: a subjunctive of command takes *nē* (lessons 17 and 19).
25. *Interfēc-erint*: perfect stem of *interficiō* + *-erint*: perfect subjunctive "they may have killed", or future perfect "they will have killed" (lessons 8, 16 and 18). *Fer-rē-tur*: imperfect subjunctive passive of *ferō*, "it would be carried", "it was being carried" in a subordinate clause; built on *ferre* (lessons 14 and 17). *Abi-isse-nt*: pluperfect subjunctive of *abeō*, "they would have gone away", "they had gone away" in a subordinate clause (lessons 14 and 18).
