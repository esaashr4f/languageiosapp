## The idea

An **indefinite pronoun** points to a person or thing without saying which one: "someone", "anything", "each", "no one" (ag1903 §309). English has a handful and uses "some" and "any" by feel. Latin has more, and chooses between them by logic. Allen and Greenough say the differences "are (with few exceptions) those of logic, not of syntax" (ag1903 §312 note). Learn what each one claims and the choice follows.

There are four questions to ask.

1. **Do I mean a particular person, though I do not name them?** *Quīdam*: "a certain man", one the speaker could identify (ag1903 §310).
2. **Do I mean some person or other, I do not know or care who?** *Aliquis*: "someone" (ag1903 §310).
3. **Is the sentence negative, or close to it, so that I mean "any at all"?** *Quisquam*, and the adjective *ūllus*: "anyone, any" after "not", "never", "without", "hardly", "than", and in questions expecting "no" (ag1903 §§311-312).
4. **Do I mean every single one, taken one at a time?** *Quisque*: "each" (ag1903 §313).

Allen and Greenough rank the first group by how definite they are: *quis* "anyone" is the least definite, *quīdam* "a certain one" the most, with *aliquis* between (ag1903 §310).

Where do these words come from? Most are built on one stem, the stem of *quis* "who?". Allen and Greenough explain that the relative, interrogative and indefinite pronouns are "originally of the same stem", which is why their forms are so alike (ag1903 §150). Small additions change the meaning: *ali-* "some(one) or other" + *quis*; *quis* + *-que*, the same *-que* as "and", gives "each" (ag1903 §151g); *quis* + *-quam* gives "anyone at all"; *quī* + *-dam* gives "a certain". The question word itself is part 6; here you need only its forms.

## How it is built

**The base forms.** *Quis* "anyone" declines like this in the singular: masculine and feminine *quis*, neuter *quid*; accusative *quem*, *quid*; genitive *cuius*; dative *cui*; ablative *quō*. The plural is *quī*, feminine *quae* or *qua*, neuter *qua*; *quōs*, *quās*; *quōrum*; *quibus* (ag1903 §§148-149). All the compounds decline their *quis* part this way and keep the addition fixed: *aliquem*, *alicuius*; *quemquam*, *cuiquam*; *quemque*, *cuique*.

**Pronoun and adjective.** Most of these have two sets of forms. The pronoun ("someone", "something") uses *quis* and *quid*: *aliquis*, *aliquid*. The adjective ("some man", "some thing") uses *quī*, *quae*, *quod* in the nominative and accusative: *aliquī vir* "some man", *aliquod perīculum* "some danger" (ag1903 §151e). In the feminine singular and neuter plural, *aliquis* has *aliqua*.

**Quīdam.** The first part declines like the relative *quī, quae, quod* (part 6), with *-dam* fixed on: *quīdam, quaedam*, neuter *quiddam* (pronoun) or *quoddam* (adjective); accusative *quendam, quandam*; genitive *cuiusdam*; dative *cuidam*; ablative *quōdam, quādam*; plural *quīdam, quaedam*, *quōsdam*, *quōrundam*, *quibusdam* (ag1903 §151c). The *m* becomes *n* before *d*: *quendam*, *quōrundam*.

**Quis after *sī*, *nisi*, *nē*, *num*.** The plain *quis* is used almost only after these four words: *sī quis* "if anyone", *nē quis* "so that no one", *num quis* "surely no one?" (ag1903 §§149b, 310a).

**Building a sentence.** Say "someone is knocking at the door, but I don't see anyone".

1. "Someone", unknown: *aliquis*, nominative.
2. "Anyone" after "not": *quisquam*, accusative *quemquam*.
3. *Aliquis ad iānuam pulsat, sed nēminem videō*, or *… sed quemquam nōn videō*. With a plain "not", Latin prefers the single negative word: *nēminem*.

Say "each man defends his own things".

1. "Each": *quisque*, which usually stands after another word, often the reflexive (ag1903 §313 note).
2. *Sua quisque dēfendit.* *Sua* refers back to *quisque*.

## Where it applies

- ***Quīdam***, "a certain", "a sort of": a definite person not named, *mulier quaedam* "a certain woman"; with an adjective, softening it: *quaedam vīs* "a kind of force". In stories, *quōdam diē* "one day" (ag1903 §310).
- ***Aliquis*, *aliquī***, "some(one), some(thing)": in positive statements, and in "not without": *nōn sine aliquō metū* (ag1903 §311). *Aliquid* takes a partitive genitive: *aliquid novī* "something new".
- ***Quis*, *quī***, "any(one)", after *sī*, *nisi*, *nē*, *num*: *sī quid* "if anything", *nē quis* "so that no one" (ag1903 §310a). *Aliquis* can replace it there for emphasis (ag1903 §310b).
- ***Quisquam***, "anyone at all", pronoun only; ***ūllus***, "any", its adjective. After a negative or a word with negative force: *nōn*, *neque*, *numquam*, *vix* "hardly", *sine* "without"; in questions expecting "no"; after comparatives: *fortior quam quisquam* "braver than anyone" (ag1903 §§311-312).
- ***Quisque***, "each": in general statements; usually after the word it goes with, especially a reflexive or relative; with superlatives, *optimus quisque* "all the best"; with ordinals, *decimus quisque* "every tenth man" (ag1903 §313). *Suum cuique* "to each his own".
- ***Ūnusquisque***, "every single one", both parts declined: *ūnīuscuiusque* (ag1903 §151g).
- ***Uterque***, "each of two" (lesson 2), the *quisque* of pairs.
- ***Quīvīs*, *quīlibet***, "any you like", in positive sentences (ag1903 §312).
- ***Nēmō***, "no one", of persons only; as an adjective with a noun of person: *nēmō Rōmānus* "no Roman"; its genitive and ablative are *nūllīus*, *nūllō* (ag1903 §314).
- ***Nihil***, "nothing", indeclinable; with a partitive genitive, *nihil novī*; as an adverb "not at all" (lesson 3).
- ***Nūllus***, "no", the adjective (lesson 2).
- **Two negatives make a positive**: *nōn nēmō* "someone", *nēmō nōn* "everyone"; *nōnnūllī* "some, several"; *nōnnumquam* "sometimes"; *nōn nihil* "something" (ag1903 §326).
- ***Nesciō quis***, "someone or other", literally "I don't know who": a fixed phrase used as a single indefinite (ag1903 §310 gives *nesciō quae* "some women or other").
- **Adverbs of the same family**: *aliquandō* "at some time", *umquam* "ever" and *usquam* "anywhere" (with negatives), *numquam* "never", *nusquam* "nowhere", *ubīque* "everywhere".

## Watch out

**Do not use *aliquis* after a negative.** *\*Nōn aliquem vīdī*: after a negative, "anyone" is *quisquam*: *nōn quemquam vīdī*, or better *nēminem vīdī* (ag1903 §311).

**Do not say *\*et nēmō* or *\*et nihil*.** "And no one" is *nec quisquam*; "and nothing" *nec quicquam*; "and never" *nec umquam* (part 2, lesson 6).

**Do not use *quisquam* in a positive statement.** *\*Quisquam vēnit* for "someone came": *aliquis vēnit*.

**Do not use *quisque* first in its clause.** *\*Quisque sua dēfendit* is unusual; *sua quisque dēfendit* (ag1903 §313 note).

**Do not make *quisque* plural for "all".** *Quisque* is singular. "All the best men" is *optimus quisque* with a singular verb, or *optimī omnēs* with a plural one.

**Do not use *nēmō* for things.** *Nēmō* is only for people (ag1903 §314). "No thing" is *nihil*; "no reason" is *nūlla causa*.

**Do not use *\*nēminis* and *\*nēmine*.** The classical genitive and ablative are *nūllīus* and *nūllō* (ag1903 §314a). Wiktionary lists *nēminis* and *nēmine* as rarer forms.

**Do not use *aliquis* after *sī* by English habit.** "If anyone" is normally *sī quis*; *sī aliquis* is possible but emphatic: "if someone, anyone at all" (ag1903 §310b).

**Do not translate *quīdam* as "some" in the sense "a few".** *Quīdam* picks out particular people: *quīdam* "certain people". "A few" is *paucī*; "several" *aliquot* or *nōnnūllī*.

**Do not confuse *quid* and *quod*.** *Aliquid* and *quiddam* are pronouns, "something"; *aliquod* and *quoddam* are adjectives, "some …". *Aliquod perīculum*, but *aliquid perīculī*.

## Connections

Spanish *alguien* and *alguno*, French *quelqu'un*, keep Latin's split between "someone or other" and "anyone at all": Spanish *no veo a nadie*, like Latin *nēminem videō*, uses a negative word, not *\*alguien*. French *chacun* "each" does the work of *quisque*, and Spanish *cada uno* that of *ūnusquisque*. Japanese builds the same set from one question word: *dare* "who", *dareka* "someone", *dare mo* with a negative "no one", just as Latin builds on *quis*.

## Exercises

1. Choose *aliquis*, *quisquam* or *quīdam*: "I saw someone (I don't know who)"; "I didn't see anyone"; "a certain man (I know him) came".
2. Say "if anyone comes, call me".
3. Translate *Nihil umquam cuiquam nocuit*.
4. Say "each soldier has his own sword".
5. Say "the bravest men always defend their country" with *quisque*.
6. Correct *\*Nōn aliquid vīdī* and *\*Nēmō perīculum est*.
7. Decline *quīdam* in the masculine singular.
8. Translate *Sine ūllā spē, nōn sine aliquā cūrā*.
9. What is the difference between *nōn nēmō* and *nēmō nōn*?

## Worked answers

1. *Aliquem vīdī* (some person or other). *Nēminem vīdī*, or *nōn quemquam vīdī* (after a negative). *Quīdam vēnit* (a particular person) (ag1903 §§310-311).
2. *Sī quis venit, mē vocā* (or *vēnerit*, future perfect, part 6). After *sī*, plain *quis* (ag1903 §310a). *Vocā* is the singular imperative (part 4).
3. "Nothing ever harmed anyone." After *nihil*, "ever" is *umquam* and "anyone" *quisquam*, here dative *cuiquam* with *noceō* (lesson 9; ag1903 §311).
4. *Suum quisque gladium habet*, or *Mīles quisque suum gladium habet*. *Quisque* after the reflexive *suum* (ag1903 §313 note).
5. *Fortissimus quisque patriam semper dēfendit.* Superlative + *quisque*, singular verb (ag1903 §313b).
6. *Nihil vīdī* (or *nōn quicquam vīdī*): after a negative, not *aliquid*. *Nūllum perīculum est*: *nēmō* is for people; with a noun of thing use *nūllus* (ag1903 §314).
7. *Quīdam*, *quendam*, *cuiusdam*, *cuidam*, *quōdam*. *-dam* never changes; *m* becomes *n* before *d* (*quendam*).
8. "Without any hope, (but) not without some anxiety." *Sine* is negative, so *ūllus*; *nōn sine* is positive in sense, so *aliquis* (ag1903 §311).
9. *Nōn nēmō* is "not no one", so "someone". *Nēmō nōn* is "no one … not", so "everyone": *nēmō nōn vēnit* "everyone came" (ag1903 §326).
