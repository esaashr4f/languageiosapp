## Before you start

This review covers all of part 5: third-declension adjectives (lesson 1); adjectives used as nouns and the ūnus group (lesson 2); adverbs (lesson 3); comparison (lesson 4); numbers (lesson 5); linking words (lesson 6); present and future participles (lesson 7); the uses of the ablative (lesson 8) and of the genitive and dative (lesson 9); and the indefinites (lesson 10). It also uses the verbs of part 4 and the cases and pronouns of part 3.

Work each exercise before reading its answer. Every answer names the lesson to reread if you went wrong.

## Passage

**A. Two brothers.** A constructed passage, using only words and grammar taught so far.

1. In oppidō quōdam habitābat senex pauper, sed vir summā prūdentiā.
2. Duōs fīliōs habēbat: alter fortior erat, alter sapientior.
3. Quōdam diē ambō patrem rogāvērunt: "Uter nostrum melior est?"
4. Senex respondit: "Neuter alterō melior est. Nam virtūs sine sapientiā nihil prōdest, et sapientia sine virtūte parum valet.
5. Fortis igitur sapienter, sapiēns fortiter agat, et alter alterī auxiliō erit."
6. Fīliī, verbōrum patris memorēs, multōs annōs fēlīciter vīxērunt, neque quisquam eōs vīcit.
7. Post mortem senis plūs quam duo mīlia cīvium ad domum eius vēnērunt, et nēmō sine lacrimīs discessit.

**B. The Gauls and the Helvetii (Caesar, Gallic War 1.1.2-3 and 1.2.1, 3, shortened).** Attested; wording from The Latin Library, in this guide's spelling. New words, glossed here: *īnstitūtum* "custom"; *Belgae* "the Belgae", a people of northern Gaul; *Orgetorīx*, a Helvetian nobleman; *Rhēnus* "the Rhine"; *Germānī* "the Germans"; *quī* "which" (a relative pronoun, part 6); *Helvētius* "Helvetian" as an adjective.

1. *Hī omnēs linguā, īnstitūtīs, lēgibus inter sē differunt.*
2. *Hōrum omnium fortissimī sunt Belgae.*
3. *Apud Helvētiōs longē nōbilissimus fuit et dītissimus Orgetorīx.*
4. *[The Helvetii are hemmed in on every side:] ūnā ex parte flūmine Rhēnō lātissimō atque altissimō, quī agrum Helvētium ā Germānīs dīvidit.*

## Exercises

7. In A5, explain *sapienter*, *fortiter*, *agat* and *alterī auxiliō*.
8. In A6, explain *verbōrum patris memorēs*, *multōs annōs*, *fēlīciter* and *neque quisquam*.
9. In A7, explain *plūs quam duo mīlia cīvium* and *nēmō*.

15. Say "Rome is much larger than Carthage" in two ways (*Carthāgō, Carthāginis* f.).
16. Say "with three thousand soldiers and two legions".
17. Say "the citizens, fearing the enemy, sent the cavalry (*equitēs*) to help the town" with a double dative.

25. Turn *Puer, quod hostēs timet, fugit* ("the boy runs away because he fears the enemy") into one clause with a participle.

## Worked answers

1. "In a certain town there lived a poor old man, but a man of the greatest good sense. He had two sons: one was braver, the other wiser. One day they both asked their father: 'Which of us is better?'"
2. "The old man answered: 'Neither is better than the other. For courage without wisdom is of no use, and wisdom without courage has too little strength. So let the brave one act wisely and the wise one bravely, and each will be a help to the other.' The sons, mindful of their father's words, lived happily for many years, and no one defeated them. After the old man's death more than two thousand citizens came to his house, and no one went away without tears."
3. "All these differ from one another in language, customs and laws. Of all these the bravest are the Belgae. Among the Helvetii by far the noblest and richest man was Orgetorix. [The Helvetii are hemmed in] on one side by the river Rhine, very wide and very deep, which separates the Helvetian land from the Germans."
4. *Quōdam*: ablative of *quīdam* with *oppidō*, "a certain town", one the storyteller could name (lesson 10). *Summā prūdentiā*: ablative of description, noun with adjective, attached to *vir* (lesson 8); *summus* is the superlative of *superus* (lesson 4).
5. *Alter … alter*: "the one … the other", because there are two (lesson 2). *Fortior*, *sapientior*: comparatives with no second term, "the braver", "the wiser" of the pair (lesson 4); *sapientior* is built on *sapient-* (lesson 1). *Ambō*: "both", declined like *duo* (lesson 5). *Uter nostrum*: "which of the two of us", *uter* with the partitive genitive *nostrum* (lessons 2 and 9).
6. *Alterō*: ablative of comparison, "than the other" (lesson 4). *Nihil*: used as an adverb, "not at all", with *prōdest* (lessons 3 and 10). *Parum*: "too little", the adverb of *parvus* (lesson 3). *Nam*: a real reason for the claim just made, standing first (lesson 6).
7. *Sapienter*: adverb of *sapiēns*, *nt*-stem + *-er*; *fortiter*: *fort-* + *-iter* (lesson 3). *Fortis* and *sapiēns* are adjectives used as nouns, "the brave one", "the wise one" (lesson 2). *Agat*: present subjunctive of command, "let him act" (part 4, lesson 19). *Alterī auxiliō*: double dative, "a help to the other" (lesson 9); *alterī* is the pronominal dative (lesson 2).
8. *Memorēs*: adjective agreeing with *fīliī*, taking the genitive *verbōrum* (lesson 9). *Multōs annōs*: accusative of duration (part 3). *Fēlīciter*: adverb of *fēlīx* (lesson 3). *Neque quisquam*: "and no one"; after *neque*, "anyone" is *quisquam*, not *nēmō* (lesson 10).
9. *Plūs quam duo mīlia*: *plūs* "more" with *quam*, here used without changing the case of *mīlia*, the subject; *mīlia* is a noun with the genitive *cīvium* (lessons 4 and 5). *Nēmō*: "no one", of persons (lesson 10).
10. Ablatives of respect: "in language, customs, laws" (lesson 8). No conjunction, asyndeton: a quick, complete list (lesson 6).
11. *Hōrum omnium*: partitive genitive with a superlative (lesson 9). *Fortissimī*: superlative of *fortis* (lesson 4). *Longē*: "by far", strengthening a superlative; an adverb from *longus* (lesson 3). *Dītissimus*: a contracted superlative of *dīves* (Wiktionary lists *dītior*, *dītissimus* under *dīs*, the contracted form of *dīves*; lesson 4).
12. *Ūnā ex parte*: "on one side", *ūnus* as a numeral; Caesar goes on *alterā ex parte* and *tertiā* (lessons 2 and 5). *Flūmine*: ablative of means, "hemmed in by the river" (lesson 8). *Lātissimō atque altissimō*: superlatives agreeing with *flūmine*, "very wide and very deep", joined by *atque* (lessons 4 and 6). *Lātus* "wide" is lesson 3.
13. *Mīles ācer*, *mīlitem ācrem*, *mīlitis ācris*, *mīlitī ācrī*, *mīlite ācrī*. Neuter nominative plural: *ācria*. Only the masculine nominative and vocative are *ācer*; the adjective ablative is *-ī* (lesson 1).
14. *Fēminae fortēs gravia onera facile ferēbant.* *Fortēs*: third-declension, feminine plural like masculine; *gravia*: neuter plural *-ia*; *facile*: the adverb (lessons 1 and 3).
15. *Rōma multō maior est quam Carthāgō*, or *Rōma Carthāgine multō maior est*. *Multō*: degree of difference; *Carthāgine*: ablative of comparison (lessons 4 and 8).
16. *Cum tribus mīlibus mīlitum et duābus legiōnibus.* *Mīlia* is a noun with the genitive; *duo* agrees with the feminine *legiōnibus* (lesson 5).
17. *Cīvēs, hostēs timentēs, equitēs oppidō auxiliō mīsērunt.* *Timentēs*: present participle agreeing with *cīvēs* (lesson 7). *Oppidō auxiliō*: double dative (lesson 9).
18. *Quendam in forō vīdī, sed cum nūllō locūtus sum*, or *… sed nēminī dīxī*. *In forō*: *in* + ablative of *forum*, *-ī* n. "forum, public square" (glossed here; taught in part 6, lesson 2). *Quendam*: a particular man (lesson 10). After a negative, "anyone" is *quisquam*, but Latin prefers the negative word: *cum nūllō* uses *nūllus* for the ablative of *nēmō* (lessons 2 and 10).
19. *Sua quisque laudat, et nēmō aliēnīs contentus est.* *Quisque* after the reflexive; *contentus* with the ablative; *aliēnus* "another's" in place of the genitive of *alius* (lessons 2, 8 and 10).
20. *In urbe ingentī omnēs mīlitēs fortēs erant.* The adjective ablative is *-ī*; *fortis* must agree with *mīlitēs*, nominative plural (lesson 1).
21. *Dux sapientior est quam frāter suus.* *Sapiēns* has its own comparative; after *quam* the second term is in the same case as the first, nominative (lesson 4).
22. *Gladiīs et saxīs pugnābant, sed nēminem vīcērunt* (or *nōn quemquam*). Tools take the plain ablative of means; after a negative, not *aliquis* (lessons 8 and 10).
23. *Tōtīus urbis cīvēs ūnī puerō pecūniam dedērunt.* The ūnus group has genitive *-īus* and dative *-ī* (lesson 2).
24. *Ama-nt-ibus*: dative or ablative plural of the present participle *amāns*, "to/for/by/with (people) loving", or as a noun "to/for lovers" (lessons 7 and 2). *Ven-tūr-ī*: future participle of *veniō*, nominative plural masculine "(they) about to come", or genitive singular masculine/neuter "of (one) about to come" (lesson 7). *Simil-lim-us*: superlative of *similis*, one of the six in *-lis*, "very like, most like", masculine nominative singular (lesson 4). *Cuius-dam*: genitive singular of *quīdam*, any gender, "of a certain (person or thing)" (lesson 10).
25. *Puer hostēs timēns fugit.* The present participle *timēns* agrees with *puer* and takes the object *hostēs*; the English link "because" is left to context (lesson 7).
