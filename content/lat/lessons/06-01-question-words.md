## The idea

Part 2 (lesson 6) taught the questions that expect "yes" or "no": *-ne*, *nōnne*, *num*. This lesson teaches the other kind, the question about a detail: who, what, which, where, when, why, how, how many. Allen and Greenough describe it as a question "concerning some special circumstance", formed "by prefixing to the sentence an interrogative pronoun or adverb as in English" (ag1903 §333). An **interrogative** is a question word.

Two things make Latin questions easier than English ones.

1. **The word order does not change.** English turns "you saw him" into "whom did you see?", adding "did". Latin only puts the question word first: *vīdistī* "you saw", *quem vīdistī?* "whom did you see?". Latin questions "are not distinguished by the order of words, as in English" (ag1903 §331).
2. **The question word is declined.** Its case shows its job in the sentence, exactly as a noun's case does.

Nearly all the question words share one stem, *qu-*, and that is not an accident. Lesson 10 of part 5 showed that the indefinites *aliquis*, *quisquam*, *quīdam* are built on *quis*. Allen and Greenough say the relative, interrogative and indefinite pronouns are "originally of the same stem", and that "the interrogative sense is doubtless the original one" (ag1903 §150). Wiktionary traces *quis* to an Indo-European *kʷís*. So the question word is the root of the whole family: "who?" came first, and "someone" and "who" in "the man who came" (next lesson) grew out of it.

The adverbs grew from the same root. Wiktionary derives *cūr* "why?" from Old Latin *quōr*, *ubi* "where?" from older *cubī*, *unde* from older *cunde*. Why *ubi* and *unde* lost their *c* is not settled (Wiktionary).

## How it is built

**"Who?" and "what?": the pronoun *quis*.** It is declined like the indefinite *quis* of lesson 10 of part 5 (ag1903 §148):

- singular: *quis* "who?", neuter *quid* "what?"; accusative *quem*, *quid*; genitive *cuius* "whose?"; dative *cui* "to whom?"; ablative *quō* "by whom?, with what?";
- plural: *quī*, feminine *quae*, neuter *quae*; *quōs*, *quās*, *quae*; *quōrum*, *quārum*, *quōrum*; *quibus*.

The masculine singular covers men and women alike when you do not know who it is: *quis venit?* "who is coming?" (ag1903 §148a).

**"Which?", "what?" with a noun: the adjective *quī, quae, quod*.** When the question word goes with a noun, Latin uses a second set of forms, which agree with the noun like any adjective (ag1903 §148b): *quod templum vidēs?* "which temple do you see?"; *quae puella?* "which girl?". The neuter is the clearest sign: pronoun *quid*, adjective *quod*. This is the same pronoun-and-adjective split as *aliquid* and *aliquod* (lesson 10 of part 5).

The line is not strict: *quis homō?* "what man?" is common, while *quī homō?* tends to ask "what kind of man?" (ag1903 §148b note).

**The question adverbs.** Learn them in sets.

- Place: *ubi* "where?" (at rest), *quō* "where to?", *unde* "where from?", *quā* "by what way?".
- Time: *quandō* "when?"; *quotiēns* "how often?".
- Reason: *cūr* "why?"; *quārē* "for what reason?" (lesson 6 of part 5).
- Manner: *quōmodo* "how?, in what way?", also written *quō modō*, "by what means"; *quemadmodum*, "in what manner", is the same idea in the accusative.
- Amount and kind: *quantus* "how big?, how much?", *quālis* "of what kind?", *quot* "how many?" (not declined), *quantum* "how much?".

**Building a question.** Say "to whom did you give the book?".

1. Write the statement: *librum dedistī* "you gave the book".
2. The unknown is the person you gave it to, so it is the dative: *cui*.
3. Put the question word first: *Cui librum dedistī?*

Say "which city are we living in?".

1. "In a city": *in urbe*, ablative.
2. "Which" goes with *urbs*, so it is the adjective, feminine ablative singular: *quā*.
3. A preposition stays in front of its question word: *In quā urbe vīvimus?*

**Emphasis.** The enclitic *-nam* added to a question word gives "who on earth?": *quisnam*, *ubinam*. *Tandem* "pray, at last" does the same as a separate word: *quō ūsque tandem* "how long, pray?" (ag1903 §333a).

**Alternative questions.** To ask "is it this or that?", put *utrum* or *-ne* in the first half and *an* "or" in the second (ag1903 §335). *Utrum* is the neuter of *uter* "which of two?" (lesson 2 of part 5). "Or not" is *annōn*, or *necne* (ag1903 §335 note). The first word may be left out: *Sunt haec tua verba necne?* "are these your words or not?" (Cicero, quoted in ag1903 §335a).

**Exclamations** use the same words with a different tone of voice (ag1903 §333 note): *quālis vir erat!* "what a man he was!".

## Where it applies

- **Who, whom, whose, to whom, by whom**: every case of *quis*: *cuius est liber?* "whose is the book?"; *ā quō laudātus est?* "by whom was he praised?".
- **What**, as subject or object: *quid accidit?* "what happened?" (Wiktionary); *quid fēcistī?*.
- **What** with a partitive genitive: *quid novī?* "what news?", literally "what of new?" (lesson 9 of part 5). *Quid cōnsiliī?* "what plan?".
- **Which, what** with a noun: *quod templum*, *quā in urbe*, *quam rem pūblicam*.
- **Which of two**: *uter*: *uter vestrum?* "which of you two?" (lesson 2 of part 5).
- **Place**: *ubi*, *quō*, *unde*, *quā*. Also *ubi gentium?* "where in the world?" (ag1903 §346a).
- **Time**: *quandō*; for "how long?" use *quam diū* (*quam* "how" + *diū* "for a long time"): *quam diū etiam furor iste tuus nōs ēlūdet?* "how much longer will that madness of yours make fools of us?" (Cicero, Against Catiline 1.1).
- **Cause**: *cūr*, *quārē*, *quam ob rem*. The neuter *quid* can also mean "why?": Allen and Greenough list it among neuter pronouns used adverbially (ag1903 §390c): *quid moror?* "why do I delay?".
- **Manner**: *quōmodo*, *quemadmodum*.
- **Amount, size, kind, number**: *quantus*, *quālis*, *quot*, *quotiēns*.
- **Alternatives**: *utrum … an*, *-ne … an*, *… an*, *… necne* (ag1903 §335).
- **Indignant questions** with *an* alone: "what, do you think…?" (ag1903 §335b).
- **Deliberative questions** with the subjunctive: *quid faciam?* "what am I to do?" (lesson 19 of part 4).
- **Exclamations**: *quālis artifex pereō!*; *quot calamitātēs!* (ag1903 §333 note).
- **Reported questions**, "I do not know where he is", use the same words with the subjunctive: lesson 11 of this part.

## Watch out

**Do not move the verb or add a helper.** English "where *does* he live?" and French *où habite-t-il?* tempt you to rearrange. *\*Ubi facit habitāre?* is nonsense. Keep the statement as it is and put the question word in front: *Ubi habitat?* (ag1903 §331).

**Do not use *quid* with a noun.** *\*Quid templum vidēs?* uses the pronoun where an adjective is needed. "Which temple": *quod templum vidēs?* (ag1903 §148b). In the masculine and feminine the two sets overlap more (*quis homō*, *quī homō*), but in the neuter they never do.

**Do not use one word for every English "where".** English "where" covers rest, goal and origin; Latin splits them, as Spanish splits *dónde*, *adónde*, *de dónde*. *\*Ubi vādis?* asks "in what place are you going?". The goal of motion is *quō*: *Quō vādis?* "Where are you from?" is *Unde es?*, not *\*Ubi es?*

**Do not put the question word in the nominative by default.** English "who did you see?" uses the subject form; Latin does not. *\*Quis vīdistī?* has no subject for "you saw". The person seen is the object: *Quem vīdistī?*

**Do not invent a plural *\*quēs*.** The plural is *quī*, *quae*, *quae* (ag1903 §148); *quēs* is Old Latin.

**Do not join alternatives with *aut*.** "Did he come or not?" is a choice between two answers: *Vēnitne an nōn?*, or *… necne?* *Aut* and *vel* belong to a single question with an alternative detail inside it: *quaerō num iniūstē aut improbē fēcerit* asks whether he acted either unjustly or wickedly, not which (ag1903 §335 note).

**Do not add *-ne* to a question that already has a question word.** *\*Quisne venit?* doubles the question; say *Quis venit?* (Horace's *quantane?* "how big?" is colloquial: ag1903 §332 note 2.)

**Do not read every *quam* as "than".** *Quam* can be the feminine accusative of the adjective *quī* (*quam rem pūblicam?*), "how" before an adjective or adverb (*quam diū?*, *quam pulchra!*), or "than" after a comparative (part 5). Ask what it is attached to.

**Do not use *quōmodo* for "how are you?".** The idiom is *quid agis?* (Wiktionary). *Quōmodo* asks for a method: *quōmodo possumus viam scīre?* "how can we know the way?" (Vulgate, John 14:5).

## Connections

Spanish and French kept the Latin question words, worn down: *quis* gave Spanish *quién* (from the accusative *quem*, Wiktionary), *quid* gave *qué*, *quandō* gave *cuándo* and *quand*, *quālis* gave *cuál* and *quel*, *quantus* gave *cuánto*. Spanish *dónde* is *de* "from" + Old Spanish *onde*, from Latin *unde* "from where"; its meaning has shifted to plain "where" (Wiktionary). Japanese and Korean also leave the word order alone in questions, as Latin does: *nani o mimashita ka* "what did you see?" puts the question word where the answer would go.

## Exercises

1. Say "who is calling?", "whom are you calling?" and "whose voice is this?".
2. Say "which book are you reading?" and explain why the question word is not *quid*.
3. Translate *Unde venīs et quō vādis?* and explain the two adverbs.
4. Say "how many legions has Caesar?" and "how big is the danger?".
5. Turn each statement into a question about the underlined idea: *Mārcus [in urbe] habitat*; *[Mārcus] venit*; *Puer [mātrī] librum dedit*.
6. Translate *Utrum pāx an bellum erit?* and turn it into "will there be peace or not?" in two ways.
7. In *In quā urbe vīvimus?* explain *quā*; in *Quis custōdiet ipsōs custōdēs?* explain *quis* and *custōdiet*.
8. Correct *\*Quis vīdistī in viā?* and *\*Ubi vādis?*
9. Translate *Quandō Rōmam veniēs? Cūr nōn statim?*

## Worked answers

1. *Quis vocat?*: subject, nominative. *Quem vocās?*: object, accusative. *Cuius vōx haec est?*: possession, genitive *cuius* (ag1903 §148).
2. *Quem librum legis?* The question word goes with a noun, so it is the adjective *quī*, here masculine accusative singular agreeing with *librum* (ag1903 §148b). *Quid* is a pronoun and stands alone.
3. "Where are you coming from, and where are you going?" *Unde* asks for the origin, *quō* for the goal.
4. *Quot legiōnēs Caesar habet?* *Quot* does not change. *Quantum est perīculum?* *Quantus* agrees with *perīculum*, neuter nominative singular.
5. *Ubi Mārcus habitat?* (or *Ubi habitat Mārcus?*) "Where does Marcus live?". *Quis venit?* "Who is coming?". *Cui puer librum dedit?* "To whom did the boy give the book?": the receiver is dative, so *cui*.
6. "Will there be peace or war?" *Utrum … an* marks an alternative question (ag1903 §335). "Or not": *Utrum pāx erit annōn?* or *Pāxne erit necne?* (ag1903 §335 note).
7. *Quā*: ablative singular feminine of the adjective *quī*, agreeing with *urbe* after *in* (ag1903 §148b). *Quis*: nominative, the subject of *custōdiet*. *Custōdiet*: future of the fourth-conjugation *custōdiō*, with *-e-* as in *audiet* (part 4, lesson 5).
8. *Quem in viā vīdistī?* The person seen is the object, so accusative; the question word goes first. *Quō vādis?* Motion towards a place takes *quō*, not *ubi*.
9. "When will you come to Rome? Why not at once?" *Quandō* asks for a time; *Rōmam*: town name, accusative for "to" (part 3, lesson 13); *veniēs*: future (part 4, lesson 5).
