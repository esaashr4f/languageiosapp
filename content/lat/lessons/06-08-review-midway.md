## Before you start

This review covers the first half of part 6: question words (lesson 1), relative clauses (lesson 2), clauses of time and cause with the indicative (lesson 3), the ablative absolute (lesson 4), reported statements and the six infinitives (lesson 5), the sequence of tenses (lesson 6) and purpose clauses (lesson 7). It also uses the participles, comparison and case uses of part 5 and the verb system of part 4.

Work each exercise before reading its answer. Every answer names the lesson to reread if you went wrong.

## Passage

**A. The envoy.** A constructed passage, using only words and grammar taught so far, except *brevī* "soon" (the ablative of *brevis*, "in a short time"), glossed here.

1. Ubi hostēs ad flūmen pervēnērunt, cōnsul, quī in castrīs erat, lēgātum ad eōs mīsit.
2. Lēgātus, quem cōnsul dēlēgerat, vir summā prūdentiā erat.
3. Hīs rēbus parātīs, lēgātus ad hostēs contendit ut pācem peteret.
4. Dux hostium "Quis es," inquit, "et cūr vēnistī?"
5. Lēgātus respondit sē ā cōnsule missum esse, quod Rōmānī bellum nōn cuperent.
6. Dux negāvit sē pācem facere velle, et dīxit hostēs, quī multō plūrēs essent, urbem brevī captūrōs esse.
7. Lēgātus, nē ipse caperētur, celeriter in castra rediit.

**B. Caesar and Liscus (Caesar, Gallic War 1.18.1-2).** Attested; wording from The Latin Library, in this guide's spelling. New words, glossed here: *Liscus*, an Aeduan magistrate; *Dīviciācus*, Dumnorix's brother; *dēsignō* "point to, mean"; *iactō* "throw about, discuss openly"; *conventus* "meeting".

1. *Caesar hāc ōrātiōne Liscī Dumnorīgem, Dīviciācī frātrem, dēsignārī sentiēbat;*
2. *sed, quod plūribus praesentibus eās rēs iactārī nōlēbat, celeriter concilium dīmittit, Liscum retinet.*
3. *Quaerit ex sōlō ea quae in conventū dīxerat.*

**C. Thomas (Vulgate, John 20:24-26, 31, shortened).** Attested. New words, glossed here: *discipulus* "disciple"; *Didymus* "the Twin"; *intus* "inside"; *Chrīstus* "Christ".

1. *Thōmās autem, ūnus ex duodecim, quī dīcitur Didymus, nōn erat cum eīs quandō vēnit Iēsūs.*
2. *Dīxērunt ergō eī aliī discipulī: "Vīdimus Dominum!"*
3. *Et post diēs octō iterum erant discipulī eius intus et Thōmās cum eīs. Venit Iēsūs iānuīs clausīs et stetit in mediō.*
4. *Haec autem scrīpta sunt ut crēdātis quia Iēsūs est Chrīstus.*

## Exercises

1. Translate passage A, sentences 1 to 4.
2. Translate passage A, sentences 5 to 7.
3. In A1, explain *ubi … pervēnērunt* and *quī in castrīs erat*.
4. In A2 and A3, explain *quem*, *summā prūdentiā* and *hīs rēbus parātīs*.
5. In A3 and A7, explain *ut pācem peteret* and *nē ipse caperētur*, including the tenses.
6. In A4, explain *quis* and *cūr*, and say why the word order is the same as in a statement.
7. In A5, explain *sē … missum esse* and *quod … cuperent*.
8. In A6, explain *negāvit*, *quī … essent*, *multō plūrēs* and *captūrōs esse*.
9. Translate passage B.
10. In B1, explain *dēsignārī* and why *Dumnorīgem* is accusative.
11. In B2, explain *quod … nōlēbat* (why indicative?), *plūribus praesentibus*, and the change from *sentiēbat* to *dīmittit*.
12. In B3, explain *ea quae* and the tense of *dīxerat*.
13. Translate passage C.
14. In C1, explain *quī dīcitur Didymus* and *quandō vēnit*.
15. In C3 and C4, explain *iānuīs clausīs*, *ut crēdātis* and *quia Iēsūs est Chrīstus*, and say what a classical writer would have put instead of the *quia* clause.
16. Say "Who are the men whom you sent to the city, and why did they go?"
17. Say "the soldiers said that they had seen the enemy and that they would fight".
18. Say "when the signal had been given, the cavalry hurried to the hill to help the legion" with an ablative absolute and a purpose clause.
19. Say "he hid the letter so that no one would read it".
20. Correct *\*Mīles quī vīdistī frāter meus est*.
21. Correct *\*Dīxit quod hostēs fūgērunt* and *\*Spērat vincere* (meant as "he hopes he will win").
22. Correct *\*Caesare urbem captā, Caesar discessit*.
23. Correct *\*Vēnērunt vidēre urbem* and *\*Fūgit ut nōn caperētur*.
24. Correct *\*Dīxit librum quem legit bonum esse* (reporting "the book I am reading is good").
25. Give all six infinitives of *mittō*, and say which agree with a subject accusative.

## Worked answers

1. "When the enemy reached the river, the consul, who was in the camp, sent an envoy to them. The envoy, whom the consul had chosen, was a man of the greatest good sense. When these preparations had been made, the envoy hurried to the enemy to ask for peace. The enemy's leader said: 'Who are you, and why have you come?'"
2. "The envoy replied that he had been sent by the consul, because (as he said) the Romans did not want war. The leader refused to make peace, and said that the enemy, who (he said) were much more numerous, would soon take the city. The envoy, so as not to be captured himself, quickly went back to the camp."
3. *Ubi … pervēnērunt*: a temporal clause with the perfect indicative for a single event, "when they had reached" (lesson 3; ag1903 §543). *Quī in castrīs erat*: a relative clause; *quī* is masculine singular from *cōnsul* and nominative as the subject of *erat* (lesson 2; ag1903 §305).
4. *Quem*: masculine singular from *lēgātus*, accusative as the object of *dēlēgerat* (lesson 2). *Summā prūdentiā*: ablative of description (part 5, lesson 8). *Hīs rēbus parātīs*: ablative absolute with the perfect participle, "these things having been prepared", "when everything was ready" (lesson 4; ag1903 §419).
5. Both are purpose clauses (lesson 7). *Ut* for a positive purpose, *nē* for a negative one (ag1903 §531). Both main verbs, *contendit* and *rediit*, are perfect, so secondary sequence: imperfect subjunctive (lesson 6; ag1903 §484). *Caperētur* is passive.
6. *Quis*: nominative, the predicate of *es*, "who?" (lesson 1). *Cūr*: "why?". Latin questions keep the order of a statement and only put the question word first (ag1903 §331).
7. *Sē … missum esse*: a reported statement; *sē* refers to the speaker, the envoy; *missum esse* is the perfect passive infinitive, agreeing with *sē*, because the sending came before the replying (lesson 5; ag1903 §584). *Quod … cuperent*: a reason inside reported speech, so subjunctive; imperfect after the secondary *respondit* (lesson 6; ag1903 §§540, 580).
8. *Negāvit*: "said that … not", the usual Latin for a negative report (lesson 5; ag1903 §580b). *Quī … essent*: a relative clause inside reported speech, so subjunctive, imperfect by secondary sequence (lesson 6). *Multō plūrēs*: *plūrēs* "more", with *multō* the ablative of degree of difference (part 5, lessons 4 and 8). *Captūrōs esse*: future active infinitive, agreeing with *hostēs* (lesson 5).
9. "Caesar realised that Dumnorix, Diviciacus' brother, was meant by this speech of Liscus; but because he did not want these matters discussed openly with more people present, he quickly dismissed the council and kept Liscus back. He asked him, alone, about what he had said in the meeting."
10. *Dēsignārī*: present passive infinitive, "to be meant", simultaneous with *sentiēbat* (lesson 5). *Dumnorīgem* is the subject accusative of that infinitive in a reported statement after *sentiēbat*, a verb of perceiving (lesson 5; ag1903 §579).
11. *Quod … nōlēbat*: "because he did not want"; indicative because Caesar the writer vouches for his own reason (lesson 3; ag1903 §540). *Plūribus praesentibus*: an ablative absolute of adjective and adjective, no participle, "more people being present" (lesson 4; ag1903 §419a). Caesar moves from the imperfect for the background (*sentiēbat*) to the historic present for the actions (*dīmittit*, *retinet*; part 4, lesson 7).
12. *Ea quae*: "those things which"; the antecedent *ea* is neuter plural, and *quae* agrees with it, accusative as the object of *dīxerat* (lesson 2). *Dīxerat*: pluperfect indicative, because he had spoken before the questioning; a relative clause in plain narrative keeps the indicative (lesson 2).
13. "But Thomas, one of the twelve, who is called the Twin, was not with them when Jesus came. So the other disciples said to him: 'We have seen the Lord!' And eight days later his disciples were inside again, and Thomas was with them. Jesus came, though the doors were shut, and stood among them. But these things have been written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ."
14. *Quī dīcitur Didymus*: a relative clause, *quī* masculine singular from *Thōmās*, nominative as subject of the passive *dīcitur* (lesson 2). *Quandō vēnit*: "when he came"; Allen and Greenough list *quandō* as a relative "when" (ag1903 §540a note), here with the perfect indicative like *ubi* (lesson 3).
15. *Iānuīs clausīs*: ablative absolute, "the doors having been shut", best "although the doors were shut" (lesson 4). *Ut crēdātis*: purpose; present subjunctive because *scrīpta sunt* "have been written" looks at the present, a perfect with primary sequence (lessons 6 and 7; ag1903 §485a). *Quia Iēsūs est Chrīstus*: late Latin "that" clause for a reported statement. A classical writer would write *ut crēdātis Iēsūm esse Chrīstum* (lesson 5).
16. *Quī sunt virī quōs ad urbem mīsistī, et cūr iērunt?* *Quī*: plural of the question pronoun (lesson 1); *quōs*: relative, masculine plural from *virī*, accusative object of *mīsistī* (lesson 2); *iērunt*: perfect of *eō* (part 4, lesson 14).
17. *Mīlitēs dīxērunt sē hostēs vīdisse et sē pugnātūrōs esse.* Perfect infinitive for the earlier seeing, future infinitive for the later fighting; *sē* for the speakers (lesson 5; ag1903 §584).
18. *Signō datō, equitēs ad collem contendērunt ut legiōnī auxiliō essent* (or *ut legiōnem iuvārent*). Ablative absolute *signō datō* (lesson 4); purpose with *ut* + imperfect after the secondary *contendērunt* (lessons 6 and 7); *auxiliō* is the dative of purpose (part 5, lesson 9).
19. *Epistulam cēlāvit nē quis eam legeret.* Negative purpose *nē* with *quis* "anyone"; imperfect after a secondary verb (lesson 7; part 5, lesson 10).
20. *Mīles quem vīdistī frāter meus est.* The relative takes its case from its own clause: the soldier is the object of *vīdistī* (lesson 2; ag1903 §305).
21. *Dīxit hostēs fūgisse*: classical Latin reports with the accusative and infinitive, not a "that" clause (lesson 5). *Spērat sē victūrum esse*: verbs of hoping take the future infinitive with an expressed subject (lesson 5; ag1903 §§580c, 581).
22. *Urbe captā, Caesar discessit.* The ablative absolute is the city and its participle, not the main subject; and *captā* must agree with *urbe* (lesson 4; ag1903 §419 note).
23. *Vēnērunt ut urbem vidērent*: purpose is not expressed by the infinitive in prose (lesson 7; ag1903 §533). *Fūgit nē caperētur*: negative purpose takes *nē* (lesson 7; ag1903 §531).
24. *Dīxit librum quem legeret bonum esse.* A clause inside a reported statement is subjunctive, and after the secondary *dīxit* the simultaneous reading is imperfect (lesson 6; ag1903 §§580, 484b).
25. *Mittere, mittī, mīsisse, missum esse, missūrum esse, missum īrī* (Wiktionary). *Missum esse* and *missūrum esse* contain participles that agree with the subject accusative (*urbem missam esse*, *eōs missūrōs esse*); *missum īrī* never changes (lesson 5).
