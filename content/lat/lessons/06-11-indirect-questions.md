## The idea

A direct question gives the speaker's exact words: *Ubi est?* "Where is he?". An **indirect question** builds the question into a larger sentence: "I don't know *where he is*", "he asked *what I was doing*" (ag1903 §330). It depends on a verb of asking, knowing, telling, doubting, seeing, or any word implying uncertainty (ag1903 §573).

Latin changes only one thing: the verb goes into the subjunctive (ag1903 §574).

- direct: *Ubi est?* "Where is he?"
- indirect: *Nesciō ubi sit.* "I don't know where he is."

The question word stays the same; the word order stays the same. Allen and Greenough put it plainly: the form of an indirect question "is in Latin the same as that of direct; the difference being only in the verb" (ag1903 §331 note). The subjunctive's tense then follows the sequence of tenses (lesson 6).

Why the subjunctive? The clause is no longer the speaker's own question: it reports someone else's, or names an unknown. Allen and Greenough treat it as a reported construction, like the clauses inside reported statements (ag1903 §577), and say the subjunctive there serves mainly to mark the clause as subordinate. They add that the details of its origin are not certain. Note also that this is a fact of Latin, not of logic: English and Spanish keep the indicative, "I know where he *is*", *sé dónde está*.

This lesson brings together the question words of lesson 1 and the sequence of lesson 6. You already have nearly all you need.

## How it is built

**Building an indirect question.** Say "I asked him what he was doing".

1. The direct question: *Quid facis?*
2. The main verb: *rogāvī*, secondary.
3. The doing is simultaneous with the asking: imperfect subjunctive, *faceret*.
4. *Rogāvī eum quid faceret.*

Say "I don't know where he has gone".

1. Direct: *Quō iit?*
2. *Nesciō* is primary; the going is earlier: perfect subjunctive, *ierit*.
3. *Nesciō quō ierit.*

**The future.** Because the present subjunctive could be read as present, Latin uses the future participle with *sim* or *essem* for a future question (ag1903 §575a): *nesciō quid factūrus sit* "I don't know what he will do"; *nescīvī quid factūrus esset* "I didn't know what he would do".

**Yes-or-no questions: "whether".** The particles *-ne* and *num* both mean "whether" in an indirect question; *num* loses its expectation of "no" (ag1903 §332b note): *rogāvit num domī esset* "he asked whether he was at home".

**Alternatives: "whether … or".** *Utrum … an*, *-ne … an*, and for "or not" *necne*, which is commoner than *annōn* in indirect questions (ag1903 §335 note): *quaesīvī fuisset necne* "I asked whether he had been there or not".

**Deliberative questions** keep their subjunctive, changing only tense if needed (ag1903 §575b): *quō mē vertam?* "where am I to turn?" becomes *quō mē vertam nesciō* "I don't know where to turn".

**Exclamations** become indirect the same way: *quam sīs audāx omnēs intellegere potuērunt* "all could see how bold you are" (Cicero, quoted in ag1903 §574).

**Fixed phrases with the indicative.** *Nesciō quis* "someone or other" has become a single indefinite pronoun and takes no subjunctive (ag1903 §575d): *nesciō quis venit* "someone is coming". So also *mīrum quantum* "amazingly", "it's a wonder how much": *mīrum quantum prōfuit* "it helped enormously".

## Where it applies

- **After verbs of asking**: *rogō*, *interrogō*, *quaerō* (*ex* or *ā* + ablative of the person), *percontor* "inquire".
- **After verbs of knowing and not knowing**: *sciō*, *nesciō*, *ignōrō*, *intellegō*, *cognōscō*, *meminī*; *nōvī* "I know".
- **After verbs of telling and showing**: *dīcō*, *doceō*, *ostendō*, *narrō*, *expōnō* "explain": *quid ipse sentiam expōnam* "I will explain what I think myself" (Cicero, quoted in ag1903 §574).
- **After verbs of seeing and considering**: *videō*, *cōgitō*, *dēlīberō*, *cōnsulō*, *prōspiciō*: *prōspiciō quī concursūs futūrī sint* "I foresee what crowds there will be" (Cicero, quoted in ag1903 §575a).
- **After words of doubt and uncertainty**: *dubitō*, *dubium est*, *incertus*: *incertī quātenus …* "uncertain how far…" (Livy, quoted in ag1903 §574).
- **As subject**: *cōnstat*, *appāret*, *nihil interest* "it makes no difference": *doleam necne doleam nihil interest* (Cicero, quoted in ag1903 §574).
- **In apposition to a noun or pronoun**: *hoc dubium est, uter nostrum sit …* "this is doubtful, which of us is…" (ag1903 §574).
- **Exclamations** reported: *quam sīs audāx* (ag1903 §574).
- **The anticipated subject**, colloquial: *nōstī Mārcellum quam tardus sit* "you know how slow Marcellus is", where Marcellus is pulled into the main clause as object (Cicero's letters, quoted in ag1903 §576).
- **"See if"**: *sī* "whether" after verbs of trying and waiting (ag1903 §576a); lesson 13 teaches *sī*.
- **Fixed phrases with the indicative**: *nesciō quis*, *nesciō quō*, *mīrum quantum*, *sānē quam* (ag1903 §575d).

## Watch out

**Do not keep the indicative.** *\*Nesciō ubi est* follows English, Spanish *no sé dónde está* and French *je ne sais pas où il est*. Classical Latin requires *nesciō ubi sit* (ag1903 §574). The indicative is early, poetic or late Latin (§575c; the Vulgate's *nescīmus quō vādis*).

**Do not confuse an indirect question with a relative clause.** *Sciō quid fēcerit* "I know what he did" (indirect question: "what did he do?", subjunctive). *Sciō id quod fēcit* "I know the thing that he did" (relative, indicative). Ask whether there is an antecedent: a relative has one, an indirect question does not.

**Do not use the accusative and infinitive for a question.** *\*Rogāvit eum ubi esse* mixes two constructions. A question is reported with its question word and the subjunctive: *rogāvit ubi esset*.

**Do not use a present subjunctive for a future question when it could mislead.** *Nesciō quid faciat* is "I don't know what he is doing". For "what he will do", say *quid factūrus sit* (ag1903 §575a and note).

**Do not translate *num* as "surely not" here.** In an indirect question *num* is "whether" (ag1903 §332b note): *rogāvit num vēnisset* "he asked whether he had come".

**Do not use *sī* for "whether" in prose questions.** English "I asked if he was there" tempts *sī*. Latin uses *num* or *-ne*: *rogāvī num ibi esset*. *Sī* "whether" is limited to "to see if" after verbs of trying (ag1903 §576a).

**Do not apply the subjunctive to *nesciō quis*.** *Nesciō quis venit* "someone or other is coming", indicative, because *nesciō quis* is now one word for "someone" (ag1903 §575d). *Nesciō quis veniat* is the true question "I don't know who is coming".

**Do not forget the sequence.** *\*Rogāvit quid faciam* puts a primary tense after a secondary verb: *rogāvit quid facerem* (lesson 6; ag1903 §575).

## Connections

Spanish and French keep the question word and the indicative in reported questions: *no sé dónde está*, *je ne sais pas où il est*. Only Latin marks them with the subjunctive. Japanese turns a question into an embedded clause with *ka* (*doko ni iru ka shiranai* "I don't know where he is"), and Korean with *-ji* (*eodi inneunji molla*): the same question word, plus a special ending on the verb, which is close to what the Latin subjunctive does.

## Exercises

1. Make indirect after *nesciō*: *Quis venit?*; *Quid fēcit?*; *Quandō veniet?*
2. Make the same three indirect after *nescīvī*.
3. Say "he asked me why I had come".
4. Say "tell me where you live" (*dīc mihi*).
5. Say "I asked whether he was a Roman or a Greek".
6. Translate *Quaerit ex eō quid sentiat.*
7. Correct *\*Nesciō ubi est* and *\*Rogāvit quid faciam*.
8. Explain the difference between *Sciō quid dīxerit* and *Sciō id quod dīxit*.
9. Translate *Nesciō quis ad iānuam pulsat* and explain the mood.
10. In *Quid proximā, quid superiōre nocte ēgeris … quem nostrum ignōrāre arbitrāris?*, explain why *ēgeris* is perfect subjunctive.

## Worked answers

1. *Nesciō quis veniat*; *Nesciō quid fēcerit*; *Nesciō quandō ventūrus sit*. Primary sequence: present, perfect, and the future participle with *sit* for the future (ag1903 §575).
2. *Nescīvī quis venīret*; *nescīvī quid fēcisset*; *nescīvī quandō ventūrus esset*. Secondary sequence: imperfect, pluperfect, *-ūrus esset* (ag1903 §575).
3. *Mē rogāvit cūr vēnissem.* Secondary main verb, earlier action: pluperfect (lesson 6).
4. *Dīc mihi ubi habitēs.* The imperative is primary, so the present subjunctive (ag1903 §482).
5. *Rogāvī utrum Rōmānus an Graecus esset*, or *Rōmānusne an Graecus esset*. Alternative questions use *utrum … an* or *-ne … an* (ag1903 §335).
6. "He asks him what he thinks." *Quaerō ex* + ablative of the person asked; present subjunctive after the primary *quaerit*.
7. *Nesciō ubi sit*: indirect questions take the subjunctive (ag1903 §574). *Rogāvit quid facerem*: secondary sequence after *rogāvit* (lesson 6).
8. *Sciō quid dīxerit* "I know what he said": an indirect question ("what did he say?"), subjunctive. *Sciō id quod dīxit* "I know the thing he said": a relative clause with the antecedent *id*, indicative (lesson 2).
9. "Someone or other is knocking at the door." *Nesciō quis* is a fixed indefinite pronoun, so the verb is indicative (ag1903 §575d). *Pulsō* is from part 5.
10. The questions depend on *ignōrāre*, under the present *arbitrāris*, so the sequence is primary; the acts (doing, being, calling together, taking a decision) are past, so perfect subjunctive (ag1903 §§484a, 575).
