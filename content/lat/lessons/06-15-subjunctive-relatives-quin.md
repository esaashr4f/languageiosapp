## The idea

Lesson 2 taught relative clauses in the indicative: they state a fact about a particular antecedent. *Imperātor quī sē nōn continet* is "the general who (as a matter of fact) does not control himself". Put the verb in the subjunctive and the clause describes a *kind*: *imperātor quī sē nōn contineat* is "a general of the sort who does not control himself", "a general without self-control" (ag1903 §534). This is the **relative clause of characteristic**, and Allen and Greenough call it "a development peculiar to Latin" (§534).

They trace it to the potential subjunctive of part 4, lesson 19 (ag1903 §534): *quī sē nōn contineat* first meant "who would not control himself (in any case you might suppose)". From "would" it is a short step to "is the kind who". It is the same subjunctive that grew into the result clause (lesson 9): "I am not so dull *as to say this*" is a description of type turned into a consequence (§534).

The clause is most at home where the antecedent is vague or absent (ag1903 §535): *sunt quī* "there are people who", *nēmō est quī* "there is nobody who", *quis est quī?* "who is there who?", *nihil est quod* "there is nothing that", and with *sōlus*, *ūnus*, *dignus*, *idōneus*.

The second half of the lesson is two conjunctions built from the relative.

- ***Quīn*** was originally *quī* + *nē*, "how not?, why not?" (ag1903 §557). *Quīn ego hoc rogem?* "why shouldn't I ask this?" implies "there is no reason not to". When the negative was put into a main clause, "there is no doubt", "nothing prevents", *quīn* became the conjunction "that … not", "but that". It is used after negative expressions of doubting, preventing and refusing, and as *quī nōn* after general negatives (§§558-559).
- ***Quōminus*** is *quō minus* "by which the less" (ag1903 §557), the *quō* of purpose with a comparative (lesson 7). *Impedit quōminus* is "he prevents (so) that the less", "he prevents from". It is used after verbs of hindering, especially when they are not negative (§558b).

## How it is built

**Characteristic clauses.** Say "there are people who think death is the end".

1. The antecedent "people" is vague: *sunt quī*.
2. The clause describes the kind: subjunctive. Primary sequence: present.
3. *Sunt quī putent mortem fīnem esse.*

Say "there is nobody so old that he does not think he can live another year".

1. *Nēmō est tam senex quī …* (a characteristic clause after a negative; ag1903 §535a).
2. "Does not think": *nōn putet*.
3. *Nēmō est tam senex quī sē annum nōn putet posse vīvere.* (Cicero's sentence.)

**After *dignus*, *indignus*, *idōneus*, *aptus***, "worthy to", "fit to": a relative clause with the subjunctive, negative *nōn* (ag1903 §535f): *dignus est quī laudētur* "he deserves to be praised". Poets use the infinitive (§535f note 2).

**Cause and concession.** A characteristic clause can give a reason ("since he is one who…") or a concession ("although he is one who…") (ag1903 §535e). *Quippe quī*, *utpote quī*, *ut quī* make the reason explicit: *utpote quī* "inasmuch as he".

**Restriction.** *Quod sciam* "as far as I know", literally "as to what I know" (ag1903 §535d).

**Quīn after doubt.** *Nōn dubitō quīn* "I do not doubt that", with the subjunctive by sequence (ag1903 §558a). Say "there was no doubt that the Helvetii were the strongest":

1. *Nōn erat dubium*: a negative expression of doubt.
2. *Quīn* + subjunctive; secondary sequence: imperfect: *quīn plūrimum possent*.

Without a negative, *dubitō* takes an indirect question: *dubitō an veniat* "I wonder whether he is coming" (§558a note 1). And *nōn dubitō* + infinitive is "I do not hesitate to" (§558a note 2).

**Quīn after preventing and refusing, negatived.** *Nōn recūsat quīn* "he does not refuse to"; *fierī nōn potest quīn* "it cannot be that … not"; *facere nōn possum quīn* "I cannot help"; *paulum āfuit quīn* "he came close to" (ag1903 §558).

**Quīn = *quī nōn*** after a general negative: *nēmō est quīn sciat* "there is no one who does not know"; *nēmō est tam fortis quīn perturbētur* "no one is so brave as not to be shaken" (ag1903 §559).

**Quōminus and *nē* after hindering.** *Impediō*, *obstō*, *dēterreō*, *prohibeō*, *recūsō*, especially when not negatived, take *quōminus* or *nē* + subjunctive (ag1903 §558b): *tuae lacrimae mē impediunt nē plūra dīcam* "your tears prevent me from saying more". *Prohibeō* also takes the infinitive (§558b note).

## Where it applies

**Characteristic clauses** (ag1903 §535)

- **A vague or absent antecedent**: *sunt quī*, *erant quī*, *nēmō est quī*, *quis est quī?*, *nihil est quod*, *nihil videō quod timeam* "I see nothing to be afraid of" (Cicero's letters, quoted in §535a). After *nēmō est quī* the subjunctive is regular; after *sunt quī* usual; after *multī sunt quī* it depends on the sense (§535a note 1).
- ***Est cum*** "there is a time when" works the same way (§535a note 3).
- **After *ūnus*, *sōlus***: "the only one who" (§535b).
- **After a comparative with *quam quī*, *quam ut***: "too … to": *maiōrēs arborēs quam quās ferre mīles posset* "trees too big for a soldier to carry" (Livy, quoted in §535c).
- **Restriction**: *quod sciam* "as far as I know"; *quī modo* "at least any who" (§535d).
- **Cause or concession**, often with *quippe*, *utpote*, *ut* (§535e).
- **After *dignus*, *indignus*, *idōneus*, *aptus*** (§535f).
- **Relative purpose and result** are close relatives (lessons 7 and 9; ag1903 §537 note 1).

**Quīn** (ag1903 §§558-559)

- After **negative doubt**: *nōn dubitō*, *nōn est dubium*, *quis dubitat?* "who doubts?", *cavē dubitēs*.
- After **negative preventing, refusing, delaying**: *nōn recūsō*, *nōn impediō*, *nūllum tempus intermīsērunt quīn* "they let no time pass without" (Caesar, quoted in §558).
- After ***facere nōn possum*** "I cannot help", ***fierī nōn potest*** "it is impossible that … not", ***paulum*** or ***nōn multum āfuit*** "very nearly".
- As ***quī nōn***, ***quod nōn*** after general negatives, for result or characteristic (§559).

**Quōminus and *nē*** (ag1903 §558b): after *impediō*, *obstō*, *dēterreō*, *prohibeō*, *recūsō*, *teneō* "hold back", especially when not negatived; after negatives too, as an alternative to *quīn*.

## Watch out

**Do not put every relative clause into the subjunctive.** *Mīles quī fūgit* "the soldier who ran away" names a particular person: indicative. The subjunctive is for a type or a vague antecedent. If you can say "the sort of person who", it is characteristic; if you mean one known person, it is not.

**Do not use the indicative after *nēmō est quī*.** *\*Nēmō est quī hoc nescit* follows English "there is nobody who doesn't know". Latin: *nēmō est quī hoc nesciat*, or *nēmō est quīn hoc sciat* (ag1903 §§535a, 559).

**Do not use the infinitive after *dignus* in prose.** *\*Dignus est laudārī* is poetic (ag1903 §535f note 2). In prose: *dignus est quī laudētur*.

**Do not use *quīn* after a positive "doubt".** *\*Dubitō quīn veniat* is wrong. Without a negative, *dubitō* takes an indirect question: *dubitō num veniat* or *an veniat* (ag1903 §558a note 1).

**Do not translate *nōn dubitō* + infinitive as "I do not doubt".** It means "I do not hesitate": *nōn dubitō dīcere* "I do not hesitate to say" (ag1903 §558a note 2).

**Do not add a second negative after *quīn*.** *Quīn* already contains the negative *nē*: *nēmō est quīn sciat* is "no one does not know". *\*Nēmō est quīn nōn sciat* doubles it.

**Do not use *ut* after verbs of preventing.** English "prevent them from" or Spanish *impedir que* tempts *\*impedit ut*. Latin uses *quōminus* or *nē* (*quīn* if the verb is negative): *impedit quōminus veniant* (ag1903 §558b).

**Do not mistake *quīn* "why not?" for the conjunction.** In a main clause, *quīn* with the indicative is a question or an exhortation, "why not?", "indeed" (ag1903 §§449b, 557). The conjunction follows a negative main clause and takes the subjunctive.

**Do not confuse *quō minus* with *quō* + comparative in purpose.** They are the same words and the same origin (ag1903 §557), but *quōminus* after a verb of hindering means "from" and has no comparison left in it.

## Connections

Spanish and French mark the same distinction with the subjunctive in relative clauses: *busco a alguien que hable latín* "I'm looking for someone (anyone) who speaks Latin" against *conozco a alguien que habla latín* "I know someone who does"; *il n'y a personne qui sache* "there is no one who knows". This is the Latin characteristic clause, inherited in spirit. French *ne* after verbs of preventing and fearing (*empêcher qu'il ne vienne*) parallels Latin *nē* and *quōminus*.

## Exercises

1. Say "there are some who praise Caesar" and "there is nobody who does not praise Caesar" (two ways).
2. Say "he is worthy to be consul" (*dignus quī*).
3. Say "who is there who does not know this?".
4. Say "I do not doubt that you are right" (*rēctē sentīre*, or *vērum dīcere*).
5. Say "nothing prevents us from leaving" with *quōminus*.
6. Translate *Nōn recūsāvit quīn venīret* and *Paulum āfuit quīn caperētur*.
7. Explain the difference between *Mīles quī fūgit* and *Mīles quī fugiat*.
8. Correct *\*Nēmō est quī hoc nescit* and *\*Dubitō quīn veniat* (meant as "I wonder whether he is coming").
9. Correct *\*Impedīvit ut urbem dīriperent* and *\*Nēmō est quīn nōn sciat*.
10. Translate *Nēmō est tam fortis quīn reī novitāte perturbētur* and explain *quīn*.

## Worked answers

1. *Sunt quī Caesarem laudent*: characteristic clause after *sunt quī* (ag1903 §535a). *Nēmō est quī Caesarem nōn laudet*, or *nēmō est quīn Caesarem laudet* (§559).
2. *Dignus est quī cōnsul sit* (or *fīat*). *Dignus* + relative + subjunctive (ag1903 §535f).
3. *Quis est quī hoc nesciat?* A question implying "no one": characteristic subjunctive (ag1903 §535a).
4. *Nōn dubitō quīn vērum dīcās.* *Quīn* after negative doubt, present subjunctive by primary sequence (ag1903 §558a).
5. *Nihil impedit quōminus discēdāmus.* *Quōminus* after a verb of hindering (ag1903 §558b).
6. "He did not refuse to come": *quīn* after a negatived verb of refusing (ag1903 §558). "He was very nearly caught": *paulum āfuit quīn* (§558).
7. *Mīles quī fūgit*: "the soldier who ran away", a particular man and a fact (lesson 2). *Mīles quī fugiat*: "a soldier who would run away", a type (ag1903 §534).
8. *Nēmō est quī hoc nesciat*: after *nēmō est quī* the subjunctive is regular (ag1903 §535a note 1). *Dubitō an veniat* (or *num veniat*): a positive "doubt" takes an indirect question, not *quīn* (§558a note 1).
9. *Impedīvit quōminus* (or *nē*) *urbem dīriperent*: verbs of hindering do not take *ut* (ag1903 §558b). *Nēmō est quīn sciat*: *quīn* already contains the negative (§559).
10. "No one is so brave as not to be shaken by something unexpected." *Quīn* = *quī nōn* after the general negative *nēmō … tam*, a clause of result (ag1903 §559).
