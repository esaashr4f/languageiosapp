## Before you start

This review covers all of part 6, weighted towards its second half: question words (lesson 1), relative clauses (lesson 2), time and cause with the indicative (lesson 3), the ablative absolute (lesson 4), reported statements (lesson 5), the sequence of tenses (lesson 6), purpose (lesson 7), result (lesson 9), indirect commands and fears (lesson 10), indirect questions (lesson 11), *cum* clauses (lesson 12), conditions (lesson 13), concession, proviso and comparison (lesson 14), and subjunctive relatives with *quīn* and *quōminus* (lesson 15). The midway review (lesson 8) covered lessons 1 to 7 in detail.

Work each exercise before reading its answer. Every answer names the lesson to reread if you went wrong.

## Passage

**A. The besieged town.** A constructed passage, using only words and grammar taught so far.

1. Cum hostēs oppidum oppugnārent, cīvēs timēbant nē urbs caperētur.
2. Tanta erat inopia frūmentī ut nēmō esset quī nōn famē labōrāret.
3. Dux cīvitātis, vir summā auctōritāte, cīvēs hortātus est ut fortiter resisterent.
4. "Sī auxilium ā Rōmānīs petīverimus," inquit, "nōn dubitō quīn nōs servātūrī sint. Quis enim est tam dēmēns quī amīcōs dēserat?"
5. Lēgātōs igitur mīsit quī Caesarem rogārent quandō ventūrus esset.
6. Quamquam via plēna perīculōrum erat, lēgātī ad castra pervēnērunt.
7. Caesar, cum audīvisset quantō in perīculō cīvēs essent, statim profectus est, nē oppidum dēlērētur.
8. Sī tardius vēnisset, nihil oppidum servāre potuisset.

**B. Orgetorix's plan (Caesar, Gallic War 1.2.1-2).** Attested; wording from The Latin Library, in this guide's spelling (the edition spells the consul's name *Messala*). New words, glossed here: *Mārcus Messāla* and *Mārcus Pīsō*, the consuls of 61 BC; *perfacilis* "very easy"; *praestō* "excel" (+ dat.).

1. *Is, Mārcō Messālā et Mārcō Pīsōne cōnsulibus, rēgnī cupiditāte inductus coniūrātiōnem nōbilitātis fēcit,*
2. *et cīvitātī persuāsit ut dē fīnibus suīs cum omnibus cōpiīs exīrent:*
3. *perfacile esse, cum virtūte omnibus praestārent, tōtīus Galliae imperiō potīrī.*

**C. Cicero to Catiline (Against Catiline 1.10, shortened).** Attested. New words, glossed here: *ēgredior* "go out"; *aliquandō* "at last" (part 3); *ēdūcō* "lead out"; *pūrgō* "cleanse".

1. *Quae cum ita sint, Catilīna, perge quō coepistī; ēgredere aliquandō ex urbe; patent portae; proficīscere.*
2. *Ēdūc tēcum etiam omnēs tuōs; pūrgā urbem.*
3. *Magnō mē metū līberābis, dummodo inter mē atque tē mūrus intersit.*

## Exercises

1. Translate passage A, sentences 1 to 4.
2. Translate passage A, sentences 5 to 8.
3. In A1, explain *cum … oppugnārent* and *nē urbs caperētur*.
4. In A2, explain *tanta … ut* and *nēmō esset quī nōn … labōrāret*.
5. In A3, explain *ut fortiter resisterent* and say why it is not a purpose clause.
6. In A4, name the type of condition, and explain *quīn … servātūrī sint* and *quī amīcōs dēserat*.
7. In A5, explain *quī Caesarem rogārent* and *quandō ventūrus esset*.
8. In A6 and A7, explain the moods after *quamquam*, *cum audīvisset*, *quantō in perīculō … essent* and *nē … dēlērētur*.
9. In A8, name the type of condition and explain the tenses.
10. Translate passage B.
11. In B1, explain *Mārcō Messālā et Mārcō Pīsōne cōnsulibus* and *inductus*.
12. In B2, explain *persuāsit ut … exīrent*, and why the verb is plural though *cīvitātī* is singular.
13. In B3, explain the construction of *perfacile esse … potīrī* and of *cum … praestārent*.
14. Translate passage C.

## Worked answers

1. "While the enemy were attacking the town, the citizens were afraid that the city would be captured. The shortage of grain was so great that there was nobody who was not suffering from hunger. The leader of the state, a man of the greatest authority, urged the citizens to resist bravely. 'If we ask the Romans for help,' he said, 'I have no doubt that they will save us. For who is so mad as to desert his friends?'"
2. "So he sent envoys to ask Caesar when he would come. Although the road was full of dangers, the envoys reached the camp. Caesar, when he had heard in what great danger the citizens were, set out at once, so that the town should not be destroyed. If he had come more slowly, he could not have saved the town at all."
3. *Cum … oppugnārent*: narrative *cum* with the imperfect subjunctive for the background (lesson 12; ag1903 §546). *Nē urbs caperētur*: a fear clause, *nē* "that", imperfect after the secondary *timēbant* (lessons 10 and 6; ag1903 §564).
4. *Tanta … ut*: a result clause announced by *tantus* (lesson 9; ag1903 §537). *Nēmō esset quī nōn … labōrāret*: the result clause contains a characteristic clause after a general negative, "nobody who was not…" (lesson 15; ag1903 §535a); it could also be *nēmō esset quīn … labōrāret* (§559). *Famē* is an ablative of cause (part 5, lesson 8).
5. An indirect command: the *ut* clause is the object of *hortātus est*, saying what they were urged to do (lesson 10; ag1903 §563). In a purpose clause the *ut* clause would say why he urged them, not what he urged.
6. A future more vivid condition: future perfect *petīverimus* in the protasis (lesson 13; ag1903 §516c); the apodosis is *nōn dubitō …*. *Quīn … servātūrī sint*: *quīn* after negative doubt, with the future participle and *sint* to show the future (lessons 15 and 11; ag1903 §§558a, 575a). *Quī amīcōs dēserat*: characteristic clause after *quis est tam dēmēns*, a question implying "nobody" (lesson 15; ag1903 §535a).
7. *Quī Caesarem rogārent*: a relative clause of purpose, *quī* = *ut eī*, imperfect after *mīsit* (lesson 7; ag1903 §531.2). *Quandō ventūrus esset*: an indirect question about the future, future participle with *esset* by secondary sequence (lesson 11; ag1903 §575a).
8. *Quamquam … erat*: indicative, a conceded fact (lesson 14; ag1903 §527d). *Cum audīvisset*: narrative *cum*, pluperfect subjunctive for the earlier step (lesson 12). *Quantō in perīculō … essent*: an indirect question after *audīvisset*, imperfect by secondary sequence (lesson 11). *Nē … dēlērētur*: a negative purpose clause, imperfect after *profectus est* (lesson 7).
9. Past contrary to fact: pluperfect subjunctive in both clauses, *vēnisset … potuisset* (lesson 13; ag1903 §517). *Tardius* is the comparative adverb (part 5, lesson 4).
10. "In the consulship of Marcus Messala and Marcus Piso, he was led by desire for kingship to form a conspiracy of the nobility, and persuaded the state to leave its territory with all its forces: it would be very easy, he said, since they surpassed everyone in courage, to gain control of the whole of Gaul."
11. *Mārcō Messālā et Mārcō Pīsōne cōnsulibus*: an ablative absolute of nouns without a participle, the Roman way of dating a year (lesson 4; ag1903 §419a). *Inductus*: perfect participle of *indūcō*, agreeing with *is*, "induced, led on" (lesson 4 vocabulary; part 5, lesson 7).
12. An indirect command after *persuādeō*, which takes the dative *cīvitātī* (lesson 10; ag1903 §563). *Exīrent* is plural because *cīvitās* means the people of the state: agreement by sense. Imperfect after the perfect *persuāsit* (lesson 6).
13. *Perfacile esse … potīrī*: a reported statement with no verb of saying expressed; the context of persuading implies "he said" (lesson 5; ag1903 §580a). The infinitive *potīrī* is the subject of *esse*, and *perfacile* agrees with it. *Cum … praestārent*: causal *cum*, inside reported speech and so subjunctive in any case; imperfect by secondary sequence (lessons 12 and 6).
14. "Since this is so, Catiline, carry on where you started; leave the city at last; the gates are open; set out. Take all your men with you too; cleanse the city. You will free me from a great fear, provided only that there is a wall between you and me."
15. *Quae cum ita sint*: a connecting relative inside a causal *cum* clause, "since these things are so" (lessons 2 and 12). *Perge*, *pūrgā*: ordinary imperatives; *ēgredere*, *proficīscere*: imperatives of deponents (part 4, lesson 13); *ēdūc* has the short imperative like *dūc* (Wiktionary).
16. *Magnō metū*: ablative of separation with *līberābis*, "free from great fear" (part 5, lesson 8). *Dummodo … intersit*: a proviso with the present subjunctive by primary sequence after the future *līberābis* (lesson 14; ag1903 §528).
17. *Eum rogāvī cūr nōn vēnisset et num crās ventūrus esset.* Two indirect questions after a secondary verb: pluperfect for the earlier event, *-ūrus esset* for the future (lesson 11; ag1903 §575).
18. *Mīlitēs tam fessī erant ut pugnāre nōn possent, quamquam dux eōs hortābātur* (or *cum dux eōs hortārētur*). Result with *ut nōn* (lesson 9); concession with *quamquam* + indicative or *cum* + subjunctive (lessons 14 and 12).
19. *Sī in urbe fuissēs, vīdissēs quid accidisset.* Past contrary to fact (lesson 13); an indirect question in the pluperfect after a secondary verb (lesson 11).
20. *Nēmō dubitābat quīn Caesar victūrus esset.* A general negative with doubt takes *quīn*; the future participle with *esset* shows the future (lessons 15 and 11).
21. *Loquitur quasi omnia sciat.* *Quasi* + subjunctive, keeping the primary sequence (lesson 14; ag1903 §524).
22. *Timeō nē hostēs veniant.* After a verb of fearing, *nē* is "that" and *ut* "that … not" (lesson 10; ag1903 §564).
23. *Tanta tempestās erat ut nāvēs nāvigāre nōn possent*: a negative result takes *ut nōn*, not *nē* (lesson 9). *Imperāvit mīlitibus ut pugnārent*: *imperō* takes the dative (lesson 10; part 3, lesson 3).
24. *Nesciō quid faciat*: indirect questions take the subjunctive (lesson 11). *Sī hīc fuissēs, eum vīdissēs*: a past unreal condition takes the pluperfect subjunctive in both clauses (lesson 13).
25. *Nēmō est quī hoc nōn intellegat* (or *quīn hoc intellegat*): characteristic clause after *nēmō est quī* (lesson 15). *Quamquam fessus erat, tamen labōrāvit*: *quamquam* takes the indicative in classical prose (lesson 14).
