## Before you start

This review covers lessons 1 to 6 of part 7: the gerund (lesson 1), the gerundive of obligation and the gerundive in place of the gerund (lesson 2), the supine (lesson 3), impersonal verbs and the impersonal passive (lesson 4), extended reported speech (lesson 5), and word order as style (lesson 6). It also draws on part 6, especially reported statements, the sequence of tenses, purpose and conditions.

Work each exercise before reading its answer. Every answer names the lesson to reread if you went wrong.

## Passage

**A. Caesar surprised by the Nervii (Caesar, Gallic War 2.20.1, 2.21.1).** Attested; wording from The Latin Library, in this guide's spelling. New words, glossed here: *paulō longius* "a little too far"; *brevitās* "shortness"; *incursus* "onset, attack"; *necessārius* "necessary"; *fors* "chance"; *dēcucurrit* "ran down", perfect of *dēcurrō* (Caesar's text has the reduplicated form). In A4 The Latin Library prints *quam [in] partem*, marking *in* as doubtful, and the sentence goes on *et ad legiōnem decimam dēvēnit* "and reached the tenth legion".

1. *Caesarī omnia ūnō tempore erant agenda: vēxillum prōpōnendum, quod erat īnsigne, cum ad arma concurrī oportēret; signum tubā dandum; ab opere revocandī mīlitēs;*
2. *quī paulō longius aggeris petendī causā prōcesserant arcessendī; aciēs īnstruenda; mīlitēs cohortandī; signum dandum.*
3. *Quārum rērum magnam partem temporis brevitās et incursus hostium impediēbat.*
4. *Caesar, necessāriīs rēbus imperātīs, ad cohortandōs mīlitēs, quam in partem fors obtulit, dēcucurrit …*

**B. Envoys to Caesar.** Constructed, using only words and grammar taught so far.

1. Lēgātī ad Caesarem vēnērunt pācem petītum.
2. Dīxērunt sē bellī gerendī nōn esse cupidōs; sibi domum redeundum esse.
3. Sī Caesar sibi parceret, obsidēs sē datūrōs.
4. Nē sē dēspiceret: nōn enim fugiendī causā sed pācis petendae vēnisse. Quid sibi faciendum esset?
5. Caesar respondit sibi nōn licēre eīs ignōscere quī tot iniūriās fēcissent; eōs factōrum suōrum paenitēre oportēre.
6. Tum, cum diū dēlīberātum esset, obsidēs accipiendōs esse dīxit.

## Exercises

1. Translate passage A.
2. In A1, explain *Caesarī … erant agenda* and why *vēxillum prōpōnendum* has no verb.
3. In A1, explain *concurrī oportēret*.
4. In A2, explain *aggeris petendī causā* and *arcessendī*. What is the subject of *arcessendī (erant)*?
5. In A4, explain *ad cohortandōs mīlitēs* and say why it is not *ad cohortandum mīlitēs*.
6. In A4, find the frame of the period and the parts inside it.
7. Translate passage B.
8. In B1, explain *petītum*, and give two other ways of saying the same purpose.
9. In B2, explain *bellī gerendī* and *sibi … redeundum esse*.
10. In B3 and B4, name the constructions of *parceret … datūrōs*, *nē … dēspiceret* and *quid … faciendum esset?*
11. In B5, explain *sibi nōn licēre*, *quī … fēcissent* and *eōs … paenitēre oportēre*.
12. In B6, explain *dēlīberātum esset* and *accipiendōs esse*.
13. Put B4 back into direct speech, as the envoys said it to Caesar.
14. Say "there is no time for writing letters" with a gerund.
15. Say "the city must be defended by the citizens".
16. Say "they came to Rome to ask for help" with the supine, and then with *ad* and the gerundive.
17. Say "I am ashamed of my words".
18. Say "the general said that the soldiers must not be afraid; the enemy were few".
19. Correct *\*Ad capiendum urbem vēnērunt* and *\*Mihi epistulam scrībendam est*.
20. Correct *\*Ego oportet hoc facere* and *\*Mē pudet hanc rem* (meant as "I am ashamed of this thing").
21. Correct *\*Spērat urbem captam īrī* and *\*Vēnērunt ad spectātum*.
22. Correct *\*Lēgēs pārendae sunt* and *\*Ācriter pugnātī sunt* (meant as "there was fierce fighting").
23. Explain the order of *Magnō cum metū hostēs fūgērunt* and of *Quōs āmīsimus cīvīs, eōs Mārtis vīs perculit*.
24. Explain the forms *mīrābile dictū* and *maior nātū*.

## Worked answers

1. "Caesar had to do everything at the same time: the flag had to be raised, which was the signal that men must run to arms; the trumpet signal had to be given; the soldiers had to be recalled from the work; those who had gone a little too far to fetch material for the rampart had to be sent for; the battle line had to be drawn up; the soldiers had to be encouraged; the signal had to be given. A great part of these things was prevented by the shortness of the time and the onset of the enemy. Caesar, after giving the necessary orders, ran down to encourage the soldiers, to whichever part chance brought him."
2. The gerundive of obligation with *erant*, and the dative of the agent *Caesarī*: "Caesar had to do" (lesson 2; ag1903 §374). In the list *erat* and *erant* are understood; each gerundive agrees with its own subject (*vēxillum prōpōnendum*, neuter singular).
3. An impersonal passive infinitive of the intransitive *concurrō*, "that there be a running together", as the subject of the impersonal *oportet*; imperfect subjunctive in a *cum* clause by secondary sequence (lesson 4; part 6, lesson 12).
4. *Aggeris petendī causā*: the gerundive in place of a gerund with an object, in the genitive with *causā*, for purpose (lesson 2; ag1903 §504b). *Arcessendī (erant)*: a gerundive of obligation; its subject is the antecedent of *quī*, "those who had gone forward" (understood *eī*), masculine plural.
5. *Ad* + the gerundive agreeing with *mīlitēs*, for purpose (lesson 2). A gerund after a preposition does not take a direct object in classical Latin, so the noun is made the head (ag1903 §§503, 506 note 2).
6. Frame: *Caesar … dēcucurrit*. Inside: the ablative absolute *necessāriīs rēbus imperātīs*; the purpose phrase *ad cohortandōs mīlitēs*; the relative clause *quam in partem fors obtulit*, with the preposition inside the phrase (*quam in partem*) (lesson 6; ag1903 §§599d, 601).
7. "Envoys came to Caesar to ask for peace. They said that they were not eager to wage war; they had to return home. If Caesar spared them, they would give hostages. He should not despise them, for they had not come in order to flee but to seek peace. What were they to do? Caesar replied that he was not allowed to pardon men who had done so many wrongs; they ought to regret their deeds. Then, after long deliberation, he said that hostages were to be accepted."
8. *Petītum*: the *-um* supine after a verb of motion, with its own object *pācem* (lesson 3; ag1903 §509). Also *ad pācem petendam* (lesson 2) and *ut pācem peterent* (part 6, lesson 7).
9. *Bellī gerendī*: the gerundive in the genitive after the adjective *cupidōs* (lesson 2; ag1903 §504). *Sibi … redeundum esse*: an impersonal gerundive of obligation of the intransitive *redeō*, with the dative of the agent, as an infinitive in reported speech (lessons 2 and 5).
10. *Sī … parceret … datūrōs (esse)*: a future condition in reported speech, the protasis subjunctive by secondary sequence and the apodosis a future infinitive (lesson 5; ag1903 §589). *Nē … dēspiceret*: a prohibition in reported speech (§588 note 2). *Quid … faciendum esset?*: a real question, so subjunctive (§586), containing a gerundive of obligation.
11. *Sibi nōn licēre*: *licet* with the dative of the person, as an infinitive in reported speech (lessons 4 and 5). *Quī … fēcissent*: a relative clause inside reported speech, so subjunctive, pluperfect by secondary sequence (lesson 5). *Eōs … paenitēre oportēre*: *oportet* with an accusative and infinitive, the infinitive itself the impersonal *paenitet* with the accusative of the person and the genitive of the cause *factōrum suōrum* (lesson 4; ag1903 §354b).
12. *Dēlīberātum esset*: an impersonal pluperfect passive subjunctive, "when there had been deliberation", in a narrative *cum* clause (lesson 4; part 6, lesson 12). *Accipiendōs esse*: a gerundive of obligation as an infinitive in a reported statement, agreeing with *obsidēs* (lesson 2).
13. *Nōlī nōs dēspicere: nōn enim fugiendī causā sed pācis petendae vēnimus. Quid nōbīs faciendum est?* The prohibition becomes *nōlī* + infinitive; the reported statement becomes an indicative; *sē* "we" becomes *nōs*; the real question becomes indicative (lesson 5; ag1903 §§586, 588).
14. *Nōn est tempus epistulās scrībendī* or, better, *epistulārum scrībendārum*. The gerund with an object is possible in the genitive, but the gerundive is commoner (lessons 1 and 2; ag1903 §503 note 1).
15. *Urbs cīvibus dēfendenda est.* The gerundive agrees with the subject *urbs*; the agent is dative (lesson 2).
16. *Rōmam vēnērunt auxilium rogātum*; *Rōmam vēnērunt ad auxilium petendum* (or *rogandum*). No preposition with *Rōmam* or the supine; *ad* with the gerundive agreeing with *auxilium* (lessons 2 and 3).
17. *Mē verbōrum meōrum pudet.* Accusative of the person, genitive of the cause (lesson 4).
18. *Dux dīxit nē mīlitēs timērent; hostēs paucōs esse.* A prohibition in reported speech with *nē* + subjunctive, then a statement as accusative and infinitive (lesson 5).
19. *Ad urbem capiendam vēnērunt*: the gerundive agrees with the object (lesson 2). *Mihi epistula scrībenda est*: the letter is the subject (lesson 2).
20. *Mē oportet hoc facere* (or *hoc faciam oportet*): *oportet* takes the accusative (lesson 4). *Mē huius reī pudet*: the cause of the feeling is genitive, not accusative (lesson 4; ag1903 §354b).
21. *Spērat urbem captum īrī*: the supine never agrees (lesson 3). *Vēnērunt spectātum*, or *vēnērunt ad spectandum*: the supine takes no preposition; *ad* takes the gerund (lesson 3).
22. *Lēgibus pārendum est*: *pāreō* takes the dative, so the gerundive is impersonal (lesson 2). *Ācriter pugnātum est*: the impersonal passive is neuter singular (lesson 4).
23. *Magnō cum metū*: a one-syllable preposition between an emphatic adjective and its noun (lesson 6; ag1903 §599d). *Quōs āmīsimus cīvīs, eōs …*: the relative clause comes first and contains its antecedent, picked up by *eōs* (lesson 6; §599e).
24. *Dictū*: the *-ū* supine with an adjective, "wonderful in the telling" (lesson 3; ag1903 §510). *Nātū*: the same form, "greater by birth", that is, "older" (lesson 3).
