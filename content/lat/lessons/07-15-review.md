## Before you start

This review covers all of part 7, weighted towards its second half: the gerund (lesson 1), the gerundive (lesson 2), the supine (lesson 3), impersonal verbs (lesson 4), extended reported speech (lesson 5), word order (lesson 6), the hexameter and elision (lesson 8), the elegiac couplet and poetic forms (lesson 9), word formation (lesson 10), names, dates and numerals (lesson 11), early and colloquial Latin (lesson 12), late and medieval Latin (lesson 13), and inscriptions and manuscripts (lesson 14). The midway review (lesson 7) covered lessons 1 to 6 in detail.

Work each exercise before reading its answer. Every answer names the lesson to reread if you went wrong.

## Passage

**A. Caesar plays for time (Caesar, Gallic War 1.7.3–5).** Attested; wording from The Latin Library, in this guide's spelling. The Helvetii have asked leave to march through the Roman province. New words, glossed here: *temperō* "hold back, refrain"; *intercēdō* "come between, pass"; *revertor* "return".

1. *Caesar, quod memoriā tenēbat L. Cassium cōnsulem occīsum exercitumque eius ab Helvētiīs pulsum et sub iugum missum, concēdendum nōn putābat;*
2. *neque hominēs inimīcō animō, datā facultāte per prōvinciam itineris faciundī, temperātūrōs ab iniūriā et maleficiō exīstimābat.*
3. *Tamen, ut spatium intercēdere posset dum mīlitēs quōs imperāverat convenīrent, lēgātīs respondit diem sē ad dēlīberandum sūmptūrum:*
4. *sī quid vellent, ad Īd. Aprīl. reverterentur.*

**B. Catullus at his brother's grave (Catullus 101.9–10).** Attested. New words, glossed here: *mānō* "flow, drip"; *flētus* "weeping"; *avē* "hail"; *valē* "farewell".

1. *Accipe frāternō multum mānantia flētū,*
2. *atque in perpetuum, frāter, avē atque valē.*

**C. A letter from the frontier.** Constructed, using only words and grammar taught so far.

1. Mārcus Lūciō frātrī salūtem dīcit.
2. Ad hīberna vēnimus Kalendīs Novembribus; nōbīs diū ambulandum erat.
3. Hīc nōn licet nisi ōrdine īre, et dux dīcit omnia sibi parāta esse oportēre.
4. Mē nōn paenitet labōris, sed patriae et tuī saepe mē miseret.
5. Hodiē lēgātī hostium vēnērunt pācem petītum: dīxērunt sē bellī gerendī causā nōn vēnisse; quid sibi faciendum esset?
6. Dux respondit, sī obsidēs darent, sē eīs parsūrum; nē sē fallī posse putārent.
7. Haec scrībendō tempus terō. Valē.

## Worked answers

1. "Caesar, because he remembered that the consul Lucius Cassius had been killed and his army routed by the Helvetii and sent under the yoke, did not think he should grant the request; nor did he believe that men of hostile mind, if given the chance of making a march through the province, would refrain from wrongdoing and harm. Still, so that time might pass until the soldiers he had ordered could assemble, he replied to the envoys that he would take a day to think it over: if they wanted anything, they should come back on 13 April."
2. An impersonal gerundive of obligation in a reported statement, *concēdendum (esse)* "that it should be granted", with *esse* omitted (lesson 2; part 6, lesson 5).
3. *Datā facultāte*: an ablative absolute, "if the opportunity were given" (part 6). *Itineris faciundī*: the gerundive with the older *-und-* form, in the genitive after *facultāte* (lessons 2 and 12; ag1903 §179c). *Temperātūrōs (esse)*: the future infinitive in a reported statement after *exīstimābat* (part 6, lesson 5).
4. *Ad dēlīberandum*: *ad* + the gerund for purpose (lesson 1). *Sē … sūmptūrum (esse)*: a reported statement, *sē* for the speaker, future infinitive (lesson 5).
5. A command in reported speech, imperfect subjunctive after a past verb (lesson 5; ag1903 §588). *Īd. Aprīl.* = *Īdūs Aprīlēs*, 13 April (lesson 11).
6. "Receive these, wet with a brother's tears; and for ever, brother, hail and farewell."
7. B1: *ac·ci·pe | frā·ter | nō·mul | tum·mā | nan·ti·a | flē·tū*: — ∪ ∪ | — — | — — | — — | — ∪ ∪ | — —, caesura after *frāternō* (lesson 8). B2, with *atqu' in* and *av' atque* elided: *at·quin | per·pe·tu | um ‖ frā·te·ra | vat·que·va | lē*: — — | — ∪ ∪ | — ‖ — ∪ ∪ | — ∪ ∪ | — (lesson 9; ag1903 §612e).
8. *Frāternō … flētū* frames the line (lesson 6). *Mānantia* is a present participle, neuter plural, "dripping", agreeing with *haec* in the line before (part 5).
9. "Marcus to his brother Lucius, greetings. We reached winter quarters on 1 November; we had a long march. Here you may not go except in formation, and the general says everything must be ready for him. I do not regret the work, but I often feel pity for my country and for you. Today enemy envoys came to ask for peace; they said they had not come to make war; what were they to do? The general replied that if they gave hostages he would spare them; they were not to think he could be deceived. I am wasting my time writing this. Goodbye."
10. *Kalendīs Novembribus*: ablative of time with the month adjective agreeing, 1 November (lesson 11). *Nōbīs … ambulandum erat*: an impersonal gerundive of obligation of an intransitive verb, with the dative of the agent (lesson 2).
11. *Licet … īre*: the infinitive as subject of the impersonal *licet*; no person is named, so it is general (lesson 4). *Omnia sibi parāta esse oportēre*: a reported statement whose infinitive is the impersonal *oportet*, which itself takes an accusative and infinitive (lessons 4 and 5).
12. *Paenitet* and *miseret* take the person in the accusative (*mē*) and the cause in the genitive (*labōris*, *patriae*, *tuī*) (lesson 4; ag1903 §354b).
13. *Petītum*: the *-um* supine of purpose after a verb of motion (lesson 3). *Bellī gerendī causā*: the gerundive in the genitive with *causā* (lesson 2). *Quid sibi faciendum esset*: a real question in reported speech, so subjunctive, with a gerundive of obligation and *sibi* for the speakers (lessons 2 and 5).
14. A future condition in reported speech: protasis subjunctive, apodosis future infinitive (lesson 5; ag1903 §589). *Nē … putārent*: a prohibition in reported speech (§588 note 2). Direct: *Sī obsidēs dabitis, vōbīs parcam; nōlīte putāre mē fallī posse.*
15. The ablative of the gerund, "by writing", "in writing" (lesson 1). In medieval Latin it could also be read "while writing" (lesson 13).
16. *Sit*, *tē*, *afferrī*: Early Latin, in Plautus and old inscriptions (lesson 12). *Grātia*, *Ecclēsiae*: medieval spellings (lesson 13).
17. For example *D. M. Iūliae … V. A. XXV … F. C.*: *Dīs Mānibus. Iūliae … vīxit annōs XXV … faciendum cūrāvit*: "To the spirits of the dead. To Julia …; she lived 25 years; … had this made" (lesson 14).
18. *Audī-tor*: the supine stem of *audiō* + *-tor* "one who": "listener". *Perīcul-ōsus*: *perīculum* + *-ōsus* "full of": "dangerous". *Senē-scō*: *senex* + inceptive *-scō*: "grow old" (lesson 10).
19. 1776 and 49; 2024 is *MMXXIV* (lesson 11).
20. *Ad urbem videndam vēnērunt* (gerundive agreeing with the object, lesson 2). *Mihi liber legendus est* (the book is the subject, lesson 2).
21. *Mē pudet* (accusative of the person, lesson 4). *Ācriter pugnātum est* (impersonal passive, neuter singular, lesson 4).
22. *Dīxit venīrent* (or *iussit eōs venīre*): commands become subjunctive (lesson 5). *Urbem captum īrī spērat*: the supine does not agree (lesson 3).
23. It is a perfect, *tenuērunt*, with the poetic ending *-ēre* on the perfect stem *tenu-* (lesson 9; ag1903 §163a).
24. The Romans counted both the day they started from and the day they reached (bennett1918 §371.4; lesson 11).
