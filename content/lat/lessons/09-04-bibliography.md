## How the sources were used

Each entry gives the full reference, what this guide used it for, and how it can be reached. "Open" means freely available online. Sources marked as in copyright and not opened were not used as authority for any form; where a lesson mentions them, it says so.

**Where to go next.** For grammar, Allen and Greenough is the fullest free reference in English, and every section number in this guide can be looked up in it. For words, Lewis and Short (through Logeion) gives meanings with citations from the authors. For reading, The Latin Library and the Perseus Scaife viewer give the texts; the Dickinson College Commentaries offer annotated editions of many of the authors read in part 8.

## Grammars

The guide's forms and syntax were checked against these. Section numbers in lessons (ag1903 §…) refer to Allen and Greenough unless another source is named.

- **Allen, J. H. and Greenough, J. B. (1903). Allen and Greenough's New Latin Grammar for Schools and Colleges. Boston: Ginn. Digital edition ed. M. Ayer, Dickinson College Commentaries, 2014.** https://dcc.dickinson.edu/allen-greenough/ Used for: Standard reference grammar in English: forms, syntax, section numbers cited in lessons. Access: Public domain; open online. Topic-to-page index in ag-index.tsv.
- **Bennett, C. E. (1918). New Latin Grammar. 3rd ed. Boston: Allyn and Bacon.** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15665 Used for: Second reference grammar; paradigms in plain text for checking forms. Access: Public domain; Project Gutenberg ebook 15665.
- **Gildersleeve, B. L. and Lodge, G. (1895). Gildersleeve's Latin Grammar. 3rd ed. London: Macmillan.** Used for: Syntax in detail, with many cited examples. Access: Public domain; scans on archive.org.
- **Pinkster, H. (2015, 2021). The Oxford Latin Syntax. 2 vols. Oxford University Press.** Used for: Modern description of syntax, word order and tense usage; corrective to schoolbook rules. Access: In copyright.
- **Devine, A. M. and Stephens, L. D. (2006). Latin Word Order: Structured Meaning and Information. Oxford University Press.** Used for: Word order and its relation to emphasis. Access: In copyright.

## Dictionaries and word lists

Every headword, vowel length and meaning was checked in Wiktionary while drafting; the frequency list chose which words to teach.

- **Wiktionary, the free dictionary (English edition): Latin entries and inflection tables.** https://en.wiktionary.org/ Used for: Checking individual inflected forms, vowel lengths, meanings and etymologies (which cite de Vaan 2008). Access: Open (CC BY-SA). Query with tools/wikt.py.
- **Lewis, C. T. and Short, C. (1879). A Latin Dictionary. Oxford: Clarendon Press.** https://logeion.uchicago.edu/ Used for: Meanings, principal parts, usage with citations. Access: Public domain; searchable through Logeion.
- **Francese, C. (2012). Latin Core Vocabulary. Dickinson College Commentaries.** https://dcc.dickinson.edu/latin-core-list1 Used for: Frequency ranking used to choose which words to teach. Working copy in core-list.tsv (not shipped in the app). Access: Open (CC BY-SA).
- **de Vaan, M. (2008). Etymological Dictionary of Latin and the other Italic Languages. Leiden: Brill.** Used for: Etymologies of individual words, as reported in Wiktionary etymology sections. Access: In copyright; cited via wiktionary.

## Pronunciation and history of the language

The restored pronunciation and the historical explanations rest on these. Where only the books in copyright could support a claim and they were not opened, the lesson says the point is not explained here.

- **Allen, W. S. (1978). Vox Latina: A Guide to the Pronunciation of Classical Latin. 2nd ed. (1st ed. 1965). Cambridge University Press.** Used for: Authority for the restored pronunciation, syllable weight and stress. Access: In copyright; consulted through recall and secondary summaries, so pronunciation claims are checked against ag1903 and wiktionary where they overlap.
- **Weiss, M. (2020). Outline of the Historical and Comparative Grammar of Latin. 2nd ed. Ann Arbor: Beech Stave Press.** Used for: Authority for the history of sounds and forms (the why behind endings). Access: In copyright; not openly online. Historical claims resting on it alone are labelled as from recall until checked against an open source.
- **Sihler, A. L. (1995). New Comparative Grammar of Greek and Latin. Oxford University Press.** Used for: Second authority for historical explanations. Access: In copyright.
- **Clackson, J. and Horrocks, G. (2007). The Blackwell History of the Latin Language. Oxford: Blackwell.** Used for: History of the language from Early to Late Latin; background for parts 0 and 7. Access: In copyright.
- **Leonhardt, J. (2013). Latin: Story of a World Language. Trans. K. Kronenberg. Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press. (German original 2009.)** Used for: Claim in lat-00-01 that post-Roman Latin writing far outweighs what survives from antiquity. Access: In copyright; cited at second hand, book not opened.
- **Polybius, Histories 3.22 (Loeb translation by W. R. Paton, 1922–27).** https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/3*.html Used for: Ancient testimony that early Latin was hard for later Romans to read (lat-00-01). Access: Public domain; open on LacusCurtius.

## Texts

Attested sentences were quoted from these editions and are cited by work and passage.

- **The Latin Library (ed. W. L. Carey).** https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/ Used for: Plain texts of classical and later authors, used to locate and check attested sentences. Access: Open online.
- **Perseus Digital Library (Tufts University) and the Scaife Viewer.** https://scaife.perseus.org/ Used for: Texts with translations and parsing; second check on quoted passages. Access: Open online.
- **Biblia Sacra iuxta Vulgatam versionem (Stuttgart Vulgate, ed. R. Weber and R. Gryson) and the Clementine Vulgate (1592).** Used for: Late Latin reading passage. Access: Text open online (e.g. The Latin Library).
- **Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum (1863– ). Berlin. Texts via the Epigraphik-Datenbank Clauss-Slaby.** https://db.edcs.eu/ Used for: Inscriptions and graffiti, cited by volume and number. Access: Database open online.
- **Bücheler, F. (ed.) (1895–1897). Carmina Latina Epigraphica. Leipzig: Teubner (Anthologia Latina II).** https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/epitaphs.html Used for: Verse epitaphs in lat-07-14, cited by CLE number (52, 204, 439, 799) as The Latin Library's epitaphs page gives them. Access: Public domain; texts read on The Latin Library's epitaphs page 2026-11-27. The edition itself not opened.
- **Latin Wikipedia, "Scipionum elogia" (CIL VI 1285, 1287).** https://la.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scipionum_elogia Used for: Texts of the Scipio epitaphs (lat-07-12). Access: Open; opened 2026-11-27. EDCS and CIL not reachable.
- **German Wikipedia, "Caeliusstein" (CIL XIII 8648).** https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caeliusstein Used for: Text of the Caelius stone in Leiden form (lat-07-14). Access: Open; opened 2026-11-27. EDCS and CIL not reachable.
