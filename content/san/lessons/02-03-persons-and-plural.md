## The idea

So far every verb has ended in *-ti*: one other person. Sanskrit sorts every doer by two questions, and the verb answers both in its ending.

The first question is **person**: how the doer stands to the speaker. The **first person** is the speaker ("I", "we"). The **second person** is the one spoken to ("you"). The third person, from san-02-01, is anyone or anything else.

The second question is **number**. You know the singular, one. The **plural** is three or more. (Exactly two has its own forms, the dual of san-02-04; set it aside for now.) So the gloss labels in this lesson are 1, 2 and 3, combined with SG or PL: 1SG "I", 2PL "you (several)", 3PL "they".

Three persons times two numbers gives six endings: *-mi*, *-si*, *-ti* in the singular, *-maḥ*, *-tha*, *-nti* in the plural. Because the ending names the doer, a word for "I" or "you" is not needed. *gacchāmi* is "I go" by itself. Speijer (§10) notes that the subject is often only implied by the verb, as here.

Sanskrit still has the words: *aham* "I", *tvam* "you" (one person), *vayam* "we", *yūyam* "you" (several). They are added more often than in Latin or Greek, Speijer says (§10), even when not needed; and they cannot be left out when the stress is on the person: "*I* go, not you".

Nouns have a plural too. The nominative plural of *deva* is *devāḥ* "gods" (doing something), and the accusative plural is *devān* "gods" (done to). Whitney (§329a) explains *-āḥ*: the plural ending *-as* has merged with the stem's *a* into *-ās*, and the *s* is a visarga at a pause, as in the singular. The accusative plural *-ān* was once *-āns* (§329b). That lost *s* still shows itself in some joins, which part 4 explains.

## How it is built

**The verb.** Take the present stem and add the ending. Three small adjustments follow from the shape of the stem, which always ends in *a* in the verbs you know.

1. **Before *m* the stem's *a* becomes long.** Whitney (§733) lists this as a fixed feature of every present stem in *-a*: *gaccha-* + *-mi* = *gacchāmi*, + *-maḥ* = *gacchāmaḥ*.
2. **Before *-nti* nothing changes**: *gaccha-nti*. The ending is really *-anti*, and its first *a* disappears after the *a* of the stem (Whitney §550a). The guide cuts the form *gaccha-nti*.
3. **The first person plural ending is *-mas*.** Like every final *s*, it is *-maḥ* in pausa.

So to say "we cook": √pac, stem *paca-*; first person plural, *-maḥ*; lengthen the *a* before *m*: *pacāmaḥ*.

**The pronouns.** They are whole words, glossed as wholes: *aham* `1SG.NOM`, *tvam* `2SG.NOM`, *vayam* `1PL.NOM`, *yūyam* `2PL.NOM`. All four end in *m*, so they follow the rule of san-02-02: *ahaṃ gacchāmi*, but *aham atra vasāmi* "I live here" before a vowel. Their other forms come in san-03-08.

**The noun.** Replace the singular ending: *dev-aḥ* becomes *dev-āḥ* as subject, *dev-am* becomes *dev-ān* as object.

**Agreement.** The verb takes the person and number of its subject. Several gods go: *devāḥ* is third person plural, so the verb is *gacchanti*. A noun subject is always third person: "the gods" are neither the speaker nor the person spoken to.

**From meaning to form.** "You (several) see the birds."

1. Who sees: "you", several. Second person plural, *-tha*: *paśyatha*.
2. Do you need *yūyam*? Only for stress. Leave it out, or add it: *yūyaṃ ... paśyatha*.
3. What is seen: birds, several, object. *vihaga* in the accusative plural: *vihagān*.
4. Order: object before verb. *vihagān paśyatha*, or with stress on "you", *yūyaṃ vihagān paśyatha*.

## Where it applies

- **Any doer at all.** The six endings cover every subject: speaker, addressee, anyone else; one or several.
- **With or without the pronoun.** *gacchāmi* and *ahaṃ gacchāmi* both mean "I go". The pronoun is added for stress or contrast, and also, as Speijer (§10) says, quite often without any special need.
- **Stress or contrast requires the pronoun.** "You read, we cook": *yūyaṃ paṭhatha, vayaṃ pacāmaḥ*. Here the pronouns cannot be dropped (Speijer §10).
- **"We" for "I".** The first person plural may stand for a single speaker, much as Latin *nos* for *ego* (Speijer §25): an author or a king says *vayaṃ ...* of himself.
- **Plural "you" to one person, as a mark of respect** (Speijer §24). Ordinary polite address uses another word, *bhavān*, taught in san-03-17.
- **A noun subject is third person**: *gacchanti devāḥ* "the gods go" takes *-nti*, never *-tha*, even when you are talking to the gods.
- **Plural nouns as subjects and objects**: *devāḥ*, *devān*. The other cases have plurals too (part 3).
- **Masses and peoples.** A plural noun can name a people or a group: *brāhmaṇāḥ* "the brahmins (as a class)". Some words are used only in the plural (Whitney §264a); you will meet them later.

## Watch out

**The *a* is long before *m*, short elsewhere.** *\*gacchami* and *\*gacchamaḥ* are wrong: *gacchāmi*, *gacchāmaḥ* (Whitney §733). But *\*gacchāsi* and *\*gacchāti* are wrong too: *gacchasi*, *gacchati*.

**Two is not plural.** "We two" or "the two gods" take the dual forms of san-02-04. *\*devāḥ* for a pair is wrong.

**English "you" is two words in Sanskrit.** *tvam* with *-si* for one person, *yūyam* with *-tha* for several. *\*tvaṃ krīḍatha* mixes a singular pronoun with a plural verb. Say *tvaṃ krīḍasi* or *yūyaṃ krīḍatha*.

**The verb agrees with the subject, not the object.** In *nṛpaḥ paṇḍitān pṛcchati* the plural object does not make the verb plural. *\*nṛpaḥ paṇḍitān pṛcchanti* would need several kings.

**Do not confuse *-āḥ* and *-ān*.** Both are plural. *-āḥ* is the doer, *-ān* the one done to. *\*vihagāḥ paśyāmaḥ* makes the birds the doers, which clashes with the first-person verb. For "we see the birds" say *vihagān paśyāmaḥ*.

**Do not stress by habit.** Spanish speakers know this: *voy* is normal and *yo voy* is pointed. Sanskrit is looser than Spanish (Speijer §10), but a pronoun on every verb is not the default either.

**The *-maḥ* is a pausa form.** *-maḥ* is *-mas* at a pause. Before most sounds it changes as *-aḥ* does in nouns (san-02-06). Until then the examples keep it at the end of the sentence.

**Do not use *aham* as an object.** *aham* is only "I" as subject. "Me" is *mām* (san-03-08). *\*aham paśyati* does not mean "he sees me".

## Connections

Spanish keeps the six-way split and drops the pronoun as Sanskrit does: *voy, vas, va, vamos, vais, van*. The plural ending *-nti* is the one Latin wore down to *-nt* (*amant*) and Spanish to *-n* (*aman*); Whitney (§550a) gives it as *-anti*. Japanese and Korean mark no person on the verb at all.

## Exercises

1. Build "we throw" from √kṣip and "you (one) burn" from √dah. Show the step for the vowel.
2. Build "they climb the mountain" with *parvata*.
3. Take apart *jayāmaḥ* and *rohatha*.
4. Translate *yūyaṃ grāmaṃ gacchatha*, then change it to one person.
5. Translate *kākān paśyati vyāghraḥ*, and say why the verb is singular.
6. Correct *\*tvaṃ cintayatha* in two ways.
7. Say "I, not you, honour the teacher" (two clauses side by side).
8. Make the subject of *sarpaḥ phalaṃ khādati* plural: several snakes, one fruit.

## Worked answers

1. √kṣip, stem *kṣipa-*; first person plural *-maḥ*, and the *a* lengthens before *m*: *kṣipāmaḥ*. √dah, stem *daha-*; second person singular *-si*, no lengthening: *dahasi*.
2. Several others: *-nti*, *rohanti*. The mountain is the object, *parvatam*, written *parvataṃ* before *r*: *parvataṃ rohanti*.
3. *jayā-maḥ*: stem *jaya-* of √ji with the long *a* before *m*, ending first person plural: "we conquer". *roha-tha*: stem *roha-* of √ruh, ending second person plural: "you (several) climb".
4. "You (all) are going to the village." One person: *tvaṃ grāmaṃ gacchasi*. Both pronoun and ending change.
5. "The tiger sees the crows." The subject is *vyāghraḥ*, one tiger; *kākān* is the object, and the verb agrees only with the subject.
6. *tvam* is singular and *-tha* is plural. Either *tvaṃ cintayasi* "you (one) think" or *yūyaṃ cintayatha* "you (several) think".
7. *aham* stands before the vowel of *ācāryam*, so it keeps its *m*: *aham ācāryaṃ pūjayāmi*. "Not you" needs *na* "not" (san-02-08); with contrast alone: *aham ācāryaṃ pūjayāmi, tvaṃ śocasi* "I honour the teacher, you grieve". The pronouns must be there, because the stress is on the persons (Speijer §10).
8. *sarpāḥ phalaṃ khādanti* "the snakes eat the fruit". The subject becomes *-āḥ*, the verb *-nti*; *phalam* stays singular and, before *kh*, is written *phalaṃ*.
