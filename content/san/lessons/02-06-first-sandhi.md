## The idea

Until now the examples have been built so that no word changed at its edges. Real Sanskrit is not like that. When words follow each other, the last sound of one adjusts to the first sound of the next, and the writing shows it. These joins between words are **external sandhi**; the joins between the pieces of one word, which part 5 treats, are internal sandhi.

The commonest final sound in the language is the *s* of endings such as *-as*, *-ās*, *-mas*, *-tas*. Whitney (§145) says *s* would be the commonest final consonant of all, if it could stand at the end of a word. It cannot: before a pause it becomes visarga, *ḥ* (san-02-01). So *devaḥ* is the pausa form, and the real ending is *-as*. Whitney (§169) calls the changes of this *s* "a subject of first-rate importance in Sanskrit euphony".

What happens to it depends on the next sound, and the logic is phonetic. Before a voiceless stop the *s* stays a voiceless hiss and takes the place of mouth of that stop. Before a voiced sound it has to become voiced, and in Sanskrit a voiced *s* turns into *r* (Whitney §§164, 174). Words in *-as* and *-ās* go one step further, as you will see. Learn the rules once and every *-aḥ* in a text becomes readable.

The second join of this lesson you already use: final *m* becomes anusvāra *ṃ* before a consonant (Whitney §213) and stays *m* before a vowel.

## How it is built

Ask two questions about every word that ends in *-ḥ*: which vowel stands before the *ḥ*, and what sound begins the next word?

**1. Before *k, kh, p, ph*, and at a pause**, nothing changes: *devaḥ paśyati*. (In theory the *s* becomes two special breaths here, but in practice, Whitney says (§170d), it is written as visarga.)

**2. Before *t, th*** the *s* is kept as *s*; before *c, ch* it becomes the palatal *ś*; before *ṭ, ṭh* the retroflex *ṣ* (Whitney §170b–c). The hiss takes the place of the next stop: *devas tiṣṭhati*, *aśvaś carati*. This applies whatever the vowel: *devās tiṣṭhanti* too.

**3. Before *ś, ṣ, s*** the editions write visarga (Whitney §172, which notes that the grammarians also allow the sibilant itself): *devaḥ smarati*.

**4. Before a voiced sound** (any vowel, or *g gh j jh ḍ ḍh d dh b bh*, the nasals, *y r l v h*), the result depends on the vowel before the *ḥ*:

- *-aḥ* before a voiced consonant becomes *-o*: *devaḥ* + *gacchati* = *devo gacchati* (Whitney §175a). Written with the vowel sign ो.
- *-aḥ* before short *a-* also becomes *-o*, and the *a* of the next word disappears. Its loss is marked with the avagraha ऽ, written ' in IAST (san-01-08): *nṛpaḥ* + *atra* = *nṛpo 'tra*.
- *-aḥ* before any other vowel loses its *ḥ* and becomes *-a*, and the two vowels stay side by side, unmerged (Whitney §175c): *bālaḥ* + *iha* = *bāla iha*. The space stays.
- *-āḥ* before any voiced sound, vowel or consonant, loses its *ḥ* and becomes *-ā*; a vowel after it stays separate (Whitney §177): *devā gacchanti*, *kanyā atra*.
- After any other vowel, *-ḥ* before a voiced sound becomes *r* (Whitney §174): *kaviḥ* "poet" + *gacchati* = *kavir gacchati*. Before *r* itself the *r* drops and a short vowel becomes long (§179): *kavī rakṣati*. Nouns ending in *-iḥ* and *-uḥ* come in part 3; the rule is here so that you recognise them.

**5. Final *r*.** A few words end in a real *r*, which also shows as *ḥ* at a pause: *punar*, pausa *punaḥ*. Before a voiced sound *-ar* stays *-ar* (Whitney §178b): *punar gacchati*, not *-o*. The dictionary entry tells you whether a word has *r* or *s*.

**6. Final *m*** before a consonant: *ṃ*. Before a vowel: *m*. At a pause: *m*.

**Why the guide shows both lines.** The transliteration line shows the joined text, as editions print it. The morphemes line shows each word in its pausa form, as the traditional word-by-word text (the padapāṭha, san-00-03) did. To read a joined line, undo each join: *-o* before a voiced sound is *-aḥ*; *-a* before a vowel is *-aḥ*; *-ā* before a voiced sound is very often *-āḥ*; *-s*, *-ś* before *t*, *c* are *-ḥ*.

**From meaning to form.** "The messenger enters the village."

1. Words in pausa form: *dūtaḥ grāmam viśati*.
2. *dūtaḥ* is *-aḥ* before *g*, a voiced consonant: *dūto*.
3. *grāmam* before *v*, a consonant: *grāmaṃ*.
4. *viśati* stands last.
5. Result: *dūto grāmaṃ viśati*.

From now on, constructed examples apply these joins. Vowel meeting vowel and final consonants other than *ḥ* and *m* still wait for san-03-07 and san-04-07, and the examples still avoid them.

## Where it applies

- **Every word that ends in *-ḥ* and is followed by another word in the same sentence or verse**: nouns (*devaḥ*, *devāḥ*, *kanyāḥ*), verbs (*gacchāmaḥ*, *gacchataḥ*, *gacchathaḥ*) and adverbs (*punaḥ*, which is *r*). The rule does not care about the word's job, only its last sound.
- **Joins across a full stop?** Prose separated by a daṇḍa is a pause, so the word before it stands in pausa: *... gacchati devaḥ |*. Inside a sentence, every boundary is joined.
- **Verse.** Inside a half-verse every boundary is joined. The end of a half-verse counts as a pause: Manusmṛti 8.15 ends its first half with *rakṣitaḥ* although the second half begins with *t*. The epics are not always regular at the quarter-verse breaks (part 7).
- **Before a word you cannot see yet.** When you build a sentence, decide the order first, then apply the joins from left to right: each word's ending depends only on the word after it.
- **The ṃ rule**: *grāmaṃ gacchati*, *grāmam atra*, every *-m* ending (*-am*, *-ām*, *aham*, *vayam*).
- **Inside compounds and after preverbs** the same *s* rules mostly apply, with exceptions (Whitney §171b keeps *namaskāra*, from *namas*, unchanged). Part 5 deals with them.
- **Two forms of the demonstrative**, *saḥ* "he, that" and *eṣaḥ* "this", drop their *-ḥ* before every consonant: *sa gacchati* (Whitney §176a). They are taught in san-03-09.

## Watch out

**Do not look up the joined form.** *devo* is not a dictionary word. Undo the join (*devaḥ*) and look up the stem (*deva*).

**Do not give *-āḥ* the *-o*.** *\*devo gacchanti* for "the gods go" applies the singular rule to the plural ending. Long *-āḥ* only loses its *ḥ*: *devā gacchanti*.

**Do not change *-aḥ* before *k*, *kh*, *p*, *ph*.** *\*devo paśyati* is wrong: *p* is voiceless. Write *devaḥ paśyati*.

**Do not leave *-ḥ* before *t* or *c*.** *\*devaḥ tiṣṭhati* is the most common beginner's error in writing. The editions have *devas tiṣṭhati*.

**Do not merge the vowels after *-a* or *-ā*.** *\*bāleha* would apply the vowel joins of san-03-07 to *bāla iha*, but after a lost *ḥ* the vowels stay apart (Whitney §175c, §177). Equally, do not use the avagraha except before *a*: *\*nṛpo 'iha* is wrong; *nṛpa iha*.

**Do not drop the avagraha.** *nṛpo 'tra* is "the king here". Without the sign, *\*nṛpotra* or *\*nṛpo tra* hides the word *atra*. In Devanagari write ऽ, in IAST the straight apostrophe.

**A final *-ā* may be singular or plural.** Before a voiced sound, *kanyā* is either the feminine singular *kanyā* or the plural *kanyāḥ* with its *ḥ* lost. The verb decides: *kanyā gacchati* (one girl) against *kanyā gacchanti* (several).

**A final *-a* may be a vocative or a lost *-aḥ*.** *bāla iha* is "the boy here", because the next word begins with a vowel. A bare *deva* before a consonant is a vocative (san-02-09), since *-aḥ* there would show as *-o* or stay.

**Do not treat *r*-words like *s*-words.** *\*puno gacchati* is wrong: *punar* keeps *r* before voiced sounds (Whitney §178b).

**Do not apply the rules to *-e*, *-au* or *-i*.** These are vowels, and their joins are those of san-03-07.

## Connections

French does something similar at word boundaries: final consonants silent in *les* reappear before a vowel, as *z*, in *les amis*. That *z* is the voiced partner of *s*, the same step that turns Sanskrit *-s* voiced before voiced sounds. Spanish and English write the word unchanged whatever it sounds like; Sanskrit writes what is said.

## Exercises

1. Join: *naraḥ gacchati*, *naraḥ tiṣṭhati*, *naraḥ paśyati*, *naraḥ atra*.
2. Join: *devāḥ vadanti*, *devāḥ tatra*, *devāḥ iha*.
3. Undo the joins and translate: *megho 'tra*, *candras tatra*, *kanyā iha*.
4. Build "the wicked messenger runs to the village", with every join.
5. Join *gacchāmaḥ vanam* and *vanam gacchāmaḥ*. Which word changes in each, and why?
6. Correct *\*devo paśyanti* and *\*bālaḥ tatra*.
7. In *kanyā gāyanti*, is *kanyā* singular or plural? How do you know?
8. Undo every join in *dharmo rakṣati rakṣitaḥ* and say what rule each follows.
9. Join *punaḥ viśati* and *bālaḥ viśati*, and explain the difference.

## Worked answers

1. *naro gacchati* (voiced *g*: *-o*); *naras tiṣṭhati* (*t*: *s*); *naraḥ paśyati* (*p*: no change); *naro 'tra* (*a*-: *-o* and avagraha).
2. *devā vadanti*, *devās tatra*, *devā iha*. *-āḥ* before any voiced sound becomes *-ā*; before *t* it is *-ās*.
3. *meghaḥ atra* "the cloud here"; *candraḥ tatra* "the moon there"; *kanyāḥ iha* or *kanyā iha*: the next word begins with a vowel, so *-ā* could be either (a verb would decide).
4. Pausa forms: *duṣṭaḥ dūtaḥ grāmam dhāvati*. *duṣṭaḥ* before *d*: *duṣṭo*. *dūtaḥ* before *g*: *dūto*. *grāmam* before *dh*: *grāmaṃ*. Result: *duṣṭo dūto grāmaṃ dhāvati*.
5. *gacchāmo vanam*: *-maḥ* before *v* becomes *-o*; *vanam* is last and keeps *m*. *vanaṃ gacchāmaḥ*: *vanam* before *g* is written *vanaṃ*; *gacchāmaḥ* is last and stays in pausa.
6. *paśyanti* begins with voiceless *p*, and the subject is plural: *devāḥ paśyanti*. Before *t*: *bālas tatra*.
7. Plural: *gāyanti* is third person plural, so *kanyā* is *kanyāḥ* with its *ḥ* lost before the voiced *g*. The singular would be *kanyā gāyati*.
8. *dharmaḥ rakṣati rakṣitaḥ*. *dharmo*: *-aḥ* before the voiced *r* becomes *-o*. *rakṣati*: ends in a vowel before a consonant, no change. *rakṣitaḥ*: last, in pausa.
9. *punar viśati*: *punar* has a real *r*, kept before the voiced *v*. *bālo viśati*: *bālaḥ* has *-as*, which becomes *-o* before a voiced sound.
