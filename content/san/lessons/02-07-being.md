## The idea

"The king is old." English needs the word "is" to link the subject to what is said about it. A linking verb of this kind is called a **copula**. Sanskrit usually does without one. *nṛpo vṛddhaḥ* is a complete sentence: "the king (is) old".

Speijer (§2) is blunt about it. The verb "be" is commonly not added to a noun predicate; logically it is of no use, and its compulsory use in modern Western languages "is rather to be called an abuse". A **predicate** is what the sentence says about its subject. Here it is a noun or an adjective, and it stands in the nominative, like the subject (Whitney §267). So a Sanskrit sentence can be two nominatives side by side.

The verb "be" exists, of course. It is √as, with the forms *asmi* "I am", *asi* "you are", *asti* "he, she, it is", and so on. It is needed in two situations, which Speijer (§2) names: when "be" means "exist, be there", and when the tense or mood must be shown. It may also be added by choice, as in Speijer's example "I alone am guilty". Arabic and Russian work the same way: *al-maliku kabīrun* "the king (is) big" has no verb, but "the king was big" needs one.

A second verb, √bhū, means "become" and also "be". Its present *bhavati* "becomes, comes to be, is" is a regular verb of the type you know (*bhava-ti*). Speijer (§3) says it is the right verb when the stress is on the subject's taking on a quality or state.

## How it is built

**The nominal sentence.** Put the subject in the nominative and the predicate in the nominative too. If the predicate is an adjective, it agrees with the subject in gender, number and case (san-02-05). Speijer (§16.2) gives the usual order: the predicate first, then the subject. So "the king is old" is *vṛddho nṛpaḥ*. The order *nṛpo vṛddhaḥ* is also found.

**A predicate noun keeps its own gender.** "Non-violence is the highest duty": *ahiṃsā* is feminine, *dharma* masculine. The predicate *dharma* does not become feminine; only an adjective changes to agree with the noun it describes (*paramo dharmaḥ*).

**The verb √as.** It belongs to a different type from the verbs you know: the endings go straight onto the root, with no *a* between. Such verbs show a strong form of the root in the singular and a weak one elsewhere. √as is extreme: its weak form loses the vowel altogether (Whitney §636). So:

- Singular: *as-mi*, *asi* (for *as-si*, Whitney §636), *as-ti*.
- Dual: *s-vaḥ*, *s-thaḥ*, *s-taḥ*.
- Plural: *s-maḥ*, *s-tha*, *s-anti*.

The endings are the ones you know, except that the third plural keeps its full *-anti*, since no stem *a* swallows its first vowel. All these forms are listed in Monier-Williams and Wiktionary.

**Joins with *asti*.** *asti* begins with *a*. A word in *-aḥ* before it becomes *-o* with avagraha (san-02-06): *nṛpaḥ asti* → *nṛpo 'sti*, *śiṣyaḥ asmi* → *śiṣyo 'smi*. The forms in *s-* begin with a consonant: *vīrāḥ stha* keeps its visarga before *s* (Whitney §172).

**From meaning to form.** "We are pupils."

1. Subject: "we", first person plural. *vayam*, or leave it to the verb.
2. Predicate: pupils, several, nominative: *śiṣyāḥ*.
3. Verb? Not needed. *vayaṃ śiṣyāḥ* is complete. To insist, add *smaḥ*: *śiṣyāḥ smaḥ*, with *-āḥ* kept before *s*.

"The boy becomes a warrior." Becoming needs *bhavati*. Subject *bālaḥ*, predicate *yodhaḥ*, both nominative. *bālaḥ* before *y* and *yodhaḥ* before *bh* both become *-o*: *bālo yodho bhavati*.

## Where it applies

- **"X is Y", Y a noun or adjective, in the present.** No verb: *nṛpo vṛddhaḥ*, *ahaṃ śiṣyaḥ*. This is the normal form (Speijer §2).
- **The same with stress or explicit person.** Add *asmi*, *asi*, *asti*: *śiṣyo 'smi*. Speijer gives the example "I alone am guilty", where the verb is added.
- **"There is", "exists".** *asti* is needed: *tatra grāmo 'sti*. Stories open with *asti* as their first word, "there is ...", even when the meaning is past; Speijer (§311) counts 45 of 71 tales in one edition of the Pañcatantra that begin this way.
- **"Become", and "be" with stress on the state reached.** *bhavati*: *bālo yodho bhavati* (Speijer §3).
- **General truths.** *bhavati* is common in rules and proverbs: "a wicked man is (always) ...". You will meet it constantly in the Hitopadeśa.
- **Past, future, wishes, commands.** Then a verb is needed, because only a verb carries tense and mood (Speijer §2): "the king was old" needs a past form of √as (part 4). √as has only the present system and a perfect (Whitney §636b); the other tenses of "be" are taken from √bhū.
- **Predicate adjectives agree**: *kanyā priyā*, *phalāni śubhāni*.
- **Predicate nouns keep their own gender and, if the sense requires, their own number**: *ahiṃsā paramo dharmaḥ*.
- **"Not to exist".** *na* "not" with *asti*, "is not, there is no ...", comes in san-02-08.

## Watch out

**Do not add "is" by reflex.** *\*nṛpo vṛddho 'sti* for "the king is old" is grammatical but marked: it sounds like "the king *is* old" or "there is an old king". The plain statement is *vṛddho nṛpaḥ* or *nṛpo vṛddhaḥ*.

**Do not put the predicate in the accusative.** Spanish and French never do, but English "it's me" or German habits may tempt. *\*śiṣyam asmi* is wrong: "be" takes the nominative, *śiṣyo 'smi* (Whitney §267).

**Do not make the predicate noun agree in gender.** *\*ahiṃsā paramā dharmā* turns *dharma* into a nonexistent feminine. Only adjectives agree.

**Do not expect the verb to agree with the predicate.** The verb agrees with the subject: "we are a crowd" is first person plural, whatever the predicate.

**Do not confuse *asti* and *bhavati*.** *bhavati* means "comes to be" or "is (as a rule)"; *asti* means "is, exists". *bālo yodho 'sti* would be "the boy is a warrior", not "becomes".

**Do not build *\*asanti* or *\*assi*.** √as loses its *a* in the dual and plural (*santi*, *smaḥ*, *stha*), and the second singular is *asi* (Whitney §636).

**An adjective before a noun is ambiguous.** *priyā kanyā* is "the girl is dear" as a sentence and "the dear girl" as a phrase inside a longer sentence (*priyā kanyā hasati* "the dear girl laughs"). If another verb follows, the adjective is a describer; if nothing follows, it is the predicate.

**Do not look for Spanish *ser* and *estar*.** Sanskrit has no such split. *asti* serves for both "is (by nature)" and "is (at the moment)". The difference that matters is the one between *asti* "is" and *bhavati* "becomes".

**Do not join *asti* with a preceding vowel yet.** *na* + *asti* and *atra* + *asti* merge their vowels (san-03-07), so this lesson avoids them.

## Connections

*asti*, Greek *estí*, Latin *est* and English *is* go back to one Indo-European verb form (Wiktionary, अस्ति); Spanish *es* and French *est* continue the Latin. Arabic and Japanese both build sentences without a copula in places: Arabic *al-baytu kabīrun* "the house (is) big", Japanese *takai* "(it is) high" with an adjective alone.

## Exercises

1. Say "the servant is thin" without a verb, in the usual order.
2. Say "I am a fool" in two ways.
3. Say "you two are kṣatriyas" with the verb.
4. Translate *pāpo bhṛtyo bhavati*, and say why *bhavati* rather than *asti*.
5. Correct *\*śiṣyān smaḥ* "we are pupils" and *\*kanyā priyaḥ*.
6. Translate *nityo dharmaḥ*, and turn it into "there is an eternal dharma".
7. Translate *rājyaṃ sthūlam*, and explain why it is odd but grammatical.
8. Give the forms of √as for "they two are" and "you (several) are", and explain the missing *a*.

## Worked answers

1. Subject *bhṛtyaḥ*, predicate *kṛśaḥ*, masculine nominative singular. Predicate first: *kṛśo bhṛtyaḥ* (*kṛśaḥ* before *bh* becomes *-o*).
2. *ahaṃ mūrkhaḥ* (no verb) or *mūrkho 'smi* (verb, no pronoun: *-aḥ* before *a*- becomes *-o* with avagraha).
3. Two of you: *yuvām*, second person dual *sthaḥ*. Predicate dual: *kṣatriyau*. *yuvāṃ kṣatriyau sthaḥ*.
4. "The servant becomes wicked" or "a servant is (as a rule) wicked". *bhavati* expresses becoming, or a general truth with stress on the quality (Speijer §3); *asti* would only state a fact.
5. "Be" takes the nominative: *śiṣyāḥ smaḥ*. The adjective agrees with the feminine *kanyā*: *kanyā priyā* (or *priyā kanyā*).
6. "Dharma is eternal" (or "the eternal dharma", as a phrase). "There is": *asti* is needed: *nityo dharmo 'sti* (*dharmaḥ* before *a*-).
7. "The kingdom is big." Grammatical: *rājya* is neuter and *sthūla* agrees as *sthūlam*. Odd, because *sthūla* means "stout, thick, bulky", a word for bodies and objects, not for countries. Grammar does not choose words for you.
8. *staḥ*, *stha*. √as is a root-class verb: endings go straight on the root, which keeps *as-* in the singular and loses its vowel in the weak forms of the dual and plural (Whitney §636).
