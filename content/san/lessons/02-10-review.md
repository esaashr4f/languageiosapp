## Before you start

This review covers part 2: the nominative and the third person singular (san-02-01), the accusative (san-02-02), persons and the plural (san-02-03), the dual (san-02-04), gender and agreement (san-02-05), the joins of final *-ḥ* and *-m* (san-02-06), being and the sentence without a verb (san-02-07), *na*, *ca*, *vā*, *eva*, *api* and questions (san-02-08), and the vocative and *iti* (san-02-09). Part 1 is assumed: read the Devanagari of each line before you look at the transliteration.

Do each exercise before you read its answer. Every answer names the lesson to reread if you went wrong.

## Exercises

1. Translate line 1. Why is there a verb here, when Sanskrit usually says "X is Y" without one?
2. Translate line 2. Explain *kākaś* and the ending of the verb.
3. Translate line 3. What does *eva* add, and why is *mṛgo* not *mṛgaḥ*?
4. Translate line 4. Name the job of *vanam*, and undo the join in *vyāghro*.
5. Translate line 5. Which words are the crow's own, and what are *bho* and *mitra*?
6. Translate line 6. Why is the verb *dhāvāva* and not *dhāvāmaḥ*, and where did its *ḥ* go?
7. Translate line 7. √pat usually means "fall"; what tells you the crow does not fall into the tree?
8. Translate line 8, and explain the avagraha.
9. Translate line 9. Where does *ca* stand, and what does it join?
10. Translate line 10, and explain why *paramo* is masculine.
11. Retell line 2 with three animals: the deer, the crow and the tiger live there.
12. Build "The two girls are not playing."
13. Build "You (several) are kṣatriyas", without a verb.
14. Build "The king asks the pupil the way."
15. Join: *devaḥ atra*; *devāḥ atra*; *punaḥ gacchati*; *phalam iti*; *bālaḥ tatra*.
16. Find and fix the error: *\*ca nṛpaḥ bhṛtyaḥ gacchati* for "the servant and the king go".
17. Find and fix the error: *\*kanyā priyaḥ* for "the girl is dear".
18. Find and fix the error: *\*phalaḥ patati* for "the fruit falls".
19. Find and fix the error: *\*devaḥ gacchāmi* for "O god, I am going".
20. Find and fix the error: *\*ubhau yodhau gacchanti* for "both warriors go".
21. Find and fix the error: *\*vanam gacchāmo* for "we go to the forest".
22. Report in Sanskrit: "'I am the king,' says the old man."
23. Ask, without a particle and then with one: "Is the messenger entering the village?"
24. Explain the difference between *kanyā gāyati* and *kanyā gāyanti*.

## Worked answers

1. "There is a pleasant forest." The verb is there because "be" here means "exist": Speijer's rule is that *asti* is required for existence, and stories often open with it (san-02-07). *vanam* is neuter, so its subject form ends in *-am*, and *ramyam* agrees with it (san-02-05). *vanam* before *r* is written *vanaṃ* (san-02-06).
2. "There live a deer and a crow." *kākaḥ* before *c* becomes *kākaś* (san-02-06). Two animals joined by *ca* take a dual verb, *vasataḥ* "they two dwell" (san-02-04, san-02-08).
3. "The deer drinks only water; the crow eats fruits." *eva* restricts: water and nothing else (san-02-08). *mṛgaḥ* before the voiced *j* becomes *mṛgo*; *kākaḥ* before *ph* keeps its visarga (san-02-06). *phalāni* is the neuter accusative plural (san-02-05).
4. "Suddenly a tiger enters the forest." *vanam* is the object of "enter", here also the place gone into (san-02-02). *vyāghro* is *vyāghraḥ* before the voiced *v* (san-02-06).
5. "'Hey, friend, a tiger is standing (there)!' says the crow." The crow's words are everything before *iti*: *bho mitra tiṣṭhati vyāghraḥ* (san-02-09). *bho* is the interjection *bhoḥ* before the voiced *m*; *mitra* is the vocative of the neuter *mitra* "friend", the bare stem (san-02-05, san-02-09). *vyāghraḥ* before *i* is *vyāghra* (san-02-06).
6. "'We two will run far away,' says the deer." The deer speaks for itself and the crow: two, so the first person dual *-vaḥ*, with the long *ā* before *v* (san-02-04). The present also serves for what is about to happen (san-02-01). *-vaḥ* before the vowel of *iti* loses its *ḥ*: *-va iti* (san-02-06).
7. "The crow flies to the tree." Monier-Williams gives √pat as both "fly" and "fall". The accusative *vṛkṣam* is a goal of motion, "to the tree", which suits flying (san-02-02).
8. "The deer too leaves the forest." *mṛgaḥ* before the short *a* of *api* becomes *mṛgo*, and the *a* is lost, marked by the avagraha: *mṛgo 'pi* (san-02-06). *api* after a word means "also" (san-02-08).
9. "The tiger does not see the deer, and is angry." *na* stands before the verb it denies (san-02-08). *ca* stands after *krudhyati*, the first (and only) word of the second sentence, and joins that sentence to the first (san-02-08).
10. "Non-violence is the highest duty." A sentence without a verb (san-02-07). *parama* describes *dharma*, which is masculine, so it is masculine too, though the subject *ahiṃsā* is feminine: predicate nouns keep their own gender, adjectives agree with their noun (san-02-05, san-02-07).
11. Three subjects take the plural: *tatra mṛgaḥ kāko vyāghraś ca vasanti*. *ca* once, after the last (san-02-08); *kākaḥ* before *v* becomes *kāko*, *vyāghraḥ* before *c* *vyāghraś* (san-02-06); *vasanti* is third plural (san-02-03).
12. *kanye na krīḍataḥ*. Feminine dual *-e*, dual verb, *na* before the verb (san-02-04, san-02-05, san-02-08).
13. *yūyaṃ kṣatriyāḥ*. Subject and predicate both nominative plural; no verb is needed (san-02-03, san-02-07).
14. *nṛpaḥ śiṣyaṃ mārgaṃ pṛcchati*: √prach takes two accusatives, the person and the thing asked (san-02-02). *nṛpaḥ* before *ś* keeps its visarga (san-02-06).
15. *devo 'tra* (*-aḥ* before *a*-); *devā atra* (*-āḥ* before any voiced sound); *punar gacchati* (a real *r* stays before a voiced sound); *phalam iti* (*m* before a vowel stays); *bālas tatra* (*-ḥ* before *t* becomes *s*) (san-02-06).
16. *ca* follows the word it adds, and two subjects need a dual verb: *bhṛtyo nṛpaś ca gacchataḥ* (san-02-04, san-02-08). Joins: *bhṛtyaḥ* before *n* is *bhṛtyo*, *nṛpaḥ* before *c* is *nṛpaś* (san-02-06).
17. The adjective agrees with the feminine *kanyā*: *kanyā priyā*, or with the predicate first, *priyā kanyā* (san-02-05, san-02-07).
18. *phala* is neuter: its subject form is *phalam*. *phalaṃ patati* (san-02-05), with *ṃ* before *p* (san-02-02).
19. To call someone, use the vocative, the bare stem: *deva gacchāmi* (san-02-09).
20. *ubhau* and *yodhau* are dual, so the verb must be dual: *ubhau yodhau gacchataḥ* (san-02-04).
21. *vanam* before *g* is written *vanaṃ*; *gacchāmaḥ* at the end of a sentence stays in pausa: *vanaṃ gacchāmaḥ* (san-02-02, san-02-06).
22. The old man's words keep the first person: "I (am) the king". With *asmi*: *nṛpo 'smi*, ending in a vowel, would merge with *iti* (san-03-07). Without the verb, the words end in *-ḥ*: *ahaṃ nṛpaḥ*. Then *iti*, then the verb and the speaker: *ahaṃ nṛpa iti vadati vṛddhaḥ* (san-02-07, san-02-09; *nṛpaḥ* before *i* is *nṛpa*, san-02-06).
23. Without a particle, the verb first: *viśati dūto grāmam*. With a particle at the head: *api dūto grāmaṃ viśati* (neutral), or *kiṃ dūto grāmaṃ viśati* (rather expecting "no") (san-02-08). *dūtaḥ* before *g* is *dūto* (san-02-06).
24. *kanyā gāyati*: one girl sings; *kanyā* is the singular. *kanyā gāyanti*: several girls sing; *kanyā* is *kanyāḥ* with its *ḥ* lost before the voiced *g*. The verb's ending shows the number (san-02-05, san-02-06).
