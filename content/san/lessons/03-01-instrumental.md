## The idea

Part 2 gave you three cases: the nominative for the subject, the accusative for the object and the goal, and the vocative for calling. Sanskrit has eight. Part 3 adds the other five, one lesson each, and then the other kinds of noun.

The first is the **instrumental** (gloss INS). Whitney (§278) calls it in origin the "with-case": it says what goes along with the action, what stands beside it. From "together with" it passes to "by means of", the same step English takes when "with" in *I walked with a friend* becomes "with" in *I cut it with a knife*. Almost every use of the case grows from that one idea, and Whitney adds that they "show nothing anomalous or difficult".

So *hastena* is "with the hand", *khaḍgena* "with a sword", *rathena* "by chariot", *śiṣyaiḥ saha* "together with the pupils". The noun in the instrumental names the companion, the tool, the means, the manner or the cause. It is never the doer of an active verb and never what is acted on.

The ending of a-stems also shows you a sound rule that runs through the whole language. The singular ending is *-ena*: *devena* "with the god". But after *nṛpa* "king" it is *nṛpeṇa*, with the retroflex *ṇ*. The **n-to-ṇ rule** is the reason, and this lesson states it in full, because from now on endings with *n* in them (*-ena*, *-āni*, and the genitive plural *-ānām* of san-03-04) meet it constantly.

## How it is built

**The endings.** For masculine and neuter a-stems the instrumental is *-ena* in the singular, *-ābhyām* in the dual and *-aiḥ* in the plural. For feminine ā-stems it is *-ayā*, *-ābhyām*, *-ābhiḥ* (Wiktionary tables for देव, सेना; Whitney §330, §364). Cut them as before, with the stem's last vowel inside the ending:

- *dev-ena*, *dev-ābhyām*, *dev-aiḥ*
- *sen-ayā*, *sen-ābhyām*, *sen-ābhiḥ*

Neuters have the same instrumental as masculines: *phalena*, *phalaiḥ*. The dual *-ābhyām* is the same for all three genders. Wiktionary also prints *devebhiḥ* beside *devaiḥ*, with a footnote: that is the Vedic form, not Classical.

**The n-to-ṇ rule.** Whitney (§189) states it like this. A dental *n* becomes retroflex *ṇ* when three things are true:

1. Earlier in the same word there is *ṛ*, *ṝ*, *r* or *ṣ*.
2. Between that sound and the *n* there is no palatal consonant other than *y* (so no *c, ch, j, ś*), no retroflex consonant (*ṭ, ṭh, ḍ, ḍh, ṇ*) and no dental (*t, th, d, dh, n, l, s*). Vowels, *y, v, h*, the velars and the labials may stand between.
3. The *n* itself is followed by a vowel, or by *n, m, y, v*.

The reason is in the tongue. *r*, *ṛ* and *ṣ* curl its tip back. Whitney's explanation (§189a) is that the tip "tends to hang there" and makes the next *n* in that curled position, unless something moves it: a retroflex stop, which uses up the curl, or a sound made with the front of the tongue at the teeth or palate, which pulls it forward. Velars and labials do not move the tip, so the curl survives them.

Test it on the words you know:

- *nṛpa* + *-ena*: *ṛ*, then *p* (labial, harmless), then *e*, then *n* before a vowel. Result: *nṛpeṇa*.
- *putra*, *vṛkṣa*, *mārga*, *śiṣya*: *putreṇa*, *vṛkṣeṇa*, *mārgeṇa*, *śiṣyeṇa*. In *vṛkṣa* the *kṣ* is a velar and the *ṣ* is itself a trigger; in *śiṣya* the *y* does not block.
- *ratha* + *-ena*: the *r* is followed by *th*, a dental, which blocks. Result: *rathena*, with dental *n*.
- *deva*, *bāla*, *hasta*: no trigger at all. *devena*, *bālena*, *hastena*.

The same rule now lets you use neuter plurals you had to avoid in part 2: *puṣpāṇi* "flowers", *kṣetrāṇi* "fields", *netrāṇi* "eyes" (Wiktionary पुष्प, क्षेत्र, नेत्र).

**From meaning to form.** "The boy touches the fruit with his hand."

1. The verb: √spṛś "touch", *spṛśati* (san-02-02; class 6).
2. The doer: *bālaḥ*, nominative.
3. What is touched: *phalam*, accusative.
4. What he touches it with: *hasta*, instrumental singular. Is there a trigger in *hasta*? No. *hastena*.
5. Order: subject first, verb last, the instrumental before the object: *bālaḥ hastena phalam spṛśati*.
6. Joins (san-02-06): *bālaḥ* before *h*, a voiced sound: *bālo*. *phalam* before *s*: *phalaṃ*. Result: *bālo hastena phalaṃ spṛśati*.

"His" is not expressed: Sanskrit leaves out an obvious owner.

**With *saha*.** To say "together with" a person, Sanskrit can use the instrumental alone, but more often it adds the word *saha* "with", which stands after the noun (Whitney §279, §284): *śiṣyaiḥ saha* "with the pupils". *saha* never changes its form, and it does not govern any other case.

## Where it applies

- **The tool or means**: *hastena* "with the hand", *khaḍgena*, *śareṇa*; also substances: *jalena* "with water" (Whitney §280).
- **The vehicle and the way**: *rathena* "by chariot"; *mārgeṇa* "by the road". Whitney (§281c) extends this to the medium passed through, *vihāyasā* "through the air".
- **The companion**, alone or with *saha*: *śiṣyaiḥ saha* (Whitney §279). In prose, *saha* is the norm.
- **Manner**, with abstract nouns: *sukhena* "happily", *duḥkhena* "with difficulty", *vegena* "with speed, swiftly", *yatnena* "with care".
- **Cause or reason**: *krodhena* "out of anger", *kṛpayā* "out of pity" (Whitney §280). The Hitopadeśa has *kṛpayā* in exactly this sense (book 1, prose after verse 1.56). The ablative of san-03-03 does this job more often.
- **Equality and likeness**, with *tulya* "equal, like" and *sama* "same, equal": *siṃhena tulyaḥ* "a match for a lion" (Whitney §281a; mw1899 gives both words with the instrumental or the genitive).
- **The part of the body that carries**: *skandhena vahati* (Whitney §281e).
- **Time within which**: "in the course of", as *māsena* "in a month" (Whitney §281d).
- **Price**: what something is bought or exchanged for (Whitney §281b); you will meet a verb of buying in part 4.
- **"What use is ...?"**, with *kim* "what" (part 6): Whitney (§281f) quotes the Rāmāyaṇa's "what is gained by much talking?" with the talking in the instrumental.
- **The agent of a passive**: "by the king". This is the commonest use of all in later prose (Whitney §282), and it waits for the passive in san-04-14.
- **Separation, with words of parting**: Whitney (§283a) notes that the instrumental sometimes does the ablative's work with verbs meaning "be parted from".

## Watch out

**The doer of an active verb is never instrumental.** *\*nṛpeṇa dūtaṃ tāḍayati* for "the king strikes the messenger" puts the king in the "by" case without a passive verb. With an active verb the doer is nominative: *nṛpo dūtaṃ tāḍayati*. The instrumental agent belongs with the passive (san-04-14).

**The companion does not count in the verb.** *\*ācāryaḥ śiṣyaiḥ saha gacchanti* makes the verb plural because several people go. But only *ācāryaḥ* is the subject: *ācāryaḥ śiṣyaiḥ saha gacchati*. To make them all subjects, use *ca* and a plural verb (san-02-08): *ācāryaḥ śiṣyāś ca gacchanti*.

**English "with" is not always the instrumental.** "Angry with someone" uses the dative (san-03-02); "a man with a sword", meaning one who has a sword, is a compound or a possessive adjective (san-03-17, san-05-11), not *\*naraḥ khaḍgena*, which means "the man, by means of a sword". "Fight with" an enemy uses *saha* in Sanskrit as in English, but means "against".

**Do not put *saha* before the noun.** *\*saha śiṣyaiḥ* is possible in verse, but in prose *saha* follows: *śiṣyaiḥ saha*. And *saha* does not replace the case ending: *\*śiṣyāḥ saha* is wrong.

**Apply the n-to-ṇ rule, and only where it belongs.** *\*nṛpena* and *\*putrena* are wrong: nothing blocks the *r* or *ṛ*. *nṛpeṇa*, *putreṇa*. Equally wrong is *\*ratheṇa*: the dental *th* blocks it. And there is no reason to write *\*bāleṇa*, since *bāla* has no trigger.

**The rule does not cross a space.** In *vīraḥ khaḍgena* the *r* of *vīraḥ* has no effect on *khaḍgena*. Only inside one word, and in some compounds and after some preverbs (Whitney §§192–193, part 5), does it apply.

**Do not give ā-stems the a-stem ending.** *\*kanyena* borrows the masculine *-ena*. Feminine ā-stems take *-ayā*: *kanyayā*, *mālayā*. In the plural *-ābhiḥ*, not *\*-aiḥ*: *mālābhiḥ*.

**Do not use the Vedic plural.** Wiktionary lists *devebhiḥ* beside *devaiḥ*. *-ebhiḥ* is the Vedic form (footnoted there). Classical prose has only *-aiḥ*.

**The plural *-aiḥ* follows the visarga rules for "other vowels".** Its vowel is *ai*, not *a*, so before a voiced sound it becomes *-air*, not *\*-ao* or *\*-o*: *śiṣyair gacchati* "goes with the pupils" (san-02-06, rule 4). The same goes for *-ābhiḥ*: *mālābhir gṛham*.

## Connections

Latin and Greek folded the old instrumental into other cases (the Latin ablative does its work: *gladio*, "with a sword"), so Sanskrit keeps a case that its European relatives lost. Japanese marks the same means with the particle *de* (*kuruma de* "by car") and the companion with *to*; Korean uses *-(eu)ro* and *-wa/-gwa*. Sanskrit's one case covers both.

## Exercises

1. Give the instrumental singular of *vṛkṣa*, *hasta*, *kṣetra*, *dūta*, *kṛpā* and *mārga*, and say for each whether the n-to-ṇ rule applies and why.
2. Build "The pupil writes the verse with a reed." (Use *śara* for the reed.)
3. Build "The boys go to the village with the messenger."
4. Translate *vīrāḥ khaḍgaiḥ sarpān kṛntanti*, and explain *khaḍgaiḥ*.
5. Translate *kanyāḥ puṣpāṇi mālābhiḥ saha vahanti*. Why *puṣpāṇi* and not *\*puṣpāni*?
6. Find and fix the error: *\*nṛpeṇa vanaṃ gacchati* for "the king goes to the forest".
7. Find and fix the error: *\*bālo yatneṇa likhati* for "the boy writes carefully".
8. Build "The deer runs swiftly", using the instrumental of *vega*.

## Worked answers

1. *vṛkṣeṇa* (ṛ, then velar *kṣ* containing *ṣ*: nothing blocks); *hastena* (no trigger); *kṣetreṇa* (the *ṣ* of *kṣ* is followed by the dental *t*, which would block, but the *r* after it is a new trigger, and only vowels stand between that *r* and the *n*); *dūtena* (no trigger); *kṛpayā* (no *n* in the ending); *mārgeṇa* (*r*, then velar *g*: nothing blocks).
2. Writer: *śiṣyaḥ*. Thing written: *ślokam*. Means: *śareṇa* (*r* triggers). Verb: *likhati*. Order and joins: *śiṣyaḥ śareṇa ślokaṃ likhati*. *śiṣyaḥ* keeps its visarga before *ś*; *ślokam* before *l* is *ślokaṃ* (san-02-06).
3. Subject *bālāḥ*, plural verb *gacchanti*, companion *dūtena saha*, goal *grāmam*: *bālā dūtena saha grāmaṃ gacchanti*. *bālāḥ* before *d* loses its *ḥ* (san-02-06).
4. "The heroes cut the snakes with their swords." *khaḍgaiḥ* is the instrumental plural *-aiḥ*, one sword each; before *s* the visarga stays (san-02-06). *sarpān* is the accusative plural, before *k*.
5. "The girls carry flowers along with garlands." *puṣpa* has *ṣ*, then the labial *p* (no block), then *ā*, then *n* before a vowel: the rule applies. *mālābhiḥ saha* is accompaniment.
6. With an active verb the doer is nominative: *nṛpo vanaṃ gacchati*. The instrumental would be an agent, which needs a passive (san-04-14).
7. *yatna* has no *r*, *ṛ* or *ṣ*: no trigger, so *yatnena*. *bālo yatnena likhati*.
8. *mṛgo vegena dhāvati*. *vegena* is manner, the instrumental used as an adverb; *mṛgaḥ* before *v* is *mṛgo*.
