## The idea

"The king gives the brahmin wealth." Two nouns receive the action of *give*, but in different ways. The wealth changes hands: it is the direct object, in the accusative (san-02-02). The brahmin is the person it goes to, the one for whose benefit it is given. Grammarians call such a person the **indirect object**. Sanskrit puts him in the **dative** (gloss DAT): *nṛpo viprāya dhanaṃ dadāti*.

Whitney (§285) defines the dative as the case of the indirect object: that "toward or in the direction of or in order to or for which anything is or is done". It is the "for-case" and the "toward-case". Three families of use follow from that.

- **The receiver or the person addressed**: give to, tell to, show to, bow to.
- **The person a feeling is aimed at**: be angry at, long for, wish harm to.
- **The purpose or result**: go for water, fight for the sake of something, lead to ruin. Whitney (§287) calls this dative of end or purpose "extremely common".

The dative is the one case that never goes with a preposition (Whitney §288). It does all its work by itself.

## How it is built

**The endings.** Masculine and neuter a-stems: *-āya* in the singular, *-ābhyām* in the dual, *-ebhyaḥ* in the plural. Feminine ā-stems: *-āyai*, *-ābhyām*, *-ābhyaḥ* (Wiktionary देव, सेना; Whitney §330, §364).

- *dev-āya*, *dev-ābhyām*, *dev-ebhyaḥ*
- *sen-āyai*, *sen-ābhyām*, *sen-ābhyaḥ*

Two facts make these easy to hold. The dual *-ābhyām* is the same for instrumental, dative and ablative in every noun you will meet; the context decides which. And the plural *-ebhyaḥ*, *-ābhyaḥ* is shared with the ablative (san-03-03). No *n* appears in these endings, so the n-to-ṇ rule of san-03-01 does not touch them.

The *-aḥ* of *-ebhyaḥ* and *-ābhyaḥ* is the familiar final *-as*, and joins exactly like the *-aḥ* of *devaḥ* (san-02-06): *brāhmaṇebhyo dadāti*, *brāhmaṇebhyas tatra*. *-āyai* ends in a vowel, so until san-03-07 the examples keep a consonant after it.

**A new verb for "give".** √dā "give" makes its present by doubling the root: *dadāti* "he gives", *dadati* "they give", *dadāmi* "I give", *dadmaḥ* "we give" (Wiktionary ददाति). This kind of present, the reduplicating class, is explained in san-04-09; for now learn these forms as they stand. The gloss treats the present stem as one piece, as usual: `dadā-ti` `give-3SG`, `dada-ti` `give-3PL`.

**From meaning to form.** "The king gives a jewel to the girl."

1. Verb: √dā, one other person: *dadāti*.
2. Giver, the subject: *nṛpaḥ*.
3. Thing given, the direct object: *ratnam*.
4. Receiver: *kanyā*, a feminine ā-stem, dative singular *kanyāyai*.
5. Order: receiver usually before the thing, verb last: *nṛpaḥ kanyāyai ratnam dadāti*.
6. Joins: *nṛpaḥ* before *k* stays; *kanyāyai* ends in a vowel before *r*: no change; *ratnam* before *d*: *ratnaṃ*. Result: *nṛpaḥ kanyāyai ratnaṃ dadāti*.

**Dative of purpose.** To say what an action is for, put the noun naming the goal in the dative: *jalāya gacchati* "goes for water". This often stands with no verb at all, in the pattern "X (is) for Y", meaning "X serves Y, X leads to Y" (Whitney §287a). The second attested example shows it.

## Where it applies

- **Giving, granting, offering**: *dadāti* with the dative of the receiver (Whitney §286a): *viprāya dhanaṃ dadāti*.
- **Telling, showing, announcing**: *nṛpāya kathāṃ kathayati* (Whitney §286b).
- **Feelings aimed at someone**: √krudh and √kup "be angry with", √druh "seek to harm", √spṛh "long for" (Whitney §286c; mw1899 under each root). *mitrāya na druhyati* "he does not betray his friend".
- **Pleasing and suiting**: "X pleases Y" puts Y in the dative (Whitney §286d); the verb, *rocate*, is middle (san-04-02).
- **Bowing and homage**: *namo devāya*; also with verbs of bowing, as √nam (Whitney §286e).
- **Greetings and blessings**: *svasti prajābhyaḥ*.
- **Adjectives of benefit and fitness**: *hita* "good for" (also with the genitive); *alam* "enough for, a match for": *alaṃ vyāghrāya vīraḥ* "the hero is a match for a tiger" (mw1899 under *alam*).
- **Purpose**: *puṣpebhyo vanaṃ gacchanti*, *yuddhāya*. With "go", the dative names what you go for; the accusative names where you go.
- **Result, often with no verb**: "X (leads) to Y": *prakopāya na śāntaye*. The Hitopadeśa says of youth, wealth, power and thoughtlessness that each alone is *anarthāya* "for ruin" (introductory verse 11).
- **Infinitive-like uses** in the older language, which part 4 touches on when it teaches the infinitive (Whitney §287b).

## Watch out

**Do not put the receiver in the accusative.** English "he gives the king a horse" has two bare nouns, and Sanskrit √prach "ask" really does take two accusatives (san-02-02). But *\*nṛpam aśvaṃ dadāti* means nothing sensible: it gives away the king. The receiver is dative: *aśvaṃ nṛpāya dadāti*.

**Do not use the dative for the place you go to.** *\*grāmāya gacchati* for "goes to the village" turns the village into a purpose, "goes for the sake of the village". Destination is accusative: *grāmaṃ gacchati*. Whitney (§285a) notes that in physical uses the two cases come close, but Classical prose keeps the accusative for goals.

**"Angry with" is not instrumental.** *\*dūtena krudhyati* would mean "is angry by means of the messenger". The person you are angry at is dative: *dūtāya krudhyati*.

**The feminine *-āyai* has no visarga.** *\*kanyāyaiḥ* confuses it with the instrumental plural *-aiḥ* of a-stems (san-03-01). Feminines never end in *-aiḥ*.

**Do not mix the masculine and feminine singulars.** *\*kanyāya* gives a feminine the a-stem ending. Feminine ā-stems take *-āyai*: *kanyāyai*, *senāyai*. Adjectives follow their noun: *priyāyai kanyāyai* "to the dear girl".

**The plural *-ebhyaḥ* is not *\*-aibhyaḥ* or *\*-ābhyaḥ*.** Masculine and neuter a-stems have *e* before *-bhyaḥ*: *devebhyaḥ*, *puṣpebhyaḥ*. Only feminines have *ā*: *kanyābhyaḥ*.

**Dual *-ābhyām* is three cases.** *hastābhyām* can be "with both hands" (instrumental), "for both hands" (dative) or "from both hands" (ablative). Read the verb before you decide.

**In later prose the genitive often replaces the dative.** Whitney (§297a) says the construction "abounds in the later language": *rājñaḥ* "of the king" for "to the king" with verbs of giving and telling. You will see it constantly in the Hitopadeśa. When you build sentences, use the dative; when you read, expect both (san-03-04).

## Connections

Spanish and French keep the dative only in pronouns (*le*, *lui*: *le doy el libro*), and Latin has a full dative case doing the same jobs as Sanskrit's, including the dative of purpose (*auxilio venire*, "come as a help"). Japanese *ni* and Korean *-ege* mark the receiver; the purpose dative is like Japanese *mizu o kumi ni iku*, "go for drawing water", with the same *ni*.

## Exercises

1. Give the dative singular and plural of *śiṣya*, *mālā*, *kṣetra* and *ācārya*.
2. Build "The girl gives alms to the beggar."
3. Build "The teacher tells the pupils the story."
4. Translate *nṛpo vīrebhyaḥ suvarṇaṃ dadāti*, and explain the join in *nṛpo*.
5. Translate *kṣatriyā yuddhāya gacchanti*. What kind of dative is *yuddhāya*?
6. Find and fix the error: *\*nṛpo viprena dhanaṃ dadāti* for "the king gives the brahmin wealth".
7. Find and fix the error: *\*bālo grāmāya gacchati* for "the boy goes to the village".
8. Translate *dānaṃ yācakebhyo hitam*, and say why there is no verb.

## Worked answers

1. *śiṣyāya*, *śiṣyebhyaḥ*; *mālāyai*, *mālābhyaḥ*; *kṣetrāya*, *kṣetrebhyaḥ*; *ācāryāya*, *ācāryebhyaḥ*. No *n* in the endings, so no ṇ (this lesson).
2. *kanyā bhikṣāṃ yācakāya dadāti*. Receiver *yācakāya*; *bhikṣām* before *y* is *bhikṣāṃ* (san-02-06). *kanyā* is singular and ends in a vowel; *b* follows, so nothing changes.
3. *ācāryaḥ śiṣyebhyaḥ kathāṃ kathayati*. Hearers in the dative plural; *-aḥ* before *k* stays (san-02-06).
4. "The king gives gold to the heroes." *vīrebhyaḥ* is dative plural; it keeps the visarga before *s*. *nṛpo* is *nṛpaḥ* before the voiced *v* (san-02-06).
5. "The kṣatriyas go to battle", more exactly "go for battle". *yuddhāya* is a dative of purpose: what they go for. *kṣatriyāḥ* before *y* loses its *ḥ* (san-02-06).
6. The receiver is dative, not instrumental (which would mean "by means of the brahmin"): *nṛpo viprāya dhanaṃ dadāti*. And *vipra* has an *r* followed by a vowel, so even the instrumental would be *vipreṇa* (san-03-01).
7. The destination is accusative: *bālo grāmaṃ gacchati*. *grāmāya* would make the village the purpose of the trip (this lesson; san-02-02).
8. "Giving is good for beggars", or "gifts are good for beggars". *hita* takes the dative; *hitam* agrees with the neuter *dānam*. "X (is) Y" needs no verb (san-02-07).
