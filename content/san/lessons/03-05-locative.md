## The idea

The last of the eight cases is the **locative** (gloss LOC), the "in-case". Whitney (§301a) calls it "the case expressing situation or location". *vane* is "in the forest", *gṛhe* "at home", *śākhāyām* "on the branch".

English needs several prepositions for this: in, on, at, among. Sanskrit has one case. Whitney (§301b) counts "amid or among, on, and at" as "unimportant variations of the sense of *in*". Time works the same way: *prabhāte* "at daybreak", *niśāyām* "at night". And so do less physical places: the sphere you are skilled *in*, the thing your affection rests *on*, the matter you are speaking *about*.

One use surprises English speakers. With verbs of throwing, putting and falling, the locative names where the thing ends up: *jale kṣipati* "throws it into the water", *bhūtale patati* "falls to the ground". Whitney (§301c) calls this a "pregnant construction": Sanskrit says where the thing comes to rest, not where it goes. German does the same with *in* and the accusative, and English *put it in the box* is not far off.

With the locative, the eight cases are complete. The next lesson sets out the whole a-stem and ā-stem system in one table.

## How it is built

**The endings.** Masculine and neuter a-stems: *-e* in the singular, *-ayoḥ* in the dual (the same as the genitive dual), *-eṣu* in the plural. Feminine ā-stems: *-āyām*, *-ayoḥ*, *-āsu* (Wiktionary देव, सेना).

- *dev-e*, *dev-ayoḥ*, *dev-eṣu*
- *sen-āyām*, *sen-ayoḥ*, *sen-āsu*

**Why *-eṣu* but *-āsu*.** Both plurals end in the same *-su*. After *e* it is written *ṣu*, after *ā* it stays *su*. This is a second retroflex rule, as regular as the n-to-ṇ rule: a dental *s* becomes retroflex *ṣ* when it comes straight after any vowel except *a* and *ā*, or after *k* or *r* (Whitney §180). Whitney's explanation is again the tongue: after these sounds it sits a little further back in the mouth, so the hiss is made at the retroflex point. *deveṣu*, but *senāsu*. You will meet the rule again in the i- and u-stems (*agniṣu*, san-03-12) and in many verb forms.

**Joins.** *-e* ends in a vowel: until san-03-07 a consonant follows it in the examples. *-āyām* is an *-m* (san-02-06). *-ayoḥ* joins as *-or* before a voiced sound.

**Choosing between the locative and the accusative of goal.** "The boy goes to the forest": the forest is a destination, so the accusative, *vanaṃ gacchati* (san-02-02). "The boy throws the stone into the well": the well is where the stone comes to rest, so the locative. Verbs of going take the accusative; verbs of putting, throwing, falling and placing take the locative.

**From meaning to form.** "A crow is sitting on the branch."

1. Verb: √sthā "stand, stay", *tiṣṭhati*. Sanskrit says a bird "stands" on a branch.
2. Subject: *kākaḥ*.
3. Place: *śākhā*, feminine, locative singular *śākhāyām*.
4. Order: place first, as a scene-setter: *śākhāyām kākaḥ tiṣṭhati*.
5. Joins: *śākhāyām* before *k*: *śākhāyāṃ*. *kākaḥ* before *t*: *kākas*. Result: *śākhāyāṃ kākas tiṣṭhati*.

## Where it applies

- **Place: in, on, at**: *gṛhe*, *vane*, *śākhāyām* (Whitney §302a).
- **Among a group**: *nareṣu śreṣṭhaḥ*; the genitive is equally possible (san-03-04).
- **At someone's house, in someone's company**: Whitney (§302c) quotes Manu's *gurau vasan* "living at the teacher's", with the person in the locative: *śiṣya ācārye vasati* "the pupil lives with the teacher" (*śiṣyaḥ* before a vowel other than *a* is *śiṣya*, san-02-06).
- **Time: at, in, on**: *prabhāte*, *niśāyām*, *divase* "by day", *kāle* "at the right time", *samaye* "at the right moment" (Whitney §302b).
- **Where something comes to rest**, with verbs of throwing, putting, falling, pouring, placing: *jale kṣipati*, *bhūtale patati*; also "put on the shoulder" (Whitney §304a). Whitney notes it even with "offer wealth to a lord", where you would expect a dative.
- **Object of feeling**: *kṛpā mṛgeṣu*, *sneho mitreṣu* "affection for friends" (mw1899 under *sneha*; Whitney §304b).
- **Sphere: in, at, about**: *śāstreṣu kuśalaḥ*; "delighted in your words" (Whitney §303).
- **"In the matter of, with regard to"**: *mā phaleṣu*. In later prose this use takes "a very wide range, touching upon genitive and dative constructions" (Whitney §303a).
- **Nouns used as prepositions**: *madhye* "in the middle (of)", with the genitive: *vanasya madhye* "in the middle of the forest" (Whitney §303e); more in san-05-03.
- **The locative absolute**, "when X has happened", is one of the commonest constructions in narrative (Whitney §303b–c). It needs participles and is taught in san-06-08.

## Watch out

**Do not use the locative for a destination of going.** *\*vane gacchati* is wrong for "goes to the forest". Destinations of going are accusative: *vanaṃ gacchati*. The locative goal belongs to verbs of putting, throwing and falling, where the thing ends up at rest.

**Do not use the accusative for where something is thrown or put.** The reverse error: *\*phalaṃ jalaṃ kṣipati* would read as "throws the fruit (and) the water". Correct: *phalaṃ jale kṣipati*.

**Do not drop the *ṣ*.** *\*devesu*, *\*vṛkṣesu* break the s-to-ṣ rule: the vowel before *-su* is *e*. Write *deveṣu*, *vṛkṣeṣu*. And do not apply it after *ā*: *\*senāṣu* is wrong; *senāsu*.

**Do not use *-e* for feminine ā-stems.** *\*śākhe* is the vocative or the nominative dual of *śākhā* (san-02-04, san-02-09), not "on the branch". The locative is *śākhāyām*.

**Do not read every *-e* as a locative.** *-e* is also the neuter dual (*phale* "two fruits"), the feminine dual (*kanye*) and the feminine vocative (*kanye* "girl!"). Gender and the verb decide.

**Do not read every *-ayoḥ* as a genitive.** It is also the locative dual. *hastayoḥ phale* is "fruits in the hands"; *hastayoḥ balam* "the strength of the hands".

**English "on", "at", "about" are often not the locative.** "On foot" (*pādābhyām*, by means of the feet) and "on the shoulder" with verbs of carrying (*skandhena*) are instrumental (san-03-01). "Talk about" uses the locative or the accusative with *prati* (san-05-03), depending on the verb.

## Connections

Latin lost most of its locative (a few forms survive: *Romae* "at Rome", *domi* "at home"), and Spanish and French replaced the cases with *en*, *à*, *dans*. Japanese *ni* and *de* split the Sanskrit locative between them: *ni* for where something is or comes to rest (*mizu ni nageru* "throw into the water", the same "pregnant" idea), *de* for where an action happens. Korean likewise has *-e* and *-eseo*.

## Exercises

1. Give the locative singular and plural of *vṛkṣa*, *niśā*, *taḍāga* and *āsana*.
2. Build "The pupils sit on the seats", with √sad.
3. Build "At night the deer stay in the forest", with √sthā.
4. Translate *kanyāḥ puṣpāṇi taḍāge kṣipanti*. Why is *taḍāge* locative and not accusative?
5. Translate *āśrame śiṣyā vedaṃ paṭhanti*.
6. Find and fix the error: *\*bālo grāme gacchati* for "the boy goes to the village".
7. Find and fix the error: *\*kākāḥ śākhāsu tiṣṭhanti* for "the crows sit on the branches", or say why it is correct.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*nṛpo yuddhesu kuśalaḥ* for "the king is skilled in battles".

## Worked answers

1. *vṛkṣe*, *vṛkṣeṣu*; *niśāyām*, *niśāsu*; *taḍāge*, *taḍāgeṣu*; *āsane*, *āsaneṣu* (this lesson; ṣ after *e*, s after *ā*).
2. *śiṣyā āsaneṣu sīdanti*. Place in the locative plural; *śiṣyāḥ* before a vowel loses its *ḥ* and the vowels stay apart (san-02-06). √sad makes *sīdati* (san-02-06 vocabulary).
3. *niśāyāṃ mṛgā vane tiṣṭhanti*. Two locatives, one of time and one of place; *niśāyām* before *m* is *niśāyāṃ*, *mṛgāḥ* before *v* is *mṛgā* (san-02-06).
4. "The girls throw flowers into the pond." With a verb of throwing the place where the flowers land is locative (this lesson; Whitney §304a).
5. "In the hermitage the pupils recite the Veda." *śiṣyāḥ* before *v* is *śiṣyā* (san-02-06).
6. The destination of going is accusative: *bālo grāmaṃ gacchati* (san-02-02). *grāme gacchati* would put the whole going inside the village: "goes about in the village".
7. It is correct. *śākhā* is a feminine ā-stem, so its locative plural is *śākhāsu*, with *s* after *ā*. *kākāḥ* before *ś* keeps its visarga (san-02-06).
8. The *s* of *-su* after *e* must be *ṣ*: *nṛpo yuddheṣu kuśalaḥ* (Whitney §180).
