## The idea

You now have all eight cases. This lesson puts them together for the commonest nouns and adjectives of the language: the a-stems (masculine and neuter, *deva*, *phala*) and the ā-stems (feminine, *senā*). Whitney (§326a) says this declension "contains the majority of all the declined stems of the language". Most of the adjectives you will meet follow it too, with *-a* for masculine and neuter and *-ā* for feminine.

It helps to see the a-stems against the rest of the system. Every Sanskrit noun class uses one underlying set of endings, which the Indian grammarians taught as the base for all declension (Whitney §310). In the singular they are: nominative *-s*, accusative *-am*, instrumental *-ā*, dative *-e*, ablative and genitive *-as*, locative *-i*. Dual: *-au*, *-bhyām*, *-os*. Plural: *-as*, *-bhis*, *-bhyas*, *-ām*, *-su*. You will see these almost unchanged in the consonant stems (san-03-15).

The a-stems are the class that departs from that set most. Whitney (§326b) says their endings "deviate more widely than any others from the normal". Some endings are the normal ones fused with the stem's *a*: the locative *-e* looks like *a* + *i*, the dative *-āya* "as if by adding *aya* to *a*" (Whitney §327d, g). Others are found in no other class of noun: the ablative *-āt* and the genitive *-asya* (Whitney §327e–f), which the pronouns share (san-03-08, san-03-09). So the most frequent declension is also the most peculiar one. Learn it well, and the others will seem regular.

## How it is built

**One table, built from what you know.** Each row below was taught in a lesson of parts 2 and 3. Read across for the numbers, down for the cases.

- Masculine *deva*: *devaḥ, devam, devena, devāya, devāt, devasya, deve, deva*; dual *devau, devau, devābhyām, devābhyām, devābhyām, devayoḥ, devayoḥ, devau*; plural *devāḥ, devān, devaiḥ, devebhyaḥ, devebhyaḥ, devānām, deveṣu, devāḥ*.
- Neuter *phala* differs only in the nominative, accusative and vocative: *phalam* (both), dual *phale*, plural *phalāni*.
- Feminine *senā*: *senā, senām, senayā, senāyai, senāyāḥ, senāyāḥ, senāyām, sene*; dual *sene, sene, senābhyām, senābhyām, senābhyām, senayoḥ, senayoḥ, sene*; plural *senāḥ, senāḥ, senābhiḥ, senābhyaḥ, senābhyaḥ, senānām, senāsu, senāḥ*.

**What is shared.** A table of 24 cells has far fewer distinct forms, and the overlaps are the same in every noun of the class:

- The dual has three forms per gender: one for nominative, accusative and vocative; *-ābhyām* for instrumental, dative, ablative; *-ayoḥ* for genitive and locative.
- Dative and ablative plural are always one form (*-ebhyaḥ*, *-ābhyaḥ*).
- Neuter nominative and accusative are always one form.
- The feminine ablative and genitive singular are one form (*-āyāḥ*), and the feminine nominative, accusative and vocative plural are one form (*-āḥ*).

**Why a few forms look odd.** Whitney explains several from history:

- The accusative plural *-ān* stands for an older *-āns*. "Abundant traces" of it are left in the Veda, and even in Classical Sanskrit it shows up "under the disguise of apparent euphonic combination" (Whitney §329b): before *t*, texts write *devāṃs tatra*, with the old *s* back. You will meet this in san-04-07.
- The genitive plural inserts an *n* after the vowel and lengthens it: *devānām* (Whitney §309f, §329f). That *n* is why the n-to-ṇ rule matters here.
- The instrumental singular *-ena* is the only form in the later language; the older *-ā* survives in the Veda (Whitney §327c). Likewise the Vedic plurals *-ebhiḥ* and neuter *-ā*, which Wiktionary footnotes.

**Reading a form you do not know.** Work from the end of the word. *-ebhyaḥ*: dative or ablative plural. *-ānām*, *-āṇām*: genitive plural. *-eṣu*, *-āsu*: locative plural. *-ena*, *-eṇa*: instrumental singular masculine or neuter. *-ayā*: instrumental singular feminine. *-āyai*: dative singular feminine. *-āyāḥ*: ablative or genitive singular feminine. *-āyām*: locative singular feminine. *-asya*: genitive singular. *-āt*: ablative singular. *-ayoḥ*, *-ābhyām*, *-au*: dual. The short endings (*-aḥ*, *-am*, *-e*, *-ā*, *-āḥ*, *-āni*) need the gender and the verb to decide.

**Adjectives follow their noun.** An adjective in *-a* (masculine, neuter) and *-ā* (feminine) takes the same case, number and gender as its noun, cell by cell: *ghoreṇa vyāghreṇa* "by the terrible tiger", *śvetāyāṃ śayyāyām* "on the white bed", *ramyāsu latāsu*. Each word applies the n-to-ṇ and s-to-ṣ rules on its own.

**From meaning to form.** "The deer are afraid of the terrible tigers."

1. Verb: √tras, plural: *trasyanti*. Subject: *mṛgāḥ*.
2. What they fear: ablative (san-03-03), plural: *vyāghrebhyaḥ*.
3. The adjective agrees: masculine ablative plural *ghorebhyaḥ*.
4. Order: *ghorebhyaḥ vyāghrebhyaḥ mṛgāḥ trasyanti*.
5. Joins: *-aḥ* before *v* and *m* becomes *-o*; *mṛgāḥ* before *t* becomes *mṛgās*. Result: *ghorebhyo vyāghrebhyo mṛgās trasyanti*.

## Where it applies

- **Masculine nouns in *-a***: *deva*, *nṛpa*, *vyādha*, *kapota* and hundreds more. Their gender is in the dictionary (mw1899 marks *m.*).
- **Neuter nouns in *-a***: *phala*, *vana*, *jāla*, *kṣetra*. Some words are listed as masculine or neuter (*naraka*, *śikhara*, *taḍāga*): either set of nominative and accusative forms is correct.
- **Feminine nouns in *-ā***: *senā*, *kanyā*, *latā*, *gaṅgā*.
- **Adjectives in *-a*, feminine *-ā***: *priya*, *ghora*, *śveta*, *ugra*, *durlabha*. Some adjectives make their feminine in *-ī* instead (san-03-13); the dictionary says *f. ī*.
- **Names**: *rāma*, *kṛṣṇa*, *gaṅgā*, and names of countries, often in the plural (*vidarbhāḥ*, the Vidarbha people and their land, in the Nala story).
- **Past participles in *-ta*, *-na*** (san-04-13) and **gerundives** (san-06-07) are adjectives of this kind.
- **The last member of most compounds** (part 5), even when the word alone is of another class: compounds often end in a-stems.
- **Not the pronouns.** *sa*, *eṣa*, *ya*, *ka* and *sarva*, *anya* look like a-stems but have special endings in several cells (san-03-09, san-05-02).

## Watch out

**Do not give a neuter the masculine nominative.** *\*phalaḥ* is wrong: the neuter nominative is *phalam*, plural *phalāni* (san-02-05).

**Do not give a feminine adjective the masculine form.** *\*śvetaḥ mālā* breaks agreement. *śvetā mālā*; in the instrumental *śvetayā mālayā*; in the locative plural *śvetāsu mālāsu*.

**Do not treat pronouns as a-stems.** The dative of *sa* "he" is *tasmai*, not *\*tāya* (san-03-09), and "to all" is *sarvasmai*, not *\*sarvāya* (san-05-02).

**Do not add *-ḥ* to the nominative singular feminine.** *\*kanyāḥ* is the plural. "The girl" is *kanyā*.

**Do not confuse the dual and the plural.** *devau* is two gods, *devāḥ* three or more (san-02-04). *phale* is two fruits, *phalāni* several; *kanye* two girls, *kanyāḥ* several.

**Do not mistake the five meanings of *-e*.** Masculine or neuter locative singular (*deve*), neuter nominative or accusative dual (*phale*), feminine nominative, accusative or vocative dual (*sene*), feminine vocative singular (*sene*). Gender and context decide.

**Do not mistake the meanings of *-āḥ*.** Masculine nominative or vocative plural (*devāḥ*), feminine nominative, accusative or vocative plural (*senāḥ*), and the end of the feminine ablative and genitive singular (*-āyāḥ*). And before a voiced sound every *-āḥ* is written *-ā*, which looks like a feminine nominative singular (san-02-06).

**Do not use Vedic forms from Wiktionary tables.** *devebhiḥ*, *devāsaḥ*, *phalā* (plural) and *senā* (instrumental) are footnoted there as Vedic. They appear in the Ṛgveda, not in Classical prose.

**Do not forget the retroflex rules in your own sentences.** *\*vyāghrena*, *\*putrānām*, *\*vṛkṣesu* are the three typical errors: *vyāghreṇa*, *putrāṇām*, *vṛkṣeṣu* (san-03-01, san-03-05).

## Connections

Latin's first and second declensions are the cousins of these: *rosa* (like *senā*) and *dominus*, *donum* (like *deva*, *phala*). The neuter rule "nominative and accusative always alike" holds in Latin, Greek and Sanskrit alike.

## Exercises

1. Decline *vyādha* in the singular and *jāla* in the plural, all eight cases.
2. Give the instrumental, dative and locative plural of *śṛgāla* and *mālā*.
3. Identify every possible case and number of *kapotāḥ*, *jāle*, *latāyāḥ*, *mūṣakābhyām* and *śukayoḥ*.
4. Build "The cat looks at the fish in the pond."
5. Build "The tortoise lives with the fish in the water."
6. Find and fix the error: *\*nīleṣu meghebhyo jalaṃ patati* for "water falls from the dark clouds".
7. Find and fix the error: *\*ugrāḥ senā nagaraṃ rakṣati* for "the mighty army protects the town".
8. Find and fix the error: *\*kanyā śvetaṃ mālāṃ bhūṣayati* for "the girl adorns the white garland".

## Worked answers

1. *vyādhaḥ, vyādham, vyādhena, vyādhāya, vyādhāt, vyādhasya, vyādhe, vyādha*. *jālāni, jālāni, jālaiḥ, jālebhyaḥ, jālebhyaḥ, jālānām, jāleṣu, jālāni* (this lesson). No *r* or *ṣ* in either stem.
2. *śṛgālaiḥ, śṛgālebhyaḥ, śṛgāleṣu*; *mālābhiḥ, mālābhyaḥ, mālāsu*. The *ṛ* of *śṛgāla* does not touch the instrumental plural, which has no *n*; in the singular the *l*, a dental, blocks it: *śṛgālena* (san-03-01).
3. *kapotāḥ*: nominative or vocative plural. *jāle* (neuter): locative singular, or nominative, accusative or vocative dual. *latāyāḥ*: ablative or genitive singular. *mūṣakābhyām*: instrumental, dative or ablative dual. *śukayoḥ*: genitive or locative dual (this lesson).
4. *mārjāras taḍāge matsyān paśyati*. The fish are "in the pond": locative. *mārjāraḥ* before *t* is *mārjāras*; *matsyān* before *p* is unchanged (san-02-06, san-03-05).
5. *kūrmo matsyaiḥ saha jale vasati*. Companion in the instrumental with *saha* (san-03-01); place in the locative. *kūrmaḥ* before *m* is *kūrmo*.
6. The sentence mixes cases: *nīleṣu* is locative, *meghebhyaḥ* ablative. The adjective must match its noun: *nīlebhyo meghebhyo jalaṃ patati* "water falls from the dark clouds" (this lesson; san-03-03).
7. *senā* is singular, so its adjective is the feminine nominative singular *ugrā*: *ugrā senā nagaraṃ rakṣati*. *\*ugrāḥ* is plural (this lesson).
8. *mālā* is feminine: *śvetāṃ mālām*. And to adorn something *with* the garland, use the instrumental: *kanyā śvetayā mālayā gṛhaṃ bhūṣayati*. As corrected for the words given: *kanyā śvetāṃ mālāṃ bhūṣayati* "the girl decorates the white garland" (san-02-05, san-03-01).
