## The idea

Since san-02-06 the examples have never let a word that ends in a vowel stand before a word that begins with one. Real texts are full of such meetings, and Classical Sanskrit does not tolerate them. Whitney (§125) puts it plainly: two vowels side by side, with no consonant between, are "forbidden by the euphony" of the Classical language.

There are three ways out, and every rule in this lesson is one of them (Whitney §125):

1. **Fuse** the two vowels into one: *ca* + *api* = *cāpi*; *tatra* + *eva* = *tatraiva*.
2. **Turn the first into a semivowel**: *i* becomes *y*, *u* becomes *v*, *ṛ* becomes *r*: *asti* + *atra* = *asty atra*.
3. **Split a diphthong**: the *e* of *vane* is really *a* + *i*; its *i* turns to *y* before a vowel and is then dropped, leaving *a*: *vane* + *iha* = *vana iha*.

Only one meeting is left alone: a few endings, above all the dual *-ī*, *-ū*, *-e*, never change (Whitney §138). The grammarians call them *pragṛhya*, "to be held apart".

The rules look many but rest on the vowel system you learned in san-01-02. *e* and *o* are historically *ai* and *au* (*a* + *i*, *a* + *u*); *ai* and *au* are *ā* + *i*, *ā* + *u*. Once you see the vowels that way, the joins follow.

## How it is built

Ask: what is the last vowel of the first word, and what is the first vowel of the next? Then apply the first rule that fits.

**1. Like vowels fuse into the long one** (Whitney §126). *a* or *ā* with *a* or *ā* gives *ā*; *i/ī* with *i/ī* gives *ī*; *u/ū* with *u/ū* gives *ū*.

- *ca* + *api* = *cāpi*; *kanyā* + *atra* = *kanyātra*; *gacchāmi* + *iti* = *gacchāmīti*.

**2. *a* or *ā* with an unlike vowel** (Whitney §127). The *a* and the next vowel make one vowel one step up the scale (san-03-11 will name these steps):

- with *i, ī*: *e*. *saha* + *iha* = *saheha*.
- with *u, ū*: *o*. *praśnasya* + *uttaram* = *praśnasyottaram*.
- with *ṛ*: *ar*. *mahā* + *ṛṣiḥ* = *maharṣiḥ* "great seer".
- with *e, ai*: *ai*. *tatra* + *eva* = *tatraiva*.
- with *o, au*: *au*. This is rare between words.

**3. *i, u, ṛ* (short or long) before an unlike vowel become *y, v, r*** (Whitney §129). The space stays after the semivowel.

- *asti* + *atra* = *asty atra*; *iti* + *eva* = *ity eva*; *tu* + *eva* = *tv eva*.

**4. *e* and *o* before *a*: the *a* disappears** (Whitney §135). Its loss is marked with the avagraha, as after *-o* from *-aḥ* (san-02-06): *vane* + *atra* = *vane 'tra*.

**5. *e* before any other vowel becomes *a*, and the two vowels stay apart** (Whitney §133). *nagare* + *iha* = *nagara iha*. **ai** before any vowel becomes *ā*, apart: *kanyāyai* + *adya* = *kanyāyā adya*. Whitney (§133b) reads the surviving hiatus as "a plain indication of the comparatively recent loss of the intervening consonantal sound", the *y*.

**6. *au* before any vowel becomes *āv*** (Whitney §134b), and the space stays after it: *devau* + *atra* = *devāv atra*.

**7. Exceptions: the *pragṛhya* endings** (Whitney §138). Dual endings in *-ī*, *-ū*, *-e*, of nouns and verbs, never join: *phale iha*, *kanye atra*. Interjections such as *he* "O" and *aho* "ah" do not join either.

**8. After a lost *-ḥ*, nothing more happens.** The hiatus of san-02-06 (*bāla iha*, *devā atra*) is final. Whitney (§132, §175c) notes that such vowels "remain unchanged". Do not fuse them again.

**How this guide writes the result.** When two vowels fuse into one, the words are written without a space: *cāpi*, *tatraiva*. When the first vowel only becomes a semivowel or loses its second half, the space stays: *asty atra*, *devāv atra*, *nagara iha*. The Devanagari follows the transliteration letter for letter (san-01-09). The morphemes line restores both words.

**Splitting, which is the hard direction.** A fused vowel can come from several pairs. *ā* may be *a* + *a*, *a* + *ā*, *ā* + *a* or *ā* + *ā*. *e* may be *a* + *i* or *a* + *ī*; *o* may be *a* + *u* or *a* + *ū*. Whitney's own transliteration marked which, with one apostrophe or two (§126a); editions do not. Try each split, and keep the one that gives two real words that fit the sentence.

**From meaning to form.** "'I am going,' says the boy."

1. The words: *gacchāmi iti vadati bālaḥ*.
2. *gacchāmi* ends in *i*, *iti* begins with *i*: like vowels, long *ī*, one word: *gacchāmīti*.
3. *iti* before *v*: no change. *vadati* before *b*: no change.
4. Result: *gacchāmīti vadati bālaḥ*.

So from this lesson on, a quotation may end in any form: *iti* joins with it like any word beginning with *i* (Whitney §126).

## Where it applies

- **Between any two words in a sentence or a half-verse**, whatever their grammar: noun and noun, noun and particle, verb and *iti*.
- **With *iti***: *gacchāmīti*, *vadatīti*, *ity eva*, *ity uktvā* "having said so" (part 4). Every quotation can now end in any word.
- **With the small words that begin with vowels**: *api*, *iva*, *eva*, *iti*, *iha*, *atra*, *adya*, *adhunā*. *ca* + *eva* = *caiva* "and also"; *na* + *iti* = *neti*; *na* + *eva* = *naiva* "not at all"; *iva* after *ā*: *kanyeva* "like a girl" (*kanyā* + *iva*).
- **Inside compounds**: *jana* + *adhipa* = *janādhipa* (Bhagavad Gītā 2.12); *mahā* + *ṛṣi* = *maharṣi*. The rules are the same "in internal and in external sandhi" (Whitney §125e), though inside words some special results appear (san-05-07).
- **After a preverb**: *prati* + *uvāca* = *pratyuvāca* "he answered" (san-04-04); some preverbs make *ār* instead of *ar* with an initial *ṛ* (Whitney §137a).
- **In verse at the break between quarters**, editions sometimes leave a hiatus (Whitney §125d). The epics do this more than Classical poets (part 7).
- **Not after a lost *-ḥ***: *bāla iha*, *devā atra* stay as they are (rule 8).
- **Not after dual *-ī*, *-ū*, *-e*** (rule 7); you will meet the dual *-ī* and *-ū* in san-03-12.

## Watch out

**Do not fuse after a lost visarga.** *\*bāleha* for *bālaḥ iha* fuses two vowels that have just been brought together by the loss of *ḥ*. Correct: *bāla iha* (san-02-06, rule 8).

**Do not fuse a dual ending.** *\*phaleha* for "two fruits here" treats the dual *-e* like the locative *-e*. Dual *-e* never changes: *phale iha*.

**Do not leave a hiatus where the rules require a join.** *\*ca api*, *\*asti atra* and *\*gacchāmi iti* are wrong in a text: *cāpi*, *asty atra*, *gacchāmīti*. Write the joined form; the morphemes line undoes it.

**Do not write *\*nagareha* or *\*nagaraiha*.** Locative *-e* before *i* gives *a* and a hiatus: *nagara iha*. Rule 2 (*a* + *i* = *e*) applies only to a real final *a*.

**Do not confuse *-ā* from *-āyai* with the genitive.** *kanyāyā adya* may be *kanyāyai adya* (dative) or *kanyāyāḥ adya* (ablative or genitive, with *ḥ* lost before a vowel). Both are possible joins; the sentence decides.

**Do not undo *-a* before a vowel to the stem.** A word ending in *-a* before a vowel, in a text, can be: *-aḥ* with lost *ḥ* (*bāla iha*), locative *-e* split (*nagara iha*), or a real vocative *-a*, which would have fused (*deva* + *iha* = *deveha*). Since a real final *a* always fuses, a word written with *-a* and a space before a vowel is never a bare stem.

**Do not split *e* or *o* in the wrong place.** *saheha* is *saha iha*, not *\*sah eha*. When *e* or *o* sits at the join, look for *a* at the end of the first word and *i/u* at the start of the second.

**Do not add a space after a fused vowel.** *\*tatra iva* for *tatraiva* is wrong in spacing as well as in sound: the guide writes merged words together (san-01-09).

**Do not forget the avagraha after *e*.** *\*vane tra* hides the word *atra*. Write *vane 'tra*, with ऽ in Devanagari.

## Connections

French elides vowels too (*le ami* → *l'ami*), and Spanish runs vowels together in speech (*la amiga* sounds like *lamiga*), but neither writes the fusion as Sanskrit does. Japanese and Korean avoid vowel clashes inside words in their own ways; Korean *a* + *i* in verb endings (*ga-* + *-a* → *ga*) is a living fusion of the same kind.

## Exercises

1. Join: *ca + iha*, *na + asti*, *tatra + api*, *iti + uktam*, *vane + iha*, *devau + iha*.
2. Join, and explain why the results differ: *kanyā + iha* (singular) and *kanyāḥ + iha*.
3. Split and translate: *naiva*, *caiva*, *neti*, *kanyeva*.
4. Build "'We are going there,' say the boys", and join every boundary.
5. Split and translate *gṛha udyānaṃ ramyam*.
6. Build "Indra lives here in heaven", with *iha*.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*phaleha patataḥ* for "two fruits fall here".
8. Find and fix the error: *\*bāleha krīḍati* for "the boy plays here".
9. Split every join in *na tv evāhaṃ jātu nāsam*.

## Worked answers

1. *ceha* (a + i = e); *nāsti* (a + a = ā); *tatrāpi* (a + a = ā); *ity uktam* (i before u: y); *vana iha* (e before i: a, hiatus); *devāv iha* (au: āv) (this lesson).
2. *kanyeha*: the singular *kanyā* ends in a real *ā*, which fuses with *i* to *e* (rule 2). *kanyā iha*: the plural *kanyāḥ* loses its *ḥ* before a vowel, and the hiatus stays (san-02-06, rule 8).
3. *na eva* "not at all"; *ca eva* "and indeed"; *na iti* "'no' (thus)"; *kanyā iva* "like a girl" (rules 1–2).
4. *tatra gacchāma iti vadanti bālāḥ*. *gacchāmaḥ* before *i* loses its *ḥ*, and the hiatus stays (san-02-06); *iti*, *vadanti*, *bālāḥ* need nothing more. *tatra* before *g*: no change.
5. *gṛhe udyānam ramyam* "In the house (there is) a pleasant garden": locative *-e* before *u* becomes *a* with a hiatus (rule 5); *udyānam* before *r* is *udyānaṃ* (san-02-06).
6. *indraḥ svarge iha vasati*: *indraḥ* before *s* keeps the visarga; *svarge* before *i* becomes *svarga*: *indraḥ svarga iha vasati* (rule 5).
7. The dual *-e* is pragṛhya: *phale iha patataḥ* (rule 7).
8. *bālaḥ* before *i* becomes *bāla*, and the vowels stay apart: *bāla iha krīḍati* (rule 8; san-02-06).
9. *na tu eva aham jātu na āsam*: *tv* is *tu* before a vowel (rule 3), *evāham* is *eva* + *aham* (rule 1), *ahaṃ* is *aham* before *j* (san-02-06), *nāsam* is *na* + *āsam* (rule 1).
