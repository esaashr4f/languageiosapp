## The idea

You met the nominatives in part 2: *aham* "I", *tvam* "you (one)", *āvām* and *yuvām* for pairs, *vayam* and *yūyam* for groups. Now the pronouns take every case: *mām* "me", *mayā* "by me", *mama* "my", *tvayi* "in you".

Do not expect them to look like nouns. Whitney (§491) calls the pronouns of the first and second person "the most irregular and peculiar of all, being made up of fragments coming from various roots". *aham* and *mām* and *vayam* and *asmān* share no stem you could cut out. Sanskrit grammarians gave them artificial stems, *mad*, *asmad*, *tvad*, *yuṣmad*, for use in compounds (Whitney §494), but no form of the paradigm is built on them in a way you could predict. So the glosses treat each form as a whole: *mām* `1SG.ACC`, *tava* `2SG.GEN` (dossier §4).

They have **no gender** (Whitney §491). A woman says *aham* as a man does; "you" is *tvam* to a king or a girl.

They have a second feature you have not met: **enclitic forms**. An **enclitic** is a word with no stress of its own that leans on the word before it (san-02-08 defined the term for *ca* and *vā*). Beside the full forms *mām*, *mama*, *mahyam*, the pronouns have short ones: *mā* "me", *me* "to me, my", *te* "to you, your", *naḥ* "us, to us, our", *vaḥ* "you, to you, your", and the duals *nau*, *vām*. Whitney (§491b) explains that they are unaccented, "and hence they are not allowed to stand at the beginning of a sentence, or elsewhere where any emphasis is laid". English has the same contrast in speech: "give 'em to *me*" against "give 'em to me".

## How it is built

**The forms.** Learn them by number and by row; the table at the end has them all (Wiktionary अहम्, त्वम्; Whitney §491).

- Singular "I": *aham, mām (mā), mayā, mahyam (me), mat or mattaḥ, mama (me), mayi*.
- Singular "you": *tvam, tvām (tvā), tvayā, tubhyam (te), tvat or tvattaḥ, tava (te), tvayi*.
- Dual "we two": *āvām, āvām (nau), āvābhyām, āvābhyām (nau), āvābhyām, āvayoḥ (nau), āvayoḥ*. "You two" goes the same way on *yuva-*: *yuvām*, *yuvābhyām*, *yuvayoḥ*, enclitic *vām*.
- Plural "we": *vayam, asmān (naḥ), asmābhiḥ, asmabhyam (naḥ), asmat or asmattaḥ, asmākam (naḥ), asmāsu*. "You" (plural) on *yuṣma-*: *yūyam, yuṣmān (vaḥ), yuṣmābhiḥ, yuṣmabhyam (vaḥ), yuṣmat or yuṣmattaḥ, yuṣmākam (vaḥ), yuṣmāsu*.

**Patterns that help.** Whitney (§493) points out three:

- The dative in *-bhyam* (*mahyam*, *tubhyam*, *asmabhyam*, *yuṣmabhyam*) is found only here; it is plainly related to the *-bhyām*, *-bhyaḥ* of nouns.
- The ablative ends in *-t* in all numbers (*mat*, *asmat*), the same ending as the a-stem *-āt*. *mattaḥ*, *tvattaḥ* are common alternatives; *-taḥ* "from" is an adverb suffix (san-05-03).
- The plural rests on a stem *asma-*/*yuṣma-* with the same endings as the singular in the nominative, dative and ablative. Whitney calls this "unparalleled elsewhere in the language".

**The enclitics, cell by cell.** *mā*, *tvā*, *nau*, *vām*, *naḥ*, *vaḥ* stand for the accusative. *me*, *te*, *nau*, *vām*, *naḥ*, *vaḥ* stand for the dative and the genitive. There is no enclitic for the nominative, instrumental, ablative or locative.

**Choosing full or short.** Use the full form at the start of a sentence, for contrast ("*my* house, not yours") and after a pause. Use the enclitic everywhere else, especially for "my" and "your": *putras te* "your son", *mitraṃ me* "my friend". In prose the genitive enclitic usually follows its noun or the first word of the sentence.

**Joins.** The pronouns obey the rules you know: *mām* before a consonant is *māṃ*; *naḥ* before a voiced sound is *no*; *te* before *a* takes the avagraha (*te 'pi*); *me* + *iti* is *ma iti* (san-03-07). *mat*, *tvat*, *asmat* end in *t*: until san-04-07 they stand before *k, p, t, s* or at the end; *mattaḥ*, *tvattaḥ* avoid the problem.

**From meaning to form.** "Your son is well."

1. "Your", one person, genitive: *tava*, or the enclitic *te*. Nothing is contrasted, and it will not stand first: *te*.
2. Subject: *putraḥ*. Predicate adjective: *kuśalaḥ* "well" (san-03-05).
3. Order: noun first, enclitic leaning on it: *putraḥ te kuśalaḥ*.
4. Joins: *putraḥ* before *t*: *putras*. *te* before *k*: no change. Result: *putras te kuśalaḥ*.

## Where it applies

- **Every case and number**, with the forms above; no gender distinctions.
- **Enclitics for the object**: *nṛpo no rakṣati*, *paśyati mā*.
- **Enclitics for "my, your, our"**: *putras te*, *mitraṃ me*, *gṛhaṃ naḥ*. This is the commonest use by far.
- **Enclitics for "to me, to you"**: *phalaṃ me dadāti* "he gives me a fruit".
- **Full forms first in a sentence or verse quarter**, and for contrast: *mama gṛhaṃ na tava* "my house, not yours".
- **The genitive with adjectives** (san-03-04): *priyo me* "dear to me", *na me dveṣyaḥ* (Bhagavad Gītā 9.29).
- **The ablative for comparison and source**: *mattaḥ* "from me"; with comparatives, "than me" (san-05-06).
- **The stems *mat-*, *tvat-*, *asmat-*, *yuṣmat-* in compounds** (Whitney §494): *matsakhi* "my friend" in the Veda. You will meet compounds in part 5, and the possessive adjectives *madīya* "my" and *māmaka* in san-07-05.
- **Polite address** does not use *tvam* at all, but *bhavān* "Your Honour" with a third-person verb (san-03-17).
- **Reflexive "myself, yourself"** uses *ātman* and *svayam* (san-03-16), not these forms.

## Watch out

**Never begin a sentence with an enclitic.** *\*me putraḥ kuśalaḥ* and *\*te gṛhaṃ ramyam* break Whitney's rule (§491b). Put the noun first (*putro me kuśalaḥ*) or use the full form (*mama putraḥ kuśalaḥ*).

**Do not use an enclitic where there is contrast.** "You, not me" needs the full forms: *tvāṃ paśyati na mām* "he sees you, not me". *\*tvā paśyati na mā* puts the stress on words that cannot carry it.

**There is no enclitic instrumental, ablative or locative.** *\*me saha* for "with me" is wrong: *mayā saha*.

**Do not confuse *me* with *mām*.** *me* is "to me" or "my", never "me" as object. "He sees me" is *paśyati mām* or, after the first word, *nṛpo mā paśyati* with the enclitic *mā*. *\*nṛpo me paśyati* is wrong in Classical Sanskrit.

**Do not confuse *te* "your" with *te* "they".** The demonstrative *te* (san-03-09) means "they, those" (masculine plural) or "those two" (feminine and neuter dual). *putras te* is "your son" because *te* follows a noun as an enclitic; *te gacchanti* is "they go", with a plural verb and *te* first, where an enclitic could not stand.

**Do not confuse *naḥ* with *na*.** *naḥ* "us, our" has a visarga; *na* "not" has none. Before a voiced sound *naḥ* becomes *no*, so *no* in a text may be "us" or "our": *nṛpo no rakṣati*.

**Do not give *aham* a feminine.** *\*ahā* or *\*tvā* (as a feminine of *tvam*) do not exist. *tvā* is the enclitic accusative.

**Do not use *mat* as a general "my".** *mat* is the ablative "from me" and a compound stem; "my" on its own is *mama* or *me*.

**Watch the old *-ā*-forms of Wiktionary.** The tables list Vedic *tvā* as an instrumental and *me*, *tve* as locatives, footnoted. Classical prose uses *tvayā*, *mayi*, *tvayi*.

## Connections

French and Spanish have the same split between stressed and unstressed pronouns: *moi* against *me*, *mí* against *me*. *Il me voit* uses the clitic, *c'est moi* the full form, as Sanskrit uses *mā* after the first word and *mām* for emphasis.

## Exercises

1. Give the Sanskrit for "by you (one)", "to us (full form)", "from you (several)", "in me", "of you two".
2. Build "My friend is going with you", with the enclitic for "my".
3. Build "We two give you (one) the book."
4. Translate *tava putro mama gṛhe vasati*.
5. Translate *mitraṃ me dhārmikam*, and say why *me* is possible here.
6. Find and fix the error: *\*me gṛhaṃ dūre* for "my house is far away".
7. Find and fix the error: *\*nṛpo me paśyati* for "the king sees me".
8. Find and fix the error: *\*te saha gacchāmi* for "I go with you".

## Worked answers

1. *tvayā*; *asmabhyam*; *yuṣmat* or *yuṣmattaḥ*; *mayi*; *yuvayoḥ* (or the enclitic *vām*) (this lesson).
2. *mitraṃ me tvayā saha gacchati*. *me* leans on the first word; "with you" is instrumental with *saha*, never enclitic. *mitram* before *m* is *mitraṃ* (san-02-06, san-03-01).
3. *āvāṃ tubhyaṃ pustakaṃ dadvaḥ*. *dadvaḥ* is the 1DU of √dā (Wiktionary ददाति). *tubhyam* is the full dative; after the first word *te* would also do: *āvāṃ te pustakaṃ dadvaḥ* (san-03-02).
4. "Your son lives in my house." Two full genitives; *tava* stands first, *mama* is contrastive and full. *putraḥ* before *m* is *putro* (san-02-06).
5. "My friend is righteous." *me* follows the first word, *mitram*, and nothing is contrasted (this lesson).
6. An enclitic cannot stand first: *mama gṛhaṃ dūre*, or *gṛhaṃ me dūre*.
7. *me* is dative or genitive, never accusative: *nṛpo māṃ paśyati*, or with the enclitic accusative, *nṛpo mā paśyati*.
8. There is no enclitic instrumental: *tvayā saha gacchāmi* (san-03-01).
