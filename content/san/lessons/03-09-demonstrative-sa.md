## The idea

Sanskrit has no separate word for "he", "she", "it" or "they". The verb ending already says "third person" (san-02-01), and when a word is needed it borrows a **demonstrative**, a pointing word meaning "that" (gloss DEM). Whitney (§495) describes the stem *ta-* as the simplest demonstrative, "which answers also the purpose of a personal pronoun of the third person".

So one set of forms does three jobs:

- **"he, she, it, they"**, referring back to someone already mentioned: *sa gacchati* "he goes".
- **"that"** before a noun: *sā kanyā* "that girl".
- **"the"** in effect, in narrative, pointing back to a person just introduced: *asti nṛpaḥ ... sa nṛpaḥ ...* "there was a king ... the king ...". Sanskrit has no article; *sa* fills the gap when one is needed.

Its partner **eṣa, eṣā, etad** means "this (here)", pointing at something present to the speaker: *eṣa vyāghraḥ* "here is the tiger!" (Whitney §499b: "the usual demonstrative of nearer position"). It is built on the same stem with *e-* in front, and inflects exactly like *sa*. The dossier glosses it lower case, `this`.

Both have genders, which the personal pronouns did not: *saḥ* (m.), *sā* (f.), *tat* (n.). The pronoun takes the gender of the noun it stands for, not of the person's sex: a pronoun for *mitra* "friend", a neuter noun, is *tat*.

## How it is built

**The stem.** The grammarians give the stem as *tad* (Whitney §497), which is also the neuter nominative; that is the dictionary form. In fact every form is built on *ta-* (*tā-* for the feminine), except the nominative singular masculine and feminine, which have *s-*: *saḥ*, *sā* (Whitney §495a).

**The pronominal endings.** Most cells look like the a-stems and ā-stems of san-03-06: *tam*, *tena*, *tau*, *tābhyām*, *tayoḥ*, *tān*, *taiḥ*, *tebhyaḥ*, *teṣu*; feminine *tām*, *tayā*, *tāḥ*, *tābhiḥ*, *tāsu*. But a few cells have special endings, which Whitney (§495) calls "the general pronominal declension" because many other words share them (*eṣa*, *ya*, *ka*, *sarva*, *anya*: san-05-02, san-06-01, san-06-02). Whitney (§496) lists them:

1. **Neuter nominative and accusative singular in *-t*** (properly *-d*): *tat*, not *\*tam*.
2. **Masculine and neuter dative, ablative and locative singular with *-sma-***: *tasmai*, *tasmāt*, *tasmin*.
3. **Feminine dative, ablative-genitive and locative singular with *-sy-***: *tasyai*, *tasyāḥ*, *tasyām*.
4. **Masculine nominative plural in *-e***: *te*, not *\*tāḥ*.
5. **Genitive plural with *-s-* instead of *-n-***: *teṣām*, *tāsām*, not *\*tānām*.

The dual is "precisely that of noun-stems in a and ā" (Whitney §496b).

**Joining *saḥ* and *eṣaḥ*.** These two forms break the visarga rules of san-02-06. Before any consonant they lose the *-ḥ* completely: *sa gacchati*, *eṣa vyāghraḥ*, *sa paśyati*, even before *k* and *p* (Whitney §176a). Before *a-* they behave like any *-aḥ*: *so 'ham*. Before other vowels, like *-aḥ* too: *sa iha*. At the end of a sentence: *saḥ*. The morphemes line always gives the pausa form *saḥ*.

**Joining *tat*.** *tat* ends in *t*. Its joins come in san-04-07 (*tad gacchati*, *tac ca*, *tan na*). Until then the examples put it before *k, p, t, s* or at the end.

**From meaning to form.** "The hunter sees a deer and strikes it."

1. First clause: *vyādhaḥ mṛgam paśyati*.
2. "It": the deer, masculine singular, object: *tam*.
3. Second clause with *ca* after its first word: *tam ca tāḍayati*.
4. Joins: *vyādhaḥ* before *m*: *vyādho*; *mṛgam* before *p*: *mṛgaṃ*; *tam* before *c*: *taṃ*. Result: *vyādho mṛgaṃ paśyati taṃ ca tāḍayati*.

## Where it applies

- **"He, she, it, they" referring back**: *sa gacchati*, *taṃ tāḍayati*, *tasya putraḥ*. The pronoun is often left out when the verb is enough; use it when the subject changes or needs stress.
- **"That" before a noun**: *sā kanyā*, *tat phalam*, *tasmin gṛhe*.
- **"The" in narrative**: after a person or thing is introduced, *sa* marks it as known: *sa nṛpaḥ* "the king (we have been talking about)". The Hitopadeśa frame story does this: *sa bhūpatiḥ* "that king" (prose after introductory verse 9).
- **With *aham*, *tvam* for emphasis**: *so 'ham* "I here", *sa tvam* "you there", *te vayam* "we here" (Whitney §498).
- **As the answer to a relative**: "who ... he" (*yaḥ ... saḥ*), the commonest sentence frame in the language (san-06-02).
- **Neuter *tat* as "that, it" for a fact or a thing just said**: *tat satyam* "that is true".
- **Adverbs built on *ta-***: *tatra* "there", *tathā* "so", *tadā* "then", *tataḥ* "from there, then" (Whitney §497). *tasmāt*, the ablative, means "therefore".
- **Derivatives and compounds on *tad-***: *tadīya* "his, its", *tattva* "thatness, truth" (Whitney §497); part 5.
- ***eṣa* for what is here and now**: *eṣa vyāghraḥ*, "here comes ...", *eṣa aham* "here I am" (Whitney §499c).

## Watch out

**Do not apply the ordinary visarga rule to *saḥ*.** *\*saḥ paśyati* and *\*so gacchati* follow the rules for *devaḥ*. For *saḥ* and *eṣaḥ* the rule is special: before every consonant, *sa*, *eṣa* (*sa paśyati*, *sa gacchati*, *eṣa vyāghraḥ*).

**Do not decline the demonstrative like a noun.** *\*tāya*, *\*tāt*, *\*te* (as the locative singular) and *\*tānām* apply a-stem endings. The pronoun has *tasmai*, *tasmāt*, *tasmin*, *teṣām*.

**Do not use *tam* for the neuter.** *\*tam phalam* is wrong: the neuter nominative and accusative is *tat*. *tam* is masculine accusative.

**Do not use *tāḥ* for masculine "they".** *\*tāḥ gacchanti* for "they (men) go" uses the feminine plural. Masculine: *te gacchanti*.

**Choose the gender of the noun, not of the person.** If "he" refers back to *mitra* "friend", a neuter noun, the pronoun is neuter: *tat*. A pronoun for *kanyā* is *sā*, for *nṛpa* *saḥ*.

**Do not confuse the demonstrative *te* with the enclitic *te*.** At the head of a clause and with a plural verb, *te* is "they". After a noun, it may be "your" (san-03-08). Also, *te* is the feminine and neuter dual: *te kanye* "those two girls".

**Do not overuse it.** English needs "he" in every clause; Sanskrit does not. *\*sa nṛpaḥ sa gacchati sa paśyati* is heavy. Once the subject is clear, the verb alone carries it: *nṛpo gacchati paśyati ca*.

**Do not join *saḥ* with a following vowel in prose.** In some verse, especially the Veda, *sa* fuses with the next vowel (Whitney §176b: *sa* + *eṣaḥ*); Classical prose keeps *sa iha*, *sa uvāca*.

## Connections

Latin made its third-person pronouns the same way, from demonstratives (*is*, *ille*), and *ille* became Spanish *él* and French *il*, and also the articles *el*, *le*. Sanskrit *sa nṛpaḥ* "the king" is that process caught at the beginning. Greek *ho*, *hē*, *to* (the article) is the same old pronoun as *sa*, *sā*, *tad*, with *s-* in the masculine and feminine nominative and *t-* elsewhere.

## Exercises

1. Give the Sanskrit for "to her", "from him", "of them (women)", "in that (neuter thing)", "by those (men)".
2. Build "That boy plays with her."
3. Build "Here is the vulture; it lives in the hollow of the tree."
4. Translate *tasya gṛhe vānarās tiṣṭhanti*, and say which noun *tasya* could refer to.
5. Translate *te mṛgāḥ sarpebhyas trasyanti*.
6. Find and fix the error: *\*saḥ gacchati* (in a text).
7. Find and fix the error: *\*tānām gṛham* for "their house".
8. Find and fix the error: *\*tam phalaṃ khādati* for "he eats that fruit".

## Worked answers

1. *tasyai*; *tasmāt*; *tāsām*; *tasmin*; *taiḥ* (this lesson).
2. *sa bālas tayā saha krīḍati*. *saḥ* drops its visarga before *b*; *bālaḥ* before *t* is *bālas*; "with her" is the feminine instrumental with *saha* (san-03-01).
3. *eṣa gṛdhraḥ | sa vṛkṣasya koṭare vasati*. *eṣa* and *sa* lose their visarga before consonants; *gṛdhraḥ* stands before the daṇḍa, in pausa. In the Hitopadeśa an old vulture lives in exactly such a hollow (book 1).
4. "Monkeys are standing (that is, staying) in his house." *tasya* is masculine or neuter singular: "his", or "its", referring back to some man or to a masculine or neuter noun mentioned before. *vānarāḥ* before *t* is *vānarās* (san-02-06).
5. "Those deer are afraid of snakes", or "they, the deer, ...". *te* agrees with *mṛgāḥ*, masculine plural; *-ebhyaḥ* before *t* is *-ebhyas* (san-02-06, san-03-03).
6. *saḥ* loses its visarga before any consonant: *sa gacchati* (Whitney §176a).
7. The genitive plural of the pronoun has *-ṣām*: *teṣāṃ gṛham* (Whitney §496c).
8. The neuter is *tat*: *tat phalaṃ khādati*. *tat* before *ph* keeps its *t* (this lesson).
