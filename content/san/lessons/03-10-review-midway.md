## Before you start

This review covers the first half of part 3: the instrumental and the n-to-ṇ rule (san-03-01), the dative (san-03-02), the ablative (san-03-03), the genitive (san-03-04), the locative and the s-to-ṣ rule (san-03-05), the a- and ā-stems in full (san-03-06), vowel sandhi (san-03-07), the personal pronouns with their enclitics (san-03-08), and the demonstratives *sa* and *eṣa* (san-03-09). Part 2 is assumed throughout, especially the visarga joins of san-02-06.

Do each exercise before reading its answer. Every answer names the lesson to reread if you went wrong. Final consonants other than *-ḥ* and *-m* are still kept where they do not change (san-04-07).

## Exercises

1. Translate lines 1 and 2. What do *gaṅgāyāḥ* and *tasya* each depend on?
2. Translate line 3. Explain *sa* (not *saḥ*), *'ndho* and *vṛddhaś*.
3. Translate line 4. Name the case of *kṛpayā* and of *tasmai*, and explain *tasmā āhāram*.
4. Translate line 5. Why is *teṣām* not *\*tānām*, and why can *śāvakān* stand before *k* unchanged?
5. Translate lines 6 and 7. Why does the cat go *vṛkṣam* but the chicks fear *mārjārāt*?
6. Translate line 8. Which words are the cat's own, and why is it *te* and not *tava*?
7. Translate line 9, which has no verb. What does the locative *mārjāre* express?
8. Translate lines 10 and 11. Name each locative and say whether it is place or time.
9. Translate line 12. Find the n-to-ṇ rule at work, and say what *hi* does.
10. Retell line 4 in the singular: "a bird lives in the tree; out of pity it gives him food."
11. Build "The chicks live with the vulture in the hollow."
12. Build "'I am your friend,' the cat says to the birds", with an enclitic for "your".
13. Build "From fear of the cat the birds leave the tree."
14. Build "The girl gives the god a garland", first with the dative, then with the genitive in its place.
15. Join: *vane + atra*, *kanyā + iha*, *kanyāḥ + iha*, *tatra + eva*, *phale + iti*, *tau + api*.
16. Split and identify: *cāpi*, *nagara iha*, *gacchāmīti*, *asty atra*, *devāv iha*.
17. Find and fix the error: *\*nṛpo vīrena saha gacchati*.
18. Find and fix the error: *\*saḥ vyādhaḥ mṛgān vanaṃ tāḍayati* for "that hunter strikes the deer in the forest".
19. Find and fix the error: *\*me gṛhaṃ dūre*.
20. Find and fix the error: *\*mṛgā vyāghreṇa trasyanti*.
21. Find and fix the error: *\*tam phalaṃ madhuram*.
22. Find and fix the error: *\*kanyāḥ latāsu puṣpāni harati* for "the girl takes flowers from the creepers".
23. Explain the difference between *tasyāḥ puṣpam* and *latāyāḥ puṣpaṃ patati*.
24. Give the locative plural of *vṛkṣa* and *śākhā*, and explain the difference in the sibilant.

## Worked answers

1. "On the bank of the Ganges there is a tree. In its hollow lives a vulture." *gaṅgāyāḥ* is genitive, depending on *tīre* "the bank (of the Ganges)" (san-03-04); before *t* it becomes *gaṅgāyās* (san-02-06). *tasya* refers back to the tree, masculine *vṛkṣa*, and depends on *koṭare* (san-03-09). *asti* opens the story (san-02-07).
2. "That vulture is blind and old." *saḥ* drops its visarga before any consonant (san-03-09). *andhaḥ* after *gṛdhro*: *-aḥ* before *a-* gives *-o* and the *a* is lost, so *gṛdhro 'ndho* (san-02-06); *andho* is *andhaḥ* before the voiced *v*. *vṛddhaś* is *vṛddhaḥ* before *c*. *ca* stands after the last word it joins (san-02-08).
3. "Birds live in the tree. Out of pity they give him food." *kṛpayā* is an instrumental of cause (san-03-01); *tasmai* a dative of the receiver (san-03-02, san-03-09). *tasmai* + *āhāram*: final *ai* before a vowel becomes *ā*, and the vowels stay apart (san-03-07). *dadati* is the 3PL of √dā (san-03-02).
4. "And he protects their chicks from the crows." The pronoun's genitive plural has *-ṣām*, not *-nām* (san-03-09). *-n* does not change before *k* (san-04-07 will give all the cases). *kākebhyaḥ* is ablative "from the crows" (san-03-03), *-o* before *r* (san-02-06).
5. "One day a cat goes to the tree. The chicks are afraid of the cat." Destination of going: accusative (san-02-02). Fear: ablative (san-03-03), and *-āt* before *t* is unchanged.
6. "'I am your friend,' the cat says to the vulture." The cat's words are *ahaṃ te mitram*, closed by *iti* (san-02-09). Verbs of speaking can take the person spoken to in the accusative (Whitney §274c). *te* is the enclitic genitive, possible because it leans on *aham*; nothing is contrasted (san-03-08).
7. "The foolish vulture trusts the cat", literally "the foolish vulture's trust (is) in the cat". *viśvāsa* takes the locative (mw1899; san-03-05); the adjective *mūrkhasya* agrees with *gṛdhrasya* (san-03-06).
8. "At night the cat eats the chicks in the hollow. At daybreak the birds blame the vulture himself." *niśāyām* and *prabhāte* are locatives of time, *koṭare* of place (san-03-05). *eva* stresses *gṛdhram*: they blame him, not the cat (san-02-08).
9. "For through the cat's fault the vulture Jaradgava was killed." *doṣeṇa*: *ṣ*, then *e*, then *n*: the rule applies (san-03-01); an instrumental of cause. *hi* "for" gives the reason for a rule stated before it (san-05-04).
10. *vṛkṣe vihago vasati | sa kṛpayā tasmā āhāraṃ dadāti*. Singular verb *dadāti*; *saḥ* before *k* is *sa* (san-03-09); *vihagaḥ* before *v* is *vihago* (san-02-06).
11. *śāvakā gṛdhreṇa saha koṭare vasanti*. Companion with *saha* (san-03-01); *gṛdhra* has *r*, so *gṛdhreṇa*; place in the locative (san-03-05).
12. *ahaṃ vo mitram iti vadati mārjāro vihagān*. Several birds, so the plural "your", enclitic *vaḥ*, leaning on *aham*; before *m* it becomes *vo* (san-03-08, san-02-06). *vihagān* ends the sentence, so its *-n* meets nothing (san-04-07).
13. *vihagā vṛkṣaṃ tyajanti mārjārasya bhayāt*. *bhayāt* "out of fear" is a causal ablative (san-03-03); *mārjārasya* is an objective genitive, "fear of the cat" (san-03-04). *bhayāt* stands last, where its *t* does not change (san-04-07).
14. *kanyā devāya mālāṃ dadāti*, and with the genitive in place of the dative, *kanyā devasya mālāṃ dadāti* (san-03-02, san-03-04).
15. *vane 'tra* (e + a, avagraha); *kanyeha* (ā + i = e); *kanyā iha* (lost *ḥ*: hiatus); *tatraiva* (a + e = ai); *phale iti* (dual *-e* never joins); *tāv api* (au: āv) (san-03-07, san-02-06).
16. *ca api*; *nagare iha* (locative *-e* split); *gacchāmi iti*; *asti atra*; *devau iha* (san-03-07).
17. In *vīra* only a vowel stands between the *r* and the ending's *n*: nothing blocks, so *vīreṇa*. *nṛpo vīreṇa saha gacchati* (san-03-01).
18. Three corrections: *sa* before a consonant (san-03-09); *vyādhaḥ* before *m* is *vyādho* (san-02-06); place in the forest is locative, *vane* (san-03-05). *sa vyādho mṛgān vane tāḍayati*. *mṛgān* before *v* is unchanged.
19. An enclitic cannot begin a sentence: *mama gṛhaṃ dūre* or *gṛhaṃ me dūre* (san-03-08).
20. Fear takes the ablative: *mṛgā vyāghrāt trasyanti* (san-03-03).
21. The neuter of the demonstrative is *tat*: *tat phalaṃ madhuram* (san-03-09).
22. One girl: *kanyā*, singular; separation: ablative *latābhyaḥ*; *puṣpa* has *ṣ*, so *puṣpāṇi* (san-03-01, san-03-03). *kanyā latābhyaḥ puṣpāṇi harati*.
23. *tasyāḥ puṣpam* is "her flower": *tasyāḥ* is genitive. In *latāyāḥ puṣpaṃ patati*, *latāyāḥ* is ablative or genitive, and the verb of falling makes "from the creeper" the natural reading (san-03-03, san-03-04).
24. *vṛkṣeṣu*, *śākhāsu*. The *s* of *-su* becomes *ṣ* after any vowel except *a* and *ā* (here *e*), but stays *s* after *ā* (Whitney §180; san-03-05).
