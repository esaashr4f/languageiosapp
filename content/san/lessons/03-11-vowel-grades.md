## The idea

San-00-02 promised that Sanskrit builds words from roots by changing the root's vowel. This lesson gives that idea its full form, because the second half of part 3 depends on it. Whitney (§235) calls the two changes, **guṇa** and **vṛddhi**, "the most regular and frequent of vowel-changes, being of constant occurrence both in inflection and in derivation".

Every vowel has up to three **grades**, three strengths:

- the **simple** vowel: *i*, *u*, *ṛ* (and *a*);
- **guṇa** ("secondary quality"), made by putting *a* in front: *a* + *i* = *e*, *a* + *u* = *o*, *a* + *ṛ* = *ar*;
- **vṛddhi** ("growth"), made by putting a second *a* in front: *ā* + *i* = *ai*, *ā* + *u* = *au*, *ā* + *ṛ* = *ār*.

Whitney (§235a) gives exactly this definition. The pairs are the vowel joins of san-03-07 again: *a* + *i* made *e* between words, and it makes *e* inside a word. *a* is its own guṇa; its vṛddhi is *ā*.

Once you know the grades, a great deal of apparent irregularity disappears. *veda* "knowledge" is √vid with guṇa; *bhavati* is √bhū with guṇa; *daiva* "divine" is *deva* with vṛddhi; *pitaram* is *pitṛ* with guṇa (san-03-14); *agnaye* is *agni* with guṇa (san-03-12). The same three steps run through nouns, verbs and derivatives alike.

## How it is built

**The series** (Whitney §236):

- *a, ā*: guṇa *a*, vṛddhi *ā*
- *i, ī*: guṇa *e*, vṛddhi *ai*
- *u, ū*: guṇa *o*, vṛddhi *au*
- *ṛ*: guṇa *ar*, vṛddhi *ār*
- *ḷ*: guṇa *al* (only in √kḷp)

**Before a vowel.** A guṇa or vṛddhi *e, o, ai, au* that comes to stand before a vowel inside a word splits as in san-03-07: *e* becomes *ay*, *o* becomes *av*, *ai* becomes *āy*, *au* becomes *āv* (Whitney §131). Inside a word the *y* and *v* stay. So √bhū with guṇa is *bho-*, and with the present suffix *-a-* it becomes *bhava-*: *bhavati*. √nī "lead": guṇa *ne-*, *naya-*: *nayati*. √ji "conquer": *je-*, *jaya-*: *jayati*.

**Where guṇa appears.**

1. **In verb stems.** The commonest present stem, class 1, puts the root in guṇa: *bhū* → *bhavati*, *smṛ* → *smarati*, *budh* → *bodhati*, *ruh* → *rohati*, *tṝ* → *tarati*, *kṛṣ* → *karṣati*. Class 6 does not: *viś* → *viśati*, *spṛś* → *spṛśati*, *kṛt* → *kṛntati*. That is the difference between the two classes (san-04-01).
2. **In nouns made from roots**: *veda* (√vid "know"), *yoga* (√yuj "join"), *bhaya* (√bhī "fear"), *bodha* "understanding" (√budh), *jaya* "victory" (√ji), *kara* "hand, doer" (√kṛ).
3. **In noun endings** of the i-, u- and ṛ-stems: *agnaye*, *guroḥ*, *pitaram* (san-03-12, san-03-14).

**Where vṛddhi appears.**

1. **In derived words, on the first syllable** (Whitney §239b): a word meaning "of X, descended from X, belonging to X" puts the first vowel of X in vṛddhi. *deva* → *daiva* "divine"; *putra* → *pautra* "grandson"; *tila* "sesame" → *taila* "oil"; *śiva* → *śaiva* "of Śiva"; *manu* → *mānava* "human, descendant of Manu"; *vidyā* → *vaidya* "learned man, physician"; *kuśala* → *kauśala* "skill"; *pṛthivī* "earth" → *pārthiva* "earthly, king". With a suffix *-ya*: *madhura* → *mādhurya* "sweetness". You know several of these pairs from part 1.
2. **In some nouns from roots**: *bhāva* "being, state" (√bhū), *kāra* "act, maker" (√kṛ).
3. **In some verb stems**: class 10 *tāḍayati* (√taḍ), *dhārayati* (√dhṛ "hold"); and in the strong cases of some nouns: *dātāram* (san-03-14), *sakhāyam* (san-03-19).

**The exception for heavy syllables.** Whitney (§240) states that guṇa "does not usually take place in a heavy syllable ending with a consonant": a short vowel before two consonants, or a long vowel not at the end of the root. So √nind "blame" gives *nindati*, not *\*nendati*; √jīv "live" gives *jīvati*; √krīḍ *krīḍati*. But a final long vowel takes guṇa: *nī* → *nayati*. Vṛddhi has no such restriction (§240a).

**Which way the history runs.** Sanskrit grammar, Indian and European, teaches the simple vowel as basic and guṇa and vṛddhi as "raisings". Whitney (§237) warns that this is a point of view. The *ṛ* "has long been so clearly seen to come by abbreviation or weakening from an earlier *ar*" that many European grammarians start from *bhar*, *kar*, *vardh* and treat *bhṛ*, *kṛ*, *vṛdh* as reduced. In history the full grade is often the older one, and the simple vowel is what remains when the syllable lost its stress: Whitney (§238) connects guṇa with "an accent on the increased syllable". This guide teaches raising because it predicts forms well; remember that it is a rule of thumb, not the history. Whitney (§239) adds that vṛddhi, unlike guṇa, is "specifically Indian" in his view.

**From meaning to form.** "The physician gives the child medicine."

1. "Physician": *vaidya*, from *vidyā* "knowledge" by vṛddhi of the first syllable (*i* → *ai*): the learned man.
2. "Medicine": *auṣadha*, from *oṣadhi* "herb" by vṛddhi (*o* → *au*); *auṣadha* is in the part 1 vocabulary, *oṣadhi* is an i-stem.
3. Verb: √dā, *dadāti*. Receiver in the dative: *bālāya*.
4. Order and joins: *vaidyaḥ bālāya auṣadham dadāti*. *vaidyaḥ* before *b*: *vaidyo*. *bālāya* + *auṣadham*: *a* + *au* = *au*, one word (san-03-07). *auṣadham* before *d*: *auṣadhaṃ*. Result: *vaidyo bālāyauṣadhaṃ dadāti*.

## Where it applies

- **Present stems**: class 1 with guṇa (*bhavati*, *jayati*, *smarati*, *bodhati*); class 6 without (*viśati*, *spṛśati*); class 10 with guṇa or vṛddhi or neither, to be learned with each root (*corayati* from √cur "steal" with guṇa, *tāḍayati* with vṛddhi, *kathayati* with neither). Part 4 sets out all ten classes.
- **Other verb forms**: the future, the infinitive and many others take guṇa (*kartum* from √kṛ, san-04-16); the causative often takes vṛddhi (*kārayati*, san-07-01); the perfect and the aorist alternate between grades within one paradigm.
- **Primary nouns from roots**: *veda*, *yoga*, *bhaya*, *bodha*, *jaya*, *bhāva*, *kāra*.
- **Secondary derivatives** with vṛddhi of the first syllable: *daiva*, *pautra*, *taila*, *śaiva*, *mānava*, *vaidya*, *pārthiva*, *kauśala*, *gaurava*, *lāghava* "lightness" (from *laghu*), *saumya* "gentle" (from *soma*), *śauca* "purity" (from *śuci*), *auṣadha*, *aihika* (from *iha*). Whitney (§239b) gives *mānasa* from *manas* and *bhauma* "earthly" from *bhūmi*. Part 7 teaches this derivation in full.
- **Noun endings**: guṇa in the i- and u-stems (*agnaye*, *agneḥ*, *gurave*, *guroḥ*) and in the ṛ-stems (*pitaram*, *pitari*); vṛddhi in agent nouns (*dātāram*) and in *sakhi* (*sakhāyam*). San-03-12 to san-03-19 show each.
- **Vowel sandhi**: *a* + *i* = *e* between words (san-03-07) is the same addition that makes guṇa.

## Watch out

**Do not apply guṇa to every verb.** *\*veśati*, *\*sparśati* are wrong: class 6 keeps the simple vowel (*viśati*, *spṛśati*). The class is in the vocabulary entry.

**Do not apply guṇa to a heavy syllable.** *\*nendati*, *\*jevati* break Whitney's rule (§240): *nindati*, *jīvati*.

**Do not confuse guṇa with vṛddhi.** *\*deiva* or *\*deva* for "divine" is wrong: the derivative takes vṛddhi, *daiva*. And *e* is guṇa of *i*, not vṛddhi: the vṛddhi of *i* is *ai*.

**Do not treat *e*, *o* as simple vowels.** They are guṇa vowels, and inside words before a vowel they show their halves: *bho-* + *a* = *bhava-*, not *\*bhoa-*.

**Do not put vṛddhi on the wrong syllable.** In secondary derivatives it goes on the first syllable: *putra* → *pautra*, not *\*putrā*. *gaurava* is a special case with two changes, one on each syllable.

**The guṇa of *a* is *a*.** Roots with *a* show no change at the guṇa grade: √vad gives *vadati*, √pac *pacati*. Do not invent *\*vādati*. Their vṛddhi is *ā*: *vāda* "speech", *pāka* "cooking".

**Do not take "raising" as history.** In the reconstructed parent language the vowel *e/o* (Sanskrit *a*) of the full grade is often older than the simple vowel (Whitney §237). Use the rule to predict forms; do not use it to tell the history of a word.

## Connections

English *sing, sang, sung* and *ride, rode* show the same Indo-European alternation of vowel grades (ablaut) that lies behind guṇa; Latin *tegō* "I cover" beside *toga*, and Greek *leípō, léloipa, élipon* "leave" are other traces. Arabic changes vowels inside roots too, but its patterns are a different system: in Arabic the consonants are the root and the vowels the pattern, while in Sanskrit the vowel itself belongs to the root and steps up or down.

## Exercises

1. Give the guṇa and the vṛddhi of the root vowel in √nī, √budh, √kṛ, √vad.
2. Form class 1 present stems (3SG) from √ji, √ruh, √tṝ and √nī, and explain each.
3. Why is the 3SG of √nind *nindati* and not *\*nendati*?
4. Give the vṛddhi derivative meaning "of, from" for *śiva*, *putra*, *manu*, and say which syllable changes.
5. Split *vaidyo bālāyauṣadhaṃ dadāti* into its words and name every grade you see.
6. Translate *mānavāḥ pārthivaṃ pūjayanti*, and explain both nouns.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*sarpo gṛhaṃ veśati*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*deiva* for "divine".

## Worked answers

1. √nī: guṇa *ne*, vṛddhi *nai*. √budh: *bodh*, *baudh*. √kṛ: *kar*, *kār*. √vad: *vad* (guṇa of *a* is *a*), *vād* (this lesson; Whitney §236).
2. *jayati*: *ji* → *je*, *e* before *a* → *ay*. *rohati*: *ruh* → *roh*. *tarati*: *tṝ* → *tar*. *nayati*: *nī* → *ne* → *nay* (this lesson).
3. *nind* has a short vowel before two consonants, a heavy syllable; guṇa does not apply there (Whitney §240).
4. *śaiva*, *pautra*, *mānava*: the first syllable takes vṛddhi (*i* → *ai*, *u* → *au*, *a* → *ā*) (Whitney §239b).
5. *vaidyaḥ bālāya auṣadham dadāti*. *vaidya*: vṛddhi of *vidyā*. *auṣadha*: vṛddhi of *oṣadhi*. *dadāti*: the root *dā* has *ā*, which no grade changes (this lesson; san-03-07 for the join).
6. "The people honour the king." *mānava* "human" is the vṛddhi derivative of *manu*, the first man; *pārthiva* "earthly", hence "lord of the earth, king", is the vṛddhi derivative of *pṛthivī* "earth" (*ṛ* → *ār*). *pūjayanti* is class 10 from a heavy syllable, unchanged (san-02-03 vocabulary).
7. √viś is class 6: no guṇa. *sarpo gṛhaṃ viśati*.
8. The vṛddhi of *e* (from *deva*) is *ai*: *daiva*.
