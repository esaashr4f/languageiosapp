## The idea

The a-stems are the largest class, but not the only one. The next are nouns whose stem ends in *-i* or *-u*: *agni* "fire" (m.), *mati* "thought" (f.), *vāri* "water" (n.); *guru* "teacher" (m.), *dhenu* "cow" (f.), *madhu* "honey" (n.). Many words you already know belong here: *kavi*, *ṛṣi*, *nīti*, *śānti*, *mati*, *guru*, *laghu*, *aṇu*.

The two classes are mirror images. Whatever the i-stems do with *i*, *y*, *e*, *ay*, the u-stems do with *u*, *v*, *o*, *av*. Learn one and you have the other.

What holds the paradigm together is the vowel grades of san-03-11. In several cases the stem vowel appears in guṇa: *agnaye* "to the fire" is *agne-* + *-e*, with *e* split to *ay* before the vowel; *guroḥ* "of the teacher" is *guro-* + *-s*. Whitney (§238b) gives *mataye* from *mati* and *bhānavaḥ* from *bhānu* as examples of guṇa "in formative elements". The pattern is regular, and it is the reason these nouns look so different from the a-stems: their endings are mostly the normal endings of san-03-06, but added to a stem that changes its grade.

## How it is built

**Masculine *agni* and *guru*.** Take the stem, and in each case choose the grade:

- **Simple stem + consonant ending**: *agni-bhyām*, *agni-bhiḥ*, *agni-bhyaḥ*, *agni-ṣu* (with *ṣ* after *i*, san-03-05); *guru-bhiḥ*, *guru-ṣu*. Also the nominative *-s* and accusative *-m*: *agniḥ*, *agnim*; *guruḥ*, *gurum*.
- **Guṇa + vowel ending**: dative *agnaye*, *gurave*; nominative plural *agnayaḥ*, *guravaḥ*.
- **Guṇa + *-s***: ablative and genitive *agneḥ*, *guroḥ*. The vocative is the bare guṇa stem: *agne*, *guro*.
- **Lengthened vowel**: accusative plural *agnīn*, *gurūn*; genitive plural with *n* inserted, *agnīnām*, *gurūṇām* (n-to-ṇ after *r*, san-03-01).
- **Semivowel before a vowel**: genitive and locative dual *agnyoḥ*, *gurvoḥ*.
- **Special forms**: instrumental singular with *n*: *agninā*, *guruṇā*; locative singular in *-au*: *agnau*, *gurau*; nominative, accusative and vocative dual with a long vowel: *agnī*, *gurū*.

**Feminine *mati* and *dhenu*.** Exactly as the masculine, with three differences (Wiktionary मति, धेनु):

1. Instrumental singular *matyā*, *dhenvā*: semivowel + *ā*, no *n*.
2. Accusative plural *matīḥ*, *dhenūḥ*, not *-īn*, *-ūn*.
3. In the dative, ablative-genitive and locative singular the feminines may also take the endings of the ī-stems (san-03-13): *matyai* beside *mataye*, *matyāḥ* beside *mateḥ*, *matyām* beside *matau*. Both sets are Classical.

**Neuter *vāri* and *madhu*.** Before a vowel ending the neuter inserts *n*: *vāriṇā*, *vāriṇe*, *vāriṇaḥ*, *vāriṇi*; *madhunā*, *madhune*, *madhunaḥ*, *madhuni*; dual *vāriṇī*, *madhunī*; plural *vārīṇi*, *madhūni*. The nominative and accusative singular are the bare stem: *vāri*, *madhu*. Before consonant endings it is like the masculine: *vāribhiḥ*, *madhuṣu*. Note the ṇ in *vāri-* (after *r*) and the *n* in *madhu-*.

**Joins.** The new final sounds behave as you know: *-iḥ*, *-uḥ*, *-eḥ*, *-oḥ* are "other vowels" before a visarga, so before a voiced sound they become *-ir*, *-ur*, *-er*, *-or* (san-02-06): *agnir jvalati*, *guror vacanam*. The dual *-ī*, *-ū* are pragṛhya and never join (san-03-07): *kavī iha*. A final *-i*, *-u* elsewhere becomes *y*, *v* before a vowel: *vāry atra*; *-e* and *-o* of the vocative follow san-03-07.

**Adjectives in *-u*.** *guru* "heavy", *laghu* "light", *mṛdu* "soft", *bahu* "much", *sādhu* "good" inflect like *guru* in the masculine and like *madhu* in the neuter. Their feminine is either like *dhenu* or a separate ī-stem (*gurvī*, *bahvī*, *sādhvī*), which san-03-13 teaches.

**From meaning to form.** "He pours ghee into the fire."

1. Verb: √kṣip "throw", *kṣipati*. Object: *ghṛtam*.
2. Into the fire: where the ghee comes to rest, so locative (san-03-05). *agni*, locative singular: *agnau*.
3. Order and joins: *agnau ghṛtam kṣipati*; *agnau* before *gh*: no change; *ghṛtam* before *k*: *ghṛtaṃ*. Result: *agnau ghṛtaṃ kṣipati*.

## Where it applies

- **Masculine i-stems**: *agni*, *muni*, *kavi*, *ṛṣi*, *giri*, *ari*, *kapi*.
- **Masculine u-stems**: *guru*, *śatru*, *vāyu*, *paśu*, *bandhu*, *mṛtyu*.
- **Feminine i-stems**: very many abstract nouns in *-ti* (*mati*, *nīti*, *śānti*, *bhakti*, *kīrti*, *buddhi*), and *bhūmi*, *rātri*, *oṣadhi*.
- **Feminine u-stems**: *dhenu*, *rajju*; fewer than the i-stems.
- **Neuters**: *vāri*, *madhu*, *aśru*; few, but common. A few neuters (*akṣi* "eye", *asthi* "bone", *dadhi* "curds") are irregular: their weak cases come from a stem in *-n* (*dadhnā*), and they are not taught here.
- **Adjectives in *-i* and *-u***: *laghu*, *guru*, *bahu*, *mṛdu*, *sādhu*; *śuci* "pure". Masculine like *guru*, neuter like *madhu*; for the neuter adjective, Wiktionary also prints masculine-type forms (*gurave*, *guroḥ*) as alternatives in the oblique cases.
- **The last member of many compounds**, and the masculine nouns of agent in *-i*.
- **Not *pati* and *sakhi***, which have irregular forms (san-03-19), except *pati* at the end of a compound (*nṛpati*, *bhūpati*), which is regular.

## Watch out

**Do not give the masculine instrumental to a feminine.** *\*matinā*, *\*dhenunā* are wrong: the feminine is *matyā*, *dhenvā*. Only masculines and neuters have *-nā*.

**Do not give the masculine accusative plural to a feminine.** *\*dhenūn* is wrong: *dhenūḥ*. Masculine *gurūn*, feminine *dhenūḥ*.

**Do not join the dual *-ī*, *-ū*.** *\*kavyiha* or *\*kavīha* for "two poets here" ignores the pragṛhya rule: *kavī iha*.

**Do not read *-eḥ* and *-oḥ* as anything but ablative or genitive singular.** *agneḥ* is "of the fire" or "from the fire", never a nominative. The nominative is *agniḥ*.

**Do not confuse *-ayaḥ* with *-aya-* in verbs.** *munayaḥ* "sages" and *corayati* "steals" share a look but nothing else. The noun ending stands at the end of a word, after a guṇa stem.

**Do not forget the inserted *n* in the neuter.** *\*vārau*, *\*madhoḥ* use the masculine pattern. For neuter nouns use *vāriṇi*, *madhunaḥ*.

**Do not forget the retroflex rules.** *gurūṇām*, *vārīṇi*, *vāriṇā* (after *r*); *agniṣu*, *guruṣu* (s after *i*, *u*). But *agnīnām* and *madhūnām*: no trigger.

**Do not treat *-i* and *-ī* stems alike.** *mati* (short *i*) and *nadī* (long *ī*) have different nominatives (*matiḥ*, *nadī*) and many different cases; *nadī* comes in san-03-13.

**Do not use Vedic forms from the tables.** Wiktionary footnotes *agnā* (locative) and *matī* (instrumental) as Vedic.

## Connections

Latin's fourth declension (*manus*, *manūs*) and the i-stems of the third (*ignis* "fire", cognate with *agni*) are the European relatives of these. Spanish and French keep the word *mente*, *mens*, from the same root as *mati* (√man "think"), in adverbs like *rápidamente* "with a quick mind".

## Exercises

1. Decline *muni* in the singular and *śatru* in the plural.
2. Give the instrumental and accusative plural of *bhūmi* and of *giri*, and explain the difference.
3. Build "The monkeys sit on the mountain."
4. Build "The enemies are afraid of the wind and the fire", with the ablative.
5. Translate *bandhavo guroḥ kīrtiṃ smaranti*.
6. Translate *vāyunā vṛkṣāḥ patanti*, and name the use of the instrumental.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*munir matinā jayati*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*gurū iha* for "the teacher here".

## Worked answers

1. *muniḥ, munim, muninā, munaye, muneḥ, muneḥ, munau, mune*. *śatravaḥ, śatrūn, śatrubhiḥ, śatrubhyaḥ, śatrubhyaḥ, śatrūṇām, śatruṣu, śatravaḥ* (this lesson; *ṇ* after *r* in *śatrūṇām*, *ṣ* after *u*).
2. *bhūmyā*, *bhūmīḥ* (feminine); *giriṇā*, *girīn* (masculine; *ṇ* after *r*). The feminine has no *n* in the instrumental and *-īḥ* in the accusative plural (this lesson).
3. *kapayo girau tiṣṭhanti* (or *sīdanti*). Nominative plural with guṇa; locative *-au* (this lesson; san-03-05). *kapayaḥ* before *g* is *kapayo*.
4. *śatravo vāyor agneś ca trasyanti*. The ablatives of the u-stem and the i-stem are *vāyoḥ*, *agneḥ*. *-oḥ* before the vowel *a* becomes *-or*; *-eḥ* before *c* becomes *-eś* (san-02-06, san-03-03).
5. "The relatives remember the teacher's fame." *bandhavaḥ*, nominative plural with guṇa, before *g* is *bandhavo*; *guroḥ* genitive; *kīrtim* accusative singular feminine (this lesson).
6. "The trees fall because of the wind", or "by the wind". An instrumental of cause or means (san-03-01).
7. *mati* is feminine: *matyā*. *munir matyā jayati* "the sage wins by thought" (this lesson).
8. *gurū* is the dual. The singular nominative is *guruḥ*, which before a vowel becomes *gurur*: *gurur iha* (san-02-06).
