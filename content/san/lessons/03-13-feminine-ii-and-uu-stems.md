## The idea

The ā-stems are not the only feminines. A second large class ends in long *-ī*: *nadī* "river", *devī* "goddess", *nārī* "woman", *patnī* "wife", *pṛthivī* "earth". A smaller one ends in long *-ū*: *vadhū* "bride, wife", *śvaśrū* "mother-in-law", *camū* "army".

These stems are almost all feminine, and many of them are made from another word. *devī* is the feminine of *deva*, *kumārī* of *kumāra*, *putrī* of *putra*, *brāhmaṇī* of *brāhmaṇa*. Adjectives do the same: *sundara* "beautiful" has the feminine *sundarī*, *guru* "heavy" *gurvī*, *mṛdu* "soft" *mṛdvī*. So this lesson is also the lesson on how Sanskrit makes feminines.

The declension itself has one simple logic. Before a vowel ending, *ī* becomes *y* and *ū* becomes *v*, exactly as in vowel sandhi (san-03-07): *nadī* + *-ai* = *nadyai*, *vadhū* + *-ai* = *vadhvai*. Before a consonant ending the long vowel stays: *nadībhiḥ*, *vadhūbhiḥ*. The endings are the feminine set you saw in the "optional" forms of *mati* in san-03-12: *-ai*, *-āḥ*, *-ām* in the dative, ablative-genitive and locative singular.

## How it is built

**The *nadī* type** (Wiktionary नदी, देवी):

- Singular: *nadī*, *nadīm*, *nadyā*, *nadyai*, *nadyāḥ* (ablative and genitive), *nadyām*, vocative *nadi* (short).
- Dual: *nadyau*, *nadībhyām*, *nadyoḥ*.
- Plural: *nadyaḥ* (nominative and vocative), *nadīḥ* (accusative), *nadībhiḥ*, *nadībhyaḥ*, *nadīnām*, *nadīṣu*.

Notice four things. The nominative singular has **no *-ḥ***: *nadī*, *devī*, like *senā*. The vocative shortens the vowel: *devi* "O goddess!", *nadi*. The nominative and accusative plural differ (*nadyaḥ*, *nadīḥ*), unlike *senāḥ*. And the genitive plural inserts *n*, which follows the n-to-ṇ rule: *nārīṇām*, but *nadīnām*.

Wiktionary keeps two tables, for *devī* and *nadī*, because the Veda inflected them differently (Vedic nominative *nadīḥ*, footnoted). In Classical Sanskrit they are one type.

**The *vadhū* type** (Wiktionary वधू) is the same with *ū*/*v*, except that its nominative singular **has *-ḥ***: *vadhūḥ*.

- *vadhūḥ*, *vadhūm*, *vadhvā*, *vadhvai*, *vadhvāḥ*, *vadhvām*, vocative *vadhu*; dual *vadhvau*, *vadhūbhyām*, *vadhvoḥ*; plural *vadhvaḥ*, *vadhūḥ*, *vadhūbhiḥ*, *vadhūbhyaḥ*, *vadhūnām*, *vadhūṣu*.

**One-syllable stems: *dhī*, *śrī*, *bhū*.** A few short nouns in *-ī*, *-ū*, mostly made directly from roots, keep the full vowel as *iy*, *uv* before vowel endings and take *-ḥ* in the nominative: *dhīḥ* "thought", *dhiyam*, *dhiyā*; *śrīḥ* "splendour, fortune", *śriyam*, *śriyā*, *śriyaḥ* (Wiktionary धी, श्री). In the dative, ablative-genitive and locative singular they may take either *śriye*, *śriyaḥ*, *śriyi* or *śriyai*, *śriyāḥ*, *śriyām*. These are rare enough to look up when you meet them.

***lakṣmī*** "fortune, Lakṣmī" is a *nadī*-type noun with an irregular nominative *lakṣmīḥ*, with *-ḥ* (mw1899; Wiktionary). The rest of its forms are regular.

**Making feminines.** Four patterns cover most of what you will meet.

1. **a-stem adjectives**: most take *-ā* (*priyā*, san-02-05), but some take *-ī*, and the dictionary says so (mw1899 marks *mf(ī)n.*): *sundara* → *sundarī*, *gaura* "fair" → *gaurī*.
2. **a-stem nouns of persons and animals**: often *-ī*: *deva* → *devī*, *kumāra* → *kumārī*, *putra* → *putrī*, *brāhmaṇa* → *brāhmaṇī*. Some take *-ā* instead (*bāla* → *bālā*, *śiṣya* → *śiṣyā*). Learn the feminine with the noun.
3. **u-stem adjectives**: add *-ī* to the stem, with *u* → *v*: *guru* → *gurvī*, *mṛdu* → *mṛdvī*, *sādhu* → *sādhvī*, *bahu* → *bahvī*. Some may also stay as u-stems (mw1899: *bahu* mf(*vī* or *u*)n.).
4. **Consonant and ṛ-stems**: the feminine is always in *-ī*: *dātṛ* → *dātrī* (san-03-14), *balin* → *balinī*, *bhagavat* → *bhagavatī* (san-03-17).

**Joins.** *-ī*, *-ū* at the end of a word are not pragṛhya unless they are dual endings: *nadī* + *atra* = *nady atra* (san-03-07). *-īḥ*, *-ūḥ*, *-yaḥ* follow the visarga rules: *nāryo gāyanti*, *vadhūr gacchati*.

**From meaning to form.** "Her voice is soft and sweet."

1. "Her": feminine genitive of *sa*, *tasyāḥ* (san-03-09).
2. "Voice": *vāṇī*, an ī-stem, nominative *vāṇī* (no *-ḥ*).
3. "Soft": *mṛdu*, feminine *mṛdvī*; "sweet": *madhura*, feminine *madhurā*. Both agree with *vāṇī*.
4. No verb: *tasyāḥ vāṇī mṛdvī madhurā ca*.
5. Joins: *tasyāḥ* before *v*: *tasyā*. The rest end in vowels before consonants. Result: *tasyā vāṇī mṛdvī madhurā ca*.

## Where it applies

- **Feminine nouns in *-ī***: *nadī*, *nārī*, *patnī*, *jananī*, *pṛthivī*, *nagarī*, *vāṇī*, *maitrī* "friendship", *sakhī* "woman friend". Names: *sarasvatī*, *damayantī* (the heroine of the Nala story).
- **Feminines made from a-stems** with *-ī*: *devī*, *kumārī*, *putrī*, *brāhmaṇī*; adjectives *sundarī*, *gaurī*.
- **Feminines of u-stem adjectives**: *gurvī*, *mṛdvī*, *bahvī*, *sādhvī*.
- **Feminines of agent nouns and possessive adjectives**: *dātrī*, *kartrī* (san-03-14); *balinī*, *bhagavatī*, *dhīmatī* (san-03-17); present participles (*gacchantī*, san-04-20).
- **Feminine nouns in *-ū***: *vadhū*, *śvaśrū*, *camū*.
- **One-syllable root nouns**: *dhī*, *śrī*, *bhū* "earth"; and *strī* "woman", which is irregular (san-03-19).
- **The last member of compounds** that end in a feminine ī-stem.

## Watch out

**Do not add *-ḥ* to *nadī*, *devī*.** *\*nadīḥ* as a nominative singular is Vedic (Wiktionary footnote). Classical *nadī*. But do add it to *vadhūḥ*, *lakṣmīḥ*, *śrīḥ*, *dhīḥ*.

**Do not use the nominative plural as accusative.** *\*nadyaḥ paśyati* for "he sees the rivers" is wrong: the accusative is *nadīḥ*. Unlike the ā-stems, these feminines distinguish the two.

**Do not leave *ī* unchanged before a vowel ending.** *\*nadīām*, *\*nadīai* are not Sanskrit: *nadyām*, *nadyai*.

**Do not confuse *devī* "goddess" with *devi* "O goddess".** The vocative has short *i*.

**Do not confuse *nadyāḥ* with *nadyaḥ*.** *nadyāḥ* (long *ā*) is ablative or genitive singular; *nadyaḥ* (short *a*) is nominative plural.

**Do not give every a-stem adjective a feminine in *-ī*.** *\*priyī* is wrong: *priyā*. Only some adjectives take *-ī*, and the dictionary marks them. When mw1899 writes *mf(ā)n.* or *mfn.*, the feminine is *-ā*; when it writes *mf(ī)n.*, *-ī*.

**Do not join *-ī* as if it were dual.** *nadī* + *atra* is *nady atra*, with *y* (san-03-07). Only the dual *-ī* is pragṛhya (*kavī iha*, san-03-12).

**Do not mix up *mati* (short) and *nadī* (long).** *matiḥ* has *-ḥ*, *nadī* none; *matyai* and *nadyai* coincide; *matīḥ* and *nadīḥ* coincide in the accusative plural; but *mataye*, *mateḥ*, *matau* have no *nadī*-type counterparts.

## Connections

Hindi still marks feminines with *-ī*: *laṛkā* "boy", *laṛkī* "girl"; *beṭā* "son", *beṭī* "daughter". Spanish *-a* (*niño*, *niña*) does the job of the Sanskrit ā-stems, but has no counterpart to the *-ī* class.

## Exercises

1. Decline *nārī* in the singular and *camū* in the plural.
2. Give the feminine of *kumāra*, *sundara*, *priya*, *sādhu* and *bahu*.
3. Build "The women are standing in the river", with √sthā.
4. Build "The mother gives the bride a jewel."
5. Translate *pṛthivyāṃ bahavo mānavā vasanti*.
6. Translate *sarasvatyai namaḥ*, and explain each form.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*nadīḥ samudraṃ gacchati* for "the river flows to the sea".
8. Find and fix the error: *\*nṛpo nadyaḥ paśyati* for "the king sees the rivers".

## Worked answers

1. *nārī, nārīm, nāryā, nāryai, nāryāḥ, nāryāḥ, nāryām, nāri*. *camvaḥ, camūḥ, camūbhiḥ, camūbhyaḥ, camūbhyaḥ, camūnām, camūṣu, camvaḥ* (this lesson).
2. *kumārī*, *sundarī*, *priyā* (the ā-type), *sādhvī*, *bahvī* or *bahu* (this lesson; mw1899).
3. *nāryo nadyāṃ tiṣṭhanti*. Nominative plural *nāryaḥ*, before *n* *nāryo*; locative *nadyām*, before *t* *nadyāṃ* (san-02-06).
4. *jananī vadhvai ratnaṃ dadāti*. Dative *vadhvai*, with *ū* → *v* (this lesson; san-03-02).
5. "Many people live on the earth." *pṛthivyām* locative; *bahavaḥ*, the u-stem nominative plural with guṇa, agrees with *mānavāḥ* (san-03-12); *bahavaḥ* before *m* is *bahavo*, *mānavāḥ* before *v* *mānavā* (san-02-06).
6. "Homage to Sarasvatī!" *sarasvatyai* is the dative of the ī-stem, with *namaḥ* (san-03-02). *namaḥ* at the end stays in pausa.
7. The nominative singular of *nadī* has no *-ḥ*: *nadī samudraṃ gacchati* (this lesson).
8. The accusative plural is *nadīḥ*: *nṛpo nadīḥ paśyati* (this lesson).
