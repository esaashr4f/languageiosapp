## The idea

The nouns of this lesson are few in kind but constant in use: the words for family, *pitṛ* "father", *mātṛ* "mother", *bhrātṛ* "brother", *svasṛ* "sister", *duhitṛ* "daughter", and the **agent nouns** in *-tṛ*, "one who does": *dātṛ* "giver", *kartṛ* "maker, doer", *netṛ* "leader", *śrotṛ* "hearer". Whitney (§369) calls this declension "comparatively limited", "almost entirely composed of derivative nouns formed with the suffix *tṛ*".

They teach the most important idea in the rest of part 3: **strong and weak stems**. A noun of this kind has more than one stem, and the case decides which one you use. In the nominative, the accusative singular and dual, and the nominative plural, the stem is "fuller"; in the rest it is "briefer" (Whitney §369b). *pitaram* "father" (accusative) has *pitar-*; *pitrā* "by the father" has *pitr-*; *pitṛbhiḥ* "by the fathers" has *pitṛ-*.

Whitney (§311a) puts the distinction "in evident connection with the phenomena of accent": the five strong cases are "the five cases whose endings are never accented", and a syllable that keeps the accent tends to keep its full vowel (compare guṇa and accent, §238, san-03-11). You do not need the accent to use the rule, but it explains why the same five cases are strong in every class of noun that has the distinction: the consonant stems of san-03-16 and san-03-17 follow the same pattern.

The **strong cases** are: nominative singular, accusative singular, nominative-accusative-vocative dual, nominative plural (and the vocative singular, which goes with them here). Every other case is **weak**. Some later classes also split the weak cases in two (san-03-16).

## How it is built

**The three stem forms of *pitṛ*** (Whitney §370):

- **Strong**: guṇa, *pitar-*: *pitaram*, *pitarau*, *pitaraḥ*. Agent nouns and the two relationship nouns *svasṛ* and *naptṛ* "grandson" take vṛddhi instead, *-tār-*: *dātāram*, *svasāram*.
- **Weak before a vowel**: *ṛ* becomes *r* (as in sandhi, san-03-07): *pitrā*, *pitre*, *pitroḥ*.
- **Weak before a consonant**: the simple *ṛ*: *pitṛbhyām*, *pitṛbhiḥ*, *pitṛṣu* (s → ṣ after *ṛ*, san-03-05).

**The special endings** (Whitney §371):

- Nominative singular *-ā*: *pitā*, *mātā*, *dātā*. Whitney explains it as standing "for original *-ars* or *-ārs*": the *r* and the *s* were both lost.
- Vocative singular *-ar*: *pitar*, which at a pause is *pitaḥ*. This is a real *r* (like *punar*, san-02-06), so before a voiced sound it stays *pitar*.
- Ablative and genitive singular *-ur*, at a pause *-uḥ*: *pituḥ*, *mātuḥ*, *dātuḥ*. Also a real *r*: *pitur gṛham*.
- Locative singular *-ari*: *pitari*, *dātari* (guṇa, even for agent nouns).
- Accusative plural with a lengthened vowel: masculine *-ṝn* (*pitṝn*, *dātṝn*), feminine *-ṝḥ* (*mātṝḥ*, *svasṝḥ*), as in the i- and u-stems (san-03-12).
- Genitive plural with *n* and a lengthened vowel: *pitṝṇām* (with ṇ after the *ṛ*).

**Feminines.** *mātṛ*, *duhitṛ*, *svasṛ* are feminine but decline like *pitṛ*, except in the accusative plural *-ṝḥ*. An agent noun makes a separate feminine in *-trī*, an ī-stem (san-03-13): *dātṛ* → *dātrī* "giver (woman)", *kartṛ* → *kartrī*.

**Agent nouns and their objects.** An agent noun takes its object in the genitive: *dhanasya dātā* "giver of wealth". Monier-Williams notes that *dātṛ*, *śrotṛ*, *vaktṛ* also take the accusative, but the genitive is the Classical norm.

**From meaning to form.** "The son remembers his father."

1. Verb: *smarati*. Subject: *putraḥ*.
2. Object: *pitṛ*, accusative singular. Is the accusative singular strong? Yes. *pitṛ* is a relationship noun: guṇa, *pitar-*. Ending *-am*: *pitaram*.
3. Order: *putraḥ pitaram smarati*. Joins: *putraḥ* before *p* stays; *pitaram* before *s*: *pitaraṃ*. Result: *putraḥ pitaraṃ smarati*.

## Where it applies

- **Relationship nouns** with guṇa in the strong cases: *pitṛ*, *mātṛ*, *bhrātṛ*, *duhitṛ*, *jāmātṛ* "son-in-law".
- **Relationship nouns with vṛddhi**: *svasṛ*, *naptṛ* (Whitney §370).
- **Agent nouns in *-tṛ***, all with vṛddhi: *dātṛ*, *kartṛ*, *netṛ*, *śrotṛ*, *vaktṛ*, *bhartṛ* "supporter, husband". They are made from almost any root with guṇa: √nī → *netṛ*, √śru → *śrotṛ*, √kṛ → *kartṛ*, √vac → *vaktṛ*.
- **Agent nouns used as verbs**: the periphrastic future *kartā* "he will do" is the nominative of *kartṛ* (san-04-17).
- **Feminine agent nouns in *-trī***: *dātrī*, *kartrī*, *netrī*, declined like *nadī*.
- **Neuter forms** of agent nouns, used as adjectives with neuter nouns, are rare; the grammarians prescribe them (Whitney §375), but you can leave them until you meet one.
- ***nṛ* "man"** is a ṛ-stem with several irregular forms (Whitney §371i); it is common only as the first member of compounds (*nṛpa* "protector of men", the word you know).

## Watch out

**Do not give the nominative a visarga.** *\*pitāḥ*, *\*dātāḥ* are wrong: *pitā*, *dātā*. The nominative plural has one: *pitaraḥ*.

**Do not use the strong stem in a weak case.** *\*pitareṇa*, *\*pitarasya* treat *pitar-* as an a-stem. The instrumental is *pitrā*, the genitive *pituḥ*.

**Do not give a relationship noun vṛddhi.** *\*pitāram* is wrong: *pitaram*. And do not give an agent noun guṇa: *\*dātaram* is wrong: *dātāram*. *svasṛ* and *naptṛ* are the exceptions with vṛddhi (*svasāram*).

**Do not treat *-uḥ* and *-aḥ* as ordinary visarga.** They come from *r*: *pitur gṛham*, *pitar gacchāmi*, not *\*pituo*, *\*pito*. Like *punar*, they keep *r* before voiced sounds.

**Do not confuse *pituḥ* with a nominative.** It is ablative or genitive: "of the father", "from the father". The nominative is *pitā*.

**Do not use *-ṝn* for feminines.** The feminine accusative plural is *mātṝḥ*, *svasṝḥ*; the masculine *pitṝn*, *dātṝn*.

**Do not forget the ṇ in *pitṝṇām*.** The *ṛ* triggers the n-to-ṇ rule (san-03-01).

**Do not use the masculine agent noun for a woman without checking.** "She is the giver" is *sā dātrī*, with the feminine ī-stem.

## Connections

These are among the clearest words linking Sanskrit to Latin, Greek and Germanic (Wiktionary gives these cognates under each Sanskrit word). *pitṛ* (nominative *pitā*) answers to Latin *pater*, Spanish *padre*, French *père*; *mātṛ* to *mater*, *madre*, *mère*; *bhrātṛ* to *frater* (French *frère*); *duhitṛ* to English *daughter* and Greek *thugatēr*; *svasṛ* to *soror*, French *sœur*. Latin *-tor* in *dator* "giver", *actor* is the same agent suffix as Sanskrit *-tṛ* (*dātā*, *kartā*), and has a long vowel there too (*datōrem*, *dātāram*), though Latin spread it to every case but the nominative (Wiktionary *-tor*).

## Exercises

1. Decline *bhrātṛ* in the singular and *duhitṛ* in the plural.
2. Give the accusative singular and the instrumental plural of *netṛ*, *svasṛ*, *jāmātṛ*.
3. Build "The daughter gives the mother a garland."
4. Build "The leader of the army protects the town."
5. Translate *svasur bhartā vīraḥ*, and explain *svasur*.
6. Translate *śrotāro vaktāraṃ pūjayanti*.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*pitāḥ putraṃ paśyati*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*kanyā pitareṇa saha gacchati*.
9. Find and fix the error: *\*dātaraṃ namāmi*.

## Worked answers

1. *bhrātā, bhrātaram, bhrātrā, bhrātre, bhrātuḥ, bhrātuḥ, bhrātari, bhrātaḥ*. *duhitaraḥ, duhitṝḥ, duhitṛbhiḥ, duhitṛbhyaḥ, duhitṛbhyaḥ, duhitṝṇām, duhitṛṣu, duhitaraḥ* (this lesson; feminine accusative plural in *-ṝḥ*).
2. *netāram*, *netṛbhiḥ* (agent: vṛddhi); *svasāram*, *svasṛbhiḥ*; *jāmātaram*, *jāmātṛbhiḥ* (relationship noun: guṇa) (Whitney §370).
3. *duhitā mātre mālāṃ dadāti*. Dative *mātre*, weak (this lesson; san-03-02).
4. *senāyā netā nagaraṃ rakṣati*. Genitive *senāyāḥ* before *n* is *senāyā* (san-02-06); *netā* nominative.
5. "The sister's husband is a hero." *svasuḥ* is the genitive, a real *r*: before the voiced *bh* it is *svasur* (this lesson).
6. "The listeners honour the speaker." *śrotāraḥ* nominative plural, before *v* *śrotāro*; *vaktāram* accusative, both with vṛddhi (agent nouns).
7. No visarga in the nominative singular: *pitā putraṃ paśyati*.
8. The instrumental is weak: *pitrā*. *kanyā pitrā saha gacchati*.
9. Agent nouns take vṛddhi in the strong cases: *dātāraṃ namāmi* (Whitney §370).
