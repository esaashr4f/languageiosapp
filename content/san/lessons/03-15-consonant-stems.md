## The idea

So far every stem ended in a vowel, and the endings fused with it. Now the stem ends in a consonant: *vāc* "speech" (f.), *marut* "wind" (m.), *āpad* "misfortune" (f.), *manas* "mind" (n.), *tapas* "heat, austerity" (n.). Here you can at last see the **normal endings** of san-03-06 almost unchanged: *vāc-am*, *vāc-ā*, *vāc-e*, *vāc-aḥ*, *vāc-i*; *vāg-bhiḥ*, *vāk-ṣu*. Whitney (§310) took these as the base set for all declension, and with consonant stems they are added as they stand.

The nouns of this lesson have **one stem** throughout: no strong or weak forms, unlike *pitṛ*. (The next two lessons bring consonant stems that do alternate.) What changes is only the last consonant, and only in three places: at the end of the word, before *-bh-* (*-bhyām*, *-bhiḥ*, *-bhyaḥ*), and before *-su*.

The reason is a rule you already know from between words. A word can end in only one consonant, and only in certain consonants (the pausa forms of san-02-01). And a consonant takes on the voicing of the sound next to it. Sanskrit grammarians treat *-bh-* and *-su* endings as if they began a new word, so the stem's last consonant behaves as it would at the end of a word before *b* or *s* (consonant sandhi in full comes in san-04-07). So *vāc* + *bhiḥ* has *g*, voiced like *bh*; *vāc* + *su* has *k*, voiceless like *s*; *vāc* alone has *k*.

## How it is built

**Stems in a stop: *vāc*, *marut*, *āpad*, *suhṛd*, *diś*.** Three rules (Wiktionary वाच्, मरुत्, आपद्, सुहृद्, दिश्):

1. **Before a vowel ending**: the stem as it is. *vācam*, *vācā*, *vāce*, *vācaḥ*, *vāci*; *marutam*, *marutā*; *āpadam*, *āpadā*.
2. **At the end of the word** (nominative and vocative singular, which have no ending: the *-s* is lost because only one final consonant is allowed): the final becomes a voiceless stop. *d* → *t*: *āpat*, *suhṛt*. *t* stays: *marut*. A palatal goes back to a velar: *c* → *k*, *vāk*; *ś* → *k*, *dik*. Whitney (§142) states it: "No palatal is allowed as final."
3. **Before *-bh-***: voiced: *vāgbhiḥ*, *marudbhiḥ*, *āpadbhiḥ*, *digbhiḥ*. **Before *-su***: voiceless: *vākṣu*, *marutsu*, *āpatsu*, *dikṣu* (*k* + *s* is written *kṣ*, with the s-to-ṣ rule of san-03-05).

The plural nominative and accusative are the same (*vācaḥ*, *marutaḥ*), and the genitive plural has no inserted *n*: *vācām*, *marutām*.

**Stems in *-s*: *manas*, *havis*, *cakṣus*.** These are mostly neuters (Wiktionary मनस्, हविस्, चक्षुस्):

1. **Before a vowel ending**: *manas-ā*, *manas-e*, *manas-aḥ*, *manas-i*; dual *manas-ī*. After *i* or *u* the *s* becomes *ṣ*: *haviṣā*, *cakṣuṣā*.
2. **At the end**: visarga: *manaḥ*, *haviḥ*, *cakṣuḥ* (nominative, accusative, vocative singular).
3. **Before *-bh-***: exactly the visarga rule between words (san-02-06). *-as* becomes *-o*: *manobhiḥ*; *-is*, *-us* become *-ir*, *-ur*: *havirbhiḥ*, *cakṣurbhiḥ*. **Before *-su***: visarga or a doubled *s*: *manaḥsu* or *manassu*; *haviḥṣu* or *haviṣṣu*.
4. **Neuter nominative-accusative plural**: a nasal and a long vowel before the *s*: *manāṃsi*, *havīṃṣi*, *cakṣūṃṣi*. These are the only strong forms of the neuter (Whitney §311b): the stem swells there and nowhere else.

**Masculine and feminine *-as* stems** (*candramas* "moon", *apsaras* "nymph") lengthen the vowel in the nominative singular: *candramāḥ*, *apsarāḥ* (Wiktionary चन्द्रमस्). Otherwise they are like *manas*: *candramasam*, *candramasā*.

**Joins.** The final stops of the nominative (*vāk*, *marut*, *āpat*) are consonants that change before many sounds (san-04-07). Until then the examples keep them at the end of a sentence or before *k, p, t, s*. The *-s* stems at the end of a word follow the visarga rules you know: *mano vane*, *tapasaḥ phalam*.

**From meaning to form.** "Sages meditate on the god with their minds."

1. Verb: √dhyai "meditate on", *dhyāyanti*. Subject: *munayaḥ* (san-03-12). Object: *devam*.
2. "With their minds": *manas*, instrumental plural. The ending begins with *bh*: *-as* behaves as before a voiced consonant, *-o*: *manobhiḥ*.
3. Order: *munayaḥ manobhiḥ devam dhyāyanti*. Joins: *munayaḥ* before *m*: *munayo*; *manobhiḥ* before *d*: *manobhir*; *devam* before *dh*: *devaṃ*. Result: *munayo manobhir devaṃ dhyāyanti*.

## Where it applies

- **Root nouns**, made from a bare root: *vāc* (√vac "speak"), *diś* (√diś "point"), *āpad* "misfortune" and *sampad* "success" and *vipad* "misfortune" (√pad "fall" with preverbs), *suhṛd* "friend" (*su* "good" + *hṛd* "heart").
- **Stems in *-t* and *-d***: *marut*, *sarit* "river", *vidyut* "lightning".
- **Neuters in *-as***: a large class of abstract and concrete nouns: *manas*, *tapas*, *yaśas* "fame", *payas* "milk, water", *śiras* "head", *tejas* "splendour", *vacas* "word", *namas* "homage" (part 1). Their first member in compounds is *mano-*, *tapo-*, *yaśo-* (*manoratha*, san-03-01).
- **Neuters in *-is* and *-us***: *havis* "oblation", *cakṣus* "eye", *dhanus* "bow", *āyus* "life".
- **Masculines and feminines in *-as***: *candramas*, *apsaras*; also the comparatives in *-īyas* (san-05-06), which however have strong forms.
- **The last member of compounds** that is a root noun: *-vid* "knowing", *-bhṛt* "bearing" give consonant stems (part 5).

## Watch out

**Do not give the nominative singular an ending.** *\*vākḥ*, *\*maruts* are impossible: a word ends in one consonant only. *vāk*, *marut*.

**Do not keep a palatal at the end.** *\*vāc* as a nominative is wrong in a sentence: *vāk*. The dictionary form *vāc* is the stem, not a word.

**Do not voice before *-su* or unvoice before *-bh-*.** *\*vāgsu*, *\*marutbhiḥ* are wrong: *vākṣu*, *marudbhiḥ*. The *-bh-* endings voice, *-su* unvoices.

**Do not write *\*manasbhiḥ* or *\*manaḥbhiḥ*.** The *-as* before *bh* is *-o*: *manobhiḥ*. Before a vowel it is *manas-*: *manasā*.

**Do not apply the a-stem endings.** *\*manasena*, *\*vācasya* treat the stems as a-stems. The endings attach directly: *manasā*, *vācaḥ*.

**Do not give a neuter s-stem a masculine nominative.** *\*manāḥ* is wrong for "mind": *manaḥ*. Only masculine and feminine *-as* stems lengthen: *candramāḥ*.

**Do not forget the nasal in the neuter plural.** *\*manāsi*, *\*dhanūṣi* are wrong: *manāṃsi*, *dhanūṃṣi*.

**Do not confuse *vācaḥ* singular and plural.** It is ablative or genitive singular ("of speech") and also nominative or accusative plural ("words"). The verb and context decide.

## Connections

Latin consonant-stem nouns behave in the same way at the end of a word: *rex* is *reg-s* (the *g* unvoiced before *s*, like *vāk* from *vāc*), and *lux*, *lucis*. Latin neuters in *-us*, *-eris* and *-us*, *-oris* (*genus*, *tempus*) are the cousins of the Sanskrit s-stems: *genus*, *generis* answers to Sanskrit *janas*, *janasaḥ*, with Latin's *s* between vowels turned to *r*.

## Exercises

1. Decline *sarit* in the singular and *yaśas* in the plural.
2. Give the instrumental plural and the locative plural of *vidyut*, *diś* and *śiras*.
3. Build "The heroes conquer the enemies with their bows."
4. Build "Lightning is in the clouds", with the plural *megheṣu*.
5. Translate *tejasā sūryo jvalati*.
6. Translate *saritaḥ samudraṃ gacchanti*, and say whether *saritaḥ* is singular or plural.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*munayo manasbhir dhyāyanti*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*kavir madhurayā vācayā vadati*.

## Worked answers

1. *sarit, saritam, saritā, sarite, saritaḥ, saritaḥ, sariti, sarit*. *yaśāṃsi, yaśāṃsi, yaśobhiḥ, yaśobhyaḥ, yaśobhyaḥ, yaśasām, yaśaḥsu (or yaśassu), yaśāṃsi* (this lesson).
2. *vidyudbhiḥ*, *vidyutsu*; *digbhiḥ*, *dikṣu*; *śirobhiḥ*, *śiraḥsu* or *śirassu* (this lesson).
3. *vīrā dhanurbhiḥ śatrūn jayanti*. *dhanus* before *-bh-* is *dhanur-*; *dhanurbhiḥ* before *ś* keeps its visarga (san-02-06); *śatrūn* is the u-stem accusative plural (san-03-12).
4. *megheṣu vidyut*. No verb is needed; *vidyut* ends the sentence, so its *t* stays (this lesson; san-02-07).
5. "The sun blazes with splendour." *tejasā* instrumental; *sūryaḥ* before *j* is *sūryo* (san-02-06).
6. "The rivers flow to the sea." *saritaḥ* could be singular (ablative or genitive) or plural (nominative or accusative); the plural verb *gacchanti* makes it the plural subject.
7. *manas* before *-bh-* is *mano-*: *munayo manobhir dhyāyanti* (this lesson).
8. *vāc* is a consonant stem: the instrumental is *vācā*, and the adjective agrees as a feminine: *kavir madhurayā vācā vadati* (this lesson; san-03-06).
