## The idea

Some of the most important nouns in the language end in *-an*: *rājan* "king", *ātman* "self", *brahman* "the absolute", *karman* "action", *nāman* "name", *janman* "birth". You have met three already: *rājā* in the Nala line (san-02-06), *karman* and *ātman* in part 1, *nāma* "by name". This lesson shows how they are declined.

They take the idea of san-03-14 one step further. The ṛ-stems had a strong and a weak stem. The an-stems have **three**: Whitney (§421) says "the stem has a triple form".

- **Strong** (the five strong cases of san-03-14): the *a* of the suffix is lengthened: *rājān-am*, *rājān-aḥ*.
- **Middle** (weak cases whose ending begins with a consonant, *-bhyām*, *-bhiḥ*, *-bhyaḥ*, *-su*): the final *n* is dropped: *rāja-bhiḥ*, *rāja-su*.
- **Weakest** (weak cases whose ending begins with a vowel): the *a* of the suffix is "struck out altogether": *rājñ-ā*, *rājñ-aḥ*.

The nominative singular loses its *n* too, leaving *rājā*, *ātmā* (masculine) and *nāma*, *karma* (neuter).

Whitney (§311a) gives the grades their names and puts the distinction "in evident connection with the phenomena of accent". So *rājānam* (strong), *rājabhiḥ* (middle), *rājñā* (weakest) are one word at three strengths. You saw the same three levels in the vowels of san-03-11; here they belong to a whole suffix.

## How it is built

**Masculine *rājan*** (Wiktionary राजन्; Whitney §424):

- Strong: *rājā* (nominative, *n* lost), *rājānam*, dual *rājānau*, plural *rājānaḥ*. Vocative: the bare stem *rājan* (Whitney §422).
- Middle: *rājabhyām*, *rājabhiḥ*, *rājabhyaḥ*, *rājasu*.
- Weakest: *rājñā*, *rājñe*, *rājñaḥ* (ablative and genitive), *rājñi* or *rājani* (locative: the *a* may be kept, Whitney §421b); dual *rājñoḥ*; plural accusative *rājñaḥ*, genitive *rājñām*.

Why *rājñ-* and not *\*rājn-*? When the *a* falls out, the *n* comes to stand after the palatal *j* and takes its place of mouth: *jñ*, as in *jñāna*.

**After *m* or *v* with a consonant before it, the *a* stays.** Whitney (§421b): "after the *m* or *v* of *man* or *van*, when these are preceded by another consonant, the *a* is always retained, to avoid a too great accumulation of consonants". So *ātman* has *ātmanā*, *ātmane*, *ātmanaḥ* (not *\*ātmnā*), and *karman* *karmaṇā* (not *\*karmṇā*), *janman* *janmanā*, *aśman* *aśmanā*. But *nāman*, with a vowel before the *m*, drops it: *nāmnā*. Likewise *mahiman* "greatness": *mahimnā* (Wiktionary महिमन्).

**Neuters *nāman*, *karman*.** As always in the neuter (Whitney §421a), the only strong case is the nominative-accusative plural: *nāmāni*, *karmāṇi*. The singular is middle: *nāma*, *karma*. The dual may be weakest or not: *nāmnī* or *nāmanī*; *karmaṇī*. The vocative singular is *nāma* or *nāman*. Everything else is as the masculine.

**The n-to-ṇ rule** runs through these stems: *karmaṇā*, *karmāṇi*, *brahmaṇā* (*r*, then labial *m* or *h*: no block), but *rājñā*, *nāmnā*, *ātmanā*.

**Joins.** The vocative *rājan* ends in *n*, after a short vowel; before a vowel it would double (san-04-07), so for now it stands before a consonant such as *k*. The nominatives *rājā*, *ātmā*, *nāma*, *karma* end in vowels and join by san-03-07: *rājāsīt* "there was a king".

**"Oneself": *ātman* and *svayam*.** *ātman* is "breath, soul, self", and it also serves as the **reflexive pronoun**, the word for "oneself" when the doer acts on himself. Monier-Williams: it is used "as reflexive pronoun for all three persons and all three genders", in the masculine singular. So *sā ātmānaṃ rakṣati* is "she protects herself", with a masculine noun for a woman; *vayam ātmānaṃ rakṣāmaḥ* "we protect ourselves". *ātmanaḥ* "one's own": *ātmano gṛham* "one's own house".

*svayam* "self, in person, of one's own accord" is an indeclinable that goes with any subject (mw1899): *rājā svayam* "the king himself". It never changes form, and it does not stand as an object: "he sees himself" needs *ātmānam*.

**From meaning to form.** "She protects herself."

1. Subject: *sā* (san-03-09). Verb: *rakṣati*.
2. Object, the same person: the reflexive *ātman*, accusative, a strong case: *ātmānam*. Masculine, though the subject is a woman.
3. Joins: *sā* + *ātmānam*: *ā* + *ā* = *ā*, one word (san-03-07): *sātmānam*. Before *r*: *sātmānaṃ*. Result: *sātmānaṃ rakṣati*.

## Where it applies

- **Masculine an-stems**: *rājan*, *ātman*, *brahman* (m.) "the god Brahmā; a brahmin", *adhvan* "road", *aśman* "stone", *mahiman* "greatness", *pāpman* "evil". Also the many agent and action words in *-man* (*-iman*).
- **Neuter an-stems**: *karman*, *nāman*, *janman*, *brahman* (n.) "the sacred word; the absolute", *veśman* "house", *carman* "skin", *varman* "armour", *vartman* "path".
- **The first member of a compound** uses the middle stem: *rāja-putra* "king's son, prince", *karma-phala* "fruit of action", *ātma-jñāna* "knowledge of the self" (part 5).
- **The reflexive *ātman***: object (*ātmānaṃ rakṣati*), genitive "one's own" (*ātmano gṛham*), instrumental "by oneself" (*ātmanā*), for any person, gender or number (mw1899).
- ***svayam***: "himself, herself, themselves" stressing the subject, or "of one's own accord" (mw1899).
- **Possessive adjectives in *-vat*, *-mat* and *-in*** are related but different: san-03-17.
- **Irregular an-stems**: *ahan* "day", *śvan* "dog", *yuvan* "young man", *maghavan* (a name of Indra) have peculiar forms (san-03-19 takes *ahan*).

## Watch out

**Do not keep the *n* in the nominative.** *\*rājan* as a nominative is the vocative. "The king" is *rājā*; the neuter *karma*, not *\*karman*.

**Do not use the strong stem in a weak case.** *\*rājānena*, *\*rājānasya* treat *rājān-* as an a-stem. Use *rājñā*, *rājñaḥ*.

**Do not drop the *a* after a consonant + *m* or *v*.** *\*ātmnā*, *\*karmṇā* are wrong (Whitney §421b): *ātmanā*, *karmaṇā*. But *\*nāmanā* is wrong the other way: *nāmnā*.

**Do not confuse the accusative plural with the nominative plural.** *rājānaḥ* (strong) is nominative; *rājñaḥ* (weakest) is accusative plural, and also ablative and genitive singular.

**Do not give the reflexive the subject's gender or number.** *\*sā ātmanīṃ rakṣati*, *\*vayam ātmanaḥ rakṣāmaḥ* (meaning "ourselves") are wrong. The reflexive is masculine singular (Whitney §514a: the singular, "extremely rarely" another number): *sātmānaṃ rakṣati*, *vayam ātmānaṃ rakṣāmaḥ*.

**Do not use *svayam* as an object.** *\*svayaṃ paśyati* means "he sees in person", not "he sees himself". For the object, use *ātmānam*.

**Do not use a personal pronoun for "oneself".** *\*sa taṃ rakṣati* means "he protects him" (someone else). "He protects himself" is *sa ātmānaṃ rakṣati*.

**Do not forget the n-to-ṇ rule.** *karmaṇā*, *karmāṇi*, *brahmaṇā*; but *rājñā* (the *n* is palatal next to *j*) and *ātmanā* (no trigger).

## Connections

Latin *nomen*, *nominis* (Spanish *nombre*, French *nom*) is the cousin of *nāman*. Latin *homo*, *hominis* has lost its *n* in the nominative exactly as *rājā* has. The Spanish and French reflexive *se* does the work of *ātmānam* in the third person (*ella se protege*, "she protects herself", *sātmānaṃ rakṣati*); Sanskrit uses *ātmānam* for every person, where Spanish says *me*, *te*, *nos*.

## Exercises

1. Decline *adhvan* in the singular.
2. Give the instrumental singular, locative plural and nominative plural of *aśman*, *mahiman* and *veśman*.
3. Build "The prince (the king's son) lives in the palace", with the compound *rājaputra*.
4. Build "We remember our own deeds", with *ātmanaḥ* and *karmāṇi*.
5. Translate *brahmaṇo nāma śreṣṭham*, and identify each form.
6. Translate *rājā svayaṃ śatrūn jayati*.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*rājan nagaraṃ gacchati* for "the king goes to the town".
8. Find and fix the error: *\*ātmnā jayati*.
9. Find and fix the error: *\*kanyā svayaṃ rakṣati* for "the girl protects herself".

## Worked answers

1. *adhvā, adhvānam, adhvanā, adhvane, adhvanaḥ, adhvanaḥ, adhvani, adhvan* (this lesson). The *a* stays in the weakest cases because *v* has *dh* before it (Whitney §421b).
2. *aśmanā*, *aśmasu*, *aśmānaḥ*; *mahimnā*, *mahimasu*, *mahimānaḥ*; *veśmanā*, *veśmasu*, *veśmāni* (neuter: strong *-āni* in the plural) (this lesson; Wiktionary अश्मन्, महिमन्).
3. *rājaputraḥ prāsāde vasati*. *rāja-* is the middle stem, used as the first member of a compound (this lesson; part 5). *rājaputraḥ* before *p* keeps its visarga.
4. *vayam ātmanaḥ karmāṇi smarāmaḥ*. *ātmanaḥ* "one's own", genitive, keeping the *a* (Whitney §421b); *karmāṇi* strong neuter plural with ṇ.
5. "The name of Brahman is the best", or "Brahmā's name is supreme". *brahmaṇaḥ* is the weakest genitive, keeping *a* because *m* has *h* before it; before *n* it becomes *brahmaṇo* (san-02-06). *nāma* nominative neuter; *śreṣṭham* agrees.
6. "The king himself conquers the enemies." *svayam* stresses the subject; *śatrūn* accusative plural (san-03-12).
7. The nominative is *rājā*; *rājan* is the vocative. *rājā nagaraṃ gacchati*.
8. *ātman* keeps the *a* after a consonant + *m*: *ātmanā jayati* "he conquers by himself" (Whitney §421b).
9. *svayam* cannot be the object. Use the reflexive: *kanyātmānaṃ rakṣati* (*kanyā* + *ātmānam*, san-03-07).
