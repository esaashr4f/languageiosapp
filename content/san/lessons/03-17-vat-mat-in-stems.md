## The idea

Sanskrit has a quick way to say "having X, endowed with X": add a suffix to the noun. From *dhana* "wealth" you get *dhanavat* and *dhanin*, both "having wealth, rich". From *buddhi* "intelligence", *buddhimat* "intelligent". From *bala* "strength", *balavat* and *balin* "strong". From *bhaga* "good fortune", *bhagavat* "fortunate, blessed", and so *bhagavān*, "the Blessed One", the title of Kṛṣṇa in the Gītā and of the Buddha.

Whitney (§438a) says of *-in* that "almost any noun in *a* in the language may form a possessive derivative adjective with this suffix". The three suffixes *-vat*, *-mat*, *-in* make adjectives, and many of these adjectives are also used as nouns: *pakṣin* "having wings" is "a bird", *hastin* "having a hand", meaning a trunk, is "an elephant", *śaśin* "having a hare", the mark on the moon, is "the moon".

Grammatically they belong to the consonant stems with more than one form. The *-vat* and *-mat* adjectives have a strong stem in the five strong cases, like *rājan* (san-03-16): *bhagavant-*. The *-in* adjectives lose their *n* before consonant endings, like *rājan*'s middle stem: *bali-*. Each has a few peculiarities to learn, but no new principle.

One of these words matters for conversation. *bhavat* "Your Honour" is the polite word for "you". It is a noun, so its verb is in the **third person**: *bhavān gacchati* "Your Honour is going", that is, "you are going, sir".

## How it is built

**-vat and -mat** (Wiktionary भगवत्; Whitney §452):

- **Strong** (the five strong cases): *-vant-*, *-mant-*: *bhagavantam*, *bhagavantau*, *bhagavantaḥ*.
- **Nominative singular masculine**: *-vān*, *-mān*, with a long vowel (Whitney §452; compare *iyān*): *bhagavān*, *dhīmān*. The *t* and the *s* are lost; the *n* stays.
- **Vocative singular**: *-van*, *-man*: *bhagavan* "O Blessed One".
- **Weak before a vowel**: *-vat-*: *bhagavatā*, *bhagavate*, *bhagavataḥ*, *bhagavati*; accusative plural *bhagavataḥ*; genitive plural *bhagavatām*.
- **Weak before a consonant**: the *t* voiced before *-bh-*, *-vad-*: *bhagavadbhiḥ*; before *-su* unvoiced: *bhagavatsu* (as *marut*, san-03-15).
- **Neuter**: *bhagavat* (nominative-accusative singular), *bhagavatī* (dual), *bhagavanti* (plural, strong).
- **Feminine**: always from the weak stem with *-ī* (Whitney §452b): *bhagavatī*, *dhīmatī*, declined like *nadī* (san-03-13).

In the words you will use, *-mat* follows stems in *-i* and *-ī* (*buddhimat*, *dhīmat*, *śrīmat*), and *-vat* stems in *-a* (*dhanavat*, *balavat*, *guṇavat*).

**-in** (Wiktionary बलिन्; Whitney §439):

- The stem is *balin-* before vowel endings: *balinam*, *balinā*, *baline*, *balinaḥ*, *balini*; plural *balinaḥ*, *balinām*.
- It drops its *n* before consonant endings: *balibhyām*, *balibhiḥ*, *baliṣu* (*ṣ* after *i*).
- The nominative singular masculine drops the *n* and lengthens the vowel "by way of compensation" (Whitney §439): *balī*. The vocative is the bare stem: *balin*.
- Neuter: *bali*, dual *balinī*, plural *balīni*. Feminine: *balinī* (an ī-stem).
- Unlike *rājan*, it never loses the vowel of the suffix (Whitney §439a): *balinā*, not *\*balnā*.
- The n-to-ṇ rule applies to the *n* of the suffix when the stem has a trigger: *mantriṇā*, *pakṣiṇaḥ*; but *svāminam*, *balinā*.

***mahat* "great"** is like *-vat*, but with a long *ā* in all the strong forms (Whitney §450b): *mahān*, *mahāntam*, *mahāntaḥ*, neuter plural *mahānti*; weak *mahatā*, *mahati*; feminine *mahatī*.

**Polite *bhavān*.** *bhavat* "Your Honour" declines like *bhagavat*: *bhavān* (nominative), *bhavantam*, *bhavatā*, *bhavataḥ*; plural *bhavantaḥ*; feminine *bhavatī* "Madam". It takes a **third-person** verb: *bhavān paśyati* "you (sir) see", *bhavantaḥ paśyanti* "you (gentlemen) see". Sanskrit has no honorific forms of the verb (dossier §4); politeness lies in the choice of the noun. The interjection *bhoḥ* (san-02-09) is said to be an old vocative of this word (mw1899).

**Joins.** *-vān*, *-mān*, *bhavān* end in *n* after a long vowel. Until san-04-07 the examples put them before a vowel, before *k* or *p*, or at the end. *balī*, *mahatī* end in vowels and follow san-03-07.

**From meaning to form.** "The king deliberates with his clever minister."

1. Verb: √cint "think, reflect", *cintayati*. Subject: *rājā*.
2. "With his minister": *mantrin*, instrumental singular; the stem keeps its *n* before a vowel, and *r* turns it to *ṇ*: *mantriṇā*. Add *saha*.
3. "Clever": *buddhimat*, instrumental singular, a weak case before a vowel: *buddhimatā*, agreeing with *mantriṇā*.
4. Order: *buddhimatā mantriṇā saha rājā cintayati*. No joins change anything. Result as it stands.

## Where it applies

- **"Having X" adjectives** from nouns: *dhanavat*, *balavat*, *guṇavat* "having good qualities, virtuous" (from *guṇa*), *bhagavat*; *buddhimat*, *dhīmat*, *śrīmat* "glorious, illustrious"; *balin*, *dhanin*, *sukhin* "happy", *duḥkhin* "unhappy", *yogin* "practising yoga, a yogi", *tapasvin* "practising austerity, an ascetic".
- **Adjectives used as nouns**: *pakṣin* "bird", *hastin* "elephant", *śaśin* "moon", *mantrin* "minister", *svāmin* "owner, master", *yogin*, *tapasvin*.
- **Titles and epithets**: *bhagavān* (Kṛṣṇa, Viṣṇu, the Buddha), *śrīmān* before names of the great.
- **Polite address**: *bhavān*, *bhavatī*, *bhavantaḥ*, with third-person verbs. Prose dialogue in the Hitopadeśa uses it constantly between social equals and to superiors.
- ***mahat* "great"**: strong forms with long *ā*; as the first member of a compound it becomes *mahā-* (*mahārāja*, *mahātman*) (part 5).
- **Present participles in *-at*** (*gacchat*, *bhavat* "being") look similar but have a short-vowel nominative *gacchan*, *bhavan*: san-04-20. *bhavān* "Your Honour" and *bhavan* "being" are distinguished only by the vowel.
- **The perfect participle in *-vas*** (*vidvān* "knowing, learned", san-04-18) has its own three stems.

## Watch out

**Do not use a short vowel in the nominative of *-vat*, *-mat*.** *\*bhagavan* as a nominative is the vocative. "The Blessed One (says)" is *bhagavān*. The short *-an* nominative belongs to present participles (san-04-20).

**Do not use the strong stem in weak cases.** *\*bhagavantena*, *\*bhagavantasya* treat *-vant-* as an a-stem. The weak cases have *-vat-* and normal endings: *bhagavatā*, *bhagavataḥ*.

**Do not keep the *n* of *-in* before a consonant.** *\*balinbhiḥ*, *\*balinsu* are wrong: *balibhiḥ*, *baliṣu*.

**Do not give *-in* a nominative in *-n*.** *\*balin* as a nominative is the vocative: "the strong man" is *balī*.

**Do not use a second-person verb with *bhavān*.** *\*bhavān gacchasi* mixes polite and familiar. *bhavān gacchati*, or familiar *tvaṃ gacchasi*.

**Do not mix *bhavān* and *tvam* in one address** without a reason. A speaker who is angry or intimate may switch to *tvam*; a story uses the switch for effect. When you write, choose one and keep it.

**Do not make the feminine from the strong stem.** *\*bhagavantī* is wrong (it would be a participle form); the possessive's feminine is *bhagavatī* (Whitney §452b).

**Do not forget *mahat*'s long *ā*.** *\*mahantam* is wrong: *mahāntam*.

## Connections

Spanish *usted* and Italian *Lei* work like *bhavān*: a noun-like polite "you" with a third-person verb (*usted va*, "you go", from *vuestra merced*, "your grace"). Japanese and Korean go further and mark respect in the verb itself; Sanskrit, like Spanish, marks it only in the choice of the word for "you".

## Exercises

1. Decline *dhīmat* (masculine) in the singular.
2. Give the nominative singular masculine, the instrumental plural and the locative plural of *śrīmat*, *dhanin*, *svāmin*.
3. Build "The sage is happy", with *sukhin*.
4. Build "Sir, are you going to the forest?", with *api* and *bhavān*.
5. Translate *hastinaḥ svāmī balavān*.
6. Translate *mahatyāṃ nadyāṃ matsyāś caranti*.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*bhagavan paśyati* for "the Lord sees".
8. Find and fix the error: *\*balinbhir yodhaiḥ saha*.
9. Find and fix the error: *\*bhavān vadasi* for "you say, sir".

## Worked answers

1. *dhīmān, dhīmantam, dhīmatā, dhīmate, dhīmataḥ, dhīmataḥ, dhīmati, dhīman* (this lesson; Whitney §452).
2. *śrīmān*, *śrīmadbhiḥ*, *śrīmatsu*; *dhanī*, *dhanibhiḥ*, *dhaniṣu*; *svāmī*, *svāmibhiḥ*, *svāmiṣu* (this lesson).
3. *muniḥ sukhī*. *muniḥ* before *s* keeps its visarga (san-02-06); *sukhī* is the nominative of the in-stem *sukhin*.
4. *api bhavān vanaṃ gacchati*. *bhavān* takes the third person; *api* opens the question (san-02-08). *bhavān* stands before *v*: by the full rules of san-04-07 its *n* does not change there.
5. "The elephant's owner is strong." *hastinaḥ* genitive singular (stem keeps *n* before the vowel ending); *svāmī* nominative; *balavān* predicate.
6. "Fish move in the great river." *mahatyām* is the feminine *mahatī* in the locative, agreeing with *nadyām* (san-03-13).
7. The nominative has long *ā*: *bhagavān paśyati*. *bhagavan* is the vocative.
8. The *n* of *-in* is dropped before a consonant ending: *balibhir yodhaiḥ saha* (Whitney §439).
9. *bhavān* takes a third-person verb: *bhavān vadati* (this lesson).
