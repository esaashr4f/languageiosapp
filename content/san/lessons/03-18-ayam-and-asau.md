## The idea

San-03-09 gave you two pointing words, *sa* "that, he" and *eṣa* "this". Classical Sanskrit has two more: ***ayam*** (m.), ***iyam*** (f.), ***idam*** (n.) "this", and ***asau*** (m., f.), ***adaḥ*** (n.) "that, yonder". The dossier glosses them `this` and `yonder` (lower case, as lexical words).

Speijer (§271) sorts the four by distance. *ayam* and *eṣa* point "at something near to the speaker or his time"; *sa* and *asau* point at "something remote either by space or by time". He compares *ayam* and *eṣa* to Latin *hic*, *sa* and *asau* to Latin *ille*. Between the near pair the difference is slight: Speijer (§272) calls *eṣa* "the emphatic *ayam*", and adds that the two "are sometimes used almost promiscuously". Between the far pair, *asau* is the one that points into the distance, "yon, yonder" (Whitney §501).

So:

- ***ayam***: "this (here, with me, now)": *ayaṃ vṛkṣaḥ* "this tree"; *ayaṃ janaḥ* "this person here", a way to say "I" modestly.
- ***eṣa***: "this one here!", pointing, often with a gesture.
- ***sa***: "that, the one we spoke of", "he".
- ***asau***: "that one over there", and in grammar and law "so-and-so".

The forms are the reason this pair has its own lesson. Whitney (§501) says their declension is "so irregularly made up" that they must be given in full. *ayam* is "pieced together from a number of defective stems" (Whitney §502): *a-* (*asmai*, *asya*, *asmin*, *ebhiḥ*), *ana-* (*anena*, *anayā*), *ima-* (*imam*, *ime*, *imāni*) and *i-* (*ayam*, *iyam*, *idam*). *asau* uses a stem *amu-*, shifting to *amī-* in part of the plural (Whitney §503). The endings, though, are the pronominal endings you know from *sa*: *-smai*, *-smāt*, *-smin*, *-syāḥ*, *-ṣām*.

## How it is built

**ayam, iyam, idam** (Wiktionary इदम्). Learn it by stem:

- From ***i-***: the nominative singular: *ayam* (m.), *iyam* (f.), *idam* (n., also accusative).
- From ***ima-***: the other strong-looking forms: accusative singular *imam*, *imām*; dual *imau* (m.), *ime* (f., n.); nominative plural *ime* (m.), *imāḥ* (f.), *imāni* (n.); accusative plural *imān*, *imāḥ*, *imāni*.
- From ***ana-***: instrumental singular *anena* (m., n.), *anayā* (f.); genitive-locative dual *anayoḥ*.
- From ***a-***, with the pronominal endings: *asmai*, *asmāt*, *asya*, *asmin* (m., n.); *asyai*, *asyāḥ*, *asyām* (f.); dual *ābhyām*; plural *ebhiḥ*, *ebhyaḥ*, *eṣām*, *eṣu* (m., n.); *ābhiḥ*, *ābhyaḥ*, *āsām*, *āsu* (f.).

Notice how the *a-* forms look like those of *sa* without the *t*: *tasmai* / *asmai*, *tasya* / *asya*, *teṣām* / *eṣām*. That makes them easy to recognise.

**asau, adaḥ** (Wiktionary अदस्):

- Nominative singular: *asau* (m. and f.), *adaḥ* (n., also accusative).
- Masculine: *amum*, *amunā*, *amuṣmai*, *amuṣmāt*, *amuṣya*, *amuṣmin*; dual *amū*, *amūbhyām*, *amuyoḥ*; plural *amī*, *amūn*, *amībhiḥ*, *amībhyaḥ*, *amīṣām*, *amīṣu*.
- Feminine: *amūm*, *amuyā*, *amuṣyai*, *amuṣyāḥ*, *amuṣyām*; plural *amūḥ*, *amūbhiḥ*, *amūṣām*, *amūṣu*.
- Neuter plural *amūni*.

Two curiosities, both in Whitney (§503): *amuṣya* is "the only example in the language of the ending *sya* added to any other than an a-stem", and the masculine plural *amī* is pragṛhya, like a dual ending: it never joins with a following vowel (san-03-07).

**The enclitic *ena-*.** Beside these, Sanskrit has a short unstressed pronoun used only for "him, her, it" as an object, never first and never stressed (Whitney §500): *enam*, *enām*, *enat* (accusative), *enena*, *enayā* (instrumental), *enau*, *ene*, *enayoḥ*, *enān*, *enāḥ*, *enāni*. It is to *ayam* what *mā* is to *mām* (san-03-08). You will mostly read it, rarely need it.

**Joins.** *ayam*, *iyam*, *idam*, *imam*, *imām*, *amum* end in *-m* (san-02-06). *asau* before a vowel becomes *asāv* (*au*, san-03-07). *adaḥ*, *amūḥ*, *imāḥ* follow the visarga rules. The duals *ime* and *amū* are pragṛhya like every dual in *-e*, *-ū* (san-03-07), and so is the plural *amī* (Whitney §503); the plural *ime* is not.

**From meaning to form.** "We go to the village by this road."

1. Verb: *gacchāmaḥ*. Goal: *grāmam*. "By this road": instrumental of the way (san-03-01), *mārgeṇa*.
2. "This", near: *ayam*, masculine instrumental singular, from the stem *ana-*: *anena*.
3. Order and joins: *anena mārgeṇa grāmaṃ gacchāmaḥ*.

## Where it applies

- ***ayam*** for what is here, now, before the speaker: *ayaṃ vṛkṣaḥ*, *asmin vane*, *ime bālāḥ*. Also for what has just been said or is about to be said: *idam* "this (that follows)".
- ***ayaṃ janaḥ*** "this person" for "I" in modest or formal speech (mw1899 under *jana*).
- ***eṣa*** with a pointing gesture, "here is!": *eṣa vyāghraḥ* (san-03-09); with *ayam* almost interchangeable (Speijer §272).
- ***sa*** for what is absent or already mentioned: "that, the said, he" (san-03-09).
- ***asau*** for what is far away in space or time: *asau parvataḥ*; also "such-and-such, so-and-so" in rules and formulas, where a name is to be supplied (mw1899 under *adas*).
- **Contrast "this ... that"**: *ayam ... asau*, or *ayam ... sa* (Speijer §271 gives examples).
- **Adverbs from the same stems** (Whitney §502c): *atra* "here", *ataḥ* "hence", *atha* "then"; *iha* "here", *itaḥ* "from here", *idānīm* "now". You know several of them already.
- ***ena-*** as an unstressed "him, her, it" object, after the first word (Whitney §500).

## Watch out

**Do not build these forms like *sa*.** *\*ayasya*, *\*idena*, *\*ayān* do not exist. Learn the stems: *asya*, *anena*, *imān*.

**Do not confuse *ime* with *te*.** Both are masculine nominative plural and feminine-neuter dual: *ime bālāḥ* "these boys", *te bālāḥ* "those boys"; *ime kanye* "these two girls".

**Do not use *iyam* for the neuter.** *\*iyaṃ phalam* is wrong: *idaṃ phalam*. *iyam* is feminine.

**Do not use *idam* as the masculine accusative.** *\*idaṃ vṛkṣaṃ paśyati* is wrong: *imaṃ vṛkṣaṃ paśyati*. *idam* is neuter only.

**Do not give *asau* a gender ending.** *asau* serves both masculine and feminine in the nominative: *asau nārī* "that woman over there". The neuter is *adaḥ*.

**Do not join *amī* with a vowel.** *\*amyatra* is wrong: *amī atra* (Whitney §503, pragṛhya).

**Do not start a sentence with *enam*.** It is unstressed, like *mā* and *te* (Whitney §500). For a stressed "him", use *tam* or *imam*.

**Do not overuse *ayam* for "the".** As with *sa*, use it when you point; a noun on its own is already "the" or "a".

## Connections

Latin has the same three-way system that Speijer compares (*hic* "this, near me", *iste* "that, near you", *ille* "that, yonder"), and Spanish keeps it (*este*, *ese*, *aquel*). Japanese and Korean have a clear three-way set (*kore, sore, are*; *i, geu, jeo*), with *are* and *jeo* close to Sanskrit *asau*: "that one over there".

## Exercises

1. Give the Sanskrit for "of these (women)", "in this (house)", "to that man yonder", "by this (woman)", "these fruits".
2. Build "This king protects these subjects."
3. Build "That traveller over there is a merchant."
4. Translate *asmin dvīpe vaṇijo vasanti*.
5. Translate *imāni puṣpāṇi tasyai kanyāyai dadāmi*, and say who is near and who is not.
6. Find and fix the error: *\*idaṃ nṛpaṃ paśyāmi* for "I see this king".
7. Find and fix the error: *\*ayaṃ kanyā gāyati* for "this girl sings".
8. Find and fix the error: *\*ayasya gṛham* for "his house (this man's house)".

## Worked answers

1. *āsām*; *asmin* (*gṛhe*); *amuṣmai*; *anayā*; *imāni* (*phalāni*) (this lesson).
2. *ayaṃ nṛpa imāḥ prajā rakṣati*. *nṛpaḥ* before *i* is *nṛpa* (san-02-06); *imāḥ prajāḥ* feminine accusative plural; *prajāḥ* before *r* is *prajā*.
3. *asau pathiko vaṇik*. *pathikaḥ* before *v* is *pathiko*; *vaṇik* is the nominative of *vaṇij*, with *j* → *k* at the end (san-03-15).
4. "Merchants live on this island." *vaṇijaḥ* nominative plural (before *v*, *vaṇijo*); *asmin dvīpe* locative (this lesson).
5. "I give these flowers to that girl." The flowers are with the speaker (*imāni*); the girl has been mentioned or is away from him (*tasyai*, san-03-09).
6. *idam* is neuter; "king" is masculine accusative: *imaṃ nṛpaṃ paśyāmi*.
7. *kanyā* is feminine: *iyaṃ kanyā gāyati*.
8. The genitive of *ayam* is *asya*: *asya gṛham*.
