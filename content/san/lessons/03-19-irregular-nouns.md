## The idea

Nine nouns do not fit any class of part 3. They are among the oldest and commonest words in the language: man, woman, cow, sky, water, road, day, friend, husband. In every language the most frequent words keep irregular forms longest (English *man, men*; *child, children*), and Sanskrit is no different.

None of them is chaotic. Each is one of the classes you know with a twist: a strong stem that looks unexpected, two stems that share the paradigm, or a few odd endings. Whitney's descriptions show the pattern for each:

- ***puṃs*** "man, male" (often written *pums*) has a strong stem *pumāṃs-* and a weak stem *puṃs-*, which loses its *s* before *bh* (Whitney §394).
- ***strī*** "woman" is a one-syllable ī-stem like *dhī* (san-03-13), but without *-ḥ* in the nominative.
- ***go*** "cow, bull" and ***dyo*** "sky, heaven" are the two stems in *-o*. *go* is strengthened to *gau-* in most strong cases and has short forms *gām*, *gāḥ* (Whitney §361c). *dyo* has a second stem *dyu*, which becomes *div* before a vowel (Whitney §361d).
- ***ap*** "water" is feminine and plural only: "the waters".
- ***path*** "road" takes its strong stem from *panthan*, its middle from *pathi* and its weakest from *path* (Whitney §395, §433).
- ***ahan*** "day" is an an-stem in the strong and weakest cases, but takes its middle forms and nominative from *ahar* or *ahas* (Whitney §430a).
- ***sakhi*** "friend" (m.) has a vṛddhi strong stem *sakhāy-* and a few i-stem endings of its own (Whitney §343a).
- ***pati*** "husband" follows *sakhi* in four cases of the singular; as "lord" and in compounds it is a regular i-stem (Whitney §343d).

Learn them by recognition: when you meet *gām*, *divi*, *apsu*, *pathā*, *ahnā*, *sakhyuḥ*, you should know the word at once. The table at the end has them all.

## How it is built

**puṃs** (Wiktionary पुंस्; Whitney §394). Strong: *pumān*, *pumāṃsam*, *pumāṃsau*, *pumāṃsaḥ*; vocative *puman*. Weakest (before vowels): *puṃsā*, *puṃse*, *puṃsaḥ*, *puṃsi*, accusative plural *puṃsaḥ*, genitive plural *puṃsām*. Middle: *pumbhyām*, *pumbhiḥ*, and also locative plural *puṃsu*.

**strī** (Wiktionary स्त्री). *strī*, *striyam* or *strīm*, *striyā*, *striyai*, *striyāḥ*, *striyām*, vocative *stri*; dual *striyau*, *strībhyām*, *striyoḥ*; plural *striyaḥ*, *striyaḥ* or *strīḥ*, *strībhiḥ*, *strībhyaḥ*, *strīṇām*, *strīṣu*. Before a vowel ending the *ī* becomes *iy*, as in *dhiyā*, not *y*: the cluster *stry-* would be too heavy.

**go** (Wiktionary गो; Whitney §361c). Strong with *gau-*/*gāv-*: *gauḥ*, *gāvau*, *gāvaḥ*; short accusatives *gām*, *gāḥ*; ablative-genitive *goḥ*; otherwise the stem *go-* with normal endings: *gavā*, *gave*, *gavi*, *gavoḥ*, *gavām*, *gobhiḥ*, *goṣu*. It is masculine ("bull") or feminine ("cow").

**dyo / div** (Wiktionary द्यु; Whitney §361d). *dyauḥ* "the sky", accusative *dyām* or *divam*; then *divā*, *dive*, *divaḥ*, *divi*; plural *divaḥ*, *dyubhiḥ*. The locative *divi* "in heaven" is the form you will meet most. The instrumental *divā* is also an adverb, "by day".

**ap** (Wiktionary अप्). Plural only: *āpaḥ* (nominative), *apaḥ* (accusative), *adbhiḥ*, *adbhyaḥ*, *apām*, *apsu*. Before *bh* the *p* appears as *d*: *adbhiḥ*, *adbhyaḥ*. Learn these forms as they are.

**path** (Wiktionary पथिन्; Whitney §433). Strong: *panthāḥ* (nominative, with an added *-s*), *panthānam*, *panthānau*, *panthānaḥ*. Middle: *pathibhiḥ*, *pathiṣu*. Weakest: *pathā*, *pathe*, *pathaḥ*, *pathi*; accusative plural *pathaḥ*; genitive plural *pathām*.

**ahan** (Whitney §430a). Nominative-accusative *ahaḥ*; weakest *ahnā*, *ahne*, *ahnaḥ*, *ahni* or *ahani*; plural *ahāni*; middle from *ahas*: *ahobhiḥ*, *ahobhyām*. Wiktionary (अहर्) lists Vedic middle forms *ahabhiḥ* as well; Whitney (§430b) calls them the oldest language. In compounds the first member is *ahar-* or *ahas-*: *ahar ahar* "day after day".

**sakhi** (Wiktionary सखि; Whitney §343a). Strong with vṛddhi: *sakhā* (nominative, no ending), *sakhāyam*, *sakhāyau*, *sakhāyaḥ*. Instrumental and dative "without inserted *n* or guṇa": *sakhyā*, *sakhye*. Ablative-genitive *sakhyuḥ*; locative *sakhyau*; vocative *sakhe*. The rest like *agni*: *sakhīn*, *sakhibhiḥ*, *sakhīnām*. The feminine is *sakhī* (san-03-13).

**pati** (Wiktionary पति; Whitney §343d). Meaning "husband" and standing alone: *patyā*, *patye*, *patyuḥ*, *patyau* in the four cells where *sakhi* is odd; otherwise like *agni* (*patiḥ*, *patim*, *patayaḥ*). Meaning "lord", and always at the end of a compound, it is regular: *nṛpatinā* "by the king", *bhūpateḥ* "of the lord of the earth".

## Where it applies

- ***puṃs***: "man, male", often in contrast with *strī*; and in grammar "masculine" (*puṃliṅga* "masculine gender"). In compounds *puṃs-*, *pum-* (Whitney §394b).
- ***strī***: "woman, wife"; grammar "feminine" (*strīliṅga*). Manu's *na strī svātantryam arhati* (san-03-14) has the nominative.
- ***go***: "cow, bull", also "cattle" in the plural; in compounds *go-* (*gopa*, *gopī* "cowherd woman", *gotra* "lineage", Whitney §361f).
- ***dyo/div***: "sky, heaven" (*divi* "in heaven", *divaḥ* "from heaven"), and *divā* "by day". The old sky-god *dyauḥ* is mostly Vedic.
- ***ap***: "water(s)", always plural; *apsu* "in the waters". The singular word for water in prose is *jala* or *udaka*.
- ***path***: "road, way", literal and figurative ("the path of dharma"). *mārga* and *adhvan* are ordinary synonyms.
- ***ahan***: "day"; *ahar ahar* "day after day"; *ahaḥ* also a nominative. *dina* and *divasa* are regular synonyms.
- ***sakhi***: "friend, companion" (m.); at the end of compounds often replaced by *sakha* (Whitney §343b).
- ***pati***: "husband" (irregular forms), "lord, master" (regular), and at the end of compounds (*nṛpati*, *bhūpati*, *senāpati* "general") always regular.
- **Others to recognise**: *nau* "boat" is regular with normal endings (*nauḥ*, *nāvam*, *nāvā*, Whitney §361a); *śvan* "dog" (*śvā*, *śvānam*, *śunā*) and *yuvan* "young man" (*yuvā*, *yūnā*) are an-stems with odd weakest forms; look them up when you meet them.

## Watch out

**Do not regularise *go*.** *\*gom*, *\*gauṃ*, *\*goḥ* as nominative are wrong. *gauḥ* "cow" (nominative), *gām* (accusative), *goḥ* "of the cow".

**Do not use *ap* in the singular.** *\*āp jalaṃ* or *\*apaḥ* for "the water (singular)" are wrong: "water" with *ap* is always *āpaḥ*, plural.

**Do not mix up the three stems of *path*.** *\*panthena*, *\*pathānam* are wrong: *pathā*, *panthānam*. The nominative *panthāḥ* has a visarga, unlike *rājā*.

**Do not give *strī* a visarga.** *\*strīḥ* in the nominative singular is wrong: *strī*. But it does have one in the accusative plural *strīḥ*.

**Do not decline *sakhi* like *agni*.** *\*sakhiḥ*, *\*sakhinā* are wrong: *sakhā*, *sakhyā*.

**Do not decline *pati* "husband" like *nṛpati*.** *\*patinā saha* "with her husband" uses the regular form. Correct: *patyā saha*. But *\*nṛpatyā* is wrong: *nṛpatinā*.

**Do not read *gavām* as a verb.** It is the genitive plural "of cows". The stem *go* becomes *gav-* before a vowel, as *o* becomes *av* (san-03-11).

**Do not read *divā* as "by the sky" in prose.** It is almost always the adverb "by day".

**Do not confuse *puṃsu* with *pumbhyaḥ*.** Both lose *s*: Whitney (§394) notes the loss before *bh* is necessary, and in the locative plural it follows by analogy.

## Connections

*go* is the same word as English *cow* (Wiktionary gives both from one Indo-European source), and *nau* as Latin *navis*, Spanish *nave*, French *nef*. *dyauḥ* answers to Greek *Zeus* and to Latin *dies* "day" (Spanish *día*), the same family as *divā* "by day"; *pati* answers to Latin *potis* "able" and Greek *posis* "husband" (Wiktionary etymologies). *go* is cognate with Latin *bōs* as well as English *cow*.

## Exercises

1. Give the instrumental singular and locative plural of *go*, *ap*, *pathin* and *sakhi*.
2. Build "The cowherd leads the cows to the grass."
3. Build "The woman lives in heaven with her husband."
4. Translate *sakhyur gṛhaṃ dūre*.
5. Translate *pumāṃsaḥ striyaś ca grāme vasanti*.
6. Find and fix the error: *\*goḥ kṣetre carati* for "the cow grazes in the field".
7. Find and fix the error: *\*nārī patinā saha vasati* for "the woman lives with her husband".
8. Find and fix the error: *\*āp patati* for "water falls".

## Worked answers

1. *gavā*, *goṣu*; (*ap* has no singular) *apsu*; *pathā*, *pathiṣu*; *sakhyā*, *sakhiṣu* (this lesson).
2. *gopo gās tṛṇaṃ nayati*. Short accusative plural *gāḥ*, before *t* *gās*; *tṛṇam* the goal; *gopaḥ* before *g* is *gopo* (san-02-06).
3. *strī patyā saha divi vasati*. *patyā* "with her husband" (irregular); *divi* "in heaven" (this lesson).
4. "The friend's house is far away." *sakhyuḥ* is the genitive of *sakhi*, with the ending *-us* (Whitney §343a); before the voiced *g* it becomes *sakhyur*, by the visarga rule for vowels other than *a* (san-02-06).
5. "Men and women live in the village." *pumāṃsaḥ* strong nominative plural of *puṃs*; *striyaḥ* nominative plural of *strī*, before *c* *striyaś* (this lesson).
6. The nominative is *gauḥ*; *goḥ* is genitive. *gauḥ kṣetre carati*.
7. *pati* "husband" has the instrumental *patyā*: *nārī patyā saha vasati* (Whitney §343d).
8. *ap* is plural only: *āpaḥ patanti* "the waters fall", with a plural verb.
