## Before you start

This review covers the whole of part 3, with the weight on its second half. The cases: instrumental (san-03-01), dative (san-03-02), ablative (san-03-03), genitive (san-03-04), locative (san-03-05), and the a- and ā-stems in full (san-03-06). The joins: vowel sandhi (san-03-07), with the n-to-ṇ and s-to-ṣ rules (san-03-01, san-03-05). The pronouns: *aham* and *tvam* with their enclitics (san-03-08), *sa* and *eṣa* (san-03-09), *ayam* and *asau* (san-03-18). The vowel grades (san-03-11). The other stems: i- and u-stems (san-03-12), ī- and ū-stems and feminines (san-03-13), ṛ-stems and the strong and weak cases (san-03-14), consonant stems (san-03-15), an-stems and the reflexive (san-03-16), *-vat*, *-mat*, *-in* and polite *bhavān* (san-03-17), and the irregular nouns (san-03-19).

Do each exercise before reading its answer. Every answer names the lesson to reread.

## Exercises

1. Translate line 1. Why is *vidarbheṣu* plural, and what kind of stem is *duhitṛ*?
2. Translate line 2. Explain *sakhībhiḥ* and the join *sahodyāne*.
3. Translate lines 3 and 4. What case is *damayantīm*, and why is it not dative?
4. Translate line 5. Identify *damayanti* and *mahīpatiḥ*, and say whether *pati* is regular here.
5. Translate line 6. Give the stem and case of *aśvinoḥ*, and explain *sadṛśo* and *samās*.
6. Translate line 7. Name the two ways it says "among", and undo *cāpi*.
7. Translate line 8. Which noun does each *viśiṣṭa* form agree with, and what is *guṇavān*?
8. Translate line 9, which has no verb. What does the locative *nale* express?
9. Rewrite line 7 with full forms for emphasis: "*You* are the jewel among women", using the genitive of *strī*.
10. Build "Nala is a strong and wise king", with *balin* and *dhīmat*.
11. Build "The swans tell the king about Damayantī's beauty", with *rūpa* in the accusative and the king in the dative.
12. Build "The king protects himself and his subjects", with the reflexive.
13. Build "Sir, you are the lord of the earth", with *bhavān* and *bhūpati*.
14. Build "The cows drink water in the river", with *ap* in the accusative.
15. Build "The father gives his daughter to the king", with *pitṛ*, *duhitṛ*, *rājan*.
16. Decline *mahīpati* in the singular, and say why it differs from *pati* "husband".
17. Give the instrumental singular of *karman*, *ātman*, *nāman*, *rājan*, and explain the differences.
18. Find and fix the error: *\*nṛpasya putrī pitareṇa saha gacchati*.
19. Find and fix the error: *\*damayantī svayaṃ paśyati* for "Damayantī looks at herself".
20. Find and fix the error: *\*te gṛhaṃ dūre* for "your house is far away".
21. Find and fix the error: *\*ayaṃ kanyā mama sakhī*.
22. Find and fix the error: *\*manasbhiḥ cintayanti*.
23. Find and fix the error: *\*gauḥ kṣetreṣu caranti* for "the cows graze in the fields".
24. Explain the grade of the vowel in *vaidarbhī* "the Vidarbha princess" (the word the epic uses for Damayantī), and in *rūpeṇa*'s retroflex *ṇ*.

## Worked answers

1. "In Vidarbha lives Bhīma's daughter, Damayantī." Countries are often named by the people in the plural: *vidarbheṣu* "among the Vidarbhas" (san-03-06, san-03-05). *duhitṛ* is a ṛ-stem; *duhitā* its nominative, without visarga (san-03-14). No verb: "lives" or "is" is understood (san-02-07).
2. "She plays in the garden with her friends." *sakhībhiḥ* is the instrumental plural of the ī-stem *sakhī* "woman friend", with *saha* (san-03-13, san-03-01). *saha* + *udyāne*: *a* + *u* = *o*, written as one word (san-03-07).
3. "Swans fly down there. A swan speaks to Damayantī." *damayantīm* is accusative: verbs of speaking can take the person addressed in the accusative (Whitney §274c). The dative would be "speaks for Damayantī" or, with √kath, "tells Damayantī" (san-03-02). *haṃsaḥ* before *d* is *haṃso* (san-02-06).
4. "Damayantī, among the Niṣadhas there is a lord of the earth named Nala." *damayanti* is the vocative of the ī-stem, with short *i* (san-03-13). *mahīpati* "earth-lord, king" is a compound; at the end of a compound *pati* is regular (san-03-19). *nalaḥ* before *n* is *nalo* (san-02-06).
5. "He is like the Aśvins in beauty; men are not his equals." *aśvinoḥ* is the genitive dual of the in-stem *aśvin* (san-03-17), depending on *sadṛśaḥ* "like" (an adjective with the genitive, san-03-04). *rūpe* is a locative of respect, "in beauty" (san-03-05). *sadṛśaḥ* before *r* is *sadṛśo*; *samāḥ* before *t* is *samās* (san-02-06). *tasya* is the genitive with *sama* "equal to him" (san-03-09, san-03-04).
6. "And you are the jewel among women, and Nala the best among men." "Among" as a genitive plural, *nārīṇām* (with ṇ after *r*), and as a locative plural, *nareṣu* (with ṣ after *e*) (san-03-04, san-03-05). *cāpi* is *ca* + *api* (san-03-07). *tvam* before *c* is *tvaṃ*.
7. "The union of an excellent woman with an excellent man would be fortunate." *viśiṣṭāyāḥ* is feminine genitive (the woman, Damayantī), *viśiṣṭena* masculine instrumental (with the man, Nala), the instrumental of accompaniment (san-03-01, san-03-04). *viśiṣṭāyāḥ* before *v* loses its *ḥ* (san-02-06). *guṇavān* "having good qualities", here "auspicious, fortunate", is the nominative of a *-vat* stem agreeing with *saṃgamaḥ* (san-03-17).
8. "Then Damayantī's heart is set on Nala." *nale* is the locative of the object of feeling (san-03-05). *damayantyāḥ* genitive (san-03-13) before *m* loses its *ḥ*. *manaḥ* is the s-stem nominative (san-03-15).
9. *tvaṃ strīṇāṃ ratnam*, or with emphasis on "you", *tvam eva strīṇāṃ ratnam*. *strīṇām* is the genitive plural of *strī* (san-03-19); *tvam* is the full form, which is all the nominative has (san-03-08).
10. *nalo balī dhīmān rājā*. *balī* and *dhīmān* are the nominatives of *balin* and *dhīmat* (san-03-17); *rājā* of *rājan* (san-03-16). *nalaḥ* before *b* is *nalo*.
11. *haṃsā nṛpāya damayantyā rūpaṃ kathayanti*, or with *rājan*, *haṃsā rājñe damayantyā rūpaṃ kathayanti*. Dative of the hearer (san-03-02), *rājñe* weakest (san-03-16); *damayantyāḥ* before *r* is *damayantyā* (san-02-06).
12. *nṛpa ātmānaṃ prajāś ca rakṣati*. The reflexive *ātmānam*, masculine singular (san-03-16); *nṛpaḥ* before *ā* is *nṛpa* (san-02-06); *prajāḥ* before *c* is *prajāś*.
13. *bhavān bhūpatiḥ*. Polite "you" is a noun taking the third person; no verb is needed (san-03-17, san-02-07). *bhūpati* is regular (san-03-19).
14. *gāvo nadyām apaḥ pibanti*. *gāvaḥ* strong nominative plural of *go*; *apaḥ* accusative plural of *ap*; *nadyām* locative of *nadī* (san-03-19, san-03-13). *gāvaḥ* before *n* is *gāvo*; *nadyām* keeps *m* before the vowel *a*.
15. *pitā duhitaraṃ rājñe dadāti*. *pitā* nominative, *duhitaram* strong accusative with guṇa (san-03-14); *rājñe* weakest dative (san-03-16).
16. *mahīpatiḥ, mahīpatim, mahīpatinā, mahīpataye, mahīpateḥ, mahīpateḥ, mahīpatau, mahīpate*: a regular i-stem like *agni* (san-03-12). Only *pati* "husband" standing alone has *patyā*, *patye*, *patyuḥ*, *patyau* (san-03-19).
17. *karmaṇā*, *ātmanā*, *nāmnā*, *rājñā*. All weakest cases. *karman* and *ātman* keep the *a* because *m* has a consonant before it; *nāman* and *rājan* lose it (Whitney §421b). *karmaṇā* has ṇ after *r*; *rājñā* has ñ after *j* (san-03-16).
18. The instrumental of *pitṛ* is weak: *pitrā*. *nṛpasya putrī pitrā saha gacchati* (san-03-14).
19. *svayam* cannot be the object. Use the reflexive: *damayantī ātmānaṃ paśyati*, joined *damayanty ātmānaṃ paśyati* (*ī* before a vowel becomes *y*, san-03-07; san-03-16).
20. An enclitic cannot begin a sentence: *tava gṛhaṃ dūre*, or *gṛhaṃ te dūre* (san-03-08).
21. *kanyā* is feminine: *iyaṃ kanyā mama sakhī* (san-03-18).
22. *manas* before *-bh-* is *mano-*: *manobhiś cintayanti*, with *-bhiḥ* before *c* as *-bhiś* (san-03-15, san-02-06).
23. The plural verb needs a plural subject: *gāvaḥ kṣetreṣu caranti* (san-03-19).
24. *vaidarbhī*, the feminine of *vaidarbha* "belonging to Vidarbha", has vṛddhi of the first syllable, *i* → *ai* (san-03-11), and the feminine *-ī* (san-03-13). In *rūpeṇa*, the *r* turns the *n* of *-ena* into *ṇ*, since only a vowel and the labial *p* stand between (san-03-01).
