## The idea

Part 2 gave you about sixty verbs, each with a 3SG to learn: *gacchati*, *paśyati*, *likhati*, *pūjayati*. Part 4 asks how a root becomes such a form, because every tense is built the same way: root, stem, ending.

A Sanskrit verb is a set of **systems**. Each system is a family of forms built on one stem. The **present system** is built on the **present stem** (*gaccha-*, *paśya-*) and contains four things: the present, the past tense called the imperfect (san-04-03), the imperative (san-04-05) and the optative (san-04-06). The other systems, the future, the perfect and the aorist, are built straight on the root (san-04-17 to san-04-19), and so are the participles, the absolutive and the infinitive. This is why dictionaries list a verb under its root: *gacchati* and *gata* "gone" are both under √gam.

The present system matters most in practice. Whitney (§600a) counted its forms against those of all the other systems together: six to one in the Hitopadeśa, eight to one in the Śakuntalā, thirty to one in Manu.

The present stem is also where roots differ most. Some add a vowel, some a syllable, some a nasal, some repeat themselves. The Indian grammarians sorted the roots into ten **present classes** (Sanskrit *gaṇa* "group") by the way they make this stem. A present class is the group of roots that build their present stem in the same way. Each class is named after the root that heads its list in the Pāṇinian *dhātupāṭha*, the "recitation of roots", with *ādi* "beginning with": class 1 *bhvādi* (√bhū and the rest), 2 *adādi* (√ad), 3 *juhotyādi* (√hu, from *juhoti*), 4 *divādi* (√div), 5 *svādi* (√su), 6 *tudādi* (√tud), 7 *rudhādi* (√rudh), 8 *tanādi* (√tan), 9 *kryādi* (√krī), 10 *curādi* (√cur). Whitney (§602) remarks that the order of the list is "wholly artificial and unsystematic (the ground of which has never been discovered)".

The ten classes fall into two groups, and the split is the most useful fact about them (Whitney §§602–606). In four classes, 1, 4, 6 and 10, the stem ends in *a* and never changes: *bhava-* is *bhava-* in every person and number. These are the **thematic** classes, from the old term "theme vowel" for that stem-final *a*. In the other six the endings go straight onto a stem that changes shape between persons, as *asti* "is" and *santi* "are" do (san-02-07). They are the **athematic** classes, and san-04-08 to san-04-12 teach them. This lesson covers the thematic four, which include most verbs in any text.

## How it is built

**Class 1 (bhvādi): root in guṇa + *a*.** The root takes guṇa wherever guṇa is possible (san-03-11), and *-a-* follows: √bhū → *bho-* → *bhava-*; √ji → *jaya-*; √budh → *bodha-*; √vṛṣ "rain" → *varṣa-*. A root with *a* stays as it is (√vad → *vada-*), and a heavy syllable resists guṇa (√jīv → *jīva-*, √nand "rejoice" → *nanda-*, √śaṃs "praise" → *śaṃsa-*). Whitney (§744) counts this class as nearly half of all present stems.

Why guṇa? In Vedic, which marks the accent, a class 1 stem is accented on the root syllable, *bháva-* (Whitney §734), and Whitney (§238) links guṇa with an accented syllable. The accent is not written in Classical texts; the vowel is its trace.

**Class 6 (tudādi): weak root + *a*.** The root stays in its simple form and *-a-* follows: √tud "strike" → *tuda-*; √viś → *viśa-*; √sṛj "send out, create" → *sṛja-*; √diś "point out" → *diśa-*. Here the Vedic accent lies on the *-a-* (*tudá-*, Whitney §751), so the root had no accent and no guṇa. Class 1 and class 6 therefore differ only in the root vowel: *bodhati* against *tudati*.

A few class 6 roots put a nasal before their last consonant in the present (Whitney §758): √sic "sprinkle" → *siñca-*; √muc → *muñca-*; √kṛt → *kṛnta-*. The nasal belongs to the present stem only.

**Class 4 (divādi): weak root + *ya*.** The root stays simple and *-ya-* follows: √naś → *naśya-*; √muh "be bewildered" → *muhya-*; √tṛp "be satisfied" → *tṛpya-*. Roots in *-am* lengthen the vowel (Whitney §763): √śam "become calm" → *śāmya-*, and √bhram has *bhrāmyati* beside *bhramati*.

Class 4 is the one thematic class with a leaning in meaning (Whitney §762). Its members so far: *naśyati* "perishes", *tuṣyati* "is pleased", *kupyati* "is angry", *sidhyati* "succeeds", *muhyati*, *tṛpyati*, *hṛṣyati* "rejoices", *śuṣyati* "dries up". Most name a state the subject gets into, not an act done to something. Whitney connects the class with the passive, which also has *-ya-* (san-04-14).

**Class 10 (curādi): strengthened root + *aya*.** The stem adds *-aya-*, and the root is usually strengthened: √cur "steal" → *coraya-* (guṇa); √taḍ → *tāḍaya-* (vṛddhi of *a*); √kṣal "wash" → *kṣālaya-*. Some roots keep their vowel: √gaṇ "count" → *gaṇaya-*, √rac "compose" → *racaya-*, √kath → *kathaya-*. Learn the grade with each verb; the vocabulary gives the 3SG. Whitney (§775) adds that this is "no proper class" but a set of derived verbs, some causatives and some made from nouns: √varṇ "describe" is made from *varṇa* "colour" (Monier-Williams). San-07-01 returns to this.

**The endings.** All four classes take the endings you know from part 2, on the stem as it stands: *-ti, -taḥ, -nti; -si, -thaḥ, -tha; -mi, -vaḥ, -maḥ*. The stem's *a* is lengthened before the *m* and *v* of the first person: *tudāmi*, *tudāvaḥ*, *tudāmaḥ*.

**The chain of thought.** To say "the poet praises the king":

1. The root is √śaṃs, listed as class 1. Class 1 means guṇa + *a*.
2. *ṃ* + *s* after the short *a* make a heavy syllable, and *a* has no guṇa anyway: the stem is *śaṃsa-*.
3. Third person singular: *śaṃsati*.
4. Subject *kaviḥ*, object *nṛpam*: *kaviḥ nṛpam śaṃsati*. The *-m* before *ś* becomes *ṃ* (san-02-06): *kavir nṛpaṃ śaṃsati*, with *-iḥ* before *n* as *-ir*.

**Using the dictionary.** Monier-Williams opens a verb entry with root, class and voice: "tud cl. 6. P. tudati". P. means active endings; Ā. means middle (next lesson). Only the class tells you the stem.

## Where it applies

- **Every form of the present system** uses the stem built here: present *tudati*, imperfect *atudat* (san-04-03), imperative *tudatu* (san-04-05), optative *tudet* (san-04-06), and the present participle *tudat* (san-04-20).
- **Class 1** covers the bulk of common verbs, with guṇa: *bhavati*, *jayati*, *smarati*, *bodhati*, *varṣati*, *nayati*, *harati*.
- **Class 1 with an unchanged root** where guṇa cannot apply: root vowel *a* (*vadati*, *pacati*, *tyajati*), or a heavy syllable (*jīvati*, *nindati*, *nandati*, *śaṃsati*, *krīḍati*).
- **Class 1 with an irregular stem**, to be learned one by one: *gacchati* (√gam) and *yacchati* (√yam "hold, offer") with *-ccha-* (Whitney §747), *tiṣṭhati* (√sthā), *pibati* (√pā), *jighrati* (√ghrā), *sīdati* (√sad), *krāmati* (√kram).
- **Class 6** with a simple root: *tudati*, *viśati*, *spṛśati*, *likhati*, *kṣipati*, *diśati*, *sṛjati*; with a nasal: *muñcati*, *siñcati*, *kṛntati*; with irregular stems: *icchati* (√iṣ), *pṛcchati* (√prach).
- **Class 4**, mostly states: *naśyati*, *muhyati*, *tṛpyati*, *hṛṣyati*, *śuṣyati*, *lubhyati*, and *śāmyati* with a long vowel. Also *paśyati*, which is the present of "see" (san-02-01).
- **Class 10** with guṇa (*corayati*, *pūjayati*), vṛddhi (*tāḍayati*, *kṣālayati*) or no change (*gaṇayati*, *racayati*, *kathayati*); and verbs made from nouns (*varṇayati* from *varṇa*). Some causatives look identical (san-07-01).
- **A root in two classes**: √bhram *bhramati* and *bhrāmyati*; √bhūṣ class 10 "adorn" and class 1 "strive".

## Watch out

**Do not build every present with guṇa.** *\*todati*, *\*veśati*, *\*sarjati* are wrong: √tud, √viś and √sṛj are class 6. Classical *tudati*, *viśati*, *sṛjati*. (Monier-Williams notes *sarjati* once in the Atharvaveda; it is not Classical.)

**Do not add guṇa to class 4.** *\*mohyati* for "is bewildered" mixes two classes: class 4 keeps the root weak, *muhyati*. The form with guṇa, *mohayati*, exists, but it is the causative "bewilders (someone)" (san-07-01).

**Do not take the present stem for the root.** *\*gacch* and *\*tiṣṭh* are not roots and not headwords in Monier-Williams: look under √gam and √sthā. Outside the present the root reappears: *gata* "gone", *sthita* "standing" (san-04-13).

**Do not carry the class sign out of the present system.** The *-ya-* of *naśyati* and the nasal of *siñcati* belong to the present stem. The past participle of √sic is *sikta*, not *\*siñcita* (Monier-Williams); the future of √naś has no *-ya-*. The other systems start from the root.

**Do not assume a class 10 grade.** *\*cūrayati* and *\*curayati* are wrong for "steals": √cur takes guṇa, *corayati*. And *\*rācayati* is wrong: √rac keeps its *a*. The vocabulary entry decides.

**Class 4 *-ya-* is not the passive.** *naśyati* "perishes" has active endings; the passive takes middle endings, *-yate* (san-04-14). A class 4 verb with *-yati* is active in form even when its meaning is a state.

## Connections

Greek sorts its verbs by the same kinds of present stem: thematic *lúō* "I loose" against athematic *títhēmi* "I put", which repeats its first consonant like Sanskrit class 3. Latin *legō*, *legis*, *legit* is a thematic verb of the same type as *bhavati*.

## Exercises

1. Give the class and the 3SG present of √tud, √śam, √cur and √vṛṣ, and say what each class does to the root.
2. √sṛj is class 6 and √śaṃs class 1. Why does neither show a vowel change in the present?
3. Build "The birds are pleased with the water" with √tṛp.
4. Build "The poets praise the gods" with √śaṃs.
5. Split *nārī latāṃ jalena siñcati* and explain the *ñ* of *siñcati*.
6. Find and fix the error: *\*bālo mṛgaṃ todati* for "the boy pokes the deer".
7. Find and fix the error: *\*cauro dhanaṃ curayati*.
8. Translate *nṛpo dhanaṃ gaṇayati, prajāś ca hṛṣyanti*. What links the two class 4 and class 10 verbs to their roots?

## Worked answers

1. √tud: class 6, *tudati*, simple root + *a*. √śam: class 4, *śāmyati*, simple root + *ya* with the long vowel of roots in *-am* (Whitney §763). √cur: class 10, *corayati*, guṇa + *aya*. √vṛṣ: class 1, *varṣati*, guṇa (*ṛ* → *ar*) + *a* (this lesson).
2. √sṛj is class 6, which never takes guṇa: *sṛjati*. √śaṃs is class 1 but its *a* has no guṇa, and *ṃs* makes the syllable heavy anyway: *śaṃsati* (this lesson; san-03-11).
3. *vihagā jalena tṛpyanti*. √tṛp class 4, *tṛpya-*; 3PL *-nti*; the cause in the instrumental. *vihagāḥ* before the voiced *j* drops its *-ḥ* (san-02-06).
4. *devān kavayaḥ śaṃsanti*. The object comes first because *-n* before *ś* changes in a way taught only in san-04-07, and *-n* before *k* does not change. *kavayaḥ* is the NOM.PL of the i-stem *kavi* (san-03-12); *-aḥ* stays before *ś* (Whitney §172).
5. *nār-ī lat-ām jal-ena siñca-ti*, "the woman waters the creeper". √sic is class 6 and puts a nasal before its last consonant in the present stem (Whitney §758). Before *c* the nasal is *ñ*, the palatal nasal (san-01-03). It is absent outside the present: past participle *sikta*.
6. √tud is class 6: *bālo mṛgaṃ tudati*.
7. √cur takes guṇa in class 10: *cauro dhanaṃ corayati*. *caura* is not an error: Monier-Williams gives it as a synonym of *cora*.
8. "The king counts the money, and the subjects rejoice." *gaṇayati* is class 10 with no change of the root vowel; *hṛṣyanti* is class 4, simple root + *ya*. *prajāḥ* before *c* becomes *prajāś* (san-02-06).
