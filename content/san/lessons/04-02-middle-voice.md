## The idea

Every Sanskrit verb form belongs to one of two sets of endings. The set you know, *-ti, -taḥ, -nti* and the rest, is the **active**. The second set, *-te, -ete, -ante* and the rest, is the **middle**. A **voice** is one such set of endings with the meaning it carries.

The Indian grammarians called the active *parasmaipada* "word for another" and the middle *ātmanepada* "word for oneself". Whitney (§529) paraphrases the names as "transitive" and "reflexive" and says they name "the original foundation" of the difference: the active presents an action aimed outward, the middle an action that comes back to the subject, done to oneself, for oneself, or happening in oneself.

The classic example is sacrifice. Monier-Williams (under √yaj) notes that the older language uses the active *yajati* of a priest who sacrifices on someone's behalf, and the middle *yajate* of the man who "makes an offering on his own account". The priest acts for another; the patron acts for himself.

In the language you will read, this contrast survives in only some verbs. Whitney (§529) warns that the distinction is "in no small measure blurred, or even altogether effaced", and that in the epics the voice is "very often determined by metrical considerations alone". So treat the middle mainly as a fact about each verb, learned with it, and look for the "for oneself" meaning only where a verb has both voices.

Verbs fall into three groups (Whitney §530):

1. **Active only**: *gacchati*, *paśyati*, *tudati*, most verbs in part 2.
2. **Middle only**: √labh "get" (*labhate*), √man "think" (*manyate*), √vṛt "turn, be" (*vartate*), √vṛdh "grow" (*vardhate*), √jan "be born" (*jāyate*). The dictionary marks them Ā. (*ātmanepada*). Many describe states, feelings, perception, speech and growth: things that happen in the subject.
3. **Both**: √yaj, and verbs like √nī, √hṛ, √pac, √bhaj, where the middle can add "for oneself" (*pacate* "cooks for himself").

A verb's voice can also change with a prefix: √ji "conquer" is active, but *vi-ji* and *parā-ji* are middle (*vijayate*); √ram "enjoy oneself" is middle, but *vi-ram* "stop" is active (*viramati*). Monier-Williams cites the Pāṇinian rules for each (Pāṇ. 1.3.19, 1.3.83). Prefixes are taught in san-04-04.

## How it is built

The middle uses the same present stem as the active. Only the endings change.

**The endings on a thematic stem** (*labha-*, Wiktionary लभते):

- 3rd person: *-te, -ete, -ante* → *labhate*, *labhete*, *labhante*
- 2nd person: *-se, -ethe, -dhve* → *labhase*, *labhethe*, *labhadhve*
- 1st person: *-e, -āvahe, -āmahe* → *labhe*, *labhāvahe*, *labhāmahe*

**Why they look like this.** Most middle endings are the active ones with *-e* in place of *-i*: *-ti* → *-te*, *-si* → *-se*, *-nti* → *-nte* (with the stem's *a*: *-ante*). The first person dual and plural have *-vahe*, *-mahe* beside the active *-vaḥ*, *-maḥ*, with the long *ā* of the thematic stem before *v* and *m* (san-04-01). In 1SG the stem's *a* and the ending have merged: *labh-a* + *e* → *labhe*.

The dual *-ete*, *-ethe* come from *a* + *āte*, *āthe*: the endings that athematic verbs show unchanged (*āsāte* "the two sit", san-04-08). In a thematic stem the stem-final *a* and the *ā* have merged into *e*. Whitney (§735) gives the thematic paradigm in this form.

**Class changes nothing else.** Class 4 *manya-* gives *manyate*, *manyante*; class 6 *lajja-* gives *lajjate*; class 10 verbs can take the middle too (Wiktionary gives *corayate*).

**Two irregular stems.** √jan "be born" makes *jāya-* (class 4), and √mṛ "die" makes *mriya-* (class 6). Both are middle in the present system. Whitney (§773) notes that *mriyate* looks like a passive (san-04-14) and probably was one in origin.

**The chain of thought.** To say "the fool thinks himself a scholar":

1. "Think, consider" is √man, class 4, middle only: stem *manya-*, 3SG middle *manyate*.
2. "Consider X to be Y" takes two accusatives: *ātmānam* "himself" (san-03-16) and *paṇḍitam*.
3. Subject *mūrkhaḥ*. Before *p* the *-ḥ* stays (san-02-06): *mūrkhaḥ paṇḍitam ātmānaṃ manyate*. Final *-m* before a vowel stays *m*.

## Where it applies

- **Middle-only verbs** in every form of the present system: *labhate*, *manyate*, *vartate*, *vardhate*, *sevate*, *īkṣate* "sees", *bhāṣate*, *ramate*, *modate* "rejoices", *kampate*, *yatate* "strives", *śobhate* "shines, looks fine", *vandate* "greets", *sahate* "bears", *lajjate* "is ashamed", *śaṅkate* "fears, suspects", *kṣamate* "forgives", *edhate* "thrives", *yudhyate* "fights", *jāyate*, *mriyate*.
- **Verbs with both voices**, where the middle says the action benefits or concerns the subject: *yajati / yajate*; *pacati / pacate* "cooks / cooks for himself"; *nayati / nayate*, *harati / harate*.
- **√budh** has a class 4 middle *budhyate* "awakes, understands" beside class 1 *bodhati* (san-02-08): a second present stem, with the middle.
- **Feelings with a case.** √ruc "please" takes the person pleased in the dative or genitive (*rocate*); √ram "delight in" takes the thing or place in the locative (Monier-Williams).
- **Voice changed by a prefix**: *vijayate* "conquers", *parājayate* "is defeated" (middle from active √ji); *viramati* "stops" (active from middle √ram). San-04-04 adds more.
- **The middle as passive.** Outside the present system, middle forms can also have passive meaning (Whitney §531); san-04-14 explains when.
- **In the epics** a middle verb may appear with active endings and the reverse, for metre (Whitney §529a). San-07-08 treats these forms.

## Watch out

**Do not give a middle-only verb active endings.** *\*labhati*, *\*manyati*, *\*vartati* are wrong in Classical prose. Learn the voice with the verb: *labhate*, *manyate*, *vartate*.

**Do not give an active verb middle endings.** *\*gacchate* for "goes" is wrong; √gam is active (only with *sam-* does it become middle, san-04-04).

**The middle is not the passive.** *bālaḥ phalaṃ labhate* is "the boy gets a fruit", not "is got". The passive has its own stem with *-ya-* (*labhyate* "is got", san-04-14). Read the middle as the subject acting.

**Do not translate every middle as "himself".** English has no middle voice, so the "for oneself" sense is usually absent from the translation: *vardhate* "grows", *manyate* "thinks". Add "himself" only where the verb has both voices and the contrast matters (*yajate*).

**Do not use *-āte* on a thematic stem.** *\*labhāte* is wrong for "the two get": thematic stems merge *a* + *ā* into *e*, giving *labhete*. *-āte* belongs to athematic verbs (san-04-08).

**2PL is *-dhve*, not *\*-thve*.** The active has *-tha*, the middle *-dhve*: *labhadhve* "you (all) get".

**Do not confuse middle *-e* with other *-e*s.** *labhe* is "I get"; *vane* is the locative "in the forest"; *te* alone is the enclitic "your" (san-03-08). A verb stem in front of *-e* settles it.

**Do not assume the middle form of a "both" verb is always reflexive.** *pacate* can be plain "cooks" in later texts. Whitney (§529) says the contrast is often effaced; take the "for oneself" reading when context supports it.

## Connections

Greek has the same two voices with the same kind of split: *lúō* "I loose", *lúomai* "I loose for myself, ransom", and many middle-only verbs such as *boúlomai* "I want" and *gígnomai* "I become, am born", from the same root as *jāyate* (Wiktionary, γίγνομαι). Spanish and French express the same idea with a reflexive pronoun: *se alegra*, *il se réjouit* "rejoices" correspond in sense to *modate*, and Latin "deponent" verbs (*nascitur* "is born", *loquitur* "speaks") have passive-looking endings with active meaning, much as a middle-only verb does.

## Exercises

1. Give the six singular and plural forms of √sev in the present middle.
2. Build "We two greet the teacher" with √vand.
3. Build "The warriors fight in the battle" with √yudh.
4. Translate *nṛpaḥ prajābhyo dhanaṃ yacchati, prajāś ca nandanti*, then rewrite the last verb with √mud. What changes?
5. Explain the difference between *vipro yajati* and *vipro yajate*.
6. Find and fix the error: *\*kumāro dhanaṃ labhati*.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*kanye lajjāte* for "the two girls are ashamed".
8. Split *śiṣyā ācāryaṃ sevante* and say why *śiṣyāḥ* has no *-ḥ*.

## Worked answers

1. *sevate*, *sevase*, *seve*; *sevante*, *sevadhve*, *sevāmahe*. Stem *seva-*, middle endings, long *ā* before *m* (this lesson; san-04-01).
2. *āvām ācāryaṃ vandāvahe*. 1DU middle *-āvahe*; *āvām* "we two" (san-02-04). *ācāryam* before *v* is written *ācāryaṃ* (san-02-06).
3. *yodhā yuddhe yudhyante*. √yudh class 4, middle only: *yudhya-* + *-nte*. *yodhāḥ* loses *-ḥ* before the voiced *y* (san-02-06); the place in the locative (san-03-05).
4. "The king gives wealth to the subjects, and the subjects rejoice." With √mud: *prajāś ca modante*. The verb is middle only, so the ending changes from *-nti* to *-nte*; the meaning stays (this lesson).
5. *vipro yajati*: the priest sacrifices on someone else's behalf (active, "word for another"). *vipro yajate*: he sacrifices for himself (middle, "word for oneself"). Monier-Williams notes this use under √yaj; later texts often blur it (Whitney §529).
6. √labh is middle only: *kumāro dhanaṃ labhate*.
7. A thematic stem merges *a* + *ā* into *e*: *kanye lajjete*. *-āte* is athematic (this lesson).
8. *śiṣy-āḥ ācāry-am seva-nte*. *-āḥ* before a vowel drops its *-ḥ*, and the two vowels stay apart (san-02-06, san-03-07).
