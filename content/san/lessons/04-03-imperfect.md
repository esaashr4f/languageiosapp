## The idea

Sanskrit marks past time on a verb with a prefix. A short *a-* placed in front of the stem says "this happened": *gacchati* "goes", *agacchat* "went". This *a-* is called the **augment**: a prefix that marks a verb form as past. Whitney (§586) calls it "a sign of past time". Greek has the same prefix (*e-* in *épheugon* "I was fleeing"), so it is older than either language.

The **imperfect** is the past tense made from the present stem: augment + present stem + a second set of endings. It belongs to the present system (san-04-01), so every verb whose present you know has an imperfect you can build.

The name comes from Greek grammar, where the same formation means an ongoing past ("was going"). In Sanskrit it does not. Whitney (§779) says the imperfect "is the tense of narration; it expresses simple past time, without any other implication". Translate it with the English simple past: *agacchat* "he went".

Sanskrit has two more past tenses, the perfect and the aorist (san-04-18, san-04-19), and a past participle that later prose uses more than any of them (san-04-13). Pāṇini assigns each a different shade (the imperfect to the past "not of today"), but Whitney (§532) states that in the later language imperfect, perfect and aorist are "so many undiscriminated past tenses". Treat Pāṇini's distinction as the grammarians' rule, not as a guide to reading.

## How it is built

**Step 1: the present stem.** Build it as in san-04-01: *gaccha-*, *paśya-*, *tuda-*, *coraya-*, *labha-*.

**Step 2: the augment.** Put *a-* in front: *agaccha-*, *apaśya-*, *atuda-*, *acoraya-*, *alabha-*.

If the stem begins with a vowel, the augment does not combine by the ordinary vowel sandhi of san-03-07. It takes **vṛddhi** with the vowel instead (Whitney §585): *a* + *i* or *ī* → *ai*, *a* + *u* → *au*, *a* + *ṛ* → *ār*, *a* + *a* or *ā* → *ā*. So √iṣ, present *iccha-*, gives *aiccha-*; √īkṣ gives *aikṣa-*. Ordinary sandhi would give *e* (*a* + *i*); the augment gives the heavier *ai*, which keeps it audible.

**Step 3: the secondary endings.** The imperfect uses shorter endings than the present, called **secondary endings**. The present endings are *primary*. Most secondary endings are the primary ones without their final *-i* (active) or with a different final vowel (middle):

- active: 3SG *-t* (present *-ti*), 3DU *-tām*, 3PL *-n* (present *-nti*); 2SG *-ḥ* (present *-si*), 2DU *-tam*, 2PL *-ta*; 1SG *-m* (present *-mi*), 1DU *-va*, 1PL *-ma*.
- middle: 3SG *-ta* (present *-te*), 3DU *-etām*, 3PL *-nta*; 2SG *-thāḥ*, 2DU *-ethām*, 2PL *-dhvam*; 1SG *-e*, 1DU *-āvahi*, 1PL *-āmahi*.

On a thematic stem: *agacchat*, *agacchatām*, *agacchan*; *agacchaḥ*, *agacchatam*, *agacchata*; *agaccham*, *agacchāva*, *agacchāma*. Middle: *alabhata*, *alabhetām*, *alabhanta*; *alabhathāḥ*, *alabhethām*, *alabhadhvam*; *alabhe*, *alabhāvahi*, *alabhāmahi* (all from the Wiktionary tables). As in the present, the thematic *a* lengthens before *v* and *m* in the first person, and merges with *e* or *ā* in the middle dual and 1SG.

The 2SG *-ḥ* is a final *-s*, so it behaves like any visarga in sandhi (san-02-06): *agacchaḥ* before a voiced sound becomes *agaccho*.

**Why the endings are shorter.** The same secondary endings appear wherever an augment does (the aorist, the conditional) and in the optative (san-04-06). Whitney (§586) groups the augment tenses together. Why the primary endings have an extra *-i* is not certain; the guide does not try to explain it.

**√as "be".** Its imperfect is irregular: 3SG *āsīt* "was", 3PL *āsan* "were", 1SG *āsam*. The augment *a-* + *as-* gives *ās-*, and the 2SG and 3SG add an *ī* (Whitney §636). The form is glossed whole: `be.PST.3SG`.

**The chain of thought.** "The girl wanted a fruit."

1. Root √iṣ, class 6, present stem *iccha-* (san-02-02).
2. Past narration: imperfect. Augment before the vowel *i*: vṛddhi, *aiccha-*.
3. 3SG secondary *-t*: *aicchat*.
4. *kanyā phalam aicchat*. *phalam* before a vowel keeps *m*. The verb stands last, so its *-t* is at the end (the joins of a final *-t* come in san-04-07).

**Narrating in the present.** Sanskrit also tells past events in the present tense, especially with *purā* "formerly" (Whitney §§777–778): *purā ... vasati* "there once lived ...". Stories in the Hitopadeśa often open this way and then switch to the imperfect.

## Where it applies

- **Narrating past events** in prose and verse: *nṛpo vanam agacchat*; the story frames of the Hitopadeśa and Pañcatantra are full of *apaśyat*, *acintayat*, *avadat*.
- **Introducing speech**: *avadat*, *abravīt* (√brū, san-04-08), followed by the words and often closed by *iti*.
- **Setting a scene** with *āsīt* "there was": *āsīd rājā* (MBh 3.50.1).
- **Every present stem**: class 1 *abhavat*, class 4 *apaśyat*, class 6 *atudat*, class 10 *acintayat*, and the middle *alabhata*, *amanyata*, *aikṣata*.
- **Past states as well as events**: *āsīt* "was", *amanyata* "believed", *ajīvat* "lived". The imperfect says nothing about whether the action was finished or ongoing (Whitney §779).
- **The present for the past**: with *purā* "formerly", and in lively narration (Whitney §§777–778). Expect a story to move between present and imperfect.
- **Other augment forms**: the aorist (san-04-19) and the conditional (san-06-06) also take the augment, and the augment sits after any prefix (*āgacchat* = *ā-a-gacchat*, san-04-04).

## Watch out

**Do not translate the imperfect as "was doing".** *agacchat* is "went", not "was going": Whitney (§779) says it marks "simple past time, without any other implication". The name is borrowed from Greek grammar.

**Do not keep the primary endings.** *\*agacchati* is wrong: the augment takes the secondary endings, *agacchat*. And *\*gacchat* without the augment is not a past tense in Classical prose (Whitney §587d notes that the epics sometimes drop the augment for metre).

**Do not use ordinary sandhi for the augment.** *\*ecchat* for "wanted" is wrong: the augment takes vṛddhi, *aicchat*. *\*ekṣata* is wrong for "saw": *aikṣata*.

**Do not confuse *agacchata* with *agacchat*.** *agacchata* is 2PL active, "you (all) went"; it also looks like a middle 3SG (*alabhata*), but √gam on its own has no middle. The verb's voice tells you which.

**Do not make the 3PL *\*agacchant*.** The *-nt* of *-nti* loses its *t* at the end of a word, because Sanskrit allows only one consonant there (Whitney §150): *agacchan*.

**Do not use *\*asat* or *\*āsat* for "was".** √as has the irregular *āsīt*; *āsan* "they were" is regular.

**Do not mix the 2SG with the 3SG.** *akrośaḥ* "you cried out", *akrośat* "he cried out". In sandhi the 2SG may appear as *akrośo* or *akrośa*, the 3SG as *akrośad* (san-04-07): the final sound changes, the person does not.

## Connections

Greek has the same augment and the same secondary endings: Sanskrit *abharat* "he carried" corresponds to Greek *épheren*, augment and all. Latin and the Romance languages lost the augment; Spanish *iba* and French *allait* are imperfects in the Greek and Latin sense (ongoing past), which the Sanskrit imperfect is not. Japanese marks the past with a suffix (*-ta*), not a prefix.

## Exercises

1. Give the imperfect 3SG, 3PL and 1SG of √bhū, √paś and √cint.
2. Give the imperfect 3SG and 3PL middle of √man and √īkṣ.
3. Build "The hunter saw a deer in the forest" with √paś.
4. Build "We fled from the tiger" with √dru and the ablative.
5. Build "The two pupils got food" with √labh.
6. Find and fix the error: *\*kanyā phalam ecchat*.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*nṛpo vanaṃ gacchat* for "the king went to the forest".
8. Translate *purā vane muniḥ vasati*, then retell it with the imperfect. Write out the sandhi.

## Worked answers

1. √bhū: *abhavat*, *abhavan*, *abhavam*. √paś: *apaśyat*, *apaśyan*, *apaśyam*. √cint: *acintayat*, *acintayan*, *acintayam* (this lesson; stems from san-04-01).
2. √man: *amanyata*, *amanyanta*. √īkṣ: *aikṣata*, *aikṣanta*, with vṛddhi of the augment before *ī* (Whitney §585; this lesson).
3. *vyādho vane hariṇam apaśyat* (or *mṛgam*). *vyādhaḥ* before *v* becomes *vyādho* (san-02-06); *hariṇam* keeps *m* before a vowel; the verb stands last.
4. *vayaṃ vyāghrād adravāma* would need the join of *-t* before a vowel (san-04-07). Put the verb before the noun at the end: *vayam adravāma vyāghrāt*. 1PL active *-ma* with long *ā* (this lesson); the ablative of fear (san-03-03).
5. *śiṣyāv annam alabhetām*. Middle 3DU *-etām*; *śiṣyau* before a vowel is *śiṣyāv* (san-03-07).
6. The augment takes vṛddhi before a vowel: *kanyā phalam aicchat* (Whitney §585).
7. The past needs the augment: *nṛpo vanam agacchat*. *vanam* keeps *m* before the vowel *a-*.
8. "Once a sage lived in the forest." The sandhi: *muniḥ* before *v* becomes *munir*: *purā vane munir vasati*. With the imperfect: *purā vane munir avasat*. *munir* stays before the vowel; *avasat* = *a-vasa-t*.
