## The idea

Sanskrit makes new verbs by putting a small word in front of a root: √gam "go", *ā-gam* "come"; √viś "enter", *upa-viś* "sit down"; √sthā "stand", *ut-sthā* "stand up". This front word is a **preverb**: a prefix that joins a verb and changes its meaning, usually by adding a direction. The Indian term is *upasarga*, "addition". English does the same job with separate words (*come in*, *stand up*, *give back*); Sanskrit writes them as one word with the verb.

Most preverbs began as adverbs of place. Whitney (§1081) shows that in the Veda they could stand apart from the verb, even after it, while "in classical Sanskrit, the prefix stands immediately before the verbal form". So a Classical compound verb is one word, and the prefix is in front of everything else, including the augment.

Pāṇini enumerates the upasargas in one rule (Pāṇ. 1.4.58, as Monier-Williams cites it under *upasarga*), and the tradition speaks of twenty of them. Whitney (§1077) gives the nineteen that work as verbal prefixes "throughout the whole history of the language". Pāṇini's list adds *su* "well" and *dus* "ill", which you meet mainly on nouns (*sulabha*, *durlabha*, san-03-06), and Whitney's adds *antar* "between", which the tradition classes separately.

The meaning of a compound verb starts from its parts and often drifts. *ā-gam* "go hither" is plain "come"; *adhi-gam* "go over to" is "attain, find"; *ati-kram* "stride beyond" is "transgress"; *anu-gam* "go after" is "follow". Whitney (§1077b) says the drift is "seldom in such a way that the steps of transition from the fundamental sense are not easy to trace". The guide gives each preverb one stock gloss, and the translation carries the drift.

## How it is built

**The nineteen preverbs**, with the guide's stock glosses, in Whitney's order (§1077): *ati* `beyond`, *adhi* `over`, *anu* `after`, *antar* `between`, *apa* `away.from`, *api* `on`, *abhi* `towards`, *ava* `down`, *ā* `hither`, *ud* `up`, *upa* `near`, *ni* `down.into`, *nis* `out`, *parā* `away`, *pari* `around`, *pra* `forth`, *prati* `back`, *vi* `apart`, *sam* `together`.

Whitney (§1077a) ranks them by frequency in the oldest texts: *pra*, *ā*, *vi*, *sam*, *abhi*, *ni*, *ud*, *pari*, *anu*, *upa*, *prati* are the commonest. He adds that *api* has become a conjunction ("also") in the later language and is rare as a prefix.

**Step 1: the compound verb.** Prefix + present form: *ā* + *gacchati* → *āgacchati* "comes". The present stem and its class do not change: *upaviśati* is class 6 like *viśati*.

**Step 2: the join.** The prefix and the verb meet as two words would, with the sandhi of san-02-06 and san-03-07:

- vowel + consonant: no change. *anu* + *gacchati* → *anugacchati*.
- vowel + vowel: vowel sandhi. *prati* + *ā* + *gacchati* → *pratyāgacchati* "comes back"; *upa* + *ā* → *upā*.
- *nis*, *dus* end in *-s*: before a voiced sound *-r* (*nirgacchati* "goes out"), as a visarga would.
- *ud* ends in a consonant, which changes before the next sound (*uttiṣṭhati* "stands up", *utpatati* "flies up"). These joins are the consonant sandhi of san-04-07; learn the few common forms as words until then.

In the morphemes line the prefix is written in its pausa form, like the left member of any join: *ut-tiṣṭha-ti*, *niḥ-gaccha-ti*, *prati-ā-gaccha-ti*.

**Step 3: the augment goes after the prefix.** The augment belongs to the verb, not to the compound, so it stands between prefix and stem: *ā-a-gacchat* → *āgacchat* "came"; *upa-a-viśat* → *upāviśat* "sat down"; *pra-a-viśat* → *prāviśat* "entered"; *ut-a-tiṣṭhat* → *udatiṣṭhat* "stood up". A prefix ending in *i* or *u* turns into *y* or *v* before it: *adhi-a-gacchat* → *adhyagacchat*; *anu-a-gacchat* → *anvagacchat*. When you meet a past form, look for the *a* after the prefix: *pratyāgacchat* is *prati-ā-a-gacchat* (the *ā* of the prefix has swallowed the augment).

**Step 4: case and voice.** A prefix can change what the verb governs. Verbs of motion take the goal in the accusative (*grāmam āgacchati*), but *nis-gam* "go out" takes the place left in the ablative (*guhāyā nirgacchati*, Monier-Williams). A prefix can also change voice: *sam-gam* "meet" is middle (*saṃgacchate*, Pāṇ. 1.3.29), while √gam alone is active; compare *vijayate* in san-04-02.

**More than one prefix.** Two prefixes are "quite usual" (Whitney §1080): *prati-ā-gam* "come back", *sam-ā-gam* "come together". Each new prefix modifies the compound behind it. Whitney adds that *ā* is almost never put in front of another prefix: it stands next to the root.

**The chain of thought.** "The pupil sat down."

1. "Sit down" is *upa-viś*: √viś "enter", class 6, stem *viśa-*, with *upa* "near".
2. Past narration: imperfect, augment after the prefix: *upa-a-viśa-t*.
3. *upa* + *a* → *upā*: *upāviśat*.
4. Subject *śiṣyaḥ*, which loses its *-ḥ* before *u* (san-02-06): *śiṣya upāviśat* "the pupil sat down".

## Where it applies

- **Direction added to a motion verb**: *āgacchati* "comes", *nirgacchati* "goes out", *pratyāgacchati* "comes back", *avatarati* "comes down, descends", *nipatati* "falls down, swoops", *utpatati* "flies up", *ārohati* "climbs onto".
- **Posture**: *upaviśati* "sits down" (*viś* "enter" + "near"), *uttiṣṭhati* "stands up".
- **A changed meaning**: *adhigacchati* "attains, finds", *atikrāmati* "goes beyond, transgresses", *anugacchati* "follows", *ānayati* "brings" (*ā* + √nī "lead"), *apanayati* "leads away, removes", *pariharati* "avoids", *parityajati* "abandons", *praviśati* "enters".
- **A changed voice**: *saṃgacchate* "meets" (middle), *vijayate* "conquers" (middle), *viramati* "stops" (active) (san-04-02).
- **A changed case**: the ablative with *nis-* (*guhāyā nirgacchati*); the accusative or locative with *pra-viś* (Monier-Williams).
- **The augment in every past form**: imperfect *prāviśat*, *upāviśat*, *udatiṣṭhat*; the same holds for the aorist and conditional (san-04-19, san-06-06).
- **Nouns from compound verbs keep the prefix**: *upadeśa* "instruction" (*upa-diś*), *prabhava* "origin" (*pra-bhū*), *upakāra* "help" (*upa-kṛ*), *saṃsāra* (*sam-sṛ*): words you already know are built this way.

## Watch out

**Do not put the augment before the prefix.** *\*aāgacchat* and *\*apraviśat* are wrong. The augment belongs to the verb stem: *āgacchat*, *prāviśat* (Whitney §1081; examples above).

**Do not read the augment as part of the prefix.** *upāviśat* is *upa-a-viśat*, not a prefix *\*upā*. *pratyāgacchat* is past only because of the context and the secondary ending *-t*; the augment has merged into the *ā*. Check the ending.

**Do not translate the parts of every compound.** *adhigacchati* is "finds, attains", not "goes over". *upaviśati* is "sits down", not "enters near". Look up the compound: Monier-Williams lists compound verbs under the root, prefix by prefix (*upa-* √viś under √viś).

**Do not separate the prefix from its verb.** *\*ā nṛpo gacchati* for "the king comes" is Vedic word order at best (Whitney §1081a). Classical: *nṛpa āgacchati*.

**Do not keep √gam's voice in every compound.** *\*saṃgacchati* for "meets" is rare (Monier-Williams: "rarely P."); the norm is *saṃgacchate*.

**Do not change the class.** *\*upaveśati* is wrong: *upa-viś* is class 6 like √viś, *upaviśati*.

**Do not put *ā* outside another prefix.** *\*ā-prati-gacchati* is wrong for "comes back"; the order is *prati-ā-*, *pratyāgacchati* (Whitney §1080).

## Connections

Latin and the Romance languages build verbs the same way: *in-trō*, Spanish *sobre-vivir*, French *re-venir* "come back", with prefixes that were once adverbs. Greek is closest: its prefixes take the augment after them as Sanskrit's do (*apo-é-bēn* "went away"). German separable verbs (*aufstehen* "stand up", *er steht auf*) show the older free word order that Whitney describes for the Veda.

## Exercises

1. Give the stock gloss of *ni*, *prati*, *sam*, *ud*, *vi* and *ā*.
2. Form the present 3SG of *ā-nī*, *upa-viś* and *anu-gam*, and say which class each belongs to.
3. Form the imperfect 3SG of *ā-gam*, *pra-viś*, *ud-sthā* and *adhi-gam*, and explain where the augment goes.
4. Build "The thieves come out of the house" with *nis-gam*.
5. Build "The boy brought water" with *ā-nī*.
6. Split *pratyāgacchat* into its pieces and gloss it.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*nṛpo nagaram apraviśat*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*dūtau saṃgacchataḥ* for "the two messengers meet".

## Worked answers

1. *ni* `down.into`, *prati* `back`, *sam* `together`, *ud* `up`, *vi* `apart`, *ā* `hither` (this lesson; Whitney §1077).
2. *ānayati* (class 1, like *nayati*), *upaviśati* (class 6), *anugacchati* (class 1). A prefix never changes the class (this lesson).
3. *āgacchat* (*ā-a-*, the augment merges with *ā*), *prāviśat* (*pra-a-* → *prā*), *udatiṣṭhat* (*ud-a-*), *adhyagacchat* (*adhi-a-* → *adhya*). The augment always sits between prefix and stem (Whitney §1081; this lesson).
4. *corā gṛhān nirgacchanti* needs the join of *-t* before *n* (san-04-07). Avoid it with the plural *gṛhebhyaḥ*: *corā gṛhebhyo nirgacchanti*. Ablative of the place left; *-aḥ* before *n* is *-o* (san-02-06).
5. *bālo jalam ānayat*. *ā-a-naya-t*: the augment merges into the prefix's *ā*, so the past is seen only in the secondary ending *-t*.
6. *prati-ā-a-gaccha-t* `back-hither-PST-go-3SG` "came back". *prati* + *ā* → *pratyā*; *ā* + *a* → *ā* (san-03-07; this lesson).
7. The augment follows the prefix: *nṛpo nagaraṃ prāviśat*.
8. *sam-gam* is middle: *dūtau saṃgacchete* (Monier-Williams; Pāṇ. 1.3.29).
