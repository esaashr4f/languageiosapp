## The idea

The **imperative** is the verb form that tells someone to do something: "go!", "let him go", "let us go". A **mood** is a set of verb forms that says how the speaker relates to the action: as a fact (the indicative you have used so far), as a command (the imperative), or as a wish or rule (the optative, next lesson). The imperative belongs to the present system: it is built on the present stem you already know (san-04-01).

Sanskrit has imperatives in all three persons. English "go!" is second person. Sanskrit also has *gacchatu* "let him go, may he go" in the third person and *gacchāni* "let me go" in the first. Whitney (§575) describes the three moods of command, request and wish as differing "in degree", with "no sharp line of division" between them: the third-person imperative often sounds like a wish (*jayatu* "may he be victorious").

The first persons have a history. Whitney (§§574, 578) explains that they are relics of the **subjunctive**, a mood of the Vedic language that otherwise died out. In the Classical language they joined the imperative paradigm, and every grammar lists them there. San-07-10 returns to the subjunctive.

The imperative also serves for polite requests. Sanskrit has no special polite verb; politeness comes from the polite pronoun *bhavān* with a third-person imperative (san-03-17): *bhavān upaviśatu* "let Your Honour sit down", that is, "please sit down".

## How it is built

**Step 1: the present stem.** *gaccha-*, *tuda-*, *paśya-*, *coraya-*, *labha-*.

**Step 2: the imperative endings** (Wiktionary tables for गच्छति, लभते):

- active: 3SG *-tu*, 3DU *-tām*, 3PL *-ntu*; 2SG no ending, 2DU *-tam*, 2PL *-ta*; 1SG *-āni*, 1DU *-āva*, 1PL *-āma*.
- middle: 3SG *-tām*, 3DU *-etām*, 3PL *-ntām*; 2SG *-sva*, 2DU *-ethām*, 2PL *-dhvam*; 1SG *-ai*, 1DU *-āvahai*, 1PL *-āmahai*.

So: *gacchatu*, *gacchatām*, *gacchantu*; *gaccha*, *gacchatam*, *gacchata*; *gacchāni*, *gacchāva*, *gacchāma*. Middle: *labhatām*, *labhetām*, *labhantām*; *labhasva*, *labhethām*, *labhadhvam*; *labhai*, *labhāvahai*, *labhāmahai*.

**Why the 2SG has no ending.** A thematic stem ends in a vowel and can stand as a word, so the bare stem serves as "go!": *gaccha*, *paṭha*, *tiṣṭha*. This holds for every *a*-stem. The athematic verbs need an ending here (*edhi* "be!", below; *-hi* or *-dhi* in san-04-08).

**Many forms repeat other forms.** The 2DU *-tam* and 2PL *-ta* are the same as the imperfect's, without the augment: *agacchata* "you went", *gacchata* "go!". The 3DU middle *-etām* is the same as the imperfect's (*alabhetām*). The augment and the context keep them apart.

**The 1st persons** have a long *ā*: *-āni*, *-āva*, *-āma*. Compare the present *gacchāmi*, *gacchāvaḥ*, *gacchāmaḥ*: the imperative has no *-ḥ* in the dual and plural, and *-āni* in the singular. These forms say what the speaker wants or means to do: *gacchāma* "let us go", *gacchāni* "let me go, I shall go".

**√as "be"** is athematic and has its own forms: *astu* "let it be", *santu* "let them be"; 2SG *edhi* "be!", 2PL *sta*; 1SG *asāni*. *astu* is very common in wishes and blessings: *svasti te 'stu* "may it go well with you".

**The chain of thought.** "Please sit down here, sir."

1. "Sit down" is *upa-viś*, present stem *upaviśa-* (san-04-04).
2. Polite address: *bhavān* with the 3SG imperative *-tu*: *upaviśatu*.
3. "Here" is *atra*. *atra* + *upaviśatu*: *a* + *u* → *o* (san-03-07): *atropaviśatu*.
4. *bhavān atropaviśatu*. After a long vowel, final *-n* before a vowel does not change.

## Where it applies

- **Orders** in the 2nd person: *āgaccha*, *tiṣṭha*, *paṭhata*.
- **Requests to a superior** with *bhavān* and the 3rd person: *bhavān upaviśatu*; with *deva* "Your Majesty" and the 2nd person: *deva kṣamasva*.
- **Wishes and blessings** in the 3rd person: *nṛpo jayatu*, *svasti te 'stu*, *prajāḥ sukhena jīvantu*. Monier-Williams cites *vijayasva rājan* "be victorious, O king", that is, "hail to you, O king", with the middle of *vi-ji*.
- **Proposals and intentions** in the 1st person: *gacchāma* "let us go"; *gacchāni* "let me go", also "I will go" (Whitney §578).
- **Permission**: *gacchatu* "let him go" can mean "he may go".
- **Granting or conceding** with *astu* "so be it", *evam astu* "let it be so".
- **Prohibition**: Classical Sanskrit forbids with *mā* and an aorist form without augment (san-04-19). In the epics and later *mā* with the imperative "becomes an ordinary form of prohibition" (Whitney §579c): *mā prayaccha* in the Hitopadeśa verse above.

## Watch out

**Do not add *-hi* to a thematic 2SG.** *\*gacchahi* is wrong: thematic stems use the bare stem, *gaccha*. *-hi* and *-dhi* belong to athematic verbs (*edhi*, san-04-08).

**Do not use the present 1st persons for "let us".** *gacchāmaḥ* is "we go"; "let us go" is *gacchāma*, without *-ḥ*. Likewise *gacchāmi* "I go", but *gacchāni* "let me go".

**Do not confuse imperative and imperfect 2PL.** *gacchata* "go!" and *agacchata* "you went" share the ending *-ta*. The augment marks the past.

**Do not use a 2nd-person imperative to someone you address as *bhavān*.** *\*bhavān upaviśa* mixes persons. *bhavān* takes the 3rd person: *bhavān upaviśatu*.

**Do not use the active ending on a middle verb.** *\*kṣama* for "forgive!" is wrong: √kṣam is middle, so the 2SG is *kṣamasva*; *\*labha* likewise, *labhasva*.

**Do not form *\*asa* or *\*astu* for "be!" (2SG).** The 2SG of √as is *edhi*. *astu* is 3SG, "let it be".

**Do not forbid with *na* and the imperative.** *\*na gaccha* for "don't go" is not the Classical construction. Use *mā* with the aorist (san-04-19); with *na*, use the optative (san-04-06; Whitney §580).

## Connections

Latin and Greek have third-person imperatives too (Latin *estō* "let him be", Greek *éstō*), and Spanish and French make their "let him" forms with the subjunctive (*que venga*, *qu'il vienne*), which is close to how Sanskrit's first-person imperatives began. Japanese has a blunt imperative (*ike* "go!") but makes most requests with other forms (*itte kudasai*); Sanskrit uses the third person and *bhavān* for the same courtesy.

1. *tudatu*, *tudatām*, *tudantu*; *tuda*, *tudatam*, *tudata*; *tudāni*, *tudāva*, *tudāma* (this lesson, on the stem *tuda-* of san-04-01).
2. √sev: *sevasva*, *sevantām*. √yat: *yatasva*, *yatantām* (middle endings; san-04-02).
3. *bālā udyāne krīḍata*. *bālāḥ* (vocative plural, same as the nominative) loses its *-ḥ* before the vowel *u*.
4. *devās tvāṃ rakṣantu*. 3PL *-ntu*; *devāḥ* before *t* becomes *devās* (san-02-06).
5. *āvāṃ ślokaṃ paṭhāva*. 1DU imperative *-āva*.
6. *bhavān upaviśatu*. Polite *bhavān* takes the 3SG imperative (this lesson; san-03-17).
7. A thematic 2SG is the bare stem: *he śiṣya, pustakaṃ likha*.
8. The 1PL imperative has no *-ḥ*: *vayaṃ grāmaṃ gacchāma*.
