## The idea

The **optative** is the mood of what is wished, required or possible: "may he live long", "one should honour a guest", "it might rain". Its name means "of wishing", and Whitney (§573) says that wish is its "primary office". From wish it spread in two directions. A wish addressed to someone becomes a gentle request; a general wish about how things ought to be becomes a rule, "what should or ought to be"; and a weakened wish becomes possibility, "what may or can be".

In the Classical language this one mood does the work of several English words: *may*, *should*, *would*, *might*, *could*. It also absorbed the jobs of the Vedic subjunctive (Whitney §574a). Context decides the translation.

The optative is the mood of the law books and of advice. Whitney (§580) notes that in a text of rules such as Manu its forms "may come to outnumber the indicative and imperative together". When you read *bhavet*, *gacchet*, *kuryāt*, ask first: is this a rule?

Like the imperative, the optative belongs to the present system and is built on the present stem.

## How it is built

**The mood sign.** Every optative form contains a mood sign between stem and ending (Whitney §565):

- after a thematic stem: *-ī-*, which merges with the stem's *a* into *e*: *bhava-* + *ī* → *bhave-*.
- after an athematic stem: *-yā-* in the active: *s-* (weak stem of √as) + *yā* → *syā-*.

**The endings** are the secondary endings of the imperfect (san-04-03), with two differences: 3PL active *-uḥ* (not *-n*), and 3PL middle *-ran*.

Thematic active (*bhavati*): *bhavet*, *bhavetām*, *bhaveyuḥ*; *bhaveḥ*, *bhavetam*, *bhaveta*; *bhaveyam*, *bhaveva*, *bhavema*. Before the vowel endings *-uḥ*, *-am* a *y* appears (Whitney §565a): *bhave-y-uḥ*, *bhave-y-am*.

Thematic middle (*labhate*): *labheta*, *labheyātām*, *labheran*; *labhethāḥ*, *labheyāthām*, *labhedhvam*; *labheya*, *labhevahi*, *labhemahi*.

√as (athematic): *syāt*, *syātām*, *syuḥ*; *syāḥ*, *syātam*, *syāta*; *syām*, *syāva*, *syāma*. The *ā* of *yā* drops before *-uḥ*: *syuḥ*. (All forms from the Wiktionary tables for भवति, लभते, अस्ति.)

**Telling the optative from other forms.** The thematic optative has *e* where the present has *a*: *bhavati* "is", *bhavet* "may be, should be". It has no augment, so *bhaveta* (2PL) is not past. And the 3PL *-eyuḥ* has no equivalent anywhere else.

**How it is glossed.** The mood sign is a separate piece: `bhav-e-t` `be-OPT-3SG`; `labh-e-ta` `get-OPT-3SG.MID`; `s-yā-t` `be-OPT-3SG`. The *-e-* stands for stem vowel and mood sign together.

**Negation.** *na* with the optative forbids or advises against: *na corayet* "one should not steal". Whitney (§580) calls this "the correlative of the prescriptive optative", and it is extremely common. It is the normal way to say "must not" in rules; *mā* with an aorist is the direct "don't!" (san-04-19).

**The chain of thought.** "A householder should honour his guests."

1. "Should" in a general rule: optative, 3SG, with the general subject in the singular.
2. √pūj, class 10, stem *pūjaya-*; + *ī* → *pūjaye-*; 3SG *-t*: *pūjayet*.
3. Subject *gṛhasthaḥ*, object *atithim*. *gṛhasthaḥ* before *a-* becomes *gṛhastho 'tithim* with avagraha (san-03-07); *atithim* before *p* is written *atithiṃ*.
4. *gṛhastho 'tithiṃ pūjayet*. The verb stands last, so the final *-t* needs no join.

## Where it applies

- **Rules and duties**: *nṛpaḥ prajāḥ pālayet*; the law books (Manu) and the manuals of conduct use it throughout (Whitney §580).
- **Prohibitions in rules** with *na*: *dhanaṃ na corayet*, *nātmānam avamanyeta*.
- **Wishes**: *putraś ciraṃ jīvet*; *tava kalyāṇaṃ syāt*. The imperative *jīvatu* and the optative *jīvet* overlap here (Whitney §575).
- **Gentle requests**, softer than the imperative (Whitney §573a).
- **Possibility and likelihood**: *adya megho varṣet* "it might rain"; also "what is usual".
- **The speaker's wish or intention** in the 1st person: *vaseyam* "I would like to live".
- **Hypothetical "would"**: *saṃgamo guṇavān bhavet* "the union would be fortunate" (MBh 3.50.29, in the review of part 3).
- **Conditions** "if he should ...": with *yadi* "if" in the *if*-clause and often in the main clause too (san-06-05).
- **Dependent clauses** of purpose and of "whoever" (Whitney §581); part 6 shows them.
- **Precative**: a rare form built from the optative in *-yāt* on the root (*bhūyāt* "may he be"), recognition only (san-07-06).

## Watch out

**Do not read the optative as past.** *bhaveta* "you should be" looks like an imperfect (*abhavata*), but it has no augment and it has *e*. Check for both.

**Do not make the 3PL with *-n*.** *\*bhaven* is wrong: the optative 3PL is *bhaveyuḥ*, and the middle *labheran*.

**Do not use *-yā-* on a thematic stem.** *\*bhavayāt* and *\*gacchyāt* are wrong. *-yā-* belongs to athematic stems (*syāt*, and *kuryāt* in san-04-11); thematic stems take *-e-*: *bhavet*, *gacchet*.

**Do not use *\*asyāt* for "may it be".** √as takes its weak stem *s-* in the optative: *syāt*.

**Do not put *mā* with the optative.** *\*mā corayet* is not the construction. Rules use *na* with the optative (*na corayet*, Whitney §580); direct prohibitions use *mā* with an aorist (san-04-19).

**Do not translate every optative as "should".** A wish (*jīvet* "may he live"), a possibility (*varṣet* "it might rain") and a hypothesis (*bhavet* "would be") are all optatives. The context, not the form, decides.

**Do not confuse *-eta* middle 3SG with *-eta* active 2PL.** *seveta* (√sev, middle only) is "he should serve"; *bhaveta* (√bhū, active) is "you (all) should be". The verb's voice settles it.

## Connections

Greek has an optative with the same mood sign (*-oi-* in thematic verbs, *-iē-* in others, the cognate of Sanskrit *-e-* and *-yā-*), used for wishes and possibility. Latin merged it into the subjunctive: *sit* "may he be" (Old Latin *siet*) is the cognate of *syāt*. (Spanish *sea* is not: Wiktionary derives it from Latin *sedeat* "may he sit".) English "may he live long" and "one should" are the usual translations.

## Exercises

1. Give the optative 3SG, 3PL and 1SG of √gam, √paś and √cint.
2. Give the optative 3SG and 3PL middle of √labh and √man.
3. Build "One should not tell a lie" with *anṛtam* and √vad.
4. Build "May you be happy" (*sukhin*, 2SG) with √as.
5. Build "A king should conquer his enemies" with √ji.
6. Translate *gṛhastho 'tithiṃ pūjayet*, and say how you know it is not a present.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*śiṣyāḥ guruṃ seveyuḥ* for "pupils should serve the teacher".
8. Find and fix the error: *\*nṛpaḥ sukhī bhavyāt*.

## Worked answers

1. √gam: *gacchet*, *gaccheyuḥ*, *gaccheyam*. √paś: *paśyet*, *paśyeyuḥ*, *paśyeyam*. √cint: *cintayet*, *cintayeyuḥ*, *cintayeyam* (this lesson).
2. *labheta*, *labheran*; *manyeta*, *manyeran* (middle optative *-eta*, *-eran*).
3. *anṛtaṃ na vadet*. A rule: optative 3SG with *na* (Whitney §580). *anṛtam* before *n* is written *anṛtaṃ*.
4. *sukhī syāḥ* (or *tvaṃ sukhī syāḥ*). 2SG optative of √as: *syāḥ*; *sukhin* has NOM.SG *sukhī* (san-03-17).
5. *nṛpaḥ śatrūñ jayet* needs the join of *-n* before *j* (san-04-07). Put the object first: *śatrūn nṛpo jayet*. *śatru* ACC.PL *śatrūn* (san-03-12); *-n* before *n* does not change; *nṛpaḥ* before *j* is *nṛpo*.
6. "A householder should honour his guest." *pūjayet* has *e* where the present has *a* (*pūjayati*), and the ending *-t*, not *-ti*: optative (this lesson).
7. Two faults. √sev is middle: *severan*. And *śiṣyāḥ* before *g* is *śiṣyā*: *śiṣyā guruṃ severan*.
8. A thematic stem takes *-e-*, not *-yā-*: *nṛpaḥ sukhī bhavet*.
