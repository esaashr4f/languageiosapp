## The idea

You can now join a word that ends in *-ḥ* or *-m* (san-02-06) and a word that ends in a vowel (san-03-07). The third and last kind of external sandhi joins a word that ends in any other consonant: the *-t* of *agacchat*, *bhavet*, *tat*; the *-n* of *agacchan*, *devān*, *bhavān*; the *-k* of *vāk*. Until now the examples kept these words at the end of a sentence or before *k*, *p*, *t*, *s*. From this lesson on nothing is avoided.

The rules follow from two facts.

First, **only a few consonants can end a word**. Whitney (§§139–150) lists them: in pausa a word ends in a vowel or in one of *k, ṭ, t, p, ṅ, n, m*, or *ḥ*, and only one consonant may stand there (§150). Every stem-final *c, j, d, dh, bh* and so on is turned into one of these first: *vāc* → *vāk*, *tad* → *tat*, *suhṛd* → *suhṛt*. So in practice you only need rules for *k, ṭ, t, p* and for *n* (and *ṅ*, rare).

Second, **the final consonant takes on the voice of the next sound**. Sanskrit avoids putting a voiceless sound next to a voiced one; it changes the first to match the second (Whitney §159, "regressive assimilation"). A **voiced** sound is one made with the vocal cords vibrating: all vowels, *g gh j jh ḍ ḍh d dh b bh*, the nasals, *y r l v* and *h*. So *-t* before a vowel or a voiced consonant becomes *-d*: *āsīt* + *rājā* → *āsīd rājā*. That one rule covers most joins.

The rest are refinements where the two sounds come even closer: the final takes the next sound's place in the mouth as well as its voice (*tat* + *ca* → *tac ca*), or becomes a nasal before a nasal (*tat* + *mama* → *tan mama*).

**Final *-n*** has rules of its own, and they have a history. Whitney (§208a) explains that nearly three quarters of all word-final *n* go back to an older *-ns*. The lost *s* reappears before *c, t* as a sibilant with anusvāra: *devān* + *ca* → *devāṃś ca*, *devān* + *tatra* → *devāṃs tatra*. This is why *-n* joins look like visarga joins.

## How it is built

**1. Final *k, ṭ, t, p* before a voiced sound: voiced.** *-k* → *-g*, *-ṭ* → *-ḍ*, *-t* → *-d*, *-p* → *-b* (Whitney §159).

- *vṛkṣāt* + *vihagaḥ* → *vṛkṣād vihagaḥ*; *āsīt* + *rājā* → *āsīd rājā*; *vāk* + *eva* → *vāg eva*.
- Before a voiceless sound nothing changes: *vṛkṣāt phalam*, *tat karoti*.

**2. Before a nasal (*m, n*): usually the nasal of its row** (Whitney §161). *tat* + *mama* → *tan mama*; *vāk* + *mama* → *vāṅ mama*. Whitney says the plain voiced form (*tad mama*) is allowed but the manuscripts "almost invariably" write the nasal.

**3. Final *-t* before palatals, *l*, and *ś*.** It takes the place of the next sound (Whitney §§162, 202–203):

- before *c, ch*: *-c* (*tat* + *ca* → *tac ca*);
- before *j*: *-j* (*tat* + *jalam* → *taj jalam*);
- before *l*: *-l* (*tat* + *labhate* → *tal labhate*);
- before *ś*: *-c*, and the *ś* becomes *ch* (*tat* + *śāstram* → *tac chāstram*). Whitney (§203) says the change of *ś* to *ch* is made "in practice almost always".
- Before *ṭ, ḍ* it becomes *ṭ, ḍ*; this is rare.

**4. Final stop before *h*: voiced, and *h* becomes the matching aspirate** (Whitney §163): *tat* + *hṛdaye* → *tad dhṛdaye*; *vāk* + *hi* → *vāg ghi*. The *h* is a voiced breath, and after *d* it is heard as the aspiration of a *dh*.

**5. Final *-n*.**

- before *c, ch*: *-ṃś* (*devān* + *ca* → *devāṃś ca*);
- before *t, th*: *-ṃs* (*vīrān* + *tatra* → *vīrāṃs tatra*);
- before *ṭ, ṭh*: *-ṃṣ*, which hardly occurs (Whitney §208);
- before *j* and *ś*: *-ñ*, and *ś* usually becomes *ch* (*śatrūn* + *jayati* → *śatrūñ jayati*; *devān* + *śaṃsati* → *devāñ chaṃsati*; Whitney §§202b, 203);
- before *l*: a nasalised *l*, written *m̐l* (*tān* + *lokān* → *tām̐l lokān*, Whitney §206); the dossier's convention is to write it only where the edition does;
- before a vowel, **after a short vowel**: doubled, *-nn* (*agacchan* + *udyānam* → *agacchann udyānam*, Whitney §210). After a long vowel it stays single (*devān atra*);
- before every other sound (*k, p, s, g, d, b, m, n, y, r, v, h*): no change.

**6. Final *-m*** you already know: *ṃ* before a consonant, *m* before a vowel (san-02-06).

**Undoing a join.** Read back to the pausa form. A *-d*, *-g*, *-b* before a voiced sound is a voiceless final: *vṛkṣād* → *vṛkṣāt*. A nasal before a nasal can be a stop: *tan mama* → *tat mama*. A *-c* or *-j* before a palatal is a *-t*. A *-ñ*, *-ṃś*, *-ṃs* is an *-n*. *ch* after a word ending in *-c* or *-ñ* is a *ś*: *tac chāstram* → *tat śāstram*. Two *n* before a vowel are one *n*.

**The chain of thought.** "And the king worships the gods and the brahmins."

1. *nṛpaḥ devān ca brāhmaṇān ca pūjayati*.
2. *nṛpaḥ* before *d*: *nṛpo* (san-02-06).
3. *devān* + *ca*: *-n* before *c* → *-ṃś*: *devāṃś ca*. The same for *brāhmaṇāṃś ca*.
4. *nṛpo devāṃś ca brāhmaṇāṃś ca pūjayati*.

## Where it applies

- **Every word ending in a stop**: ablatives in *-āt* (*vṛkṣād vihagaḥ*), neuters like *tat*, *etat*, and the relative *yat* (*yac chreyaḥ* "what is better", Bhg 2.7), verb forms in *-t* (*āsīd*, *agacchad*, *bhaved*), nouns like *vāk*, *marut*, *jagat*, *sarit*, *ṛtvik*.
- **Every word ending in *-n***: accusative plurals in *-ān*, *-īn*, *-ūn* (*devāṃś ca*, *śatrūñ jayati*), 3PL verbs in *-an* (*agacchann udyānam*), stems in *-n* and *-vat*, *-mat* (*rājan* in compounds, *bhavān*, *bhagavān*, *mahān*).
- **Preverbs ending in a consonant**: *ud* (*utpatati*, *udgacchati*), *nis* (*nirgacchati*), and *sam* (*saṃgacchate*). The seam inside a compound verb or compound noun follows the same rules (*bhīmavacanād rājā*, *tad-dhita*).
- **Compound nouns**: *jagat* + *nātha* "lord" → *jagannātha* "lord of the world" (the nasal rule inside a compound, san-05-08).
- **Verse and prose alike**: the joins run across the whole line. The epics sometimes leave a join unmade at the break between quarters of a verse (san-07-08).
- **Internal sandhi**, the joins inside a word, has partly different rules (san-05-07): *labdha* from √labh + *-ta* is not *\*labhta*.

## Watch out

**Do not voice before a voiceless sound.** *\*vṛkṣād phalaṃ patati* is wrong: *ph* is voiceless, so *-t* stays: *vṛkṣāt phalam*.

**Do not apply vowel sandhi to a consonant join.** *-t* before a vowel is *-d* with a space: *āsīd ekaḥ*, not *\*āsīdekaḥ* written as one word in `translit` (the guide's spacing rule, san-01-09), and not *\*āsīt ekaḥ*.

**Do not forget the *s* of *-n* before *c* and *t*.** *\*devān ca*, *\*devāñ ca* are wrong in Classical texts: *devāṃś ca*. *\*vīrān tatra* is wrong: *vīrāṃs tatra*. But before *k* and *p* nothing happens: *devān paśyati*.

**Do not double *-n* after a long vowel.** *\*devānn atra* is wrong: *devān atra*. Only a short vowel before the *n* doubles it: *agacchann atra*, *bhavann api*.

**Do not read *ch* as an original *ch*.** In *tac chāstram* and *devāñ chaṃsati* the *ch* is a joined *ś*. Look it up under *ś*.

**Do not take a nasal for a nasal stem.** *tan mama* is *tat mama*, not a word *\*tan*. Before a nasal, any *-n*, *-ṅ* or *-m* may stand for a stop.

**Do not use the old spellings without checking the edition.** Editions vary in a few places: *-n* before *l* is often left unchanged (Whitney §206a), and *ś* after *-t* is sometimes kept. Copy the edition; when you build a sentence, use the rules above.

**Do not forget the visarga rules.** *-ḥ* is still governed by san-02-06: *śiṣyas tac* (*-ḥ* before *t*), *nṛpo devān* (*-aḥ* before a voiced sound).

## Connections

French liaison is a small cousin of these rules: *les amis* [lez‿ami] brings back a final consonant before a vowel, as Sanskrit brings back the *s* of *-ns* before *c* and *t*. Korean consonant assimilation (*국물* gungmul "broth", spelt *guk* + *mul*: a stop becomes a nasal before a nasal) works like rule 2. Korean writes the unchanged form; Sanskrit writes what is said.

## Exercises

1. Join: *vṛkṣāt* + *patati*; *vṛkṣāt* + *bhūmau*; *vāk* + *mama*.
2. Join: *tat* + *jñānam*; *tat* + *śrutvā*; *tat* + *hi*.
3. Join: *devān* + *ca*; *devān* + *tyajati*; *devān* + *atra*; *bhavan* + *api*.
4. Undo: *tac chāstram*, *vāg eva*, *devāṃs tatra*, *tan na*.
5. Build "The pupils came from the village and sat down" with *grāmāt*, *āgacchan* and *upāviśan* joined with *ca*.
6. Build "The king saw the heroes there" with *vīrān* and *tatra*.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*nṛpo devān ca pūjayati*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*vṛkṣād phalaṃ patati*.

## Worked answers

1. *vṛkṣāt patati* (*p* is voiceless: no change); *vṛkṣād bhūmau* (*bh* is voiced: *-d*); *vāṅ mama* (before a nasal, the nasal of the velar row) (Whitney §§159, 161).
2. *taj jñānam*; *tac chrutvā*; *tad dhi* (Whitney §§202–203, 163).
3. *devāṃś ca*; *devāṃs tyajati*; *devān atra* (long vowel: no doubling); *bhavann api* (short vowel: doubled) (Whitney §§208, 210).
4. *tat śāstram*, *vāk eva*, *devān tatra*, *tat na* (this lesson).
5. *śiṣyā grāmād āgacchann upāviśaṃś ca*. *-āḥ* before *g* drops *-ḥ*; *grāmāt* before *ā* → *grāmād*; *āgacchan* after a short vowel before *u* → *āgacchann*; *upāviśan* before *c* → *upāviśaṃś* (san-04-04 for the forms).
6. *nṛpo vīrāṃs tatrāpaśyat*. *vīrān* before *t* → *vīrāṃs*; *tatra* + *apaśyat* → *tatrāpaśyat* (san-03-07).
7. *-n* before *c* becomes *-ṃś*: *nṛpo devāṃś ca pūjayati* (Whitney §208).
8. *ph* is voiceless, so *-t* stays: *vṛkṣāt phalaṃ patati*.
