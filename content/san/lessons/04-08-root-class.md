## The idea

The thematic verbs of san-04-01 put an *a* between root and ending, and their stem never changes. The other six classes have no such vowel. Their endings go straight onto the stem, and the stem itself changes between a **strong** form and a **weak** form. These are the **athematic** verbs: verbs with no theme vowel, whose stem alternates. You have met one already: *asti* "is", *santi* "are" (san-02-07).

The simplest athematic class is class 2, the **root class** (Indian name *adādi*, after √ad "eat"). Its present stem is the bare root (Whitney §611): √i "go" → *e-ti* "goes", *y-anti* "they go"; √ad → *at-ti* "eats", *ad-anti* "they eat".

**Why the stem changes.** In the parent language the accent moved inside these verbs. Whitney (§604) describes it: "the tone being now upon the ending, and now upon the root". Where the root had the accent it took guṇa, the strong form; where the ending had it, the root stayed weak. This is the same mechanism you saw in nouns with strong and weak cases (san-03-14), and the same guṇa as in san-03-11. The accent is no longer written, but the vowel still shows where it fell.

**Which forms are strong.** Whitney (§552) lists the endings that never take the accent: the **singular of the active** (present, imperfect) and the 3SG active imperative, plus **all the first persons of the imperative**, active and middle. Before these the root is strong. Everywhere else, including the whole middle voice, it is weak. Learn this list once; it applies to all six athematic classes.

**Why learn them.** The athematic verbs are few, but they are among the commonest in any text: "be", "go", "eat", "kill", "say", "know", "sit", "lie", "sleep". Their irregularities come from the meeting of a consonant-final root with a consonant-initial ending, the consonant sandhi of san-04-07 applied inside the word.

## How it is built

**Step 1: strong or weak.** Strong (guṇa) before the active singular endings: √i → *e-*; √vid → *ved-*; √stu → *stau-* (vṛddhi, a special rule for roots in *u*, Whitney §626). Weak (simple root) elsewhere: *i-*, *vid-*, *stu-*. Roots with *a* show the difference only by losing or keeping that *a*: √as → strong *as-*, weak *s-*; √han → strong *han-*, weak *ha-* before consonants and *ghn-* before vowels (Whitney §637).

**Step 2: the endings.** Primary endings as in the thematic verbs, but put directly on the stem, and with two differences:

- 3PL active *-anti* (the *a* belongs to the ending): *ad-anti*, *vid-anti*, *s-anti*.
- 3PL middle *-ate*, 3DU middle *-āte*: *ās-ate* "they sit", *ās-āte* "the two sit" (Whitney §612).

Before a vowel ending a root in *i* becomes *y*, one in *u* becomes *uv*: *y-anti*, *stuv-anti*, *bruv-anti*.

**Step 3: the joins inside the word.** The root's last consonant meets the ending's first. The rules are those of san-04-07, with a few extra ones for inside words (san-05-07):

- √ad: *ad* + *ti* → *atti* (voiceless before *t*); *ad* + *si* → *atsi*.
- √vid: *ved* + *ti* → *vetti*; *ved* + *mi* → *vedmi*.
- √dviṣ "hate": *dveṣ* + *ti* → *dveṣṭi* (the retroflex *ṣ* turns *t* retroflex).

**The imperfect.** Augment + stem (strong in the singular) + secondary endings, 3PL *-an*: *āsīt*, *āsan* (√as); *ait*, *āyan* (√i: augment *a* + *e-* → *ai*, san-04-03); *ahan*, *aghnan* (√han). Where the 3SG *-t* would stand after a root consonant, two consonants would end the word, and the second drops (Whitney §150): √han, *a-han-t* → *ahan*. Some verbs add *ī* instead to keep the ending: *āsīt*, *abravīt*.

**The imperative.** 2SG *-hi* after a vowel, *-dhi* after a consonant (Whitney §617), on the weak stem: *i-hi* "go!", *brū-hi* "say!", *ad-dhi* "eat!", *vid-dhi* "know!". √han has *jahi* "kill!" and √as *edhi* "be!": two old irregular forms. The 3SG is strong (*etu*, *vettu*, *hantu*, *astu*), the 3PL weak (*yantu*, *ghnantu*, *santu*).

**The optative** adds *-yā-* to the weak stem (san-04-06): *s-yā-t*, *i-yā-t*, *ad-yā-t*, *han-yā-t*, *brū-yā-t*, *vid-yā-t*.

**The middle** is weak throughout: *ās-te* "sits", *brū-te* "says" (√brū has both voices), *īṣ-ṭe* "rules".

**Two verbs of saying.** √brū "say" takes an *ī* before the consonant endings of its strong forms (Whitney §632): *bravī-mi*, *bravī-ṣi*, *bravī-ti*, *a-bravī-t*; weak *brū-maḥ*, *bruv-anti*. √vac "speak" has in the present only *vacmi*, *vakṣi*, *vakti* (Monier-Williams), so √brū supplies the present system, and √vac the rest: perfect *uvāca*, past participle *ukta*, future *vakṣyati*.

**√vid** "know" has the regular present *vetti*, *vittaḥ*, *vidanti*. A second present, *veda* "he knows", is formally a perfect; it is taught in san-04-18.

**The chain of thought.** "The deer eat grass."

1. √ad, class 2: endings on the root.
2. 3PL active, a weak form: *ad-* + *-anti* → *adanti*.
3. *mṛgāḥ tṛṇam adanti*; *mṛgāḥ* before *t* → *mṛgās*: *mṛgās tṛṇam adanti*.

## Where it applies

- **The common root-class verbs**: √as "be", √i "go", √yā "go, travel", √ad "eat", √han "kill", √brū "say", √vid "know", √svap "sleep" (*svapiti*, with an *i*), √rud "weep" (*roditi*, with an *i*), √dviṣ "hate" (*dveṣṭi*), √stu "praise" (*stauti*), √snā "bathe" (*snāti*); middle √ās "sit", √śī "lie", √īś "rule, be master of" (*īṣṭe*), √brū (*brūte*).
- **Compounds of these roots**, which keep the class: *adhi-i* "study" (middle *adhīte*, Monier-Williams), *upa-i* "approach" (*upaiti*), *ā-yā* "come" (*āyāti*), *prati-brū* "answer".
- **Strong forms**: present and imperfect active singular (*eti*, *ait*), 3SG imperative active (*etu*), all 1st person imperatives (*ayāni*, *asāni*).
- **Weak forms**: everything else: active dual and plural, the whole middle, the optative, the 2SG imperative (*ihi*), the present participle (san-04-20).
- **The weak root reappears** in the past participle and other forms built on the root (*ita*, *hata*, *ukta*, san-04-13): learning the weak stem now pays off later.
- **Other athematic classes** (3, 5, 7, 8, 9) follow the same strong/weak pattern on their own stems (san-04-09 to san-04-12).

## Watch out

**Do not add a theme vowel.** *\*adati*, *\*hanati*, *\*eṣati* are wrong: class 2 has no *-a-*. Classical *atti*, *hanti*, *eti*. (Epic texts sometimes give such verbs thematic forms: san-07-08.)

**Do not use the strong stem in the plural.** *\*ayanti*, *\*vedanti*, *\*stauvanti* are wrong: *yanti*, *vidanti*, *stuvanti*.

**Do not use *-nti* without *a* after a consonant root.** *\*adnti* is impossible: the athematic 3PL is *-anti*: *adanti*.

**Do not give the middle 3PL *-nte*.** *\*āsante* is wrong for "they sit": athematic middle 3PL is *-ate*, *āsate*. And the dual is *-āte*: *āsāte*.

**Do not build *\*hantanti* or *\*hananti*.** √han loses its *a* before a vowel and its *h* becomes *gh*: *ghnanti* (Whitney §637).

**Do not form the present of √vac.** *\*vacati* and *\*vacanti* are wrong. Use √brū for the present system: *bravīti*, *bruvanti*, *abravīt*. Use √vac for the perfect, future and participles.

**Do not drop the *ī* of √brū.** *\*braviti* and *\*abravit* are wrong: *bravīti*, *abravīt*. And it appears only before consonant endings of the strong forms: *bruvanti*, not *\*bravīnti*.

**Do not take *ait* for a noun or particle.** It is the imperfect of √i, "he went": *a* + *e-* + *t*, with the augment's vṛddhi (san-04-03).

## Connections

Greek keeps the same contrast of strong and weak stems in its athematic verbs: *eîmi* "I go" (Sanskrit *emi*), *ímen* "we go" (*imaḥ*); *oîda* "I know", *ísmen* "we know". Latin *est*, *sunt* and Spanish *es*, *son* still show the weak stem of √as without the vowel: Sanskrit *asti*, *santi*.

## Exercises

1. Give the present 3SG, 3PL and 1PL of √yā, √rud and √dviṣ.
2. Give the imperfect 3SG and 3PL of √i and √han, and explain each.
3. Give the 2SG imperative of √i, √brū, √ad and √han.
4. Build "The sages praise the gods" with √stu.
5. Build "The pupil studies the Veda" with *adhi-i* (middle).
6. Translate *dūto nṛpam abravīt*, then put it in the present plural ("the messengers tell the king").
7. Find and fix the error: *\*mṛgāḥ tṛṇam adanti*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*vyādhā mṛgān hananti*.

## Worked answers

1. √yā: *yāti*, *yānti*, *yāmaḥ*. √rud: *roditi*, *rudanti*, *rudimaḥ* (the *i* is added before consonant endings, Wiktionary रोदिति). √dviṣ: *dveṣṭi*, *dviṣanti*, *dviṣmaḥ* (strong *dveṣ-* only in the singular; this lesson).
2. √i: *ait* (augment *a* + strong *e-* → *ai*, + *-t*), *āyan* (*a* + weak *y-* + *-an*). √han: *ahan* (strong *han-* + *-t*, the *t* dropped because only one consonant can end a word, Whitney §150), *aghnan* (weak *ghn-* before a vowel).
3. *ihi*, *brūhi* (*-hi* after a vowel), *addhi* (*-dhi* after a consonant, Whitney §617), *jahi* (irregular).
4. *munayo devān stuvanti*. Weak *stuv-* before *-anti*; *munayaḥ* before *d* → *munayo*.
5. *śiṣyo vedam adhīte*. Middle 3SG *-te* on the weak root *i*, with *adhi* + *i* → *adhī* (san-03-07); Monier-Williams gives *adhīte* (and, more rarely, *adhīyate*).
6. "The messenger said to the king." Present plural: *dūtā nṛpaṃ bruvanti*. Weak *bruv-* before *-anti*; *dūtāḥ* before *n* drops *-ḥ*.
7. *-āḥ* before *t* becomes *-ās*: *mṛgās tṛṇam adanti* (san-02-06).
8. √han is *ghn-* before a vowel: *vyādhā mṛgān ghnanti*.
