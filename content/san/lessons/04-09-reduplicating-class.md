## The idea

Class 3 makes its present stem by repeating the start of the root: √dā "give" → *dadā-*, √hu "offer" → *juho-*, √bhī "fear" → *bibhe-*. This repetition is called **reduplication**: copying the first consonant of a root, with a vowel, in front of it. The Indian name of the class is *juhotyādi*, after *juhoti* "he offers".

You have used one of these verbs since san-03-02: *dadāti* "gives". Its strong and weak stems, *dadā-* and *dad-*, follow the athematic pattern of san-04-08: strong before the active singular endings, weak elsewhere.

Reduplication is not only a present-stem device. Sanskrit uses it in four places: the class 3 present, the perfect (*jagāma* "he went", san-04-18), one kind of aorist (san-04-19) and the desiderative (*jigamiṣati* "wants to go", san-07-02). Whitney (§589) notes that the vowel of the repeated syllable is "shorter and lighter" in the present and perfect, longer in the aorist. Learn the consonant rules once here; they hold for all four.

What reduplication meant in the parent language is not certain; the guide does not try to say why these roots took it. In Classical Sanskrit it is a form, not a meaning: *dadāti* is plain "gives".

## How it is built

**Step 1: the reduplicating syllable.** Copy the root's first consonant and its vowel, with these adjustments (Whitney §§590, 643):

1. **An aspirate is copied without its aspiration**: √dhā → *da-dhā-*; √bhī → *bi-bhī-*; √bhṛ → *bi-bhṛ-*.
2. **A velar or *h* is copied as the matching palatal**: √hu → *ju-hu-*; √hā "leave" → *ja-hā-*; (perfect √kṛ → *ca-kṛ-*, san-04-18).
3. **A long vowel is copied short**: √dā → *da-dā-*; √bhī → *bi-bhī-*.
4. ***ṛ* is copied as *i*** in the present: √bhṛ → *bi-bhṛ-*.

**Step 2: strong and weak.** The strong stem has guṇa (or keeps its *ā*): *juho-*, *bibhe-*, *bibhar-*, *dadā-*, *dadhā-*. The weak stem is the plain root after the reduplication: *juhu-*, *bibhī-* (or *bibhi-*), *bibhṛ-*. Roots in *ā* lose the *ā* in weak forms before a vowel and in most consonant forms: √dā → *dad-*, √dhā → *dadh-* (*dadati*, *dadhati*).

**Step 3: the endings.** As in the root class, with one feature special to class 3: **the 3PL has no *n*** (Whitney §646). Active *-ati*, middle *-ate*: *dadati* "they give", *juhvati* "they offer", *bibhyati* "they fear", *dadate* "they give" (middle). The imperfect 3PL is *-uḥ*, with guṇa of a final vowel before it: *adaduḥ*, *ajuhavuḥ*, *abibharuḥ*.

**Joins inside the word.** Weak *dad-* before *t* gives *tt*: *dattaḥ*, *datte*. √dhā is odd: weak *dadh-* + *-taḥ* gives *dhattaḥ*, with the aspiration moved to the front (Whitney §160c). Weak √bhī before a vowel is *bibhy-*, √hu *juhv-*.

**The imperative.** 2SG: √hu *juhudhi* (*-dhi* after *u*, an old exception), √bhī *bibhīhi*, √bhṛ *bibhṛhi*, √hā *jahihi*. √dā and √dhā have the short irregular forms ***dehi*** "give!" and ***dhehi*** "put!". The 3SG is strong (*dadātu*, *juhotu*), the 3PL *-atu* without *n* (*dadatu*).

**The optative** is weak + *-yā-*: *dadyāt*, *dadhyāt*, *juhuyāt*, *bibhīyāt*.

**Middle forms** are common with √dā and √dhā and have meanings for oneself: *datte* "takes for himself" (usually with *ā*: *ādatte* "takes", Pāṇ. 1.3.20 as cited by Monier-Williams), *dhatte* "puts on, holds".

**Compounds of √dhā** are very common: *vi-dhā* "arrange, do, make" (*vidadhāti*), *ni-dhā* "put down, deposit" (*nidadhāti*), *śrad-dhā* "believe, trust" (*śraddadhāti*), built on the old noun *śrad* "heart, trust" (Whitney §1079).

**The chain of thought.** "The priest offers the oblation into the fire."

1. "Offer (into the fire)" is √hu, class 3: reduplicate *h* as *j*: *ju-hu-*; strong *juho-* in the 3SG: *juhoti*.
2. Subject *ṛtvik*, object *haviḥ* (neuter s-stem, san-03-15), place *agnau* (locative).
3. Joins: *ṛtvik* + *agnau* → *ṛtvig agnau* (voiced before a vowel, san-04-07); *haviḥ* + *juhoti* → *havir juhoti* (san-02-06).
4. *ṛtvig agnau havir juhoti*.

## Where it applies

- **The common class 3 verbs**: √dā "give", √dhā "put, place", √hu "offer into the fire", √bhī "fear", √bhṛ "bear, support", √hā "leave, abandon", √hrī "be ashamed" (*jihreti*), middle √mā "measure" (*mimīte*).
- **Compounds**, very frequent: *ā-dā* "take" (middle *ādatte*), *pra-dā* "give, bestow", *vi-dhā* "arrange, do", *ni-dhā* "deposit", *śrad-dhā* "believe", *sam-ā-dhā* "put together, compose".
- **Strong forms**: active singular present and imperfect (*dadāti*, *adadāt*, *juhoti*, *ajuhot*), 3SG imperative (*dadātu*), first persons of the imperative (*dadāni*).
- **Weak forms**: all others, including the whole middle (*datte*, *dhatte*) and the optative (*dadyāt*).
- **The 3PL without *n*** in the present (*dadati*), imperative (*dadatu*), and with *-uḥ* in the imperfect (*adaduḥ*).
- **Reduplication elsewhere**: the perfect (*dadau* "he gave", *dadhau*, *jaghāna*), one aorist type and the desiderative use the same copying rules (san-04-18, san-04-19, san-07-02).
- **Nouns from these roots**: *dāna*, *dātṛ* (√dā); *havis*, *hotṛ* "priest" (√hu); *bhaya* (√bhī); *bhṛtya* "servant" (√bhṛ): you know several.

## Watch out

**Do not give the 3PL an *n*.** *\*dadanti*, *\*juhvanti*, *\*bibhranti* are wrong: class 3 loses the *n*, *dadati*, *juhvati*, *bibhrati* (Whitney §646).

**Do not reduplicate with the aspirate.** *\*dhadhāti*, *\*bhibharti* are wrong: *dadhāti*, *bibharti*.

**Do not reduplicate *h* as *h*.** *\*huhoti* is wrong: *juhoti*. A velar or *h* is copied as a palatal.

**Do not copy the long vowel.** *\*dādāti*, *\*bībheti* are wrong: *dadāti*, *bibheti*.

**Do not keep the *ā* in weak forms.** *\*dadāte* for "they give (middle)" and *\*dadātaḥ* for "the two give" are wrong: *dadate*, *dattaḥ*.

**Do not form *\*dadāhi* or *\*dadhāhi*.** The 2SG imperatives are *dehi* and *dhehi*.

**Do not take *datte* as "gives to another".** The middle of √dā leans towards "takes for oneself", and *ādatte* is "takes". The active *dadāti* is "gives".

**Do not confuse *dadati* "they give" with *dadāti* "he gives".** One long vowel decides between singular and plural. Read the *ā* carefully.

## Connections

Greek has the same reduplicated presents, from the same roots: *dídōmi* "I give" (Sanskrit *dadāmi*), *títhēmi* "I put" (*dadhāmi*: Greek, like Sanskrit, drops the aspiration of the copy). Latin keeps traces in *sistō* "I set" and in perfects such as *dedī* "I gave", *cucurrī* "I ran"; Spanish and French have lost reduplication.

## Exercises

1. Give the 3SG, 3PL and 1SG present of √dhā, √bhṛ and √hā.
2. State the reduplication rules that make *juhoti*, *bibharti* and *dadhāti*.
3. Give the 2SG imperative of √dā, √dhā, √hu and √bhī.
4. Build "The sons fear their father" with √bhī.
5. Build "The king gave gold to the priests" (imperfect, *suvarṇa*, *ṛtvij*).
6. Translate *vidyā dadāti vinayam*, and give the 3PL of its verb.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*dhanino dhanaṃ dadanti*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*ṛtvijo havir huhvati*.

## Worked answers

1. √dhā: *dadhāti*, *dadhati*, *dadhāmi*. √bhṛ: *bibharti*, *bibhrati*, *bibharmi*. √hā: *jahāti*, *jahati*, *jahāmi* (Wiktionary दधाति, बिभर्ति, जहाति).
2. *juhoti*: *h* copied as *j* (a palatal for *h*), strong *ho* with guṇa. *bibharti*: *bh* copied as *b* (no aspirate), *ṛ* copied as *i*, strong *bhar* with guṇa. *dadhāti*: *dh* copied as *d* (Whitney §§590, 643).
3. *dehi*, *dhehi* (irregular), *juhudhi*, *bibhīhi* (this lesson).
4. *putrāḥ pitur bibhyati*. Ablative *pituḥ* (same form as the genitive, san-03-14); *-uḥ* before *b* → *-ur*; 3PL weak *bibhy-* + *-ati*. *putrāḥ* before *p* keeps *-ḥ*.
5. *nṛpa ṛtvigbhyaḥ suvarṇam adadāt*. *nṛpaḥ* before *ṛ* loses *-ḥ* (san-02-06); *ṛtvigbhyaḥ* is the dative plural of *ṛtvij*, with *k* → *g* before *bh* (san-04-07 rule, inside the word). Imperfect *a-dadā-t*.
6. "Learning gives modesty." 3PL *dadati* (weak stem, no *n*).
7. Class 3 has no *n* in the 3PL: *dhanino dhanaṃ dadati*.
8. *h* reduplicates as *j*: *ṛtvijo havir juhvati*.
