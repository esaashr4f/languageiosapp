## The idea

Two more athematic classes make their present stem with a suffix in *u*.

- **Class 5** (*svādi*, after √su "press out") adds ***-nu-***: √su → *sunu-*, √śak "be able" → *śaknu-*, √āp "reach, obtain" → *āpnu-*. You already know one verb of this class: *śṛṇoti* "hears" (√śru, san-02-09).
- **Class 8** (*tanādi*, after √tan "stretch") adds ***-u-***: √tan → *tanu-*. Whitney (§697B) counts only "about half-a-dozen" roots here, nearly all ending in *n*, so that *tan-u-* looks just like a *nu*-stem.

The two classes are inflected alike. Each suffix has a strong form with guṇa, *-no-* and *-o-*, and a weak form, *-nu-* and *-u-*, on the athematic pattern of san-04-08: strong in the active singular, weak elsewhere. Whitney (§697) says the suffix "in the strong forms receives the accent, and is strengthened".

Class 8 matters for one verb above all: **√kṛ "do, make"**, one of the commonest words in the language. Whitney (§714) notes that in the later language it is "inflected in the present-system exclusively according to the u-class", the only such root not ending in *n*, and with two irregularities: the root itself also takes guṇa in the strong forms (*karo-*), and in the weak forms it becomes *kur-* (*kuru-*). So *karoti* "does", *kurvanti* "they do".

## How it is built

**Class 5, root ending in a vowel** (√su, √ci "gather", √śru):

- strong *-no-*: *suno-ti*, *cino-ti*, *śṛṇo-ti*;
- weak *-nu-*: *sunu-taḥ*; before a vowel ending *-nv-*: *sunv-anti*, *cinv-anti*, *śṛṇv-anti*;
- before the *v* and *m* of the 1st dual and plural the *u* may drop: *sunvaḥ* or *sunuvaḥ*, *sunmaḥ* or *sunumaḥ* (Whitney §697a).

**Class 5, root ending in a consonant** (√śak, √āp):

- strong *-no-*: *śakno-ti*, *āpno-ti*;
- weak *-nu-*; before a vowel ending *-nuv-*, because two consonants already stand before it (Whitney §697a): *śaknuv-anti*, *āpnuv-anti*;
- the *u* never drops: *śaknumaḥ*.

**The imperative 2SG.** After a root ending in a consonant, *-hi*: *āpnu-hi*, *śaknu-hi*. After a vowel, the bare stem: *sunu*, *cinu*, *śṛṇu* "listen!" (Whitney §703).

**The imperfect** has strong *-no-* in the singular, *-nv-*/*-nuv-* + *-an* in the 3PL: *aśaknot*, *aśaknuvan*; *āpnot* (augment + *āp-*: *a* + *ā* → *ā*), *āpnuvan*.

**The optative** is weak + *-yā-*: *śaknuyāt*, *āpnuyāt*.

**Class 8: √tan.** Strong *tano-*, weak *tanu-*, before a vowel *tanv-*: *tanoti*, *tanutaḥ*, *tanvanti*; middle *tanute*.

**√kṛ** (Whitney §714; Wiktionary करोति):

- strong *karo-*: *karomi*, *karoṣi*, *karoti*; imperfect *akaravam*, *akaroḥ*, *akarot*;
- weak *kuru-*: *kurutaḥ*, *kurutha*, middle *kurute*;
- *kurv-* before a vowel: *kurvanti*, *akurvan*, middle *kurvate*, *akurvata*;
- *kur-* before *m*, *v*, *y*: *kurmaḥ*, *kurvaḥ*, optative *kuryāt*;
- imperative: *karotu*, *kuru* "do!", *kurvantu*; 1SG *karavāṇi* (with *ṇ* by the n-to-ṇ rule after *r*, san-03-01); middle *kuruṣva*;
- middle optative *kurvīta*.

**Compounds.** √āp with *pra* gives the very common *prāpnoti* "attains, obtains" (*pra* + *āp* → *prāp*). √kṛ makes dozens: *upa-kṛ* "help" (*upakaroti*, with the person helped in the genitive), *sam-s-kṛ* "prepare, refine" (the source of *saṃskṛta*, san-01-09), *pari-ṣ-kṛ* "adorn"; the *s* that appears after *sam* and *pari* is a peculiarity of √kṛ (Monier-Williams). Middle *kurute* is "does for himself".

**The chain of thought.** "What did they do?"

1. "Do" is √kṛ, class 8. Past narration: imperfect.
2. 3PL: weak stem *kurv-* before the vowel ending: *a-kurv-an*.
3. In a text the middle is just as common: *a-kurv-ata* (middle 3PL secondary *-ata*, athematic).
4. "What": *kim*. *kim akurvan* or *kim akurvata*.

## Where it applies

- **Class 5 verbs**: √śak "be able", √āp "reach, obtain", √su "press out (soma)", √ci "gather, pile up", √śru "hear" (stem *śṛṇo-*, Whitney §711), √vṛ "choose, cover" (*vṛṇoti*, middle *vṛṇute* "chooses for oneself").
- **Class 8 verbs**: √tan "stretch, spread" (*tanoti*), √man in an older class 8 present (*manute*, beside class 4 *manyate*), and √kṛ.
- **Compounds of √āp and √kṛ**: *prāpnoti* "attains", *avāpnoti* "obtains"; *upakaroti* "helps", *saṃskaroti* "prepares, perfects", *anukaroti* "imitates", *alaṃkaroti* "adorns" (with *alam* "enough, fittingly", san-03-02).
- **√kṛ in every role of "make, do"**: deeds (*karma karoti*), effort (*yatnaṃ karoti*), keeping a vow (*vrataṃ karoti*), with two accusatives "make X into Y", and in later prose as a light verb with nouns of action: *praṇāmaṃ karoti* "makes a bow, bows".
- **√kṛ middle**: for oneself, or plain "does" in verse (*akurvata*, Bhg 1.1).
- **Strong and weak**, as in all athematic classes: strong in the active singular and the 1st persons of the imperative (*karavāṇi*), weak elsewhere.
- **Other systems of √kṛ** are built on the root, not on *karo-*: perfect *cakāra*, future *kariṣyati*, past participle *kṛta*, infinitive *kartum*, absolutive *kṛtvā* (san-04-13 to san-04-18).

## Watch out

**Do not make √kṛ thematic.** *\*karati* is wrong in Classical Sanskrit: *karoti* (Whitney §714). (*kara* "hand, doer" is a noun.)

**Do not use *karo-* in the plural.** *\*karonti*, *\*karomaḥ* are wrong: *kurvanti*, *kurmaḥ*.

**Do not keep the *u* in *\*kuruyāt* or *\*kurumaḥ*.** √kṛ always drops it before *m, v, y*: *kuryāt*, *kurmaḥ*, *kurvaḥ*.

**Do not mix *-nv-* and *-nuv-*.** *\*śaknvanti* and *\*sunuvanti* are wrong. After a consonant root, *-nuv-* (*śaknuvanti*); after a vowel root, *-nv-* (*sunvanti*).

**Do not add *-hi* after a vowel root.** *\*śṛṇuhi* is not the Classical form (Whitney §703): "listen!" is *śṛṇu*. But after a consonant root *-hi* is required: *āpnuhi*, not *\*āpnu*.

**Do not treat *āpnot* as present.** The augment of √āp merges with its *ā*: *āpnot* "he obtained" differs from *āpnoti* "obtains" only in the ending.

**Do not give the middle 3PL *-nte*.** Athematic middle 3PL is *-ate*: *kurvate*, *akurvata*, not *\*kurvante*.

## Connections

Greek has a *-nu-* class too, with the same strong and weak forms: *deíknūmi* "I show", *deíknumen* "we show". √kṛ has no direct descendant in Spanish or French, but Sanskrit *karman* and *kṛta* entered other languages: Hindi *karnā* "to do" and *kiyā* "did" continue √kṛ, and English *karma* is *karman*. Japanese uses *suru* "do" as a light verb with nouns of action (*benkyō suru* "study") in the way later Sanskrit uses √kṛ (*praṇāmaṃ karoti*).

## Exercises

1. Give the present 3SG, 3PL and 1PL of √śak, √ci and √tan.
2. Give the imperfect 3SG and 3PL of √kṛ, active and middle.
3. Give the 2SG imperative of √kṛ, √āp, √śru and √su.
4. Build "The sage attains peace" with *pra-āp* and *śānti*.
5. Build "Let me do your work" with the 1SG imperative of √kṛ and *te*.
6. Build "We help the poor" with *upa-kṛ* (*daridra*, genitive plural).
7. Find and fix the error: *\*te karma karonti*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*vayaṃ yatnaṃ kurumaḥ*.

## Worked answers

1. √śak: *śaknoti*, *śaknuvanti*, *śaknumaḥ*. √ci: *cinoti*, *cinvanti*, *cinumaḥ* or *cinmaḥ*. √tan: *tanoti*, *tanvanti*, *tanumaḥ* or *tanmaḥ* (Whitney §697a: the *u* may drop after a vowel or *n* root, never after a consonant root like √śak).
2. Active *akarot*, *akurvan*; middle *akuruta*, *akurvata* (Wiktionary करोति).
3. *kuru*, *āpnuhi* (consonant root: *-hi*), *śṛṇu*, *sunu* (vowel roots: bare stem; Whitney §703).
4. *muniḥ śāntiṃ prāpnoti*. *pra* + *āp* → *prāp*; strong *-no-* in the 3SG. *muniḥ* before *ś* keeps *-ḥ*.
5. *karavāṇi te kāryam*. 1SG imperative, strong *karav-* with *-āṇi* (the *ṇ* by the n-to-ṇ rule, san-03-01).
6. *vayaṃ daridrāṇām upakurmaḥ*. *kur-* before *m*; the person helped in the genitive.
7. *kar-* is strong only in the singular: *te karma kurvanti*.
8. √kṛ drops the *u* before *m*: *vayaṃ yatnaṃ kurmaḥ*.
