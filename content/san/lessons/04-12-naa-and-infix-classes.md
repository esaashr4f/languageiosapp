## The idea

The last two classes both make their present stem with an *n*, in different places.

- **Class 9** (*kryādi*, after √krī "buy") adds a suffix after the root: strong ***-nā-***, weak ***-nī-***, and before a vowel ending only ***-n-*** (Whitney §717): *krī-ṇā-ti* "buys", *krī-ṇī-taḥ* "the two buy", *krī-ṇ-anti* "they buy". You have used *jānāti* "knows" (√jñā) since san-02-09; it belongs here.
- **Class 7** (*rudhādi*, after √rudh "obstruct") puts the nasal **inside** the root, before its last consonant. In the strong forms it is the syllable *-na-*, in the weak forms a bare nasal of the same row as the consonant that follows (Whitney §683): √yuj "join" → *yu-na-k-ti* "joins", *yu-ñ-j-anti* "they join". A sound inserted inside a root like this is called an **infix**. All class 7 roots end in a consonant.

Both follow the athematic pattern you know: strong in the active singular and the 1st persons of the imperative, weak elsewhere. Only the shape of the class sign is new.

With these two, all ten classes are covered: four thematic (1, 4, 6, 10) and six athematic (2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9). Every present stem in the language is built by one of these ten methods, plus the passive (san-04-14) and the secondary conjugations of part 7.

## How it is built

**Class 9, root ending in a vowel** (√krī, √pū "purify"):

- strong *-nā-*: *krīṇāti*, *krīṇāmi*; imperfect *akrīṇāt*;
- weak *-nī-* before a consonant: *krīṇītaḥ*, *krīṇīmaḥ*, middle *krīṇīte*; optative *krīṇīyāt*;
- *-n-* before a vowel: *krīṇanti*, middle *krīṇate*.

The *n* becomes *ṇ* after *r* or *ṛ* by the n-to-ṇ rule (san-03-01): *krīṇāti*, *gṛhṇāti*; not after *j* or *p*: *jānāti*, *punāti*. Roots in *ū* shorten it: √pū → *punāti* (Whitney §728).

**Class 9, irregular roots.** √grah "seize, take" is weakened to *gṛh-* (Whitney §729): *gṛhṇāti*, *gṛhṇanti*. √jñā drops its nasal: *jā-nā-ti*, *jānanti*. √bandh "bind" drops its nasal too: *badhnāti* (Monier-Williams).

**Class 9, root ending in a consonant: the 2SG imperative.** Whitney (§722) says Classical Sanskrit does not form this person from consonant roots with the class sign; instead it uses the special ending ***-āna***: √grah *gṛhāṇa* "take!", √bandh *badhāna* "bind!", √aś "eat" *aśāna*. Vowel roots have *-nīhi*: *krīṇīhi*, *jānīhi* "know!".

**Class 7.** Find the root's last consonant and put the nasal before it.

- strong: *-na-* before it, accented in the old language: √yuj → *yunaj-*; √rudh → *ruṇadh-*; √bhid "split" → *bhinad-*; √chid "cut" → *chinad-*; √bhuj "eat, enjoy" → *bhunaj-*;
- weak: a nasal of the consonant's row: *yuñj-*, *rundh-*, *bhind-*, *chind-*, *bhuñj-*.

Then the endings join with the root consonant by the rules of san-04-07, applied inside the word:

- *yunaj* + *ti* → *yunakti* (*j* becomes *k* before *t*); weak *yuñj* + *taḥ* → *yuṅktaḥ* (the nasal follows the *k*);
- *ruṇadh* + *ti* → *ruṇaddhi* (*dh* + *t* → *ddh*, the aspiration moved to the end, Whitney §160);
- *bhinad* + *ti* → *bhinatti*; *bhind* + *anti* → *bhindanti*;
- √hiṃs "harm" has a nasal already: strong *hinas-ti*, weak *hiṃs-anti* (Whitney §696).

The imperfect 3SG loses its *-t* after the root consonant (only one consonant may end a word, Whitney §150): *ayunak*, *aruṇat*, *abhinat*. The 2SG imperative takes *-dhi*: *yuṅgdhi*, *bhinddhi*. These forms are rare in texts; recognise them.

**The middle** is common with √yuj and √bhuj: *yuṅkte* "yokes for himself, applies himself", *bhuṅkte* "eats, enjoys". Monier-Williams lists √bhuj "eat" as class 7 (a different √bhuj "bend" is class 6).

**The chain of thought.** "The merchant buys cloth in the market."

1. "Buy" is √krī, class 9. 3SG active is strong: *krī-ṇā-ti* (*ṇ* after *r*).
2. Subject *vaṇik*, object *vastram*, place *āpaṇe*.
3. Joins: *vaṇik* before the vowel *ā* → *vaṇig*; *vastram* before *k* → *vastraṃ*.
4. *vaṇig āpaṇe vastraṃ krīṇāti*.

## Where it applies

- **Class 9 verbs**: √krī "buy" (middle *krīṇīte* "buys for himself"; with *vi* "sell": *vikrīṇīte*), √jñā "know", √grah "seize, take, accept", √bandh "bind", √aś "eat" (*aśnāti*, Monier-Williams), √pū "purify".
- **Class 7 verbs**: √yuj "yoke, join; (middle) apply oneself", √rudh "obstruct, besiege", √bhid "split, break", √chid "cut", √bhuj "eat, enjoy, (middle) consume", √hiṃs "harm, injure".
- **Compounds**, which keep the class: *anu-jñā* "permit" (*anujānāti*), *vi-jñā* "understand" (*vijānāti*), *pra-grah* "hold out", *sam-grah* "collect", *ni-rudh* "restrain", *pra-yuj* "employ, use", *vi-yuj* "separate", *upa-bhuj* "enjoy".
- **The 2SG imperative in *-āna*** for class 9 roots ending in a consonant: *gṛhāṇa*, *badhāna*, *aśāna*.
- **Nouns from the same roots** show the root without the class sign: *yoga* (√yuj), *bhoga* "enjoyment" (√bhuj), *bheda* "split, difference" (√bhid), *bandhana* (√bandh), *jñāna* (√jñā), *vikraya* "sale" (√krī).
- **Other systems** go back to the root: past participles *yukta*, *bhinna*, *chinna*, *baddha*, *gṛhīta*, *jñāta*, *krīta* (san-04-13).

## Watch out

**Do not carry the nasal of class 7 into other forms.** *\*yunakta* is wrong for "joined": the past participle is *yukta*. The infix belongs to the present system (Whitney §683a names the few exceptions).

**Do not write the strong infix in weak forms.** *\*yunajanti*, *\*bhinadanti* are wrong: *yuñjanti*, *bhindanti*.

**Do not use *-nā-* before a vowel ending.** *\*krīṇānti* is wrong: *krīṇanti*. And *\*krīṇātaḥ* is wrong for the dual: weak *-nī-*, *krīṇītaḥ*.

**Do not form *\*gṛhṇīhi* or *\*grahāṇa*.** "Take!" is *gṛhāṇa*: the special *-āna* on the weakened root *gṛh-* (Whitney §§722, 729).

**Do not keep the nasal of √jñā and √bandh.** *\*jñānāti*, *\*bandhnāti* are wrong: *jānāti*, *badhnāti*.

**Do not forget the n-to-ṇ rule.** *\*krīnāti*, *\*gṛhnāti* are wrong: after *r* or *ṛ* the class sign has *ṇ*.

**Do not read *ruṇaddhi* as having an ending *-dhi*.** It is *ruṇadh* + *-ti*: the aspiration of the root's *dh* has moved onto the ending's *t* (Whitney §160).

## Connections

Latin has the same nasal infix in the present only: *iungō* "I join" (the cognate of *yunakti*) against *iūnxī*, *iūnctum*; *findō* "I split" (cognate with √bhid) against *fidī*, *fissum*. Spanish *uncir* "yoke" and French *joindre* continue *iungere*, and English *yoke* is cognate with *yuga*, *yoga*.

## Exercises

1. Give the present 3SG, 3PL and 1PL of √grah and √bandh.
2. Give the present 3SG and 3PL of √chid and √rudh, and show the join in each.
3. Give the 2SG imperative of √jñā, √grah and √krī.
4. Build "The servant buys food in the market" (*bhṛtya*, *anna*, *āpaṇa*).
5. Build "The two warriors cut the rope" with √chid and *rajju*.
6. Build "Know (2SG) the truth" with √jñā.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*janāḥ phalāni krīṇānti*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*yogī manaḥ yunajati* for "the yogi applies (yokes) his mind".

## Worked answers

1. √grah: *gṛhṇāti*, *gṛhṇanti*, *gṛhṇīmaḥ*. √bandh: *badhnāti*, *badhnanti*, *badhnīmaḥ*. Strong *-nā-*, weak *-nī-*, *-n-* before vowels; *gṛh-* and *badh-* are the weakened roots (Whitney §729; Monier-Williams).
2. √chid: *chinatti* (*chinad* + *ti*, *d* → *t* before *t*), *chindanti* (weak *chind-*). √rudh: *ruṇaddhi* (*ruṇadh* + *ti* → *ddh*, Whitney §160), *rundhanti* (this lesson).
3. *jānīhi* (vowel root: *-nīhi*), *gṛhāṇa* (consonant root: *-āna*), *krīṇīhi* (Whitney §722).
4. *bhṛtya āpaṇe 'nnaṃ krīṇāti*. *bhṛtyaḥ* before *ā* loses *-ḥ*; *āpaṇe* + *annam* → *āpaṇe 'nnam* with avagraha (san-03-07).
5. *yodhau rajjuṃ chinttaḥ*. Weak *chind-* before *-taḥ*: *d* becomes *t*, giving *chint-taḥ* (Wiktionary छिनत्ति).
6. *satyaṃ jānīhi*. Weak *jānī-* + *-hi*.
7. Before a vowel ending only *-n-*: *janāḥ phalāni krīṇanti*.
8. The 3SG is strong, *yunakti*; and *manaḥ* before *y* is *mano*: *yogī mano yunakti* (san-02-06). (In the sense "apply oneself" the middle *yuṅkte* is usual.)
