## The idea

A **participle** is an adjective made from a verb: English *gone*, *broken*, *spoken*. Sanskrit makes one by adding ***-ta*** (sometimes ***-na***) to the root: √gam → *gata* "gone", √kṛ → *kṛta* "done", √bhid → *bhinna* "split". This is the **past participle**. It is declined like any a-stem adjective (san-03-06): *gataḥ*, *gatā*, *gatam*.

It is the most useful single form in the later language. In the prose of the Hitopadeśa and the commentaries it does the work of a past tense more often than the imperfect does: *sa gataḥ* "he went", *mayā dṛṣṭam* "I saw (it)". No verb "be" is needed, because Sanskrit drops the copula freely (san-02-07): *sa gataḥ* is literally "he (is) gone".

Whitney (§952) explains its meaning. From a **transitive** verb (one that takes an object) the participle is passive: it describes what has undergone the action, *datta* "given", *ukta* "spoken". From an **intransitive** verb it has "only an indefinite past sense": *gata* "gone", *bhūta* "been, become", *patita* "fallen". So *gataḥ* means "having gone", but *dattaḥ* means "having been given", not "having given".

This passive meaning is why the doer of a transitive action stands in the **instrumental**: *nṛpeṇa śatravo jitāḥ* "the enemies (were) conquered by the king", that is, "the king conquered the enemies". The thing done is the subject, in the nominative, and the participle agrees with it. English turns it round in translation; Sanskrit does not.

For the cases where an active past is wanted with a transitive verb, Sanskrit adds the suffix ***-vat*** "having" to the participle: *kṛta* "done" → *kṛtavat* "having done". Whitney (§959) notes that this **active past participle** is rare in the old language but "quite common" later, "chiefly used predicatively ... with the value of a personal verb-form in a past tense": *sa tat kṛtavān* "he did that".

## How it is built

**Step 1: the weak root.** The participle takes the root in its weakest form (Whitney §954): no guṇa, and with the reductions you saw in the weak present stems.

- a nasal before the last consonant is dropped: √bandh → *baddha*;
- roots that weaken *va* to *u* or *ya* to *i* do so: √vac → *ukta*, √vas → *uṣita*, √vap → *upta*, √svap → *supta*; √yaj → *iṣṭa*, √prach → *pṛṣṭa*, √grah → *gṛhīta*;
- a final *-m* or *-n* after *a* is lost: √gam → *gata*, √han → *hata*, √yam → *yata*, √man → *mata*, √tan → *tata*;
- some roots in *ā* weaken it to *i* or *ī*: √sthā → *sthita*, √pā → *pīta*, √dhā → *hita* (Whitney §954c).

**Step 2: the suffix.**

- most roots add ***-ta*** directly: *kṛta*, *jita*, *śruta*, *bhūta*, *nīta*, *hṛta*, *jñāta*, *krīta*, *mṛta*.
- some add ***-ita***, with a joining *i*: *patita* "fallen", *pūjita* "honoured", *cintita*, *kathita*, *corita*. All class 10 verbs and other derived verbs take *-ita* on the strong stem without *-aya-* (*pūjaya-* → *pūj-ita*).
- about seventy roots take ***-na*** (Whitney §957), mainly roots in *ā*, roots in *ṝ*, and roots ending in *d*: *bhinna* (√bhid), *chinna* (√chid), *jīrṇa* (√jṝ "grow old", san-04-12), *hīna* "left, lacking" (√hā).

**Step 3: the joins.** The root's last consonant meets *t* by the internal sandhi of san-05-07, which works much like san-04-07: *yuj* + *ta* → *yukta*; *tyaj* + *ta* → *tyakta*; *dṛś* + *ta* → *dṛṣṭa* (*ś* + *t* → *ṣṭ*); *viś* + *ta* → *viṣṭa* (*upaviṣṭa* "seated"); *labh* + *ta* → *labdha* (*bh* + *t* → *bdh*, Whitney §160); *budh* + *ta* → *buddha*; *dah* + *ta* → *dagdha*. Learn the common ones as words; the vocabulary lists them.

Some participles are irregular: √dā → *datta* "given"; √jan → *jāta* "born"; √pac → *pakva* "cooked, ripe" (with *-va*); √śuṣ → *śuṣka* "dry" (with *-ka*).

**Step 4: agreement.** The participle agrees with its noun in gender, number and case:

- intransitive: *sa gataḥ*, *sā gatā*, *te gatāḥ* "he, she, they went";
- transitive: the thing done is the subject: *mayā ślokaḥ śrutaḥ* "I heard the verse" (the verse, m., heard by me); *mayā kathā śrutā* "I heard the story" (f.); *mayā vacanaṃ śrutam* "I heard the word" (n.).

**Step 5: the active *-tavat*.** Add *-vat* to the participle and decline it like *bhagavat* (san-03-17): NOM.SG.M *kṛtavān*, F *kṛtavatī*, N *kṛtavat*. The subject is in the nominative and the object in the accusative, as with a finite verb.

**With a copula for persons.** For the 1st and 2nd persons a form of √as is often added: *gato 'smi* "I have gone, I went", *prāptāḥ smaḥ* "we have arrived".

**How it is glossed.** Root and suffix are cut, the ending separate: `ga-ta-ḥ` `go-PST.PTCP-NOM.SG.M`; *-tavat* as one piece: `kṛ-tavān` `do-PST.PTCP.ACT.NOM.SG.M`. The translation shows whether the meaning is active or passive; the gloss does not.

**The chain of thought.** "The king saw the deer."

1. "See" is √dṛś (the present comes from √paś; san-02-01). Participle: *dṛṣṭa*.
2. √dṛś is transitive, so *dṛṣṭa* is passive: "seen". The deer is the subject: *mṛgaḥ dṛṣṭaḥ* "the deer (was) seen".
3. The king is the agent: instrumental *nṛpeṇa*.
4. *nṛpeṇa mṛgo dṛṣṭaḥ* (*mṛgaḥ* before *d* → *mṛgo*).
5. With the active participle instead: *nṛpo mṛgaṃ dṛṣṭavān*.

## Where it applies

- **As a past tense**, with no copula: *nṛpo vanaṃ gataḥ*, *nṛpeṇa śatravo jitāḥ*. Common in all later prose.
- **Intransitive verbs** give an active sense: *gata*, *āgata*, *patita*, *sthita* "standing, staying", *upaviṣṭa* "seated", *jāta* "born", *mṛta* "dead", *naṣṭa* "lost", *tuṣṭa* "pleased", *kupita* "angry", *bhīta* "afraid", *śrānta* "tired".
- **Transitive verbs** give a passive sense, with the agent in the instrumental: *mayā dṛṣṭam*, *śiṣyeṇa śloka uktaḥ*.
- **Impersonal use** of an intransitive verb, in the neuter with an instrumental: *mayā gatam* "I went" (literally "it was gone by me"). San-04-14 returns to this.
- **As an adjective**, attributive or predicative: *hataṃ mṛgam*, *jīrṇāni vāsāṃsi* "worn-out clothes", *pakvaṃ phalam* "ripe fruit".
- **As a noun**: neuter for the action or thing (*kṛtam* "deed", *uktam* "what was said", *jīvita* "life", *gīta* "song"); masculine for a person (*mṛtaḥ* "a dead man").
- **With a copula** to mark 1st and 2nd persons: *gato 'smi*, *prāptāḥ smaḥ*.
- **The active *-tavat*** for a transitive past with the object in the accusative: *jitavān*, *dṛṣṭavatī*. Mostly predicative (Whitney §959).
- **Many familiar adjectives are past participles**: *vṛddha* "old" (√vṛdh "grown"), *duṣṭa* "wicked" (√duṣ "spoiled"), *saṃskṛta* "perfected", *kḷpta* "arranged", *hita* "good for" (√dhā "placed"), *viśiṣṭa* "distinguished", *jīrṇa*.
- **In the absolute construction** with the locative, *vane gate nṛpe* "when the king had gone to the forest" (san-06-08).

## Watch out

**Do not read a transitive participle as active.** *nṛpo jitaḥ* is "the king was conquered", not "the king conquered". For "the king conquered" say *nṛpeṇa jitam* (with an object, *nṛpeṇa śatravo jitāḥ*) or *nṛpo jitavān*.

**Do not put the agent in the nominative.** *\*nṛpaḥ śatravo jitāḥ* for "the king conquered the enemies" is wrong: the agent of a passive participle is instrumental, *nṛpeṇa*.

**Do not make the participle agree with the agent.** *\*mayā kathā śrutaḥ* is wrong: the participle agrees with *kathā* (f.), *śrutā*. The agent's gender does not matter.

**Do not use the strong root.** *\*gamita*, *\*vacita*, *\*kārita* are wrong for "gone", "spoken", "done" (*kārita* exists, but it is the causative participle "caused to be done", san-07-01). Use the weak root: *gata*, *ukta*, *kṛta*.

**Do not keep a present-stem sign.** *\*gacchita*, *\*yuñjita*, *\*badhnita* are wrong: the participle is built on the root, *gata*, *yukta*, *baddha*.

**Do not assume *-ta* everywhere.** *\*bhitta*, *\*chitta* are wrong: √bhid, √chid take *-na*, *bhinna*, *chinna*. Learn the *-na* roots as you meet them.

**Do not decline *-tavat* as an a-stem.** *\*kṛtavanaḥ* is wrong: *-tavat* is a *vat*-stem, NOM.SG.M *kṛtavān*, NOM.PL *kṛtavantaḥ* (san-03-17).

**Do not translate *gata* always as "went".** As a tense it is often "has gone" or "is gone": *pañcatvaṃ gataḥ* "is dead". Context decides between past and perfect (Whitney §959 says the same of *-tavat*).

## Connections

Latin has the same suffix and the same passive meaning: *factus* "done", *dictus* "said", *captus* "taken"; Spanish *hecho*, *dicho* and French *fait*, *dit* descend from them. Spanish and French build their perfect with "have" (*he dicho*, *j'ai dit*) where Sanskrit uses the instrumental of the doer (*mayā uktam*, "said by me"); some Indo-Aryan languages, Hindi among them, kept the Sanskrit pattern, with an agent marker (*maine kahā* "I said").

## Exercises

1. Give the past participle of √śru, √nī, √tyaj, √yuj, √labh and √pat.
2. Say whether each is active or passive in sense, and why: *gata*, *datta*, *jāta*, *kṛta*.
3. Build "The girls went to the river" with the past participle.
4. Build "The boy ate the fruit" with √khād in the passive construction.
5. Rewrite your answer to 4 with *-tavat*.
6. Build "I heard the story" (*kathā*) with √śru.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*nṛpaḥ śatravo jitāḥ* for "the king conquered the enemies".
8. Find and fix the error: *\*mayā kathā śrutam*.

## Worked answers

1. *śruta*, *nīta*, *tyakta* (*j* → *k* before *t*), *yukta*, *labdha* (*bh* + *t* → *bdh*), *patita* (with *-ita*) (this lesson; Whitney §§954–956).
2. *gata* and *jāta* are active ("gone", "born"): √gam and √jan are intransitive. *datta* and *kṛta* are passive ("given", "done"): √dā and √kṛ are transitive (Whitney §952).
3. *kanyā nadīṃ gatāḥ*. The participle agrees with *kanyāḥ* in the feminine plural; *kanyāḥ* before *n* loses its *-ḥ*.
4. *bālena phalaṃ khāditam*. √khād takes *-ita*; neuter to agree with *phalam*; the agent in the instrumental.
5. *bālaḥ phalaṃ khāditavān*. *-tavat* in the nominative masculine; object in the accusative.
6. *mayā kathā śrutā*. Feminine to agree with *kathā*.
7. The agent of a passive participle is instrumental: *nṛpeṇa śatravo jitāḥ*; or with the active participle, *nṛpaḥ śatrūñ jitavān*.
8. The participle agrees with *kathā*: *mayā kathā śrutā*.
