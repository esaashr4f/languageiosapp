## The idea

A **passive** sentence makes the thing acted on its subject: "the verse is recited (by the pupil)" instead of "the pupil recites the verse". You met the idea in the past participle (san-04-13): *śiṣyeṇa śloka uktaḥ* "the verse (was) spoken by the pupil". This lesson gives the passive its own present system, for "is done", "was being done", "let it be done", "it should be done".

Sanskrit uses the passive far more than English does. Speijer (§7) says it has "a decided predilection for the passive voice", so that "in translating from that language it is often necessary to transform passive sentences into active". *mayā śrūyate* "it is heard by me" is just "I hear".

The passive has a stem of its own: the root, in its weak form, + ***-ya-***, inflected with **middle endings** (Whitney §768). √kṛ → *kriyate* "is done"; √dā → *dīyate* "is given"; √vac → *ucyate* "is said, is called". This stem is made "without any reference to the class" of the active verb (§768): *karoti* is class 8, *dadāti* class 3, but the passives *kriyate* and *dīyate* are built the same way.

The *-ya-* recalls class 4 (*naśyati*, san-04-01). The difference is in the old accent and in the endings: class 4 has the accent on the root and takes active or middle endings; the passive has the accent on *-yá-* and takes middle endings only. Since the accent is no longer written, the endings and the root's form are what tell them apart: *naśyati* "perishes" (active ending), *dṛśyate* "is seen" (passive).

A passive can be made from intransitive verbs too. Then it has no subject at all, only the doer in the instrumental: *mayā gamyate* "it is gone by me", that is, "I go". Speijer (§8) says "nothing can be more common than the use of impersonal passives". This is the **impersonal passive**: a passive verb in the 3rd singular with no subject.

## How it is built

**Step 1: the weak root.** The same weakening as in the past participle (Whitney §769): no guṇa, a nasal dropped, *va* → *u*, *ya* → *i*.

- √vac → *uc-ya-te*; √yaj → *ij-ya-te*; √vas → *uṣ-ya-te*; √svap → *sup-ya-te*;
- √prach → *pṛcch-ya-te*; √grah → *gṛh-ya-te*; √bandh → *badh-ya-te*.
- Roots that kept a full *a* in the participle keep it here: √gam → *gamyate*, √han → *hanyate*, √tyaj → *tyajyate*, √labh → *labhyate*.

**Step 2: final vowels** (Whitney §770):

- *i*, *u* are lengthened: √nī → *nīyate*, √ji → *jīyate*, √śru → *śrūyate*, √stu → *stūyate*;
- *ā* usually becomes *ī*: √dā → *dīyate*, √dhā → *dhīyate*, √sthā → *sthīyate*, √pā → *pīyate*, √gai "sing" → *gīyate*; but √jñā → *jñāyate*;
- *ṛ* becomes *ri*: √kṛ → *kriyate*, √hṛ → *hriyate*, √bhṛ → *bhriyate*; after two consonants it takes guṇa instead: √smṛ → *smaryate*;
- *ṝ* becomes *īr*: √tṝ → *tīryate*.

**Step 3: class 10 and derived verbs.** Drop *-aya-* and add *-ya-* to the strengthened root: *corayati* → *coryate*, *pūjayati* → *pūjyate*, *cintayati* → *cintyate* (Wiktionary gives *coryate*).

**Step 4: the endings.** Middle endings of a thematic stem (san-04-02): *kriyate*, *kriyete*, *kriyante*; *kriyase* ...; *kriye*, *kriyāvahe*, *kriyāmahe*. The imperfect, imperative and optative follow: *akriyata* "was done", *kriyatām* "let it be done", *kriyeta* "it should be done".

**Step 5: the sentence.** The thing acted on is in the nominative, the doer (if named) in the instrumental, and the verb agrees with the nominative: *śiṣyeṇa ślokaḥ paṭhyate* "the verse is recited by the pupil"; *śiṣyaiḥ ślokāḥ paṭhyante*.

**The impersonal passive.** With an intransitive verb, use the 3SG passive, no subject, and the doer in the instrumental: *mayā gamyate* "I go"; *tena supyate* "he sleeps". A predicate goes into the instrumental too: *tena sukhinā bhūyate* "he is happy" (Speijer §32).

**The passive imperative** is a favourite polite command: *ārabhyatām* "let it be begun", *śrūyatām* "let it be heard", that is, "listen". It avoids addressing the hearer directly.

**How it is glossed.** `kri-ya-te` `do-PASS-3SG`. The passive always has middle endings, so MID is not added (dossier).

**Passive meanings of other middle forms.** Outside the present system Sanskrit has no special passive stem; there the middle forms can serve as passives (Whitney §531). The past participle is the usual passive past (san-04-13).

**The chain of thought.** "Evenness is called yoga."

1. "Is called" is the passive of √vac: weak *uc-* + *-ya-* + *-te*: *ucyate*.
2. "Evenness" is the subject, in the nominative: *samatvam*; "yoga" the predicate, also nominative: *yogaḥ*.
3. *samatvaṃ yogaḥ ucyate*; *yogaḥ* before *u* loses *-ḥ*: *samatvaṃ yoga ucyate*. This is Bhg 2.48d.

## Where it applies

- **Transitive verbs, personal passive**: the object becomes the subject: *ślokaḥ paṭhyate*, *gāvo dīyante*, *vyāghro dṛśyate*.
- **The doer in the instrumental**, when named: *śiṣyeṇa*, *nṛpeṇa*, *mayā*. Often it is left out: *iti śrūyate* "so it is heard, so they say".
- **Intransitive verbs, impersonal passive**: *mayā gamyate*, *tena supyate*, *atra sthīyate* "one stays here". Speijer (§8) calls it extremely common.
- **"Be called, be considered"** with a nominative predicate: *samatvaṃ yoga ucyate*; *sa paṇḍito manyate* "he is thought wise".
- **Existence**: *vidyate* "is found, exists", a very common word in verse and philosophy.
- **The passive imperative** for polite orders and stage directions: *śrūyatām*, *ārabhyatām*, *gamyatām* "let it be gone", "please go".
- **The passive optative** for rules: *kriyeta* "it should be done", *dīyeta* "it should be given".
- **The passive present participle** *-yamāna*: *hanyamāne śarīre* "when the body is being killed" (Bhg 2.20; san-04-20).

## Watch out

**Do not use active endings on the passive stem.** *\*kriyati*, *\*dīyati* are wrong in Classical prose; the passive takes middle endings: *kriyate*, *dīyate* (Monier-Williams notes epic *-ti* forms; san-07-08).

**Do not use the present-class stem.** *\*karoyate*, *\*dadīyate*, *\*gacchyate* are wrong. The passive goes on the root: *kriyate*, *dīyate*, *gamyate* (Whitney §768).

**Do not strengthen the root.** *\*vacyate*, *\*yajyate* are wrong in Classical Sanskrit: *ucyate*, *ijyate* (Monier-Williams).

**Do not put the doer in the nominative.** *\*śiṣyaḥ ślokaḥ paṭhyate* is wrong for "the pupil recites the verse": the doer of a passive is instrumental, *śiṣyeṇa*.

**Do not make an impersonal passive plural.** *\*asmābhir gamyante* is wrong for "we go": the impersonal passive has no subject and stays 3SG, *asmābhir gamyate*.

**Do not confuse the passive with class 4.** *naśyati* "perishes" is class 4, active; *dṛśyate* "is seen" is passive. And *manyate* "thinks" is class 4 middle, not the passive of √man (which is also *manyate* in form: context decides; Wiktionary lists *manyate* as class 4).

**Do not confuse *mriyate* "dies" with a passive.** It looks like one, and Whitney (§773) thinks it was one in origin, but it means "dies", not "is killed"; "is killed" is *hanyate*.

## Connections

Latin also has a separate passive with its own endings (*dīcitur* "is said", *amātur* "is loved"), and uses impersonal passives of intransitive verbs as Sanskrit does: *ītur* "it is gone, people go". Spanish and French build the passive with "be" + participle (*es dicho*, *est dit*) or with a reflexive (*se dice*, *on dit*), which is closer to Sanskrit's impersonal use. Korean and Japanese passives (*-rareru*) are much rarer than Sanskrit's.

## Exercises

1. Form the present passive 3SG of √śru, √nī, √hṛ, √pā, √prach and √pūj.
2. Turn into the passive: *śiṣyaḥ pustakaṃ paṭhati*.
3. Turn into the passive: *nṛpaḥ śatrūñ jayati*.
4. Turn into the passive: *vayaṃ grāmaṃ gacchāmaḥ*; then make an impersonal passive of *vayaṃ gacchāmaḥ*.
5. Build "Let the gift be given" with the passive imperative.
6. Translate *iti śrūyate*, and say who does the hearing.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*kavinā ślokāḥ racyate*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*mayā phalaṃ khādyati*.

## Worked answers

1. *śrūyate*, *nīyate* (*i*, *u* lengthened), *hriyate* (*ṛ* → *ri*), *pīyate* (*ā* → *ī*), *pṛcchyate* (weak root), *pūjyate* (*-aya-* dropped) (Whitney §§769–770).
2. *śiṣyeṇa pustakaṃ paṭhyate*. The object *pustakam* becomes the subject (neuter, same form); the doer goes into the instrumental (this lesson).
3. *nṛpeṇa śatravo jīyante*. *śatravaḥ* nominative plural, verb 3PL; √ji lengthens *i* (Whitney §770a).
4. *asmābhir grāmo gamyate*: the goal of the motion is treated as an object and becomes the subject. Without a goal: *asmābhir gamyate* "we go", an impersonal passive in the 3SG (Speijer §8). *asmābhiḥ* before *g* is *asmābhir*.
5. *dānaṃ dīyatām*. 3SG passive imperative, *-tām*, of *dīya-*.
6. "So it is heard", "so people say". No one is named: the doer is left out, as often (this lesson).
7. The verb agrees with *ślokāḥ*: *kavinā ślokā racyante* (*-āḥ* before *r* drops *-ḥ*).
8. The passive has middle endings: *mayā phalaṃ khādyate*.
