## The idea

The **infinitive** is the verb form that names an action without a person or tense, like English "to go", "to see". Classical Sanskrit has one infinitive, made with *-tum*: √gam → *gantum* "to go", √kṛ → *kartum* "to do", √dṛś → *draṣṭum* "to see".

Whitney (§968) explains what it is: "the accusative case of a verbal noun formed by the suffix *tu*". A verbal noun in *-tu* meant "the going, the doing"; its accusative *-tum* became fixed as the infinitive. The Veda had about a dozen infinitives built from different cases of different nouns (§§969–986); the later language kept only this one.

Its origin explains its main use. As an accusative it naturally stands as the object of verbs: *gantum icchati* "he wants (the) going", "he wants to go". Whitney (§987) lists the typical partners: √śak "can" (*soḍhum aśakat* "he could bear"), √arh "ought, deserve" (*draṣṭum arhasi* "you ought to see"), √iṣ "want" (*prāptum icchanti* "they want to obtain"), and verbs of beginning. It also took over the job of a dative of purpose: *anveṣṭum āgataḥ* "he has come to look for you".

Like the absolutive, the infinitive does not change for person or number: *gantum icchāmi*, *gantum icchanti*. Its doer is the doer of the main verb.

## How it is built

**Step 1: the root with guṇa.** Unlike the past participle and the absolutive, the infinitive takes the **guṇa** grade of the root (Whitney §968): √kṛ → *kar-*, √śru → *śro-*, √nī → *ne-*, √ji → *je-*, √budh → *bodh-*, √yudh → *yodh-*, √bhū → *bhav-*.

**Step 2: *-tum* or *-itum*.** Most roots add *-tum* directly; those that take *-ita* in the participle usually take *-itum* (Whitney §968a): *patita* → *patitum*, *khādita* → *khāditum*. √bhū takes *bhavitum*. Class 10 and *-aya-* verbs keep *-aya-* and add *-itum*: *kathayitum*, *cintayitum*.

**Step 3: the joins.** The root's last consonant meets *t* as in the participle (san-04-13): √dṛś → *draṣṭum* (guṇa with *ra* for *ar*, an irregularity of √dṛś), √yudh → *yoddhum*, √labh → *labdhum*, √tyaj → *tyaktum*, √vac → *vaktum*, √viś → *veṣṭum* (*praveṣṭum* "to enter"), √vas → *vastum*.

**Common infinitives** (forms checked in Monier-Williams, Wiktionary or the GRETIL texts): *gantum*, *āgantum*, *kartum*, *draṣṭum*, *vaktum*, *śrotum*, *dātum*, *sthātum*, *bhavitum*, *labdhum*, *jetum*, *netum*, *pātum*, *hantum*, *tyaktum*, *jñātum*, *grahītum*, *yoddhum*, *prāptum*, *praveṣṭum*, *jīvitum*, *rakṣitum*, *khāditum*.

**With a prefix** nothing changes: *ā-gantum*, *pra-veṣṭum*, *pra-āptum* → *prāptum*. (Contrast the absolutive, which switches to *-ya*.)

**How it is glossed.** `gan-tum` `go-INF`, `śro-tum` `hear-INF` (dossier).

**The infinitive with a passive sense.** With the passive of √śak, and with words like *śakya* "possible", the infinitive is read passively (Whitney §988): *tyaktuṃ na śakyate* "it cannot be abandoned", *na sā śakyā netum* "she cannot be led" (Monier-Williams under *śakya*). The subject is the thing acted on; the infinitive stays unchanged.

**The chain of thought.** "The pupil wants to hear the story."

1. Main verb: √iṣ "want", *icchati*.
2. What is wanted: the hearing of the story. √śru with guṇa: *śro-* + *-tum* → *śrotum*.
3. *kathām* is the object of *śrotum*: *kathāṃ śrotum icchati*.
4. Subject *śiṣyaḥ*: *śiṣyaḥ kathāṃ śrotum icchati*. *śrotum* + *i-* keeps *m*.

## Where it applies

- **After verbs of wanting**: *icchati* (*gantum icchati*), and the noun *icchā* "wish": *śrotum icchā* "the wish to hear".
- **After √śak "can"**: *tartuṃ na śaknoti*; and its passive with a passive sense: *kartuṃ śakyate* "it can be done".
- **After √arh "ought, deserve"**: *vaktum arhasi* "you should say", a polite request in the 2nd person.
- **Purpose with verbs of motion**: *yoddhum āgacchan*, *pātum agacchat*, *cetuṃ vanaṃ gatā* (Whitney §987).
- **After verbs of beginning and daring**: *kartum ārabhate* "he begins to do", *ārabdhaḥ kartum* (Whitney §988).
- **With nouns and adjectives of time, ability, fitness**: *gantuṃ kālaḥ*, *dātuṃ samarthaḥ*, *vaktum ucitam* "it is fitting to say", *śakya* "possible": *na sā śakyā netum* "she cannot be led" (Monier-Williams).
- **In compounds with *kāma* "desire"**: the infinitive loses its *-m*: *gantukāma* "wishing to go" (san-05-11).

## Watch out

**Do not use the weak root.** *\*kṛtum*, *\*śrutum*, *\*jitum* are wrong: the infinitive takes guṇa, *kartum*, *śrotum*, *jetum* (Whitney §968). Compare *kṛta*, *śruta*, *jita* (participles), which are weak.

**Do not change the suffix after a prefix.** *\*āgamyum* is not a form: *āgantum*. The *-ya* switch belongs to the absolutive only.

**Do not inflect the infinitive.** *gantum icchanti* is right, *\*gantavaḥ icchanti* is not: *-tum* never changes, whatever the subject.

**Do not use the infinitive as a subject in ordinary prose.** "To err is human" with *\*bhramituṃ mānuṣam* is not idiomatic Sanskrit. Use a verbal noun: *bhramaḥ*. Whitney (§987) notes rare nominative uses only.

**Do not read *kartuṃ śakyate* actively.** It means "it can be done", with the thing done as the subject: *tat kartuṃ śakyate*. "He can do it" is *sa tat kartuṃ śaknoti*.

**Do not confuse *-tum* with the agent noun.** *kartum* "to do" vs *kartā* "doer" (san-03-14) and the periphrastic future *kartā* "he will do" (san-04-17): same guṇa root, different suffix.

**Do not keep *-tum* in a compound.** *\*gantumkāma* is wrong: *gantukāma*.

## Connections

Latin had a supine in *-tum* from the same kind of verbal noun, used after verbs of motion for purpose: *spectātum veniunt* "they come to watch", the exact use of *pātum agacchat*. The Spanish and French infinitives (*ir*, *aller*) come from the other Latin infinitive in *-re*. Japanese expresses purpose with *-ni iku* (*mi ni iku* "go to see"), a close functional match.

## Exercises

1. Form the infinitive of √nī, √yudh, √labh, √sthā, √pat and √cint.
2. Contrast *jita*, *jitvā* and *jetum*: say which grade of √ji each uses.
3. Build "The king wants to conquer his enemies" with √ji.
4. Build "We came to see the teacher" with √dṛś and √ā-gam (past participle).
5. Build "You ought to forgive him" (polite *bhavān*) with √kṣam and √arh.
6. Build "This cannot be heard" with the passive of √śak.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*bālaḥ śrutum icchati*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*te gantuṃ icchāmaḥ* for "they want to go".

## Worked answers

1. *netum*, *yoddhum*, *labdhum*, *sthātum*, *patitum*, *cintayitum* (guṇa where possible; *-itum* after roots with *-ita*; class 10 keeps *-ay-*) (Whitney §968).
2. *jita* and *jitvā* use the weak root *ji-*; *jetum* uses guṇa *je-* (san-04-13, san-04-15; this lesson).
3. *nṛpaḥ śatrūñ jetum icchati*. *-n* before *j* → *-ñ* (san-04-07).
4. *vayam ācāryaṃ draṣṭum āgatāḥ* (with *smaḥ* if the person needs marking). Purpose infinitive with a verb of motion.
5. *bhavāṃs taṃ kṣantum arhati*. Polite *bhavān* takes the 3SG; *-n* before *t* → *-ṃs* (san-04-07). The infinitive of √kṣam is *kṣantum* (*m* → *n* before *t*, Monier-Williams).
6. *idaṃ śrotuṃ na śakyate*. Passive of √śak, 3SG; *idam* is the subject (Whitney §988).
7. Guṇa is needed: *bālaḥ śrotum icchati*.
8. The verb must agree with *te*; the infinitive does not change, and keeps *m* before a vowel: *te gantum icchanti*.
