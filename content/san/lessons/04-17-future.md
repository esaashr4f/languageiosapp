## The idea

Sanskrit has two futures. Whitney (§931) describes them as "of very different age and character".

The **s-future** is the ordinary one. It adds *-sya-* or *-iṣya-* to the root in its guṇa grade and inflects the result exactly like a thematic present: √bhū → *bhaviṣyati* "will be"; √kṛ → *kariṣyati* "will do"; √dā → *dāsyati* "will give". Whitney calls it "an inheritance from the time of Indo-European unity". Once you can find the future stem, you already know all its endings.

The **periphrastic future** is younger: "a recent addition to the verb-system" (§931). It is the agent noun of san-03-14 used as a verb: *kartṛ* "doer", NOM.SG *kartā*, becomes *kartā* "he will do"; *dātṛ* "giver" → *dātā* "he will give". "Periphrastic" means "said round about": the form is built from a noun, with the verb "be" added in the 1st and 2nd persons (*kartāsmi* "I shall do"). The grammarians say it refers to "something to be done at a definite time to come", and Whitney (§949) notes it is "especially often used along with *śvas* tomorrow". In the Classical language the s-future is far more common.

Both futures are built on the root, outside the present system. Their stems do not depend on the present class: *kariṣyati* from √kṛ (class 8), *dāsyati* from √dā (class 3), *drakṣyati* from √dṛś, whose present comes from another root (√paś).

## How it is built

**Step 1: guṇa.** The root takes guṇa where it can (Whitney §932): √bhū → *bho-* (*bhav-* before a vowel); √nī → *ne-*; √śru → *śro-*; √ji → *je-*; √kṛ → *kar-*; √dṛś → *drak-* (with *ra* for *ar*, as in *draṣṭum*). A heavy syllable stays: √jīv → *jīviṣyati* (§932a).

**Step 2: *-sya-* or *-iṣya-*.** Some roots add *-sya-* directly, others with a joining *i*, *-iṣya-*. Whitney (§935) gives the usage root by root; there is no simple rule, but the future usually agrees with the infinitive (*-tum* / *-itum*), and roots in *ṛ* take *i* (*kariṣyati*, *mariṣyati*) although their infinitive does not.

- *-sya-*: *dāsyati* (√dā), *sthāsyati* (√sthā), *pāsyati* (√pā), *jñāsyati* (√jñā), *yāsyati* (√yā), *eṣyati* (√i), *neṣyati* (√nī), *jeṣyati* (√ji), *śroṣyati* (√śru).
- *-iṣya-*: *bhaviṣyati*, *kariṣyati*, *gamiṣyati* (√gam), *haniṣyati* (√han), *mariṣyati* (√mṛ), *jīviṣyati*, and class 10 verbs with *-ay-*: *pūjayiṣyati*, *kathayiṣyati*.

**Step 3: the joins.** A root consonant before *s* follows the internal rules of san-05-07; *s* becomes *ṣ* after *k*, *r*, and vowels other than *a*, *ā* (the s-to-ṣ rule of san-03-05): √vac → *vakṣyati*; √dṛś → *drakṣyati*; √tyaj → *tyakṣyati*; √labh → *lapsyate*; √yudh → *yotsyate*; √vas → *vatsyati* (*s* + *s* → *ts*).

**Step 4: the endings.** Present endings, active or middle, on the thematic stem: *bhaviṣyati*, *bhaviṣyataḥ*, *bhaviṣyanti*; *bhaviṣyasi* …; *bhaviṣyāmi*, *bhaviṣyāvaḥ*, *bhaviṣyāmaḥ* (Wiktionary भविष्यति). Middle-only verbs have a middle future: *lapsyate* "will get", *yotsyate* "will fight", *bhokṣyate* "will eat, enjoy".

**The periphrastic future.** Take the agent noun in *-tṛ* (guṇa root + *-tṛ*, Whitney §943): *kartṛ*, *dātṛ*, *gantṛ*, *bhavitṛ*.

- 3rd person: the nominative of the agent noun, masculine, with no verb: 3SG *kartā*, 3DU *kartārau*, 3PL *kartāraḥ*. It does not change for gender: *sā kartā* "she will do".
- 1st and 2nd persons: the nominative singular *kartā* + the present of √as: *kartāsmi* "I shall do", *kartāsi* "you will do"; plural *kartāsmaḥ*, *kartāstha*.

**How it is glossed.** `bhavi-ṣya-ti` `be-FUT-3SG`; `dā-sya-ti` `give-FUT-3SG`; periphrastic whole, `kartā` `do.FUT.3SG`, with the note "periphrastic future" (dossier); with the copula, `kartā-smi` `do.FUT-1SG`.

**The chain of thought.** "Tomorrow the king will go to the city."

1. Future: s-future of √gam. Guṇa of *a* is *a*; √gam takes *-iṣya-*: *gamiṣya-*.
2. 3SG active: *gamiṣyati*.
3. *śvaḥ nṛpaḥ nagaram gamiṣyati*. Joins: *śvaḥ* before *n* → *śvo*; *nṛpaḥ* before *n* → *nṛpo* (san-02-06); *nagaram* before *g* → *nagaraṃ*.
4. *śvo nṛpo nagaraṃ gamiṣyati*.

## Where it applies

- **Plain future**: *śvo gamiṣyati*, *kim adya bhaviṣyati*.
- **Intention and promise** in the 1st person: *kariṣyāmi* "I will do (it)", the swan's promise to Nala (*kariṣyāmi hi te priyam* "for I will do you a kindness", MBh 3.50.19d).
- **Prediction and threat**: *drakṣyasi* "you will see", *prāpsyasi svargam*.
- **In conditions**, with the future in the main clause: *yadi … gamiṣyasi …* "if you go …, you will …" (san-06-05).
- **The periphrastic future** for a definite time to come, often with *śvas* (Whitney §949): *śvaḥ kartāsmi*, *śvo dātā*.
- **Middle futures** of middle verbs: *lapsyate*, *yotsyate*, *bhokṣyate*, *maṃsyate* (√man, "will think"; the epic also has active *maṃsyati*, MBh 3.50.20d).
- **The future participle** *-ṣyant* and *-ṣyamāṇa* (*kariṣyat*, *kariṣyamāṇa*, Wiktionary करिष्यति) and the conditional (*akariṣyat*, san-06-06) are made from the same stem.
- **The present for the near future**: *gacchāmi* "I am going (to go)" (Whitney §777a); the future is not obligatory where English uses "will".

## Watch out

**Do not build the future on the present stem.** *\*gacchiṣyati*, *\*karoṣyati*, *\*paśyiṣyati* are wrong: *gamiṣyati*, *kariṣyati*, *drakṣyati*.

**Do not drop guṇa.** *\*kṛṣyati*, *\*bhūṣyati*, *\*śruṣyati* are wrong: *kariṣyati*, *bhaviṣyati*, *śroṣyati*. (*bhūṣayati* is a different verb, "adorns".)

**Do not use active endings on a middle-only verb.** *\*lapsyati* for "will get" is wrong: *lapsyate*. (The epics sometimes mix: san-07-08.)

**Do not forget the s-to-ṣ rule.** *\*kariṣyati* has *ṣ* after *i*; *\*karisyati* is wrong. After *k*: *vakṣyati*, *drakṣyati*. After *a*, *ā*: *dāsyati*, *yāsyati*, with *s*.

**Do not decline the periphrastic future for gender.** *\*sā kartrī* for "she will do" is wrong: the periphrastic future is always the masculine form, *sā kartā*. (*kartrī* is "a woman who makes", a noun.)

**Do not add *asti* in the 3rd person.** *\*kartāsti* is wrong: 3rd persons have no copula, *kartā*, *kartāraḥ*. Only 1st and 2nd persons add it: *kartāsmi*, *kartāsi*.

**Do not confuse *-iṣya-* with the infinitive's *i*.** The two usually agree, but not always: √kṛ has *kartum* but *kariṣyati*; √gam *gantum* but *gamiṣyati* (Whitney §943a notes the roots in *ṛ*, √han and √gam).

## Connections

Greek and Lithuanian have the same *-s-* future (Greek *dṓsō* "I shall give", Lithuanian *dúosiu*); Latin lost it and built new futures, and Spanish and French did so again from "infinitive + have" (*daré*, *je donnerai*), a periphrasis like Sanskrit's agent-noun future. Japanese and Korean have no grammatical future of this kind; they use the plain form with time words, as Sanskrit can use the present.

## Exercises

1. Form the future 3SG of √nī, √śru, √sthā, √han, √cint and √yudh (middle).
2. Give the future 1SG and 3PL of √kṛ and √dṛś.
3. Build "We will see the king tomorrow".
4. Build "The warriors will fight in the battle" with √yudh (middle).
5. Turn *nṛpo dhanaṃ dadāti* into the s-future and the periphrastic future.
6. Build "I will go home in the evening" with the periphrastic future of √gam.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*śiṣyāḥ kathāṃ śruṣyanti*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*sā kartrī* for "she will do".

## Worked answers

1. *neṣyati*, *śroṣyati*, *sthāsyati*, *haniṣyati*, *cintayiṣyati*, *yotsyate* (guṇa root; *-sya-* or *-iṣya-*; class 10 *-ay-iṣya-*; √yudh middle) (Whitney §§932–935).
2. √kṛ: *kariṣyāmi*, *kariṣyanti*. √dṛś: *drakṣyāmi*, *drakṣyanti*.
3. *vayaṃ śvo nṛpaṃ drakṣyāmaḥ*. *śvaḥ* before *n* → *śvo*.
4. *yodhā yuddhe yotsyante*. √yudh is middle, so the future is middle: *yot-sya-nte*.
5. *nṛpo dhanaṃ dāsyati*; *nṛpo dhanaṃ dātā* (this lesson).
6. *ahaṃ sāyaṃ gṛhaṃ gantāsmi*. Agent noun *gantṛ*, NOM *gantā*, + *asmi*; *sāyam* before *g* → *sāyaṃ*.
7. √śru takes guṇa: *śiṣyāḥ kathāṃ śroṣyanti*.
8. The periphrastic future is masculine whatever the subject: *sā kartā*.
