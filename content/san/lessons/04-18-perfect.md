## The idea

The **perfect** is the past tense built by reduplicating the root: √gam → *jagāma* "he went", √kṛ → *cakāra* "he did", √vac → *uvāca* "he said". You know reduplication from class 3 (san-04-09); the perfect uses the same copying rules on the root itself, with its own endings.

Its name comes from Greek and Latin grammar, where the same formation means "has done". In Sanskrit that meaning is clear only in the oldest texts. Whitney (§821b) says that in the later language the perfect is merely "a preterit", a past tense, and the dossier (§6.3) states the guide's rule: in Classical texts imperfect, perfect and aorist all narrate the past. Pāṇini assigns the perfect to the past "not witnessed by the speaker", but that is the grammarians' rule, not usage.

What makes the perfect important is its place in the **epics**. The Mahābhārata and the Rāmāyaṇa tell their stories mostly in the perfect: *uvāca* "he said" opens a speech on almost every page (the GRETIL text of Mahābhārata book 3 alone has it more than 1,300 times), and the Gītā introduces each speaker with *uvāca*: *arjuna uvāca* "Arjuna said". So the perfect is the past tense you will read most in part 8.

Two perfects are used as presents. √vid "know" has the perfect *veda* "he knows" (literally "he has seen, he has found out"), used beside the present *vetti* (san-04-08). And √ah, which has no other forms, gives *āha* "he says, he said" and *āhuḥ* "they say" (Whitney §801a).

## How it is built

**Step 1: reduplication** (Whitney §§782–790). As in class 3: copy the first consonant without aspiration, a velar or *h* as a palatal (san-04-09), but the vowel of the copy is ***a*** for roots with *a*, *ā* or *ṛ* (§782): √kṛ → *ca-kṛ-*, √bhṛ → *ba-bhṛ-*, √dā → *da-dā-*, √gam → *ja-gam-*, √han → *ja-ghan-*, √pat → *pa-pat-*. Roots with *i* or *u* copy *i* or *u*: √nī → *ni-nī-*, √śru → *śu-śru-*, √budh → *bu-budh-*. √bhū is irregular: *ba-bhū-*.

Roots beginning with *va* or *ya* reduplicate with *u* or *i* (§784): √vac → *u-vac-*, √vas → *u-vas-*, √yaj → *i-yaj-*. A root beginning with *a* before one consonant lengthens: √as → *ās-*, √āp → *āp-* (§783a).

**Step 2: strong and weak.** The three persons of the active singular are strong (§§792–793):

- 3SG: a final vowel takes **vṛddhi**, a medial *a* before one consonant is **lengthened**: *cakāra*, *nināya*, *jagāma*, *papāta*, *uvāca*; a medial *i, u, ṛ* takes **guṇa**: *bubodha*, *dadarśa*.
- 2SG: guṇa: *cakartha*, *jagantha*.
- 1SG: guṇa or vṛddhi: *cakara* or *cakāra*.

All other forms are weak, with the simple root, often shortened (§794): *cakr-uḥ* "they did", *jagm-uḥ* "they went" (the *a* lost), *ūc-uḥ* "they said" (*u-uc-* fused), *jaghn-uḥ* "they killed".

**The *e*-perfect.** A root with *a* between two single consonants, whose reduplication would repeat the same consonant, often has *e* and no reduplication in weak forms (§794e): √pat → *petuḥ* "they fell"; √labh → middle *lebhe* "he got"; √man → *mene* "he thought". *lebhe* and *mene* are very common.

**Step 3: the endings** (Whitney §§798–800; Wiktionary tables for चकार, जगाम):

- active: 3SG *-a*, 3DU *-atuḥ*, 3PL *-uḥ*; 2SG *-tha* or *-itha*, 2DU *-athuḥ*, 2PL *-a*; 1SG *-a*, 1DU *-iva*, 1PL *-ima*.
- middle: 3SG *-e*, 3DU *-āte*, 3PL *-ire*; 2SG *-se* or *-iṣe*, 2DU *-āthe*, 2PL *-dhve*; 1SG *-e*, 1DU *-ivahe*, 1PL *-imahe*.

The 3SG and 1SG active often look alike (*cakāra*); context decides. Roots in *ā* end in *-au* in the 1SG and 3SG: √dā → *dadau* "he gave", √sthā → *tasthau* "he stood", √jñā → *jajñau*.

**The periphrastic perfect.** Derived verbs (class 10, causatives) and roots beginning with a heavy long vowel cannot reduplicate. They form the perfect with an accusative noun in *-ām* + the perfect of √as, √kṛ or √bhū (Whitney §§1070–1073): *cintayām āsa* "he thought", *kathayām āsa* "he told", *toṣayām āsa* "he pleased" (MBh 3.50.7), *īkṣāṃ cakre* "he looked". GRETIL sometimes joins the two words; space them (dossier).

**The perfect participle.** Active in *-vas*: *vidvas* "knowing" (√vid), declined with three stems (san-03-15): strong *vidvāṃs-* (NOM *vidvān*, ACC *vidvāṃsam*), weakest *viduṣ-* (INS *viduṣā*, GEN.PL *viduṣām*), middle *vidvat-* before consonant endings (*vidvadbhiḥ*); feminine *viduṣī* (Wiktionary विद्वांस्). *vidvān* is common as an adjective and noun: "learned, a wise man". Whitney (§807) says the other perfect participles "have nearly gone out of use" in the later language.

**How it is glossed.** The reduplicated stem is one piece with `.PRF`: `jagām-a` `go.PRF-3SG`, `cakr-e` `do.PRF-3SG.MID`, `uvāc-a` `say.PRF-3SG`, `āh-a` `say.PRF-3SG`; periphrastic `cintay-ām ās-a` `think-PRF be.PRF-3SG` (dossier).

**The chain of thought.** "The king went to the forest" in epic style.

1. √gam, perfect. Reduplicate: *g* → *j*, vowel *a*: *jagam-*.
2. 3SG active: strong, medial *a* lengthened: *jagām-*, + *-a*: *jagāma*.
3. *rājā vanaṃ jagāma*.

## Where it applies

- **Epic narrative**: the main past tense of the Mahābhārata and Rāmāyaṇa: *jagāma*, *uvāca*, *dadarśa*, *jagrāha*, *babhūva* "became, was".
- **Speaker lines**: *X uvāca* "X said" before speeches (Bhg passim).
- **Classical narrative too**, beside the imperfect and the past participle, with no difference of meaning (Whitney §821b; dossier §6.3).
- **Present meaning** in two verbs: *veda*, *viduḥ* "knows, they know"; *āha*, *āhuḥ* "says, they say", the usual way to cite an opinion: *iti paṇḍitā āhuḥ*.
- **The middle perfect** of middle verbs and with passive sense: *lebhe*, *mene*, *cakre* "he did (for himself)", "it was done".
- **The periphrastic perfect** for class 10 and other derived verbs: *cintayām āsa*, *kathayām āsa*, *pūjayām āsa*; with *cakre* for some roots with a long initial vowel (*īkṣāṃ cakre*).
- **The perfect participle *vidvān*** as adjective and noun: "learned, wise"; *vidvān yuktaḥ samācaran* "the wise man, disciplined, acting" (Bhg 3.26c).

## Watch out

**Do not reduplicate *ṛ* or *a* with *i*.** *\*cikāra*, *\*bibhāra* are wrong for the perfect: the vowel is *a*, *cakāra*, *babhāra* (Whitney §782). The *i* belongs to the class 3 present (*bibharti*).

**Do not keep the strong stem in the plural.** *\*jagāmuḥ*, *\*cakāruḥ* are wrong: *jagmuḥ*, *cakruḥ*.

**Do not reduplicate √vac as *\*vavāca*.** Roots in *va* take *u*: *uvāca*, plural *ūcuḥ*.

**Do not add the augment.** *\*ajagāma* is not a form: the perfect has reduplication, not the augment.

**Do not take *veda* for the noun only.** *veda* "knowledge, the Veda" (noun) and *veda* "he knows" (verb) are the same word in form. In *ko veda* it is the verb.

**Do not reduplicate a class 10 verb.** *\*cicintaya* is impossible: class 10 and other derived verbs use the periphrastic perfect, *cintayām āsa*.

**Do not join the two words of a periphrastic perfect in this guide's spacing.** Editions and GRETIL print *cintayāmāsa*; the guide writes *cintayām āsa* (dossier §4).

**Do not read *āha* as present only.** In narrative *āha* is also "he said" (Whitney §801a); in maxims *āhuḥ* is "they say".

## Connections

Latin and Greek perfects have the same reduplication: Latin *dedī* "I gave" (Sanskrit *dadau*), *tetigī* "I touched", *cecinī* "I sang"; Greek *dédōka*, *léloipa*. In both the perfect also lost its "has done" meaning and became a narrative past, as in Sanskrit: Spanish *dí*, *hice* and French *je fis* continue Latin perfects. The Sanskrit periphrastic perfect (*cintayām āsa*, literally "he was at thinking") resembles the Romance "have" perfects only in being built from two words.

## Exercises

1. Form the perfect 3SG and 3PL of √bhū, √śru, √nī and √han.
2. Form the perfect 3SG of √pat, √sthā and √grah, and explain the vowel of each.
3. Build "The swan said to Damayantī" with *uvāca*.
4. Build "The warriors heard the sound" with √śru (perfect, 3PL) and *śabda*.
5. Build "The king told the story" with the periphrastic perfect of √kath.
6. Translate *vidvān satyaṃ veda*, and give the instrumental singular of *vidvas*.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*te vanaṃ jagāmuḥ*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*nṛpaḥ phalaṃ lalābha* for "the king got a fruit".

## Worked answers

1. *babhūva*, *babhūvuḥ*; *śuśrāva*, *śuśruvuḥ*; *nināya*, *ninyuḥ*; *jaghāna*, *jaghnuḥ* (Wiktionary perfect pages). Strong 3SG with vṛddhi of a final vowel or lengthened *a*; weak plural.
2. *papāta*: medial *a* lengthened in the 3SG. *tasthau*: root in *ā*, ending *-au*; the copy of *sth* is *t* (Whitney §590d, san-04-09). *jagrāha*: *g* → *j*, medial *a* lengthened.
3. *haṃso damayantīm uvāca*. *haṃsaḥ* before *d* → *haṃso*.
4. *yodhāḥ śabdaṃ śuśruvuḥ*. Weak *śuśruv-* + *-uḥ*; *yodhāḥ* before *ś* keeps *-ḥ*.
5. *nṛpaḥ kathāṃ kathayām āsa*. Class 10 cannot reduplicate: periphrastic perfect (Whitney §1071).
6. "The wise man knows the truth." *veda* is the perfect used as a present. INS.SG *viduṣā* (weakest stem).
7. Weak stem in the plural: *te vanaṃ jagmuḥ*.
8. √labh is middle and makes an *e*-perfect: *nṛpaḥ phalaṃ lebhe* (Whitney §794e).
