## The idea

The **aorist** is the third past tense, beside the imperfect (san-04-03) and the perfect (san-04-18). Like the imperfect it has the augment and the secondary endings; unlike it, it is not made from a present stem. It has stems of its own, built on the root in seven ways (Whitney §824). There is no "aorist present": the aorist exists only as an augmented past, plus one special use without the augment.

The name is Greek, *aóristos* "unbounded, undefined". In the oldest Sanskrit the aorist meant "has done", a recent past; Pāṇini still assigns it to the past "of today". But Whitney (§825) says that in the Classical language the aorists are mere "preterits, exchangeable with imperfects and perfects". They are also rare: Whitney (§826) counted aorist forms only twenty-one times in the Nala episode, eight in the Hitopadeśa, six in the Bhagavad Gītā. So in reading, the aim is **recognition**: see the augment, see that the stem is not the present stem, and read "did".

The aorist has one use you must be able to produce. To say "don't!", Classical Sanskrit uses the particle *mā* with an aorist form **without the augment**: *mā bhūḥ* "do not be", *mā gamaḥ* "do not go", *mā śucaḥ* "do not grieve" (Whitney §579). An augmentless past form like this is called the **injunctive**. The term comes from Vedic grammar, where such forms could also state or wish; in the Classical language the injunctive survives only after *mā*. A word that forbids, like *mā*, is a **prohibitive** particle, glossed `PROH`.

Why an augmentless aorist? Whitney (§587) explains that the Veda used forms "identical with those of augment-tenses save for the lack of an augment" for many purposes, and that "especially often and regularly after *mā* prohibitive" this use "comes down also into the later language". Without the augment the form no longer says "past"; with *mā* it says "let it not happen".

## How it is built

All aorists: augment + aorist stem + secondary endings (3PL often *-uḥ* or *-an*).

**1. Root aorist** (Whitney §§829–833): the root itself is the stem. Only a few roots, mostly in *ā*, and √bhū.

- √bhū → *abhūt* "was, became", *abhūvan*; 2SG *abhūḥ*;
- √dā → *adāt*, *aduḥ*; √sthā → *asthāt*, *asthuḥ*; √pā → *apāt*; √gā (the aorist of √i "go") → *agāt*, *aguḥ*.

**2. a-aorist**: root (weak) + *-a-*, inflected exactly like an imperfect of class 6:

- √gam → *agamat*, *agaman*; √vac → *avocat* (irregular stem *voc-*); √śak → *aśakat*; √sic → *asicat*.

**3. Reduplicated aorist**: a reduplicated stem with a long vowel in the copy, mostly with causatives: √jan → *ajījanat* "begot"; recognise it by the long *ī* or *ū* in the second syllable.

**4–7. Sigmatic aorists**, with an *s* after the root:

- **s-aorist**: vṛddhi root + *s* (*ṣ*): √kṛ → *akārṣīt*, √nī → *anaiṣīt*, √ji → *ajaiṣīt*, √śru → *aśrauṣīt*, √dṛś → *adrākṣīt*, √tyaj → *atyākṣīt*. The 2SG and 3SG add *ī* before the ending: *-ṣīḥ*, *-ṣīt*.
- **iṣ-aorist**: root (guṇa or vṛddhi) + *iṣ*: √vad → *avādīt*, √vid → *avedīt*, √grah → *agrahīt*, √vadh "kill" → *avadhīt*. The *iṣ* + *-t* merge to *-īt*.
- **siṣ-aorist**: √yā → *ayāsīt*.
- **sa-aorist**: √diś → *adikṣat*.

**Passive aorist 3SG** in *-i*, on a strong root: √vac → *avāci* "was said", √hṛ → *ahāri* "was taken" (Monier-Williams). It occurs only in the 3SG.

**Prohibition: *mā* + injunctive.** Take the aorist form and drop the augment:

- *abhūḥ* "you were" → *mā bhūḥ* "do not be";
- *agamaḥ* → *mā gamaḥ* "do not go";
- *aśucaḥ* (a-aorist of √śuc) → *mā śucaḥ* "do not grieve";
- *akārṣīḥ* → *mā kārṣīḥ* "do not do".

*mā sma* is a stronger form: *mā sma gamaḥ* (Bhg 2.3). The injunctive is glossed with `AOR` and no `PST`: `mā bhū-ḥ` `PROH be.AOR-2SG` (dossier).

Whitney (§579c, e) notes two later developments: in the epics and later, *mā* also takes the imperative (*mā prayaccha*, san-04-05), and an aorist "not deprived of augment" is "occasionally met with after *mā*". The most famous verse in the Rāmāyaṇa is an example (below).

**How it is glossed.** On the piece that carries the aorist (dossier): `a-bhū-t` `PST-be.AOR-3SG`; `a-gam-a-t` `PST-go-AOR-3SG`; `a-kār-ṣ-īt` `PST-do-AOR-3SG`; `a-vadh-īḥ` `PST-kill-AOR.2SG`.

**The chain of thought.** "Do not grieve!"

1. Prohibition: *mā* + augmentless aorist, 2SG.
2. √śuc forms an a-aorist: *aśucat*, 2SG *aśucaḥ*.
3. Drop the augment: *śucaḥ*. *mā śucaḥ*.

## Where it applies

- **Narrative past**, occasionally, in Classical prose and verse: *abhūt*, *agamat*, *avocat*, *akārṣīt*, *aśrauṣīt*. Read it as a simple past (Whitney §825).
- **Epic and Vedic texts** more often than Classical prose; the Rāmāyaṇa's first book has many (§826).
- **Prohibition with *mā***: the standard Classical "don't!": *mā bhūḥ*, *mā gamaḥ*, *mā śucaḥ*, *mā kārṣīḥ*, *mā bhaiṣīḥ*.
- **The emphatic *mā sma***: *mā sma gamaḥ*.
- **The 3rd person after *mā***: *mā bhūt* "let it not be": *mā bhūt kālasya paryayaḥ* "let there be no change of time" (Whitney §579a).
- **The passive aorist 3SG** in *-i*: *avāci*, *ahāri*, *avādi* "was said".
- **The precative**, a wish form built on the aorist system (*bhūyāt* "may he be"), is rare (san-07-06).
- **Other ways to forbid**: *na* + optative in rules (san-04-06); *mā* + imperative in later texts (san-04-05); *alam* + instrumental "enough of": *alaṃ śokena* "enough grieving" (san-03-02).

## Watch out

**Do not keep the augment after *mā* in Classical prose.** *\*mā agamaḥ* breaks the norm: drop it, *mā gamaḥ*. The Rāmāyaṇa verse keeps it, but Whitney (§579e) calls such forms anomalous.

**Do not use *na* with the injunctive.** *\*na gamaḥ* for "don't go" is wrong: the injunctive goes with *mā*. *na* forbids with the optative: *na gacchet* "one should not go".

**Do not use the present stem after *mā*.** *\*mā gaccha* is an imperative, found only in later texts; *\*mā gacchaḥ* is not a Classical form at all. The aorist stem is *gam-a-*: *mā gamaḥ*.

**Do not read an aorist as a present.** *agamat* "went" is past; it is not *gacchati*. Without the augment and without *mā* (*gamat*) the form belongs to Vedic and is not used in Classical prose.

**Do not confuse *abhūt* with *abhavat*.** Both mean "was, became". *abhavat* is the imperfect (present stem *bhava-*); *abhūt* the root aorist. In Classical texts they are interchangeable (§825).

**Do not take every *-īt* for an aorist.** *āsīt* (√as) and *abravīt* (√brū) are imperfects with an inserted *ī* (san-04-08). An aorist has a stem that is not the present stem: *akārṣīt* (not *akarot*), *avādīt* (not *avadat*).

**Do not build *\*bhūyāḥ* for "don't be".** *mā bhūḥ* uses the root aorist. *bhūyāḥ* is a precative "may you be".

## Connections

Greek has the same aorist types with the same names: root aorist *éstē* "stood" (Sanskrit *asthāt*), thematic *élipe* "left", sigmatic *édeixa* "showed". Greek also forbids with *mḗ* + aorist subjunctive, a close parallel to *mā* + injunctive. Latin merged its aorist into the perfect (*dīxī* is a sigmatic aorist in origin), and Spanish *dije*, French *je dis* continue it.

## Exercises

1. Identify the type of aorist and the root: *abhūt*, *agamat*, *akārṣīt*, *avādīt*, *ayāsīt*.
2. Give the imperfect that corresponds in meaning to each form in exercise 1.
3. Forbid: "do not go", "do not grieve", "do not be afraid", "do not do (it)".
4. Build "Let there be no fear" (*bhayam*) with *mā* and the 3SG of the root aorist of √bhū.
5. Build "Do not give in to grief" on the model of Bhg 2.3, with *śokam* and *mā sma*.
6. Split and gloss *mā kārṣīḥ*.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*na gamaḥ* for "don't go".
8. Find and fix the error: *\*mā abhūḥ* in Classical prose.

## Worked answers

1. *abhūt*: root aorist, √bhū. *agamat*: a-aorist, √gam. *akārṣīt*: s-aorist, √kṛ. *avādīt*: iṣ-aorist, √vad. *ayāsīt*: siṣ-aorist, √yā (Whitney §824; this lesson).
2. *abhavat*, *agacchat*, *akarot*, *avadat*, *ayāt* (san-04-03, san-04-08, san-04-11).
3. *mā gamaḥ*, *mā śucaḥ*, *mā bhaiṣīḥ*, *mā kārṣīḥ* (Whitney §579; this lesson).
4. *mā bhūd bhayam*. 3SG injunctive *bhūt*; *-t* before *bh* → *-d* (san-04-07).
5. *śokaṃ mā sma gamaḥ*.
6. *mā kār-ṣ-īḥ* `PROH do-AOR-2SG`: *mā*, then the s-aorist of √kṛ (vṛddhi *kār*, *ṣ*, *-īḥ*) with no augment.
7. The injunctive goes with *mā*: *mā gamaḥ*. (With *na*, use the optative: *na gaccheḥ*.)
8. After *mā* the augment is dropped: *mā bhūḥ* (Whitney §579).
