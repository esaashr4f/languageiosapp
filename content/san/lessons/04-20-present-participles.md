## The idea

The **present participle** is an adjective made from the present stem that means "doing", "while doing": *gacchant* "going", *labhamāna* "getting", *kurvāṇa* "doing (for oneself)". It describes someone in the middle of an action at the time of the main verb: *nṛpo vanaṃ gacchan siṃham apaśyat* "the king, (while) going to the forest, saw a lion".

It contrasts with the two forms you have: the past participle *gata* "gone" (san-04-13), and the absolutive *gatvā* "having gone" (san-04-15). The absolutive puts one action before another and never changes; the present participle puts two actions side by side, and is declined, agreeing with its noun in gender, number and case. So it can describe any noun in the sentence, not only the subject: *hasantīṃ kanyām apaśyat* "he saw the girl laughing".

There are two participles, one for each voice (Whitney §§584, 442):

- **active** in *-ant* (weak *-at*), declined like *bhagavat* (san-03-17) but with *-an* in the nominative singular: *gacchan*, *gacchantam*, *gacchatā*.
- **middle** in *-māna* after a thematic stem and *-āna* after an athematic one, declined like any a-stem adjective: *labhamānaḥ*, *labhamānā*, *labhamānam*; *bruvāṇaḥ*.

The passive also has a participle, *-yamāna*: *kriyamāṇa* "being done", *hanyamāna* "being killed".

You have met *sat* "being, real, good" and *asat* "unreal" (Bhg 2.16, san-04-14): the present participle of √as. And *bhavat* "Your Honour" (san-03-17) is the present participle of √bhū, "the one who is (here)", used as a polite pronoun.

## How it is built

**Active: from the 3PL present.** Take the 3PL active and drop the *-i*: *gacchanti* → *gacchant*; *kurvanti* → *kurvant*; *yanti* → *yant*; *santi* → *sant*. This gives the strong stem. Drop the *n* for the weak stem: *gacchat*, *kurvat*, *yat*, *sat* (Whitney §443).

- strong stem *-ant* in the strong cases: NOM, ACC SG and DU, NOM PL of the masculine (san-03-14 for the strong cases);
- weak stem *-at* everywhere else: INS *gacchatā*, DAT *gacchate*, GEN *gacchataḥ*, ACC.PL *gacchataḥ*, LOC.PL *gacchatsu*;
- NOM.SG.M: *-ant* loses its *t* at the end of the word (only one final consonant, Whitney §150): ***gacchan***, *kurvan*, *san*.

Verbs whose 3PL has no *n* (class 3: *dadati*, *juhvati*) have no nasal in the participle at all (Whitney §444): *dadat*, *dadatam*, *juhvat*.

**Active feminine.** Add *-ī* (Whitney §449):

- classes 1, 4, 10 add it to the strong stem: *gacchantī*, *paśyantī*, *cintayantī*, *hasantī*;
- athematic verbs add it to the weak stem: *kurvatī*, *satī*, *dadatī*, *juhvatī*;
- class 6 and roots of class 2 in *ā* may use either: *tudatī* or *tudantī*.

The feminine is declined like *nadī* (san-03-13).

**Neuter**: NOM.ACC.SG is the weak stem: *gacchat*, *sat*.

**Middle.** Thematic stems add ***-māna***: *labhamāna*, *manyamāna*, *vardhamāna*, *kampamāna*. Athematic stems add ***-āna*** to the weak stem: *bruvāṇa* (√brū), *kurvāṇa* (√kṛ), *juhvāna*, *dadāna*. √ās has the irregular *āsīna* "seated" (Wiktionary आस्ते). The *n* becomes *ṇ* by the n-to-ṇ rule after *r*: *kurvāṇa*, *kriyamāṇa*. Feminine *-ā*: *labhamānā*.

**Passive.** Passive stem + *-māna*: *kriyamāṇa*, *hanyamāna*, *dīyamāna*.

**How it is glossed.** Active NOM.SG whole: `gacchan` `go.PRS.PTCP.NOM.SG.M`; other cases with the stem as one piece: `gacchant-am` `go.PRS.PTCP-ACC.SG.M`, `gacchat-ā` `go.PRS.PTCP-INS.SG.M`. Middle: `labha-mān-aḥ` `get-PRS.PTCP.MID-NOM.SG.M`. Passive: `han-ya-mān-e` `kill-PASS-PRS.PTCP-LOC.SG.N` (dossier).

**The chain of thought.** "He saw the girl laughing."

1. "Laughing" describes the girl, who is the object: accusative singular feminine.
2. √has, class 1: 3PL *hasanti* → strong stem *hasant-*; class 1 makes the feminine on the strong stem: *hasantī*.
3. ACC.SG of a *nadī*-type stem: *hasantīm*.
4. *hasantīṃ kanyām apaśyat*.

## Where it applies

- **Simultaneous action** of any noun in the sentence: *gacchan … apaśyat*; *hasantīṃ kanyām apaśyat*.
- **Description**, attributive: *krīḍato bālān*, *kriyamāṇaṃ karma*, *vardhamānaḥ śatruḥ* "a growing enemy".
- **Means, cause or condition**, from context: *yatnaṃ kurvan* "by making an effort", *jānan* "since he knows", "if he knows".
- **Concession** with *api*: *jānann api* "even though knowing": Manu has *jānann api hi medhāvī jaḍaval loka ācaret* "though he knows, the wise man should behave among people like a dullard" (Manu 2.110c–d, GRETIL).
- **As a noun**: *sat* "a good man", *satām* "of the good"; *bhavat* "Your Honour".
- **In absolute constructions**, with a noun in the locative or genitive: *hanyamāne śarīre* "when the body is killed" (san-06-08).
- **Future participles** in *-ṣyant*, *-ṣyamāṇa* are built the same way on the future stem (*kariṣyat*, *kariṣyamāṇa*; san-04-17).
- **Middle participles** in *-māna* of passive and middle verbs very often stand for English relative clauses: *dīyamānaṃ dhanam* "the money that is being given".

## Watch out

**Do not use the strong stem in weak cases.** *\*gacchantā*, *\*gacchantasya* are wrong: *gacchatā*, *gacchataḥ*. The accusative plural is weak too: *krīḍataḥ*, not *\*krīḍantaḥ*.

**Do not keep the *t* in the nominative singular.** *\*gacchant* is impossible as a word: *gacchan* (Whitney §150).

**Do not give class 3 verbs a nasal.** *\*dadant*, *\*juhvant* are wrong: *dadat*, *juhvat* (Whitney §444).

**Do not make the wrong feminine.** *\*gacchatī* (class 1) and *\*kurvantī* (class 8) are wrong: *gacchantī*, *kurvatī* (Whitney §449).

**Do not use *-māna* on an athematic stem.** *\*bruvamāṇa*, *\*kurvamāṇa* are wrong: *bruvāṇa*, *kurvāṇa*. And *-āna* is wrong on a thematic stem: *\*labhāna*; use *labhamāna*.

**Do not use an active participle for a middle-only verb.** *\*labhant* is wrong: √labh is middle, *labhamāna*.

**Do not confuse the participle with the 3SG or the locative.** *gacchati* is "goes" (3SG) and also the locative singular of *gacchat* "in one going"; *bhavati* likewise. Sentence structure decides, and the locative use is mostly in absolute constructions.

**Do not use the present participle for a prior action.** *\*vanaṃ gacchan mṛgaṃ hatvā gṛham āgacchat* mixes the order. For "having gone", use the absolutive *gatvā*.

## Connections

Latin *-ns*, *-ntis* (*amāns*, *amantis* "loving") is the same participle, with a strong *-nt-* throughout; Spanish and French kept it mostly as adjectives (*amante*, *amant*, *présent*). Greek has both voices like Sanskrit: active *-ōn*, *-ontos* and middle *-ómenos*, the cognate of *-māna*. Japanese *-nagara* "while doing" covers the simultaneous use.

## Exercises

1. Give the strong and weak stems of the present active participle of √paś, √i, √brū and √dā.
2. Give the NOM.SG masculine and feminine of the participles of √cint, √tud and √kṛ.
3. Give the middle participle of √man, √śī and √kṛ.
4. Build "The boy saw his mother coming" with *ā-gam* (participle in the accusative feminine).
5. Build "We hear the birds singing" with √gai (accusative plural, weak stem).
6. Build "The tree, being cut, fell" with the passive participle of √chid.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*nṛpo vanaṃ gacchant siṃham apaśyat*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*kanyā hasatī āgacchat*.

## Worked answers

1. √paś: *paśyant*/*paśyat*. √i: *yant*/*yat*. √brū: *bruvant*/*bruvat*. √dā: *dadat* only, no nasal (class 3, Whitney §444). Each from the 3PL: *paśyanti*, *yanti*, *bruvanti*, *dadati*.
2. √cint: *cintayan*, *cintayantī* (class 10: strong stem). √tud: *tudan*, *tudatī* or *tudantī* (class 6: either). √kṛ: *kurvan*, *kurvatī* (athematic: weak stem) (Whitney §449).
3. *manyamāna* (thematic: *-māna*), *śayāna* (athematic: *-āna*, on the guṇa stem *śay-* of √śī), *kurvāṇa* (Wiktionary करोति).
4. *bālo mātaram āgacchantīm apaśyat*. Class 1 stem *āgacchant-* + *-ī*, accusative *-īm*.
5. *vayaṃ vihagān gāyataḥ śṛṇumaḥ*. ACC.PL of the participle is weak: *gāyataḥ*; *-n* before *g* and *-ḥ* before *ś* stay (san-04-07, san-02-06).
6. *chidyamāno vṛkṣo 'patat*. Passive stem *chidya-* + *-māna*; NOM.SG.M *chidyamānaḥ* before *v* → *-o*.
7. The NOM.SG.M ends in *-an*: *nṛpo vanaṃ gacchan siṃham apaśyat*.
8. Class 1 makes the feminine on the strong stem: *kanyā hasanty āgacchat* (*hasantī* + *ā-* → *hasanty ā-*, san-03-07).
