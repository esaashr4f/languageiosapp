## Before you start

This review covers all of part 4, with the weight on its second half: the four thematic classes (san-04-01), the middle (san-04-02), the imperfect (san-04-03), prefixes (san-04-04), imperative and optative (san-04-05, san-04-06), consonant sandhi (san-04-07), the six athematic classes (san-04-08, san-04-09, san-04-11, san-04-12), the past participle (san-04-13), the passive (san-04-14), the absolutive (san-04-15), the infinitive (san-04-16), the future (san-04-17), the perfect (san-04-18), the aorist and *mā* (san-04-19) and the present participles (san-04-20).

The passage is the Hitopadeśa's story of the greedy jackal (book 1, story 5). Lines 1 to 5 and line 10 are the text, with GRETIL's hyphens inside compounds removed; lines 6 to 9 retell the rest in short constructed sentences. A few words not yet taught are glossed in place. Translate each line before reading the answers.

## Exercises

1. Translate line 1. What tense is *āsīt*, and why is it irregular?
2. Translate line 2. Name the forms *ādāya*, *anviṣyan* and *gataḥ*, and say who does each action.
3. Translate line 3. Why is *tena* instrumental, and with what does *vyāpāditaḥ* agree?
4. Translate line 4. Explain *gacchatā*: stem, case, and why it is not *gacchan*.
5. Translate line 5. Who puts the deer down, and how do you know?
6. Translate line 6. Undo the joins *dīrgharāvo*, *jambukas* and *tatrāgacchat*.
7. Translate line 7. Undo *dṛṣṭvācintayad* and *icchāmīti*, and say why *mṛtān* is accusative.
8. Translate line 8. Explain the genders of *chinnā* and *utpatitam*.
9. Translate line 9. Rewrite it with an active verb: "the bow killed the jackal" in the imperfect of √han.
10. Translate line 10. Compare it with line 9.
11. Rewrite line 2 with finite verbs: "he took his bow, looked for a deer and went".
12. Put line 6 into the perfect and into the future.
13. Put the jackal's thought (line 7) into the optative of advice: "one should not eat a bowstring".
14. Warn the jackal: "Do not eat the bowstring!" with *mā* and the aorist of √khād (*akhādīt*).
15. Build "The hunter, carrying the deer, saw a boar" with an active main verb (imperfect of √paś) and a present participle.
16. Build "The boar was killed by the hunter" with the passive present.
17. Build "The jackal could not eat the bow" with √śak and an infinitive.
18. Find and fix the error: *\*vyādhaḥ mṛgam ādāya gacchataḥ śūkaraṃ dadarśa*.
19. Find and fix the error: *\*jambuko dhanuṣā hatā*.
20. Find and fix the error: *\*vyādho dhanur ādāya vanaṃ gatvā* as a complete sentence.
21. Find and fix the error: *\*śūkaraḥ vyādham ahanat*.
22. Find and fix the error: *\*mā khāda snāyum* in Classical prose.

## Worked answers

1. "There was a hunter called Bhairava who lived in Kalyāṇakaṭaka." *āsīt* is the imperfect of √as: augment + *as-*, with an inserted *ī* (san-04-03, san-04-08). The story opens like the Nala episode (*āsīd rājā*).
2. "One day, greedy for meat, he took his bow and, looking for a deer, went into the middle of the Vindhya forest." *ādāya*: absolutive of *ā-dā* "take", in *-ya* because of the prefix, with *ā* kept (san-04-15). *anviṣyan*: present active participle, NOM.SG.M, of *anu-iṣ* (san-04-20). *gataḥ*: past participle of √gam used as the main verb (san-04-13). The hunter, *saḥ*, does all three. *caikadā* = *ca ekadā* (san-03-07); *dhanuḥ* before *ā* is *dhanur* (san-02-06).
3. "There he killed a deer." The participle is passive (√pad causative, transitive), so the doer is instrumental, *tena*; *vyāpāditaḥ* agrees with *mṛgaḥ* (san-04-13). *mṛgaḥ* before *e* loses *-ḥ*; *ekaḥ* before *v* is *eko*.
4. "Then, as he went along carrying the deer, he saw a boar of terrible appearance." *gacchatā*: weak stem *gacchat-* in the instrumental, agreeing with *tena*, the agent of the passive *dṛṣṭaḥ*. The nominative *gacchan* would describe the boar, the grammatical subject (san-04-20).
5. "Then he put the deer on the ground and killed the boar with an arrow." The hunter: *nidhāya* belongs to the logical subject, the instrumental *tena* of the passive participle *hataḥ* (san-04-15). *tataḥ* before *t* is *tatas* (san-02-06).
6. "Then a jackal called Long-Howl came there." *dīrgharāvaḥ* before *n* → *-o*; *jambukaḥ* before *t* → *-as*; *tatra* + *āgacchat* → *tatrāgacchat* (san-02-06, san-03-07). *āgacchat* = *ā-a-gaccha-t* (san-04-04).
7. "Seeing the dead, he thought: 'I want to eat the bowstring.'" *dṛṣṭvā* + *acintayat* → *dṛṣṭvācintayat*, *-t* before *a* → *-d* (san-04-07); *icchāmi* + *iti* → *icchāmīti*. *mṛtān* is the object of *dṛṣṭvā*: the past participle of √mṛ "dead" used as a noun, accusative plural (san-04-13). *khāditum* + *icchāmi*: infinitive with √iṣ (san-04-16).
8. "Then the string was cut and the bow sprang up." *chinnā* agrees with *snāyuḥ*, feminine; *utpatitam* with *dhanuḥ*, neuter. *ut-pat* is intransitive, so *utpatitam* is active in sense (san-04-13).
9. "The jackal was killed by that bow." Active: *tad dhanur jambukam ahan*. Imperfect 3SG of √han: augment + strong *han-* + *-t*, the *t* dropped (san-04-08); *tat* + *dh* → *tad dh*.
10. "This jackal, whose habit was to hoard too much, was killed by a bow." The same passive construction as line 9; the verse adds the reason in a compound.
11. With imperfects: *sa dhanur ādatta mṛgam anvaiṣyad vindhyāṭavīmadhyaṃ cāgacchat*. *ādatta*: middle imperfect of *ā-dā* (*ā-a-dat-ta*; san-04-09); *anvaiṣyat*: *anu-a-iṣya-t*, the augment's vṛddhi before *i* (san-04-03, san-04-04).
12. Perfect: *tadā dīrgharāvo nāma jambukas tatrājagāma* (*ā-jagāma*, san-04-18). Future: *… tatrāgamiṣyati* (*ā-gamiṣyati*, san-04-17).
13. *snāyuṃ na khādet*. *na* with the optative for a rule (san-04-06).
14. *mā khādīḥ snāyum*. The augmentless iṣ-aorist 2SG *khādīḥ* with *mā* (san-04-19).
15. *vyādho mṛgaṃ vahan śūkaram apaśyat*. Participle *vahan* from *vahanti*, agreeing with *vyādhaḥ* (san-04-20). Or with *ādāya*: *vyādho mṛgam ādāya gacchan śūkaram apaśyat*.
16. *vyādhena śūkaro hanyate*. Passive of √han: *han-ya-te*; the agent in the instrumental (san-04-14).
17. *jambuko dhanuḥ khādituṃ nāśaknot*. *na* + *aśaknot* → *nāśaknot*; imperfect of √śak, class 5 (san-04-11, san-04-16).
18. The agent of a perfect active verb is nominative, and the participle must agree with it: *vyādho mṛgam ādāya gacchan śūkaraṃ dadarśa*. (*gacchataḥ* would be genitive or accusative plural.) Also *vyādhaḥ* before *m* → *vyādho* (san-02-06, san-04-20).
19. The participle agrees with *jambukaḥ*, masculine: *jambuko dhanuṣā hataḥ* (san-04-13).
20. An absolutive cannot end a sentence: it needs a main verb. *vyādho dhanur ādāya vanaṃ gataḥ* or *… vanam agacchat* (san-04-15).
21. √han has no thematic imperfect: *śūkaro vyādham ahan* (san-04-08). *śūkaraḥ* before *v* → *śūkaro*.
22. Classical prose forbids with *mā* + the augmentless aorist: *mā khādīḥ snāyum* (san-04-19). *mā* + imperative is a later usage (san-04-05).
