## The idea

You have met adjectives since san-02-05: *priyaḥ putraḥ*, *priyā kanyā*, *priyaṃ phalam*. That lesson used only a-stems. Part 3 then taught every kind of noun stem. This lesson puts the two together: an adjective can have any of those stems, and it declines exactly like a noun of the same stem.

That is the whole secret. Sanskrit has no separate adjective declension. An adjective is a noun-like word that can take all three genders. *śuci* "pure" is an i-stem and declines like *agni* in the masculine and like *vāri* in the neuter. *mṛdu* "soft" is a u-stem like *guru*. *mahat* "great" and *balavat* "strong" are vat-stems like *bhagavat* (san-03-17). Whitney (§344) says the same of the i- and u-stem adjectives: "their inflection is like that of nouns".

Because the adjective declines like a noun, it can also *be* a noun. Sanskrit does this constantly. *sādhuḥ* "good (man)" means "a good man, a holy man"; *dīnāḥ* "the wretched" means "poor people"; *priyam* "dear (thing)" means "something pleasant". English does it only with a few words (*the poor*, *the good*); Sanskrit does it with any adjective.

An adjective stands in one of three relations to a noun. **Attributive**: it describes the noun inside the same phrase, "the cold water". **Predicative**: it says something about the noun, "the water is cold". **Substantive**: it stands with no noun at all and is the noun, "the wise". The endings are the same in all three. Only word order and context tell them apart.

## How it is built

**Step 1: find the noun the adjective goes with.** Its gender, number and case decide the adjective's ending. The adjective copies all three.

**Step 2: find the adjective's stem type, and the right gender form.** The dictionary gives the masculine stem and the feminine:

- a-stems: *priya*, f. *priyā*; some take *-ī*, *sundara*, f. *sundarī* (san-03-13);
- i-stems: *śuci*, the same stem in all genders. Whitney (§344a): "No adjective in i makes a regular feminine in ī";
- u-stems: *mṛdu*, f. *mṛdvī* (san-03-13), and some keep *-u* in the feminine too;
- consonant stems: *mahat*, *balavat*, *dhīmat*, *balin*, with feminines in *-ī* (*mahatī*, *balavatī*, *balinī*; san-03-17).

**Step 3: decline it like a noun of that stem.** The masculine follows the masculine noun model, the feminine the feminine one, the neuter the neuter one.

There is one twist for neuter i- and u-stems. A neuter *noun* such as *vāri* or *madhu* inserts *n* in the dative, ablative, genitive and locative singular and in the genitive-locative dual: *vāriṇe*, *madhunaḥ* (san-03-12). A neuter *adjective* may do so or may take the masculine form instead. Whitney (§344): "the neuter adjective is allowed to take either form". So "for the soft (thing)" is *mṛdune* or *mṛdave*; Wiktionary's table of *mṛdu* lists both.

**Step 4: place it.** In prose an attributive adjective usually comes before its noun: *śītaṃ jalam* "cold water". A predicative adjective usually comes after its noun, and since the copula is dropped (san-02-07) nothing else marks it: *jalaṃ śītam* "the water (is) cold". Verse moves words freely, so there you decide from sense.

**Step 5: apply sandhi** between adjective and noun as between any two words (san-04-07).

**The chain of thought.** "The strong warriors protected the city."

1. "Protected" with the past participle (san-04-13): *rakṣita*. √rakṣ is transitive, so the participle is passive: "the city (was) protected".
2. The city is the subject: *nagaram*; the participle agrees, neuter singular *rakṣitam*.
3. The warriors are the agent: instrumental plural *yodhaiḥ*.
4. "Strong" agrees with *yodhaiḥ*: masculine instrumental plural of *balavat*. Before *-bhiḥ* the weak stem *balavat* has *d* (san-03-17): *balavadbhiḥ*.
5. Order: adjective, noun, then subject and verb. Sandhi: *-ḥ* before *y* and *n* becomes *-r*: *balavadbhir yodhair nagaraṃ rakṣitam*.

## Where it applies

- **Attributive**, before the noun in prose: *śītaṃ jalam*, *balavadbhir yodhaiḥ*. Several adjectives can stack: *śucir dakṣo 'nuraktaś ca bhṛtyaḥ*; *ca* may join them.
- **Predicative**, with no copula: *jalam uṣṇam*, *phalāni pakvāni*. With a 1st or 2nd person subject the copula usually appears: *śrāntaḥ asmi* → *śrānto 'smi* "I am tired".
- **Predicative with a verb of being or becoming**: *nṛpo dīno 'bhavat* "the king became wretched". The adjective is nominative like the subject.
- **Predicative to an object**, with verbs of making, thinking and calling: *taṃ mūḍhaṃ manyate* "he thinks him a fool". The adjective is accusative like the object.
- **Substantive, masculine or feminine** for persons: *sādhavaḥ* "good people", *dīnāḥ* "the poor", *mūḍhaḥ* "a fool", *priyā* "a beloved woman".
- **Substantive, neuter** for a thing or a quality: *priyam* "something pleasant", *satyam* "truth" (literally "the real"), *hitam* "what is good for one".
- **Neuter accusative as an adverb**: *śīghram* "quickly", *dūram* "far" (you have met these; san-05-03 takes them further).
- **Participles are adjectives** and do all of this: *hataṃ mṛgam* (san-04-13), *gacchantī kanyā* (san-04-20).
- **With a case of their own.** Many adjectives govern a noun: *bhīta* with the ablative (san-03-03), *hīna* with the instrumental, *tulya* with the instrumental or genitive, *pūrṇa* "full" with the instrumental or genitive (mw1899), *mūḍha* "at a loss" with the locative (mw1899): *jalena pūrṇā nadī* "a river full of water".
- **In compounds**, the adjective loses its ending and stands first: *mahat* becomes *mahā-* (*mahārāja*). See san-05-09.
- **With the ablative of comparison**: *dhanād vidyā śreyasī* "learning is better than wealth" (san-05-06).

## Watch out

**Do not give an adjective an English-style fixed form.** *\*priya kanyā* is wrong: the adjective must agree, *priyā kanyā*. Spanish and French agree in gender and number; Sanskrit also agrees in case, so *\*priyā kanyāyai* for "to the dear girl" fails in turn: *priyāyai kanyāyai*.

**Do not decline every adjective as an a-stem.** *\*mṛdvena*, *\*śucena* are wrong for the instrumental singular masculine: *mṛdunā*, *śucinā*, like *guruṇā* and *agninā*. Look at the stem first.

**Do not decline the adjective like the noun next to it.** The noun's stem does not matter, only its gender, number and case. *\*mahatyāṃ vane* is wrong for "in the great forest": *vana* is neuter, so *mahati vane*.

**Do not give an i-stem adjective a feminine in *-ī*.** *\*śucī kanyā* is wrong: *śuciḥ kanyā*. Only a-stems (some), u-stems and consonant stems form feminines in *-ī*.

**Do not take the neuter dative for the masculine.** *mṛdave* is the masculine form, allowed for the neuter as well; *mṛdune* is neuter only. *\*mṛdune bālāya* is wrong: *mṛdave bālāya* "to the gentle boy".

**Do not add a copula in the 3rd person by analogy with English or Spanish.** *jalam uṣṇam asti* is grammatical but heavy; the plain statement is *jalam uṣṇam*. The copula is the marked choice (san-02-07).

**Do not read every adjective after its noun as predicative in verse.** In Hitopadeśa 0.22 *bhāryā rūpavatī śatruḥ*, *rūpavatī* is attributive and *śatruḥ* the predicate. Ask which word the line is saying something about.

**Do not use the masculine for a substantive that means a thing.** *\*priyo vadati mūḍhaḥ* means "the fool, dear man, speaks". For "something pleasant" use the neuter: *priyaṃ vadati mūḍhaḥ*.

## Connections

Spanish and French make adjectives into nouns with the article: *los pobres*, *les pauvres*, *lo bueno*. Sanskrit has no article, so the ending alone does it: *dīnāḥ*, *priyam*. Latin, without an article, works exactly like Sanskrit: *boni* "good men", *bonum* "the good".

## Exercises

1. Decline "pure water" (*śuci*, *jala*) in the nominative, instrumental and locative singular.
2. Build "The fickle servant took the money" with the past participle of √hṛ.
3. Build "The two thin girls are drinking cold water."
4. Say whether each adjective is attributive, predicative or substantive: *kṛṣṇo megho dṛśyate*; *meghaḥ kṛṣṇaḥ*; *krūrā vanaṃ gacchanti*.
5. Give the dative singular of *mṛdu* in all three genders.
6. Build "The broad river is full of water."
7. Find and fix the error: *\*mahatyāṃ vane siṃho vasati* "a lion lives in the great forest".
8. Find and fix the error: *\*śucī kanyā* "a pure girl".

## Worked answers

1. NOM *śuci jalam*, INS *śucinā jalena*, LOC *śucini jale*. The neuter i-stem has *-n-* forms like *vāri* in the instrumental and locative; the adjective agrees with *jala* (neuter).
2. *capalena bhṛtyena dhanaṃ hṛtam*. The agent and its adjective are instrumental; *hṛta* agrees with *dhanam*.
3. *tanvyau kanye śītaṃ jalaṃ pibataḥ*. *tanu* has the feminine *tanvī*, which declines like *nadī*: NOM.DU *tanvyau*, as *nadyau*. *kanye* is the dual of *kanyā*; the verb is 3DU.
4. *kṛṣṇo megho dṛśyate* "a dark cloud is seen": attributive (before the noun). *meghaḥ kṛṣṇaḥ* "the cloud is dark": predicative (after the noun, no copula). *krūrā vanaṃ gacchanti* "cruel men go to the forest": substantive, masculine plural.
5. M *mṛdave*; F *mṛdvyai* (like *nadyai*); N *mṛdune* or *mṛdave* (Whitney §344).
6. *pṛthur nadī jalena pūrṇā* or *pṛthvī nadī jalena pūrṇā*. *pṛthu* may keep *-u* in the feminine (mw1899: f. *vī* or *u*). *pūrṇā* is predicative, with the instrumental.
7. *vana* is neuter, so the adjective is neuter: *mahati vane siṃho vasati*.
8. An i-stem adjective has no feminine in *-ī* (Whitney §344a): *śuciḥ kanyā*.
