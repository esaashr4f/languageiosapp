## The idea

Words such as "all", "other", "one" and "own" are adjectives in meaning: *sarve janāḥ* "all people", *anyasmin dine* "on another day". But they do not decline quite like *priya*. In some cases they take the endings of the pronoun *sa, sā, tad* (san-03-09) instead of the noun endings.

Whitney (§522) calls them adjectives "inflected, in part or wholly, according to the pronominal declension". The reason is their meaning. They do the same kind of job as pronouns: they pick out *which* or *how many* things are meant, rather than describing them. "All", "other", "which of two" sit in the same family of words as "this" and "that", and they borrowed that family's endings. Several of them are even built on pronoun roots: *katara* "which of two" on the root of *kaḥ* "who".

The pronominal endings are the ones you know from *tasmai*, *tasmāt*, *tasmin*, *te*, *teṣām* and the feminine *tasyai*, *tasyāḥ*, *tasyām*, *tāsām*. Put *sarva-* in place of *ta-* and you have most of *sarva*.

## How it is built

**Step 1: the pronominal cases.** In the masculine and neuter, five forms differ from *deva*:

- DAT.SG *-smai*: *sarvasmai* (not *sarvāya*);
- ABL.SG *-smāt*: *sarvasmāt* (not *sarvāt*);
- LOC.SG *-smin*: *sarvasmin* (not *sarve*);
- NOM.PL masculine *-e*: *sarve* (not *sarvāḥ*);
- GEN.PL *-eṣām*: *sarveṣām* (not *sarvāṇām*).

Everything else is as *deva*: *sarvaḥ*, *sarvam*, *sarveṇa*, *sarvasya*, *sarvān*, *sarvaiḥ*, *sarvebhyaḥ*, *sarveṣu*.

In the feminine the stem ends in *-ā* (*sarvā*, never *-ī*), and four forms differ from *senā*: DAT.SG *sarvasyai*, ABL and GEN.SG *sarvasyāḥ*, LOC.SG *sarvasyām*, GEN.PL *sarvāsām*. Compare *tasyai*, *tasyāḥ*, *tasyām*, *tāsām*.

**Step 2: the neuter nominative.** Here the words split into two groups (Whitney §§523–524).

- *anya* "other", *itara* "other", *katara* "which of two", *katama* "which of many" are declined like *tad* "throughout": neuter NOM and ACC.SG *anyat*, *itarat*, like *tat*.
- *sarva* "all", *viśva* "all, every", *eka* "one" have the ordinary *-am*: *sarvam*, *viśvam*, *ekam*. Whitney: "except in the nom.-acc.-voc. sing. neut., where they have the ordinary adjective form am, instead of the pronominal at".

**Step 3: the words that may go either way.** A third group takes the pronominal endings "usually, or in some of their significations, or optionally" (Whitney §525): *para* "other, further", *apara* "other, later", *pūrva* "former, eastern", *uttara* "upper, northern, later", *dakṣiṇa* "right, southern", *ubhaya* "of both kinds", and the possessive *sva* "own". Wiktionary's tables give both forms: *pare* and *parāḥ*, *parasmāt* and *parāt*, *svasmin* and *sve*. The neuter is in *-am*: *param*, *svam*.

**Step 4: meaning.** *eka* means "one", and also "a certain", "alone" and "the same"; its plural *eke* means "some". *sarva* in the singular means "whole, every", in the plural "all". *sva* is reflexive: it refers to the subject of its clause, like *ātman* and *svayam* (san-03-16). *para* used as a noun is "a stranger, an enemy"; neuter *param* as an adverb is "beyond, afterwards".

**The chain of thought.** "On another day the king went to the forest."

1. "Day" is *dina* (n.); "on" a day is the locative (san-03-05).
2. "Another" agrees: neuter locative singular of *anya*. It is pronominal: *-smin*, so *anyasmin*.
3. The verb: the imperfect *agacchat*; the goal in the accusative, *vanam*.
4. *anyasmin dine nṛpo vanam agacchat*. Sandhi: *nṛpaḥ* before *v* becomes *nṛpo*; *vanam* before a vowel keeps *m*.

## Where it applies

- **sarva, viśva**: "every" in the singular, "all" in the plural, "whole" with a single thing (*sarvaṃ jagat* "the whole world"). Pronominal except NOM/ACC.SG.N *-am*.
- **eka**: "one" (san-05-05), "a certain" (*ekasmin vane*), "alone, only" (*eka eva* "he alone"), "the same" (*ekasmin dine* can be "on one and the same day"); plural *eke* "some". Neuter *ekam*.
- **anya, itara**: "other". Fully pronominal, neuter *anyat*, *itarat*. *anya ... anya* "one ... another".
- **katara, katama** "which (of two, of many)" and **yatara, yatama** "which (relative)": like *tad*. They belong with the question words and relatives of san-06-01 and san-06-02.
- **para, apara, pūrva, uttara, dakṣiṇa, ubhaya, sva**: pronominal or ordinary; both are correct (Whitney §525, Wiktionary tables). Neuter *-am*.
- **pūrva, uttara, dakṣiṇa** for directions: *pūrvā diś* "the east", *uttarā diś* "the north", *dakṣiṇā diś* "the south". The feminine agrees with *diś*.
- **As nouns**: *sarve* "everyone", *anye* "others", *paraḥ* "a stranger", *svam* "one's own property" (mw1899: n. "one's own goods, property").
- **As adverbs**: *param* "beyond, afterwards", *pūrvam* "before" (you have met it), *anyathā* "otherwise".
- **With the ablative**, the words meaning "other than", "before" and "after" take the ablative of what is compared: *anyo rāmāt* "other than Rāma", *pūrvaṃ sūryodayāt* "before sunrise" (Whitney §292b, who quotes *ko mitrād anyaḥ* "who other than a friend?" from the Hitopadeśa).
- **In compounds** they stand in stem form: *sarvadharmān* "all duties" (Bhg 18.66, san-04-19).

## Watch out

**Do not use the noun endings for the core group.** *\*sarvāya*, *\*sarvāt*, *\*sarve* (locative) are wrong: *sarvasmai*, *sarvasmāt*, *sarvasmin*. And *\*sarvāḥ janāḥ* is wrong for "all people": *sarve janāḥ*.

**Do not mix up the two *sarve*.** *sarve* is NOM.PL masculine. It is never locative singular: "in all the village" is *sarvasmin grāme*.

**Do not give *sarva* the neuter in *-t*.** *\*sarvat* is wrong: *sarvam*. The neuter in *-t* belongs to *anya*, *itara*, *katara*, *katama* only.

**Do not give *anya* the neuter in *-m*.** *\*anyam phalam* is wrong for "another fruit": *anyat phalam*. *anyam* is the masculine accusative.

**Do not form the genitive plural with *-ānām*.** *\*sarvāṇām*, *\*anyānām* are wrong: *sarveṣām*, *anyeṣām*; feminine *sarvāsām*, *anyāsām*. The *ṣ* follows the s-to-ṣ rule after *e* (san-03-01, san-05-07).

**Do not give the feminine *-ī*.** *\*sarvī*, *\*anyī* are wrong: *sarvā*, *anyā*, like *sā*. And the feminine pronominal cases have *-sy-*: *\*sarvāyai* is wrong, *sarvasyai*.

**Do not treat every adjective as pronominal.** *\*priyasmin* is wrong for "in the dear one": only this closed set takes pronominal endings, so *priye*. Whitney (§526) notes occasional pronominal forms of *alpa* "few" and a few others; do not imitate them.

**Do not use *sva* for someone other than the subject.** *rājā bhṛtyaṃ sve gṛhe paśyati* means "the king sees the servant in his (the king's) own house". For "in the servant's house", use *tasya gṛhe*.

## Connections

Spanish and French have no special endings for these words, but Latin does: *tōtus*, *alius*, *ūnus*, *nūllus* have the pronominal genitive *-īus* and dative *-ī* (*tōtīus*, *tōtī*), as *ille* has *illīus*, *illī*. *aliud* "other (n.)" ends in *-d* like *illud* "that", just as *anyat* ends like *tat*. The parallel is close: in both languages a small set of "which, how many" adjectives took over the pronoun endings.

## Exercises

1. Decline *anya* in the masculine singular, all cases.
2. Build "There is water in every river."
3. Build "Some boys eat, others drink."
4. Give the neuter nominative singular of *sarva*, *anya*, *eka*, *itara* and *para*.
5. Build "The servant gave the money to all the Brahmins."
6. Translate *pūrvasyāṃ diśi sūryo dṛśyate*.
7. Find and fix the error: *\*sarvāḥ yodhāḥ yuddhaṃ gacchanti*.
8. Find and fix the error: *\*sa anyaṃ vacanam avadat* "he said another word".

## Worked answers

1. *anyaḥ, anyam, anyena, anyasmai, anyasmāt, anyasya, anyasmin*; VOC *anya*. Pronominal in the dative, ablative and locative; the rest as *deva* (Whitney §523).
2. *sarvasyāṃ nadyāṃ jalam asti*. *nadī* is feminine, so the feminine pronominal locative *sarvasyām*; *-m* before *n* is *ṃ*.
3. *eke bālāḥ khādanty apare pibanti* (or *aparāḥ*). *eke* is pronominal NOM.PL; *apara* may be either.
4. *sarvam*, *anyat*, *ekam*, *itarat*, *param*. Only *anya* and *itara* (with *katara*, *katama*) take *-t* (Whitney §§523–525).
5. *bhṛtyaḥ sarvebhyo brāhmaṇebhyo dhanam adadāt* (or with the past participle *bhṛtyena sarvebhyo brāhmaṇebhyo dhanaṃ dattam*). The dative plural is the same as the noun ending, *-ebhyaḥ*; before a voiced *b* it becomes *-o*.
6. "The sun is seen in the eastern quarter", that is, "the sun appears in the east". *pūrvasyām* is the feminine pronominal locative agreeing with *diśi*. (Wiktionary's table for *pūrva* gives only the ordinary forms; both are allowed, Whitney §525c.)
7. *sarve yodhā yuddhaṃ gacchanti*. NOM.PL.M of *sarva* is *sarve*; then *yodhāḥ* before *y* loses its *-ḥ* (san-02-06).
8. *vacana* is neuter, and *anya* has the neuter *anyat*: *so 'nyad vacanam avadat*. Sandhi: *saḥ* + *a* → *so 'nyad*; *anyat* before *v* → *anyad*.
