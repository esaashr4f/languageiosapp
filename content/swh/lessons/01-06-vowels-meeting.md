## The idea

Swahili builds words by putting a class prefix in front of a stem: *m-tu* "person", *wa-tu* "people". Many stems begin with a vowel, so the language constantly has to decide what happens when a prefix meets one.

It has two regular answers.

**A prefix vowel *i* or *u* turns into a glide.** A **glide** is the consonant-like version of a vowel: *y* is the glide of *i*, *w* the glide of *u*. Before another vowel, the prefix's *u* becomes *w* and its *i* becomes *y*, and the prefix stops being a syllable. *Mwaka* "year" is two syllables, *mwa-ka*, not three.

**A prefix ending in *a* merges with the stem vowel.** *A* + *a* gives *a*. *A* + *e* gives *e*. *A* + *i* gives *e*: the two vowels meet halfway. So the plural of *mwana* "child" is *wana*, not *waana*, and the plural of *mwizi* "thief" is *wezi*. The technical term for vowels fusing like this is **coalescence**.

This matters beyond nouns: the agreement prefixes of part 3 go through the same changes. The word for "many", stem *-ingi*, is *wengi* with people and *mengi* with class 6 nouns: *wa-* + *i* and *ma-* + *i*, both merged to *e*. If you know this lesson, you will recognise a prefix even when it has changed shape.

Where do the changes come from? The prefixes are older than their present spelling. The class 1 and 3 prefix *m-* goes back to a Bantu *\*mʊ̀-*, with a vowel; Wiktionary reconstructs *mwana* as *\*mʊ̀jánà*. Before a vowel that old *u* survives as the *w* of *mw-*. Before a consonant it was lost, which is why *mtu* has a syllabic *m*. The *ch* of class 7 is the same story: Polomé explains it as the old *ki-* before a vowel, where the *i* became a glide and the *k* and the glide fused into [tʃ].

These changes happen at the join between a prefix and a stem; elsewhere vowels meet freely: *njia*, *sauti*.

## How it is built

To build or take apart a word whose stem begins with a vowel:

1. Find the prefix and the stem. *-aka* "year", *-ana* "child", *-umba* "room, house".
2. If the prefix is *m-* (classes 1 and 3) and the stem begins with *a*, *e* or *i*, write *mw-*: *mwaka*, *mwezi*, *mwili*. Before *o* a thing-noun keeps plain *m-*: *moyo* "heart".
3. If the prefix is *mi-* (class 4), leave it alone: *miaka*, *miezi*, *miili*. Its *i* stays a full vowel.
4. If the prefix is *ki-* or *vi-* (classes 7 and 8) and the stem begins with any vowel except *i*, write *ch-* and *vy-*: *chumba*, *vyumba*. Before *i* they stay: *kiini* "yolk", *viini*.
5. If the prefix is *u-* (class 11), write *w-*: *wimbo* "song".
6. If the prefix is *wa-* or *ma-*, merge: *a* + *a* is *a*, *a* + *e* or *i* is *e*: *wana*, *wenzi*, *wezi*, *meno*.
7. If the prefix is the nasal of classes 9 and 10, write *ny-* before a vowel: *nyumba*, *nyimbo*.
8. Check for exceptions: many loanwords, names of peoples and a few other nouns keep both vowels (*maembe*, *Waarabu*).

The classes themselves are taught in part 3; here, read the class numbers as names.

## Watch out

**Mw- is not a syllable.** *Mwaka* is *MWA-ka*, two syllables. Compare *mtu*, where *m* is before a consonant and is a syllable: *M-tu*.

**Mi- never merges in nouns.** *Miaka* is *mi-A-ka*, three syllables, stressed on the *a*. Do not reduce it to "myaka".

**Not every vowel meeting merges.** Many loans keep both vowels: *maembe*, *maisha* "life". So do the names of peoples: Almasi and colleagues give *Mwarabu*, plural *Waarabu* "Arabs", and *Mwafrika*, plural *Waafrika* "Africans". Even *mwalimu* "teacher" has two plurals in Wiktionary, *walimu* and *waalimu*. A few native nouns also keep *ki-* before a vowel: *kiatu* "shoe", plural *viatu*, and *kioo* "mirror" from lesson 1. Polomé lists such words (*kiazi* "yam", *kioo*, *kiuno* "loin", *kiatu*) beside pairs like *chatu* "python" and *kiatu* "shoe", and treats them as a fixed exception, without a history. Learn the plural with the noun.

**Ch- and vy- are clues, not proof.** *Chumba*, plural *vyumba*, is a *ki-* noun in disguise. But *chai* "tea" is not: it belongs to class 9, with no prefix at all. The plural is the test.

**Two more mergers come later.** Polomé describes *a* + *o* giving *o* in a few pronoun forms, and *ni-* + *a-* giving *na-* in one verb tense. They are taught where they occur.

## Connections

French does something similar at word joins: *le* + *ami* is *l'ami*, and *de* + *le* is *du*. But French writes a merger only in a few short words, while Swahili does it in every class prefix.

## Exercises

1. Make the plurals: *mwana*, *mwizi*, *mwenzi*. Say which vowels merged in each.
2. *Mwaka* and *miaka*: how many syllables has each, and where is the stress?
3. The class 7 prefix is *ki-*. Why is "room" *chumba* and not *kiumba*? What is its plural?
4. Why does *kiini* "yolk" keep its *ki-*?
5. *Meno* is the plural of *jino*. Take *meno* apart: what was the prefix, what was the stem, and what happened?
6. *Wimbo* has the class 11 prefix. What was the prefix before the change, and what is its plural?
7. The plural of *embe* is *maembe*, not *membe*. Why does it break the merger rule?
8. The stem *-ingi* "many" takes class prefixes in part 3. Predict its form with *wa-* and with *ma-*, using this lesson's rules.

## Worked answers

1. *wana*, *wezi*, *wenzi*. The plural prefix is *wa-*. In *wana*, *a* + *a* gave *a*. In *wezi*, *a* + *i* gave *e*. In *wenzi*, *a* + *e* gave *e*.
2. *Mwaka*: two syllables, *MWA-ka*; the *mw* is one onset, not a syllable. *Miaka*: three, *mi-A-ka*, because *mi-* keeps its *i* as a full vowel; the stress is on the second-to-last, the *a*.
3. Before a vowel the *i* of *ki-* becomes a glide, and *k* with that glide became *ch*: *ki-* + *-umba* gives *chumba*. The plural prefix *vi-* does the same: *vyumba*.
4. Its stem begins with *i*. *Ki-* and *vi-* keep their shape before *i*: *ki-i-ni*, three syllables.
5. Prefix *ma-*, stem *-ino*. The *a* of the prefix and the *i* of the stem merged into *e*: *ma-* + *ino* gives *meno*.
6. *U-*. Before the vowel of the stem *-imbo* it became *w-*. The plural is *nyimbo*: the nasal prefix of class 10 becomes *ny-* before a vowel.
7. *Embe* is a loan, and many loans keep both vowels after *ma-* or *wa-*. Polomé lists *maembe* as one of these exceptions.
8. *Wa-* + *-ingi*: *a* + *i* merges to *e*, giving *wengi*. *Ma-* + *-ingi* likewise gives *mengi*. Wiktionary confirms both.
