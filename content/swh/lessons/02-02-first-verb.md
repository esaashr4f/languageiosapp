## The idea

A Swahili verb is a whole sentence in one word. *Ninasoma* is "I am reading": nothing else is needed. Part 0 showed why. The verb is a row of slots, and the first slots say who acts and when.

This lesson fills four slots, always in this order:

1. the **subject prefix**: who does it (*ni-* "I");
2. the **tense marker**: when (*-na-* "now, generally");
3. the **root**: the action (*-som-* "read");
4. the **final vowel**: here *-a*.

*Ni-na-som-a*. Change the first piece and the subject changes: *tunasoma* "we are reading", *wanasoma* "they are reading". Change the root and the action changes: *ninapika* "I am cooking". Each piece does one job.

The subject prefixes match the pronouns of the last lesson, and you can see the family likeness: *mimi* and *ni-*, *wewe* and *u-*, *yeye* and *a-*, *sisi* and *tu-*, *ninyi* and *m-*, *wao* and *wa-*. The prefix is not optional. The pronoun is. *Ninasoma* is a complete sentence; *Mimi ninasoma* adds weight to "I", as in "*I* am reading (whatever the others do)". Almasi and colleagues note that speakers also add the pronoun at the start of a sentence because it sounds better.

Look at "he or she" and "they". The prefixes *a-* and *wa-* are the verb prefixes of classes 1 and 2, the classes of people. Swahili has no separate "third person" on the verb: "she is reading" uses the same *a-* as "the child is reading". That is why this guide glosses them C1.SM and C2.SM. When a class 1 noun is the subject, the verb takes *a-*; with a class 2 noun, *wa-*. *Mtoto anasoma*, *watoto wanasoma*.

The marker *-na-* is the present. It covers what is happening now and what happens in general. Thompson and Schleicher point out that *wanasoma* can be "they are reading" or "they read", and only the context decides. Where does it come from? Wiktionary, following the Bantuist Carl Meinhof, derives *-na-* from *-na*, the stem of the Swahili expression for "have", which is built on *na* "with" (lesson 2.6). How "have" became a present tense is not explained there.

The **final vowel** is the last slot. In the verbs of Bantu origin it is *-a* in this tense. It is a slot of its own because it changes: lesson 2.3 replaces it with *-i* for "not". Verbs borrowed from Arabic end in *e*, *i* or *u* and keep that vowel always (Almasi ch. 5): *ninajibu* "I am answering", *ninarudi* "I am coming back". They have no final vowel slot.

## How it is built

Say "the children are writing".

1. Find the verb: "write" is *-andika*. Remove the final *-a* to find the root: *-andik-*.
2. Find the subject: *watoto*, class 2. The class 2 subject prefix is *wa-*.
3. The time is now: tense marker *-na-*.
4. Add the root and the final vowel *-a*.
5. Join the pieces into one word: *wa-na-andik-a*, *wanaandika*.
6. Put the noun subject first: *Watoto wanaandika.*

Two vowels meeting here do not merge. The merging of lesson 1.6 happens where a class prefix meets a noun stem. After *-na-*, both vowels stay and both are syllables: *wa-na-a-NDI-ka*.

The verb is quoted in its **infinitive**, the form that names the action: *kusoma* "to read, reading". It is *ku-* plus the stem. Wiktionary traces *ku-* to the Bantu prefix of class 15, and Thompson and Schleicher point out that infinitives are nouns of that class. This guide writes the stem with a hyphen, *-soma*, and gives the infinitive in every word list.

The infinitive also follows verbs like "like" and "want": *Ninapenda kusoma* "I like to read".

## Where it applies

- **Happening now.** *Ninasoma* "I'm reading (at the moment)".
- **Happening generally or regularly.** *Ninasoma* can also be "I read", "I study (these days)". Thompson and Schleicher stress that *-na-* serves both and the context chooses. A separate habitual form exists (lesson 4.13), but *-na-* is the everyday choice.
- **A process under way.** Wiktionary's example translates *wanalala* as "they are falling asleep": *-na-* can describe a change in progress.
- **Every person, and any class 1 or 2 noun.** The six subject prefixes, plus *a-* and *wa-* after any person noun or name: *Amina anapika*.
- **Thing subjects.** Every class has its own subject prefix, used in exactly this slot. Part 3 adds them class by class: the book "it" will be *ki-*, not *a-*.
- **The infinitive after another verb.** *Ninapenda kusoma*, *Ninataka kulala* "I want to sleep".
- **Citing verbs.** Dictionaries and this guide list verbs by stem (*-soma*) or infinitive (*kusoma*).
- **Loan verbs.** The same slots, without the final vowel: *ninajibu*, *unarudi*, *tunasafiri*, *wanafikiri*.

## Watch out

**Do not leave out the subject prefix.** *\*Mimi soma* loses both the "I" slot and the tense slot. A bare stem like *soma* is a command (lesson 2.7). Say *Mimi ninasoma*, or just *Ninasoma*. (The spoken *nasoma* is a different matter: a shortening of the standard form, described below.)

**Do not add *ni* to translate English "am".** "I am reading" is *Ninasoma*. *\*Mimi ni ninasoma* puts the copula of lesson 2.1 in front of a verb. *Ni* links nouns; the verb already contains the person and the tense.

**Make the prefix agree with the subject.** *\*Watoto anacheza* has a plural subject and a singular prefix. Say *Watoto wanacheza*.

**Use *m-* for "you all".** *\*Ninyi unasoma* uses the singular "you". Say *Ninyi mnasoma*.

**Do not merge vowels after *-na-*.** *\*Wanandika* for *wanaandika*, or *\*tunimba* for *tunaimba*, applies the noun rule of lesson 1.6 where it does not belong. Wiktionary's tables write both vowels: *ninaandika*, *anaona*.

**Do not give a loan verb an *-a*.** *\*Ninajiba* is not a word, and *ninarudia* means "I am repeating", another verb (lesson 2.7). Loan verbs keep their own last vowel: *ninajibu*, *ninarudi*.

**Do not build "I want to sleep" with two tensed verbs.** *\*Ninataka ninalala* is wrong. The second verb is an infinitive: *Ninataka kulala*.

**You will hear *nasoma* for *ninasoma*.** In everyday speech the *ni-* is often dropped before *-na-*. Wiktionary's tables list both. This guide writes the full standard form; lesson 7.12 explains why the short form can be confused with another tense.

**Verbs of one syllable keep *ku-*.** "I am eating" is *ninakula*, not *\*ninala*, because a stem like *-la* "eat" is too short. Almasi and colleagues list these verbs (*-la*, *-nywa*, *-ja*...) as keeping their *ku-* after *-na-*. Lesson 4.4 explains it. Until then they appear only as infinitives and in the negative.

## Connections

Japanese and Korean also build verbs by adding pieces, but after the root: 読みます, 읽어요. Swahili adds them before it. Spanish *leo*, *lees*, *lee* carries the person at the end of the verb; Swahili carries it at the front. Like Spanish, Swahili can then leave the pronoun out.

## Exercises

1. Build "we are cooking", "they are cooking" and "you (several) are cooking".
2. Take apart *Wanafunzi wanaimba* and translate it.
3. Say "the teacher knows" and "the teachers know".
4. Build "I am travelling" and "she is thinking". What is different about these two verbs?
5. Correct *\*Watoto anakimbia*.
6. Translate *Mimi ninasoma, lakini wewe unalala.* Why are the pronouns there?
7. Say "I want to sing" and "they like to write".
8. Give the infinitive of *-ona* and *-rudi*, and the stem of *kusikia*.

## Worked answers

1. Root *-pik-*, tense *-na-*, final *-a*. Change only the subject prefix: *tunapika*, *wanapika*, *mnapika*.
2. *Wanafunzi*: students, class 2. *Wa-na-imb-a*: class 2 subject prefix, present, "sing", final vowel. "The students are singing." The prefix *wa-* agrees with *wanafunzi*.
3. *Mwalimu* is class 1, so the prefix is *a-*: *Mwalimu anajua.* Plural *walimu* is class 2: *Walimu wanajua.*
4. *Ninasafiri*, *Anafikiri*. Both are loan verbs from Arabic. They keep their final *i* and have no final-vowel slot, but the subject prefix and *-na-* are added as usual.
5. *Watoto* is plural, class 2, so the prefix must be *wa-*: *Watoto wanakimbia.*
6. "I am reading, but you are sleeping." Each verb already has its subject prefix; *mimi* and *wewe* are added to contrast the two people.
7. *Ninataka kuimba.* *Wanapenda kuandika.* The first verb has the subject prefix and *-na-*; the second is an infinitive with *ku-*.
8. *Kuona*, *kurudi*. The stem of *kusikia* is *-sikia*.
