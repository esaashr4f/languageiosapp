## The idea

Lesson 2.1 gave you *ni* for saying what someone is: *Mimi ni mwalimu* "I am a teacher". It also warned that *ni* does not say where someone is. "I am at home" is not about identity, and Swahili builds it differently, much as Spanish says *soy maestro* but *estoy en casa*.

The tool is a two-piece word: a subject prefix and a place ending. *Ni-po* "I am here (at this spot)". *Yu-ko* "he or she is (over) there". *Wa-mo* "they are inside". There is no tense marker and no verb root. The prefix says who; the ending says "at a place", and which kind of place.

Why such a short word? Swahili has a verb "be", *kuwa*, but in the present tense it does not appear after a subject prefix. Polomé states this directly: the present of "be" does not occur after the subject prefix in modern Swahili, so the prefix is joined straight to a place word instead. In the past and future the verb comes back (*nilikuwapo hapa* "I was here", Polomé's example), which you will meet in part 4. Almasi and colleagues describe the same facts the other way round: in the present, the verb of being in a place is replaced by *-ko*, *-po* or *-mo*.

The three endings come from the three **place classes**. Swahili treats places as nouns with classes of their own, numbered 16, 17 and 18. Class 16 (*pa-*) is a definite spot; class 17 (*ku-*) is a general area or somewhere further off; class 18 (*mu-*) is inside. Each class has a short "reference" form built with *-o*: *pa* + *o* gives *-po*, *ku* + *o* gives *-ko*, *mu* + *o* gives *-mo*. Polomé analyses the endings exactly so. Lesson 3.13 teaches the place classes in full. For now, three endings and what they mean:

- *-po*: a definite spot, or one close to the speaker. Thompson and Schleicher add that *-po* is used even when "here" follows.
- *-ko*: a general location, or one far from the speaker.
- *-mo*: inside, or emphatically "in".

One more surprise. The class 1 prefix is *a-* on ordinary verbs (*anasoma*), but before these endings it is *yu-*: *yupo*, *yuko*, *yumo*. Thompson and Schleicher and Almasi and colleagues both point this out. It is the only prefix that changes. The other persons use the prefixes you know: *ni-*, *u-*, *tu-*, *m-*, *wa-*.

## How it is built

Say "the children are at school".

1. Find the subject: *watoto*, class 2. The prefix is *wa-*.
2. Choose the place ending. A school is a definite place; for now take the general *-ko*: *wako*.
3. Add the place: *shuleni* "at school" (*shule* plus *-ni*, the ending that turns a noun into a place; lesson 3.13).
4. *Watoto wako shuleni.*

Say "the teacher is not inside".

1. *Mwalimu*, class 1: the prefix before a place ending is *yu-*.
2. Negative: put *ha-* in front, as in lesson 2.3: *hayu-*.
3. Inside: *-mo*. *Hayumo.*
4. Add *ndani* "inside" if you want to be explicit: *Mwalimu hayumo ndani.*

The negatives follow lesson 2.3 exactly, without the final *-i*, since there is no verb root to end in a vowel. *Ha-* goes in front of *tu-*, *m-*, *wa-* and class 1 *yu-*; "I" is *si-* and "you (one)" is *hu-*: *sipo*, *hupo*, *hayupo*, *hatupo*, *hampo*, *hawapo*. Thompson and Schleicher and Wiktionary both give these tables.

To ask where someone is, put *wapi* "where?" after the form with *-ko*: *Uko wapi?* "where are you?". Polomé notes that the question word normally comes last.

## Where it applies

- **People in a place, now.** *Niko nyumbani*, *Mwalimu yuko darasani*, *Tuko Nairobi*. This is the main use.
- **Any person and any class 1 or 2 noun.** Six persons, with *yu-* for class 1. Part 3 adds every other class in the same slot: *kitabu kipo*, *miti iko* (Thompson and Schleicher's *miti iko msituni* "the trees are in the forest").
- **Here and there.** *Nipo hapa* "I'm here"; *Yuko pale* "she's there (at that spot)"; *Wako kule* "they're over there". *Hapa* and *pale* are class 16 words, *kule* is class 17.
- **Inside.** *Wamo ndani*, *Yumo chumbani* "he's in the room". Thompson and Schleicher's *kitabu kimo mfukoni* "the book is in the bag" shows *-mo* with a container.
- **Near and far, in and out.** *Yuko karibu*, *Wako mbali*, *Tuko nje* "we're outside".
- **Towns and countries.** The name follows directly: *Yuko Mombasa*. Thompson and Schleicher's *Dar es Salaam* and Almasi and colleagues' *Misri* "Egypt" appear the same way, with no ending.
- **Asking where.** *Uko wapi?*, *Watoto wako wapi?*, *Mwalimu yuko wapi?*
- **"Is she in?", "is he around?"** *Mwalimu yupo?* asks whether the teacher is here or available. The answer: *Yupo* "she's here" or *Hayupo* "she isn't".
- **Negatives.** *Sipo*, *siko*, *simo*; *hayuko*, *hawamo*, and so on, for "not at, not in".
- **In speech, *-ko* for almost everything.** Wikipedia's grammar article notes that many speakers use *niko* for every kind of "I am (somewhere)", where the strict standard distinguishes *nipo*, *niko* and *nimo*. You will hear *-ko* far more than the others.
- **Other tenses.** "I was here", "they will be there" use the verb *kuwa* with these endings, or *kuwa* alone (Almasi and colleagues: *watakuwako barabarani* or *watakuwa barabarani* "they will be on the street"). That belongs to part 4.

## Watch out

**Do not use *ni* for place.** *\*Mimi ni nyumbani* treats "at home" as an identity. Say *Niko nyumbani* or *Nipo nyumbani*.

**Do not use *a-* for class 1.** *\*Ako darasani* and *\*Mwalimu apo* use the ordinary verb prefix. Before *-po*, *-ko*, *-mo*, class 1 is *yu-*: *Yuko darasani*, *Mwalimu yupo*.

**Keep *yu-* in the negative.** *\*Hako* is not the negative of *yuko*. Put *ha-* in front of the whole prefix: *hayuko*, *hayupo*, *hayumo*.

**Do not add *-na-*.** *\*Ninako*, *\*Wanapo* put the present marker into a word that has no tense slot. The bare form already means "is now": *niko*, *wapo*.

**Do not use these forms to say what someone is.** *\*Yuko mwalimu* for "she is a teacher" mixes the two kinds of "be". Identity is *ni*: *Yeye ni mwalimu.* Location is *-po*, *-ko*, *-mo*: *Yuko shuleni.*

**Do not give place names the *-ni* ending.** *\*Nairobini* is wrong: the name already denotes a place. And *nyumbani* already has its *-ni*: *\*nyumbanini* doubles it.

**Do not use the full pronoun as the subject piece.** *\*Mimi ko nyumbani* drops the prefix. The prefix is required; the pronoun is optional and adds emphasis: *Mimi niko nyumbani* "*I* am at home".

**Do not confuse *huko* "you are not there" with *huko* "there".** The negative of *uko* is *huko* (*hu-* "not you" + *-ko*). Lesson 3.12 introduces a demonstrative *huko* "there (the place mentioned)", spelt the same. Context and position tell them apart: the negative stands where a verb would, the demonstrative where a place word would.

**Do not expect English "there is" here.** *Yuko* means "he or she is (there)", about a known person. "There is a doctor in the house" is a different construction, taught in the next lesson.

**Do not worry about choosing perfectly.** *-po* and *-ko* overlap a great deal, and speakers prefer *-ko* in casual talk. Use *-mo* when "inside" matters, *-po* for "here" and exact spots, *-ko* otherwise.

## Connections

Spanish *ser* and *estar* are the closest parallel: *soy maestro* / *estoy en casa*, *ni mwalimu* / *niko nyumbani*. Japanese makes a similar split between です for identity and いる・ある for existence in a place. Swahili goes one step further: it also tells you what kind of place, a spot, an area or an inside.

## Exercises

1. Say "we're here" and "we're not here".
2. Say "the guests are in town" and "the guests are not in town".
3. Take apart *Hayumo ndani* and translate it.
4. Ask the teacher where the students are, and give the answer "they're in the classroom".
5. Correct *\*Mwalimu ako nyumbani*.
6. Correct *\*Wazazi ni Mombasa*, meant as "the parents are in Mombasa".
7. Say "I'm not far away, I'm close by".
8. Why does "I am at home" use *niko* but "I am a farmer" use *ni*?

## Worked answers

1. Subject "we": *tu-*. Here: *-po*, with *hapa*. *Tupo hapa.* Negative: *ha-* in front: *Hatupo hapa.*
2. *Wageni*, class 2: *wa-*. A town is a general location: *-ko*. "In town" is *mjini*. *Wageni wako mjini.* Negative: *Wageni hawako mjini.*
3. *Ha-*: not. *-yu-*: class 1 subject before a place ending. *-mo*: inside. *Ndani*: inside. "He (or she) isn't inside."
4. *Wanafunzi wako wapi?* The students are class 2, so *wa-* and *-ko*, with *wapi* last. Answer: *Wako darasani.*
5. Class 1 takes *yu-* before *-ko*: *Mwalimu yuko nyumbani.*
6. *Ni* is for identity, not place. Class 2 *wa-* plus *-ko*, and the town name with no ending: *Wazazi wako Mombasa.*
7. "I" in the negative is *si-*: *Siko mbali, niko karibu.*
8. Swahili splits "be" by meaning. *Ni* links a person to what they are. Place needs a subject prefix plus a place ending, because the present of "be" does not appear after a subject prefix (Polomé); the ending *-ko* comes from the place class 17.
