## Before you start

This review teaches nothing new. It checks part 2: *ni* and *si* with people nouns (lesson 2.1), the present verb with its subject prefix, *-na-*, root and final vowel (2.2), the negative present (2.3), yes-no questions and answers (2.4), being somewhere with *-po*, *-ko*, *-mo* (2.5), having and "there is" (2.6), commands (2.7) and greetings (2.8). It also uses the sounds and spelling of part 1, especially vowels meeting (1.6).

Read the passage first and try to understand it before looking at the glosses. Then work through the exercises. Each worked answer names the lesson to return to if you went wrong.

## Exercises

1. Translate the whole passage into natural English without looking at the translation lines.
2. In the passage, find every negative form and say what its positive would be.
3. Why does Amina say *yumo* in line 6 but *wako* in line 8?
4. In line 9, *kula* starts with *ku-* but *keti* does not. Why?
5. Why does Juma answer *Sijambo* and not *Ndiyo*?
6. Say "we are students, we are not teachers".
7. Say "the farmers are not working today; they are at home".
8. Ask "do you (several) understand?" and answer "yes, we understand".
9. Say "the guests have a car, but there is no water inside", choosing between *hakuna* and *hamna*.
10. Tell a group of children to sit down and be quiet.
11. Correct: *\*Mimi ni nyumbani.*
12. Correct: *\*Watoto anakimbia.*
13. Correct: *\*Sinasoma kitabu.*
14. Correct: *\*Hapana ninaelewa* (meant as "I don't understand").
15. Correct: *\*Mwalimu ako darasani.*
16. Correct: *\*Mimi ni njaa.*
17. Correct: *\*Kusoma!* (to one person) and *\*Jibueni!*
18. Greet your teacher's father, a much older man, and give his reply. Then greet two classmates and give their reply.
19. Take apart *Hamwelewi* and *Hamna* and explain why both start with *ham-*.
20. Build "the girl is cooking, but the boy isn't helping, he's sleeping".

## Worked answers

1. "Juma: Anyone home? Amina: Come in! How are you, Juma? Juma: I'm fine. How are things? Amina: Fine, but the old man has a fever. Juma: I'm so sorry! Where is he? Amina: He's inside, sleeping. Juma: And the children? Are they at school? Amina: No, they're not at school today. They're outside playing. Are you hungry? There's food. Sit down, please, eat! Juma: Thank you very much. I'm not hungry, but I'm thirsty. Amina: Do you want tea? Juma: No, I don't drink tea. I just want water. Amina: All right. Children, come here! We've got a visitor!" (Lessons 2.2 to 2.8.)
2. *Hujambo* and *Sijambo* (greetings, lesson 2.8: their "positive" would be *una jambo*, *nina jambo*, "you/I have a problem"). *Hawako* → *wako* (lesson 2.5). *Sina* → *nina* (lesson 2.6). *Sinywi* → *ninakunywa*: the negative present drops *-na-* and the *ku-* of short verbs and ends in *-i* (lesson 2.3).
3. The old man is inside the house: *-mo*, class 18, "inside". The children are somewhere outside, a general location: *-ko*, class 17. Class 1 takes *yu-* before these endings (lesson 2.5).
4. *-la* "eat" is a short verb and keeps *ku-* in the command; *-keti* has two syllables and stands alone (lesson 2.7).
5. *Hujambo* is not a yes-no question about a fact but a greeting built as "you have no matter?". The answer uses the same shape in the first person: *si-jambo* "I have no matter" (lessons 2.8 and 2.3).
6. *Sisi ni wanafunzi, sisi si walimu.* The copula *ni*, negative *si*; plural nouns for a plural subject (lesson 2.1). *Walimu* is the merged plural of *mwalimu* (lesson 1.6).
7. *Wakulima hawafanyi kazi leo; wako nyumbani.* Negative present of *-fanya*: *hawa-*, no *-na-*, final *-i* (lesson 2.3). Location: *wa-* + *-ko* (lesson 2.5).
8. *Mnaelewa?* (or *Je, mnaelewa?*) Answer about "we": *Ndiyo, tunaelewa* (lesson 2.4).
9. *Wageni wana gari, lakini hamna maji ndani.* *Wana* is "they have" (lesson 2.6). *Hamna*, with class 18 *m-*, fits "inside" best; the general *hakuna* is also correct (lesson 2.6).
10. Plural commands: *-keti* ends in *i*, so it adds *-ni*: *ketini*; *-nyamaza* is native: *nyamazeni*. *Ketini, watoto! Nyamazeni!* (lesson 2.7).
11. *Ni* gives identity, not place: *Niko nyumbani* (or *Nipo nyumbani*) (lesson 2.5).
12. *Watoto* is class 2: *Watoto wanakimbia* (lesson 2.2).
13. The negative present has no *-na-* and ends in *-i*: *Sisomi kitabu* (lesson 2.3).
14. *Hapana* is the answer word, not "not": *Sielewi* (lessons 2.3 and 2.4). As an answer: *Hapana, sielewi.*
15. Class 1 takes *yu-* before *-ko*: *Mwalimu yuko darasani* (lesson 2.5).
16. Hunger is had, not been: *Nina njaa* (lesson 2.6).
17. *Soma!*: only short verbs keep *ku-*. *Jibuni!*: loan verbs keep their vowel and add *-ni* (lesson 2.7).
18. To an elder: *Shikamoo!*, answered *Marahaba.* To two classmates: *Hamjambo?*, answered *Hatujambo* (lesson 2.8).
19. *Ha-mw-elew-i*: negative, "you (several)" written *mw* before a vowel, "understand", negative final vowel: "you (all) don't understand" (lesson 2.3). *Ha-m-na*: negative, then *m-*, then *na* "with": "you (all) don't have", or with the class 18 *m-*, "there isn't (inside)" (lesson 2.6). Both start with *ha-* plus the prefix *m-*.
20. *Msichana anapika, lakini mvulana hasaidii, analala.* Class 1 *a-* with *-na-*; negative class 1 *ha-*, no *-na-*, final *-i* after the root *-saidi-* (lessons 2.2 and 2.3).
