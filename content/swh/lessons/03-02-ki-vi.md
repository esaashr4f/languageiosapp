## The idea

Classes 7 and 8 are the easiest pair to start with, because the noun and the verb usually show the same prefix. *Kitabu* "book" is class 7, and a class 7 subject takes *ki-*: *kitabu kinaanguka*. Its plural *vitabu* is class 8, and a class 8 subject takes *vi-*: *vitabu vinaanguka*. Almasi and colleagues call it the easiest class in Swahili as far as singular and plural are concerned.

What kind of nouns live here? Mostly things: objects people make and use (*kisu* "knife", *kikombe* "cup", *kitanda* "bed"), some body parts (*kichwa* "head", *kidole* "finger, toe"), some places (*kijiji* "village", *kisiwa* "island"). Thompson and Schleicher add abstract ideas and languages. All language names are class 7: *Kiswahili*, *Kiingereza*, *Kiarabu*.

The language names show the classes at work on one stem. *-swahili* gives *Mswahili* "a Swahili person" (class 1), *Waswahili* "Swahili people" (class 2) and *Kiswahili* "the Swahili language" (class 7). The same happens with *-ingereza*: *Mwingereza*, *Waingereza*, *Kiingereza*. Change the class, change the meaning.

Before a vowel, the prefixes change shape. Lesson 1.6 explained why: the *i* of *ki-* became a glide before another vowel, and *k* plus that glide fused into *ch*. So *ki-* + *-umba* is *chumba* "room", and *vi-* + *-umba* is *vyumba* "rooms". *Chakula* "food" behaves the same way, with plural *vyakula*; Wiktionary traces it to *cha* "of" plus *kula* "eating". Before *i*, and in a few fixed words like *kiatu* "shoe" and *kioo* "mirror", the prefix stays *ki-*.

The verb prefix does not follow the noun's change. In *chumba kiko wapi?* "where is the room?", the noun has *ch-* because its stem starts with a vowel, but the subject prefix is *ki-* because the next piece of the verb, *-ko*, starts with a consonant. Each prefix adapts to what follows it, not to the noun.

## How it is built

Say "the knives don't cut".

1. "Knife" is *kisu*, class 7. The plural is class 8: *visu*.
2. The class 8 subject prefix is *vi-*.
3. Negative present (lesson 2.3): *ha-* goes in front of the subject prefix, *-na-* drops, and the final vowel becomes *-i*: *ha-vi-kat-i*.
4. *Visu havikati.*

Say "the room has a bed".

1. "Room" is *chumba*, class 7. Subject prefix *ki-*.
2. "Have" is a subject prefix plus *na* (lesson 2.6): *kina*.
3. "Bed" is *kitanda*. It is the thing had, so it takes no agreement here.
4. *Chumba kina kitanda.*

Say "where are the shoes?".

1. "Shoe" is *kiatu*, one of the words that keeps *ki-* before a vowel. Plural *viatu*, class 8.
2. Subject *vi-*, general place *-ko*: *viko*.
3. *wapi* last: *Viatu viko wapi?*

The class 7 and 8 subject prefixes in full: *ki-* and *vi-*; negative *haki-* and *havi-* (Almasi and colleagues, table 10.1; Thompson and Schleicher, chapter 20).

## Where it applies

- **Objects and tools**: *kisu*, *kikombe*, *kijiko*, *kitanda*, *kiti*, *kiatu*, *chombo* "vessel, utensil, tool".
- **Some body parts**: *kichwa* "head", *kidole* "finger, toe". (Many other body parts are in classes 3 and 5: lessons 3.4 and 3.5.)
- **Some places**: *kijiji* "village", *kisiwa* "island", *chumba* "room", *choo* "toilet", *chuo* "college".
- **Languages**, always class 7, always singular: *Kiswahili*, *Kiingereza*, *Kifaransa*, *Kiarabu*, *Kireno* "Portuguese", *Kijerumani* "German".
- **Things with a ch- stem**: *chumba*, *chakula*, *chombo*, *choo*, *chuo*: plurals *vyumba*, *vyakula*, *vyombo*, *vyoo*, *vyuo*.
- **The thing that has or is had.** With *-na*: *Chumba kina kitanda* "the room has a bed". With the place forms: *Kitabu kiko wapi?*
- **The subject left out.** *Kinakata* "it cuts", *Vipo* "they're here": the prefix carries the reference.
- **Small versions of other nouns.** Thompson and Schleicher note that *ki-* can make a noun smaller: *kitoto* "little child" from *mtoto*. That is lesson 5.11.
- **Manner.** The class 8 prefix also makes adverbs such as *vizuri* "well" (lesson 5.7).
- **Swahili Wikipedia's *Kiswahili ilitokana*.** You will sometimes see a language name with class 9 agreement, apparently agreeing with an understood *lugha* "language". The standard agreement for *Kiswahili* is class 7.

## Watch out

**Do not change the verb prefix to *ch-*.** *\*Chumba chiko wapi?* copies the noun. The subject prefix is *ki-* before a consonant: *Chumba kiko wapi?*

**Do not make *ch-* plurals with *vi-* plus a vowel.** *\*Viumba* and *\*viakula* ignore the vowel meeting. The plural of a *ch-* noun has *vy-*: *vyumba*, *vyakula*.

**Do not invent *ch-* for the fixed exceptions.** *\*Chatu* is not "shoe" (it is "python", Polomé's example). *Kiatu*, *viatu*; *kioo*, *vioo*.

**Do not assume every *ch-* word is class 7.** *Chai* "tea" is class 9: *\*Chai kinanuka* is wrong. The test is the plural: *chumba* has *vyumba*, but *chai* has no *vi-* plural.

**Do not use *si-* for "it does not".** *\*Kisu sikati* says "I don't cut, knife". *Si-* is only "not I". Class 7 takes *ha-* plus *ki-*: *Kisu hakikati.*

**Do not pluralise a language.** *\*Viswahili* is not a word. Languages stay in class 7.

**Do not confuse the language with its speakers.** *\*Yeye ni Kiswahili* calls a person a language. A Swahili person is *Mswahili*: *Yeye ni Mswahili.*

**Do not use class 7 agreement for people in class 7.** A few class 7 nouns name people, such as *kijana* "young person" and *kipofu* "blind person". They take the agreement of classes 1 and 2: *kijana anasoma*, not *\*kijana kinasoma*. Lesson 3.9 explains why.

**Do not treat *kila* as plural.** *\*Kila vitu viko* puts a plural after "every". *Kila* takes the singular: *Kila kitu kipo.*

## Connections

Japanese has nothing like *ki-*, but the language-name pattern has a near parallel: 日本 "Japan", 日本人 "a Japanese person", 日本語 "the Japanese language" change meaning with an added element, as *Mswahili*, *Waswahili*, *Kiswahili* do. The difference is that Swahili does it with prefixes from the class system, which then echo on every verb.

## Exercises

1. Make *kikombe* plural and say "the cups are here".
2. Say "the village has a school" (*shule*).
3. Take apart *Havitoshi* and translate it.
4. Why is "room" *chumba* but "bed" *kitanda*? What are their plurals?
5. Correct *\*Kisu sikati*.
6. Correct *\*Vyumba vyako wapi?*
7. Say "the spoons are getting lost".
8. Answer the question *Kitabu kiko wapi?* with "it's inside", without repeating the noun.
9. Say "the teacher teaches German and Portuguese".

## Worked answers

1. *Kikombe* (7) → *vikombe* (8). Subject *vi-*, here *-po*: *Vikombe vipo hapa.*
2. *Kijiji* is class 7: *ki-* plus *na*. *Kijiji kina shule.* The thing had (*shule*) takes no agreement.
3. *Ha-*: not. *-vi-*: class 8 subject. *-tosh-*: be enough. *-i*: negative final vowel. "They (class 8 things) are not enough", for example about *viazi*: "there aren't enough potatoes".
4. The *ki-* of *chumba* met the vowel of the stem *-umba* and became *ch-*; *kitanda* has a consonant after *ki-*. Plurals: *vyumba*, *vitanda*.
5. *Si-* means "not I". Class 7 takes *ha-* + *ki-*: *Kisu hakikati.*
6. The subject prefix does not copy the noun's *vy-*. Before *-ko*, class 8 is *vi-*: *Vyumba viko wapi?*
7. *Kijiko* → *vijiko*, class 8: *Vijiko vinapotea.*
8. The noun is class 7: *ki-*; inside is *-mo*: *Kimo ndani.*
9. *Mwalimu anafundisha Kijerumani na Kireno.* The languages are objects, so nothing agrees with them; the verb agrees with *mwalimu*, class 1.
