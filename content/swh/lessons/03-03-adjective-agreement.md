## The idea

In lesson 0.2 you saw *kitabu kikubwa kilianguka* "the big book fell", with *ki-* three times. You can now build the first two words yourself. A Swahili adjective is a bare stem, written with a hyphen in front: *-kubwa* "big", *-zuri* "good, nice, beautiful". It cannot be used bare. Before it goes into a sentence it takes the **adjective prefix** of the noun it describes: *kitabu kikubwa* "a big book", *vitabu vikubwa* "big books", *mtoto mkubwa* "a big child", *watoto wakubwa* "big children".

Two things follow. First, the adjective comes **after** the noun, always: "book big". Second, the adjective repeats the class, so you can tell which noun it belongs to even in a long sentence.

Thompson and Schleicher give a rule of thumb: adjectives generally use the same prefixes as the nouns themselves. For the classes you know, that is exactly true. Class 1 nouns have *m-* and so do their adjectives; class 2 *wa-*; class 7 *ki-*; class 8 *vi-*.

Now compare the verb. *Mtoto mkubwa anacheza* "the big child is playing": the noun and adjective have *m-*, the verb has *a-*. So each class has (at least) two agreement prefixes, one for adjectives and one for verbs. In classes 7 and 8 they happen to be identical; in class 1 they are not. The sources used here describe the two sets without explaining their origin, so learn them as two columns of one table. Lesson 3.15 lays the whole table out.

You already know two adjective forms without having been told. *Kidogo* "a little" (lesson 2.7) is the class 7 form of *-dogo* "small", used as an adverb. *Nzuri* "fine!" (lesson 2.8) is the class 9 form of *-zuri*, which lesson 3.6 explains.

## How it is built

**Describing a noun.** Say "the big book is here".

1. "Book": *kitabu*, class 7.
2. "Big": *-kubwa*. Class 7 adjective prefix *ki-*: *kikubwa*. It follows the noun: *kitabu kikubwa*.
3. "Is here": class 7 subject *ki-* plus *-po*: *kipo hapa*.
4. *Kitabu kikubwa kipo hapa.* Three prefixes, one class.

**Saying what something is like.** Swahili uses the copula *ni* from lesson 2.1, and the adjective still agrees. Say "the food is tasty".

1. "Food": *chakula*, class 7.
2. "Tasty, sweet": *-tamu*. Class 7: *kitamu*.
3. Link with *ni*: *Chakula ni kitamu.*
4. Negative: *si* instead of *ni*: *Chakula si kitamu.*

**People and pronouns.** "I", "you" and "we" take the adjective prefixes of the people classes: class 1 for one person, class 2 for several. *Mimi ni mrefu* "I am tall"; *Sisi ni warefu* "we are tall".

**"Very".** *Sana* follows the adjective: *kitamu sana* "very tasty".

**More than one adjective.** Each takes the prefix. Almasi and colleagues' example *vitabu vikubwa vyekundu* "big red books" shows two in a row, both agreeing (*-ekundu* "red" is a vowel stem, lesson 5.1).

## Where it applies

- **Describing a noun**: noun first, adjective after, adjective prefix of the noun's class: *kitanda kikubwa*, *mtoto mdogo*.
- **Saying what something is like**, with *ni* or *si*: *Chakula ni kitamu*, *Kisu si kikali*.
- **People and pronouns**: class 1 for one, class 2 for several, whoever is speaking: *Wewe ni mrefu*, *Ninyi ni warefu*, *Sisi ni wazee*.
- **An adjective standing for a noun** already known: *ninataka kidogo* "I want a small one (cup)". *Wakubwa* alone can mean "the big ones" or, as a noun, "elders, bosses".
- **Adjectives that became nouns**: *mzee* "elder" (class 1 of *-zee*); *mkubwa* "elder, older brother, boss" and *mdogo* "younger brother" (Wiktionary), which you will use with possessives in lesson 3.10.
- **Adjectives that became adverbs**: *kidogo* "a little". Class 8 *vi-* forms are the commonest adverbs: *vizuri* "well" (lesson 5.7).
- **With *sana***: *kizuri sana* "very good".
- **Two or more adjectives**, each agreeing: *vitabu vikubwa vizuri*.
- **Every new class** adds its own adjective prefix: *m-*/*mi-* for classes 3 and 4 (lesson 3.4), none/*ma-* for 5 and 6 (3.5), a nasal for 9 and 10 (3.6), *m-* for 11 and 14 (3.7).
- **In speech and headlines** *ni* is sometimes left out before a predicate adjective (Wikipedia's grammar article; Almasi and colleagues also print *Kuandika kuzuri* "the writing is good" without *ni*, in their table of agreements). Use *ni* yourself.

## Watch out

**Do not put the adjective first.** Spanish *un gran libro* and English "a big book" put it before the noun. *\*Kikubwa kitabu* is wrong. Swahili always says *kitabu kikubwa*.

**Do not use the bare stem.** *\*Kitabu kubwa* and *\*mtoto dogo* leave out the prefix. Classes 7 and 1 need it: *kitabu kikubwa*, *mtoto mdogo*. (Class 5 and class 9 adjectives can look bare, for reasons lessons 3.5 and 3.6 explain.)

**Do not take the prefix from the meaning or the wrong noun.** *\*Kitabu mkubwa* gives a book the people prefix. The adjective copies the noun's class: *kitabu kikubwa*.

**Do not copy the verb prefix onto the adjective.** *\*Mtoto akubwa* uses the verb prefix *a-*. Class 1 adjectives take *m-*: *mtoto mkubwa*, even though the verb says *anacheza*.

**Do not drop the agreement in the predicate.** English "the food is tasty" has a bare adjective. *\*Chakula ni tamu* is wrong for class 7: *Chakula ni kitamu.*

**Do not use "have" or a place verb for a quality.** *\*Kitabu kina kikubwa* and *\*Kitabu kiko kikubwa* mix up "be" with "have" and "be at". Qualities take *ni*: *Kitabu ni kikubwa.*

**Do not negate the adjective with *ha-*.** *\*Kisu hakikali* treats the adjective like a verb. Negate the link: *Kisu si kikali.*

**Do not put *sana* first.** *\*Sana kitamu* follows English word order. *Kitamu sana.*

**Do not add a prefix to an invariable adjective.** Some adjectives, mostly from Arabic, never change: *safi* "clean", *ghali* "expensive" (Thompson and Schleicher give *kisu safi*, *kiti ghali*). *\*Kisafi* and *\*kighali* are wrong. Dictionaries show the difference with the hyphen: *-zuri* agrees, *safi* does not. Lesson 5.1 lists the common ones.

**Do not use *m-* blindly before a vowel.** Adjectives whose stem starts with a vowel, such as *-ema* "good, kind", change their prefix (*mwema*, *wema*). They are taught in lesson 5.1; until then, every adjective in this guide starts with a consonant.

## Connections

Spanish and French make adjectives agree too: *la casa blanca*, *las casas blancas*. Swahili does the same with more categories, and like Spanish it puts the adjective after the noun. Japanese and Korean put adjectives before the noun and never agree; a Swahili adjective is closer to *blanca* than to 白い.

## Exercises

1. Say "a heavy bed" and "heavy beds".
2. Say "the old man is fat".
3. Translate *Vyumba vidogo ni vichafu.*
4. Say "we are not tall, we are short".
5. Correct *\*Kitabu ni kubwa.*
6. Correct *\*Kikali kisu kiko wapi?*
7. Correct *\*Mtoto akubwa anasoma.*
8. Someone offers you a short knife. Say "no, I want a long one".
9. Why is *kidogo* in *Ninasoma kidogo* "I read a little" spelt like the adjective in *kikombe kidogo*?

## Worked answers

1. *Kitanda* is class 7, so *-zito* takes *ki-*: *kitanda kizito*. Plural class 8: *vitanda vizito*.
2. *Mzee* is class 1; the adjective after *ni* takes class 1 *m-*: *Mzee ni mnene.*
3. *Vyumba*: class 8 "rooms". *Vidogo*: class 8 "small". *Ni vichafu*: "are dirty", class 8. "The small rooms are dirty."
4. "We": class 2 adjective prefix *wa-*. Negative link *si*: *Sisi si warefu, sisi ni wafupi.*
5. A class 7 predicate adjective keeps its prefix: *Kitabu ni kikubwa.*
6. The adjective follows the noun: *Kisu kikali kiko wapi?* "Where is the sharp knife?"
7. *A-* is the verb prefix. The class 1 adjective prefix is *m-*: *Mtoto mkubwa anasoma.*
8. *Kisu* is class 7: *Hapana, ninataka kirefu.* The adjective alone stands for "a long one (knife)".
9. It is the same word: the class 7 form of *-dogo* "small", used as an adverb meaning "a little".
