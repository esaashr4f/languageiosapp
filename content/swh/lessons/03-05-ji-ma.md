## The idea

Class 5 is the first class where the noun often has no prefix at all. *Gari* "car", *duka* "shop", *soko* "market", *swali* "question", *yai* "egg": nothing at the front marks them. A smaller group starts with *ji-* or *j-*: *jicho* "eye", *jiwe* "stone", *jina* "name", *jino* "tooth", *jambo* "matter". The plural, class 6, is clear: *ma-*. *Magari*, *maduka*, *masoko*, *maswali*, *mayai*; *macho*, *mawe*, *majina*, *meno*, *mambo*.

Why is the prefix so often missing? Wiktionary's reconstructions show the class 5 prefix of the Bantu ancestor as a vowel, *\*ì-*: *jino* goes back to *\*ìjínò*, *jicho* to *\*ìjícò*. In most Swahili class 5 nouns nothing of that vowel is left. The sources used here do not explain exactly why a *ji-* survives in some short words and not in others, so treat the *ji-* nouns as a list.

Because the noun often shows nothing, the agreement does the work. Class 5 takes *li-* on the verb: *gari linafika* "the car is arriving". Class 6 takes *ya-*: *magari yanafika*. The school name of the pair, LI-YA, comes from these.

The adjective follows the noun prefix, as Thompson and Schleicher's rule of thumb predicts. A class 5 noun usually has no prefix, and neither does its adjective: *gari zuri* "a nice car", *jina refu* "a long name". Class 6 adds *ma-*: *magari mazuri*, *majina marefu*. Even *ji-* nouns take the bare adjective: *jiwe zito* "a heavy stone", not *\*jiwe jizito*.

Class 6 has a second job. Liquids and masses live there with no singular at all: *maji* "water", *maziwa* "milk", *mafuta* "oil, fat, fuel", *maisha* "life", *matata* "trouble". They are grammatically plural, so they take *ya-*: *maji yanatosha* "there's enough water".

## How it is built

**Singular to plural.** Three patterns (Almasi and colleagues, chapter 8):

1. No prefix: add *ma-*. *Gari* → *magari*; *tunda* "fruit" → *matunda*.
2. *Ji-* before a consonant stem: replace it with *ma-*. *Jicho* → *macho*; *jiwe* → *mawe*. But some keep *ji* and add *ma-*: *jina* → *majina*, *jibu* "answer" → *majibu*. Learn which.
3. *J-* or *ji-* before a vowel: *ma-* meets the vowel (lesson 1.6). *Jambo* → *mambo*; *jino* → *meno* (*ma-* + *-ino*). *Jiko* "stove, kitchen" has two plurals in Wiktionary: *majiko* and *meko*.

**Agreement.** Say "the rotten egg stinks".

1. *Yai* "egg", class 5, no prefix.
2. "Rotten": *-bovu*. Class 5 adjective: no prefix, *bovu*: *yai bovu*.
3. Class 5 subject *li-*, present: *linanuka*.
4. *Yai bovu linanuka.*

Plural: *mayai* (class 6), adjective *mabovu*, verb *ya-*: *Mayai mabovu yananuka.*

Say "the shop has no bread".

1. *Duka*, class 5: subject *li-*.
2. Negative "have": *ha-* + *li-* + *na*: *halina*.
3. *Duka halina mkate.*

The full set: subject *li-* and *ya-*, negatives *hali-* and *haya-* (Almasi and colleagues, table 10.1); adjectives zero and *ma-* (their table 13.2, and Wiktionary's concord tables for *-zuri*, *-kubwa*, *-zito*, *-refu*, *-bovu*).

## Where it applies

- **Nouns with no prefix**, many of them loans: *gari*, *duka*, *soko*, *darasa*, *dirisha*, *gazeti* "newspaper" (from English *gazette*, Wiktionary), *nanasi* "pineapple" (from Portuguese *ananás*).
- **Short *ji-* nouns**, often body parts and natural objects: *jicho*, *jino*, *jiwe*, *jina*, *jiko*, *jambo*.
- **Fruits**, whose trees are in class 3 (lesson 3.4): *embe* / *mwembe*, *chungwa* "orange" / *mchungwa* "orange tree", *tunda* "fruit".
- **Things that come in pairs or sets**: *jicho*, *sikio* "ear", *bega* "shoulder", *jino*, *jani* "leaf" (Almasi and colleagues list fruits, plant parts and paired body parts as typical).
- **Ideas and results of actions**, many made from verbs with *-o*: *wazo* "thought" (from *-waza*, Wiktionary), *jengo* "building" (from *-jenga* "build"), *sikio* "ear" (from *-sikia* "hear"), *tatizo* "problem".
- **Liquids and masses**, class 6 only: *maji*, *maziwa*, *mafuta*, *maisha*, *matata*.
- **Plural of a few other nouns**: titles and roles such as *bwana* → *mabwana* (lesson 2.8). These are people, so they take class 1 and 2 agreement (lesson 3.9).
- **Big versions of things**: class 5 can also make a noun "big" (Almasi and colleagues call these augmentatives). That is lesson 5.11.
- **The sun**: *jua* is class 5 (Wiktionary also allows class 9).

## Watch out

**Do not look for a prefix on every class 5 noun.** *Gari* is complete. *\*Jigari* is not a word. The plural is *magari*.

**Do not copy *ji-* onto the adjective.** *\*Jicho jikubwa* is wrong. Class 5 consonant-stem adjectives are bare: *jicho kubwa*.

**Do not give the class 5 adjective the class 7 or class 1 prefix.** *\*Gari kizuri* and *\*gari mzuri* borrow from other classes. Class 5: *gari zuri*. (An English speaker sometimes reaches for *ki-* because "car" feels like an object. Class is not decided by meaning.)

**Do not use class 6 agreement for one thing.** *\*Gari yanafika* mixes the singular noun with the plural prefix. One car: *Gari linafika.*

**Do not treat mass nouns as singular.** English "water is enough" and Spanish *el agua basta* are singular. *\*Maji linatosha* is wrong: *maji* is class 6, *Maji yanatosha*. The same for *maziwa*, *mafuta*.

**Do not make the singular of a mass noun by dropping *ma-*.** *\*Ji* "a water" does not exist. Use a container: *kikombe cha maji* "a cup of water" (lesson 3.11).

**Do not merge where the plural keeps *ji*.** *\*Mana* for "names" is wrong: *majina*. And *\*majino* for "teeth" is wrong: *meno*. Each pair is fixed.

**Do not confuse the class 6 prefix *ya-* with other *ya*.** Lesson 3.11 introduces *ya* meaning "of" (*maji ya moto* "hot water", literally "water of fire"). In *yanatosha* it is the subject prefix at the front of a verb.

**Do not trust *ma-* alone.** *Mama* "mother" starts with *ma* but is class 9 with class 1 agreement (lesson 3.9). *Maana* "meaning" is class 9 too (lesson 1.4). The test is a singular without *ma-*: *magari* has *gari*, *mama* has no *\*ma*.

## Connections

Arabic speakers will know the feeling of a plural that is not a suffix: *kitāb*, *kutub*. Swahili's *gari*, *magari* is simpler (one prefix, always *ma-*), but the singular-to-plural step is equally invisible to an English-trained eye that looks only at word endings. The class 6 mass nouns behave like French *les épinards* or English "the scissors": grammatically plural whatever the meaning.

## Exercises

1. Give the plurals of *duka*, *jiwe*, *jina* and *jino*.
2. Say "the pineapple is sweet" and "the pineapples are sweet".
3. Take apart *Hayatoshi* and say what it could be about.
4. Say "the market has fruit, but it has no oranges".
5. Correct *\*Gari kikubwa linafika.*
6. Correct *\*Maziwa linatosha.*
7. Translate *Mawazo mazuri yanafika polepole.*
8. Correct *\*Jicho jidogo linauma.*
9. *Gari zuri liko wapi?* has two words with no prefix. How does a listener know they are class 5?

## Worked answers

1. *Maduka* (add *ma-*); *mawe* (*ji-* replaced); *majina* (*ji* kept, *ma-* added); *meno* (*ma-* + *-ino* merged).
2. *Nanasi* is class 5: bare adjective. *Nanasi ni tamu.* Class 6: *Mananasi ni matamu.*
3. *Ha-*: not. *-ya-*: class 6 subject. *-tosh-*: be enough. *-i*: negative final vowel. "They are not enough", about a class 6 noun: *maji*, *maziwa*, *mayai*...
4. *Soko* is class 5: *lina*, *halina*. *Soko lina matunda, lakini halina machungwa.*
5. *Gari* is class 5; its adjective is bare: *Gari kubwa linafika.*
6. *Maziwa* is class 6: *Maziwa yanatosha.*
7. *Mawazo*: class 6 "thoughts". *Mazuri*: class 6 "good". *Yanafika*: class 6 "are arriving". *Polepole*: slowly. "Good ideas come slowly."
8. Adjectives do not copy *ji-*. Class 5 *-dogo* is *dogo*: *Jicho dogo linauma.*
9. The verb: *li-* is the class 5 subject prefix. The bare adjective *zuri* agrees: class 1, 3 and 7 would say *mzuri* or *kizuri*, and class 9 *nzuri* (lesson 3.6).
