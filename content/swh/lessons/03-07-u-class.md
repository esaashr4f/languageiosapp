## The idea

Two classes share the prefix *u-*. **Class 11** holds countable things, often long, thin or flat ones: *ukuta* "wall", *ulimi* "tongue", *uzi* "thread", *ubao* "plank". **Class 14** holds abstract nouns: *uhuru* "freedom", *utoto* "childhood", *uzuri* "beauty". Thompson and Schleicher give the test: a class 14 noun has no plural.

In the modern language the two are almost one class. Their prefixes are the same everywhere: *u-* on the noun, *u-* on the verb, *m-* on the adjective. Wiktionary does not even separate them (it labels both "class XI"). What still divides them is number. A class 11 noun has a plural, and that plural lives in class 10, the nasal class of lesson 3.6: *ukuta* "wall", *kuta* "walls". Class 14 has none.

Why class 10? Swahili has no class 12 or 13 to pair with 11, and the plural of class 11 is class 10. The sources used here state the pairing but do not trace its history, so take it as a fact of the system.

This explains the odd-looking plurals. To make the plural, take away *u-* and put the class 10 nasal N in its place, with all the sound rules of lesson 3.6:

- Before a voiceless sound, N disappears: *ukuta* → *kuta*, *ufunguo* "key" → *funguo*, *upepo* "wind" → *pepo*.
- Before *b*, N becomes *m*: *ubao* → *mbao*.
- Before *l* or *r*, N + *l* becomes *nd*: *ulimi* → *ndimi*. Polomé gives this exact pair.
- Before *ny*, nothing is added: *unywele* "a hair" → *nywele* "hair".
- If the stem is a single syllable, Almasi and colleagues add *nyu-*: *uso* "face" → *nyuso*, *uzi* → *nyuzi*.
- If the noun starts with *w-* before a vowel, *ny-* replaces it: *wimbo* "song" → *nyimbo*, *wakati* "time" → *nyakati*.

The adjectives follow the same path. In the singular they take *m-*, for both classes: *ukuta mrefu* "a high wall", *uhuru mzuri*. In the plural they take the class 10 form you already know: *kuta ndefu* "high walls". The verb has *u-* in the singular and class 10 *zi-* in the plural: *ukuta unaanguka*, *kuta zinaanguka*.

## How it is built

Say "the long walls are falling".

1. *Ukuta*, class 11. Plural: drop *u-*, N before *k* disappears: *kuta*, class 10.
2. *-refu* in class 10: *ndefu*.
3. Class 10 subject *zi-*: *zinaanguka*.
4. *Kuta ndefu zinaanguka.*

Say "the tongue hurts".

1. *Ulimi*, class 11: subject *u-*.
2. *Unauma*.
3. *Ulimi unauma.* (Plural: *ndimi zinauma*.)

**Making abstract nouns.** Class 14 is productive: put *u-* on an adjective stem or a noun stem and you name the quality or state.

1. *-zuri* "beautiful" → *uzuri* "beauty". *-kubwa* "big" → *ukubwa* "size". *-refu* "long, tall" → *urefu* "length, height". *-zee* "old" → *uzee* "old age".
2. *Mtoto* "child" → *utoto* "childhood". *Kweli* "truth" → *ukweli* "truthfulness, the truth" (Wiktionary gives these derivations).
3. From verbs, with final *-o*: *-penda* "love" → *upendo* "love".

Lesson 5.11 takes word-building further.

The prefixes for both classes: subject *u-*, negative *hau-*; adjective *m-*; plural (class 10) subject *zi-*, negative *hazi-*, adjective N (Almasi and colleagues, tables 10.1 and 13.2; Thompson and Schleicher pp. 116 and 153).

## Where it applies

- **Class 11, long or flat things**: *ukuta*, *ulimi*, *uzi*, *ubao*, *unywele*; and others such as *ufunguo*, *uso*, *upepo*, *wimbo*, *wakati*, *usiku*.
- **Class 14, qualities from adjectives**: *uzuri*, *ukubwa*, *urefu*, *uzee*, *uvivu* "laziness" (from *-vivu* "lazy").
- **States and periods of life from nouns**: *utoto* "childhood", *ujana* "youth", *uzee* "old age".
- **Ideas and institutions**: *uhuru* "freedom, independence", *umoja* "unity" (lesson 3.1), *ukweli* "truth".
- **Masses**: *ugali* "stiff maize porridge", *umeme* "electricity, lightning", *udongo* "soil", *wali* "cooked rice" (lesson 1.1).
- **Names of countries and regions**, *u-* + a people's stem: *Uingereza* "England, Britain", *Ulaya* "Europe", *Unguja* "Zanzibar island" (Almasi and colleagues). Remember that country names agree in class 9 (lesson 3.6).
- **The same stem across classes**: *Mwingereza* (1), *Waingereza* (2), *Kiingereza* (7), *Uingereza* (11/14): person, people, language, country.
- **Plurals in class 6.** A few *u-* nouns form plurals with *ma-* instead (Almasi and colleagues; Wiktionary): *upendo* → *mapendo* "loves", *urefu* → *marefu*.

## Watch out

**Do not make class 11 plurals with *ma-* or *mi-* by default.** *\*Makuta*, *\*mikuta* are wrong. The normal plural drops *u-* into class 10: *kuta*. Only a handful use *ma-*.

**Do not keep *u-* in the plural.** *\*Ukuta zinaanguka* has a singular noun and a plural verb. Change both: *kuta zinaanguka*.

**Do not forget the nasal changes.** *\*Limi* for "tongues" is wrong: N + *l* → *nd*, *ndimi*. *\*So* for "faces" is wrong: *nyuso*.

**Do not use *u-* on the adjective.** The noun has *u-* and so does the verb, so *\*ukuta urefu* feels natural. The adjective prefix for classes 11 and 14 is *m-*: *ukuta mrefu*, *uhuru mzuri*.

**Do not give the plural adjective *m-*.** *\*Kuta mrefu* keeps the singular adjective. Class 10: *kuta ndefu*.

**Do not pluralise abstract nouns.** English "freedoms", Spanish *libertades* exist; Swahili *\*uhuru zi-* does not. Class 14 stays singular.

**Do not confuse the class 11 subject *u-* with "you".** *Unauma* can be "you bite" or "it (the tongue) hurts". *Hauumi* "it doesn't hurt" keeps *u-* after *ha-*; "you don't bite" is *huumi*.

**Do not confuse *uzee* "old age" with *mzee* "elder".** The class decides: class 1 is the person, class 14 the state.

**Do not give countries in *U-* the *u-* agreement.** *Uingereza* looks like class 11, but agrees in class 9: *Uingereza iko mbali*, not *\*Uingereza uko mbali*.

## Connections

Class 14 does the job of English *-ness* and *-hood*, Spanish *-dad* and French *-té*: *uzuri* "beauty" from *-zuri*, like *bondad* from *bueno*. Korean and Japanese do it with a borrowed Chinese suffix or a nominaliser (さ in 美しさ, 長さ "length" is the exact counterpart of *urefu*). Swahili uses a class prefix, so the new noun comes with its own agreement.

## Exercises

1. Give the plurals of *ubao*, *uzi* and *ufunguo*.
2. Say "the walls are dirty".
3. Take apart *Hazitoshi* in *Funguo hazitoshi* and translate.
4. Make class 14 nouns from *-refu*, *-zee* and *mtoto*, and give their meanings.
5. Correct *\*Ukuta urefu unaanguka.*
6. Correct *\*Ndimi unauma.*
7. Translate *Nywele ni ndefu.*
8. Say "the wind is not howling today".
9. Why does *wimbo* have the plural *nyimbo* but *mwembe* the plural *miembe*?

## Worked answers

1. *Mbao* (N before *b* → *m*); *nyuzi* (one-syllable stem: *nyu-*); *funguo* (N disappears before *f*).
2. *Ukuta* → *kuta*, class 10. *-chafu* has no nasal: *Kuta ni chafu.*
3. *Ha-*: not. *-zi-*: class 10 subject. *-tosh-*: be enough. *-i*: negative. "There aren't enough keys."
4. *Urefu* "length, height"; *uzee* "old age"; *utoto* "childhood".
5. The class 11 adjective prefix is *m-*: *Ukuta mrefu unaanguka.*
6. *Ndimi* is class 10, so the verb takes *zi-*: *Ndimi zinauma.* For one tongue: *Ulimi unauma.*
7. *Nywele*: hair (class 10, plural of *unywele*). *Ndefu*: class 10 "long". "(Her) hair is long."
8. *Upepo* is class 11: negative *hau-*, no *-na-*, final *-i*: *Upepo hauvumi leo.*
9. *Wimbo* is class 11, whose plural is class 10: *w-* is replaced by *ny-*. *Mwembe* is class 3, whose plural is class 4 with *mi-*.
