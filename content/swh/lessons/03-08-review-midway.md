## Before you start

This review teaches nothing new. It checks the first half of part 3: what a class is (lesson 3.1), classes 7 and 8 (3.2), adjective agreement (3.3), classes 3 and 4 (3.4), 5 and 6 (3.5), 9 and 10 (3.6), and 11 and 14 (3.7). It also uses part 2: *ni* and *si*, the present with *-na-*, the negative present, *-po*, *-ko*, *-mo*, *kuna* and "have", and commands.

For every noun, ask three questions: which class, which adjective prefix, which subject prefix. Read the passage once without the glosses, then with them, then do the exercises. Every worked answer names the lesson to go back to.

## Exercises

1. Translate the whole passage into natural English without looking at the translation lines.
2. List every noun in the passage with its class number.
3. In line 1, *kidogo* has a prefix but *kubwa* has none. Explain.
4. In line 6, what does *halina* refer to, and how do you know?
5. Make line 3 singular: "the mango is sweet, but the orange isn't sweet today".
6. Make these plural: *kisu kikali*, *mti mrefu*, *jicho dogo*, *nyumba nzuri*, *ukuta mchafu*.
7. Make these singular: *miguu mizito*, *maswali magumu*, *vyumba vikubwa*, *funguo ndogo*, *nyimbo ndefu*.
8. Say "the bread isn't enough" and "the books aren't enough".
9. Say "the river is long, but the mountains are not high".
10. Say "the shop has no salt, but it has sugar".
11. Say "where is the key? It's here."
12. Correct: *\*Gari kikubwa linafika.*
13. Correct: *\*Mti anaanguka.*
14. Correct: *\*Meza refu iko hapa.*
15. Correct: *\*Maji linatosha.*
16. Correct: *\*Nyumba kubwa iko wapi?* (meant: "where are the big houses?")
17. Correct: *\*Kitabu ni kubwa.*
18. Correct: *\*Ukuta urefu unaanguka.*
19. Take apart *Haziwaki* and say what it could be about.
20. Why are *mtu*, *mti* and *mvua* not in the same class, though all three start with *m*?

## Worked answers

1. "The small village has a big market. The market has fruit, vegetables and fish. The mangoes are sweet, but the oranges aren't sweet today. The visitors are buying pineapples and bananas. An old woman is looking for a sharp knife, but there are no knives. There's a tall building nearby, but it has no door. The wind is howling, and the rain is starting. The children are running, cups are falling, trees are falling! The teacher says: Children, stand still! Be quiet! Now the wind has gone, and everything is fine again." (Lessons 3.1 to 3.7.)
2. *Kijiji* 7, *soko* 5, *matunda* 6, *mboga* 10, *samaki* 10, *maembe* 6, *machungwa* 6, *wageni* 2, *mananasi* 6, *ndizi* 10, *mwanamke* 1, *kisu* 7, *visu* 8, *jengo* 5, *mlango* 3, *upepo* 11, *mvua* 9, *watoto* 2, *vikombe* 8, *miti* 4, *mwalimu* 1, *kitu* 7. (*Mboga*, *samaki*, *ndizi* could be 9 in form; the plural meaning makes them 10.) Lessons 3.2 to 3.7.
3. *Kidogo* agrees with *kijiji* (class 7, *ki-*). *Kubwa* agrees with *soko* (class 5), whose consonant-stem adjectives have no prefix (lessons 3.3 and 3.5).
4. *Jengo*, the class 5 noun just mentioned. *Li-* is the class 5 subject prefix; *ha-* makes it negative (lesson 3.5).
5. *Embe ni tamu, lakini chungwa si tamu leo.* Both are class 5; class 5 adjectives are bare (lesson 3.5).
6. *Visu vikali* (3.2, 3.3); *miti mirefu* (3.4); *macho madogo* (3.5); *nyumba nzuri*, unchanged, with *zi-* on any verb (3.6); *kuta chafu*, class 10, no nasal before *ch* (3.7).
7. *Mguu mzito* (3.4); *swali gumu* (3.5); *chumba kikubwa* (3.2); *ufunguo mdogo* (3.7: the singular is class 11 with adjective *m-*); *wimbo mrefu* (3.7).
8. *Mkate hautoshi* (class 3, *hau-*: lesson 3.4). *Vitabu havitoshi* (class 8, *havi-*: lesson 3.2). Negative present: no *-na-*, final *-i* (lesson 2.3).
9. *Mto ni mrefu, lakini milima si mirefu.* Class 3 *m-*, class 4 *mi-* (lesson 3.4).
10. *Duka halina chumvi, lakini lina sukari.* Class 5 *li-*, negative *hali-* (lesson 3.5).
11. *Ufunguo uko wapi? Upo hapa.* Class 11 *u-* (lesson 3.7); *-po* with *hapa* (lesson 2.5).
12. *Gari* is class 5; its adjective is bare: *Gari kubwa linafika* (lesson 3.5).
13. *Mti* is class 3: *Mti unaanguka* (lessons 3.1 and 3.4).
14. Class 9 adjectives in *r* become *nd*: *Meza ndefu iko hapa* (lesson 3.6).
15. *Maji* is class 6: *Maji yanatosha* (lesson 3.5).
16. Plural class 10: *Nyumba kubwa ziko wapi?* (lesson 3.6).
17. The predicate adjective agrees: *Kitabu ni kikubwa* (lesson 3.3).
18. Class 11 adjectives take *m-*: *Ukuta mrefu unaanguka* (lesson 3.7).
19. *Ha-*: not. *-zi-*: class 10 subject. *-wak-*: burn. *-i*: negative final vowel. "They aren't burning (lit)", for example about lamps, *taa* (lessons 3.6 and 2.3).
20. The class is fixed by agreement, not by the first letter. *Mtu* takes *a-* (class 1, plural *watu*); *mti* takes *u-* (class 3, plural *miti*); *mvua* takes class 9 agreement, with *i-* on the verb and the class 9 adjective (*mvua kubwa*), and has no plural (lessons 3.1, 3.4, 3.6).
