## The idea

So far the noun's class has decided everything. This lesson adds one rule that overrides it: **a noun that names a person or an animal takes the agreement of classes 1 and 2, whatever class its own form belongs to.** Grammars call this **animate agreement**; "animate" means alive and able to move of its own accord, as people and animals are.

*Paka* "cat" is a class 9/10 noun in form: no prefix, same in the plural. A class 9 thing would take *i-* (*meza iko hapa*). But a cat takes *a-*, like a person: *paka analala* "the cat is sleeping". Several cats take *wa-*: *paka wanalala*. Its adjective takes *m-* and *wa-*: *paka mdogo* "a small cat", *paka wadogo* "small cats". Thompson and Schleicher give *daktari mzuri* "a good doctor" and *mbwa wakali* "fierce dogs"; Almasi and colleagues give *Simba hajalala* "the lion hasn't slept yet", with the class 1 negative *ha-*.

The noun itself keeps its own shape and its own plural. *Kifaru* "rhinoceros" is class 7 in form, so the plural is *vifaru*, not *\*wafaru*. *Daktari* "doctor" is class 5 in form, so the plural is *madaktari*. Only what agrees with the noun switches to classes 1 and 2: *vifaru wanakimbia* "the rhinos are running", *madaktari wanafika* "the doctors are arriving".

Why does meaning win over form here? The sources describe it as a rule of the modern language without tracing when it arose; Wikipedia's grammar article describes it as a split between the noun's own (morphological) class and the class it agrees in (its syntactic class). But you can watch the pull of animacy in a single word. Wiktionary explains *mnyama* "animal" (class 1, plural *wanyama*) as a reassignment of *nyama* "meat" (class 9) to an animate class. The same stem names a thing in class 9 and a living creature in class 1.

Some nouns name both a creature and an object, and the agreement tells them apart. Wiktionary lists *kiboko* as "hippopotamus" and also "whip" (from hippo hide); *kipepeo* is "butterfly" and also "fan". As animals they take *a-*; as objects they take class 7 *ki-*. *Ndege* is the clearest case: as "bird" it takes class 1 and 2 agreement, as "aeroplane" class 9 and 10 (Wikipedia's grammar article): *ndege mkubwa* "a big bird", *ndege kubwa* "a big plane".

## How it is built

Say "the fierce dogs are barking".

1. "Dog": *mbwa*, a class 9/10 noun in form. Plural: *mbwa*, unchanged.
2. A dog is an animal, so agreement is class 2 for the plural.
3. "Fierce": *-kali*. Class 2 adjective: *wakali*. *Mbwa wakali*.
4. Class 2 subject *wa-*: *wanabweka*.
5. *Mbwa wakali wanabweka.*

Say "the young people are not here".

1. "Young person": *kijana*, class 7 in form; plural *vijana*, class 8.
2. People: agreement class 2.
3. Negative class 2 with *-po*: *hawapo*.
4. *Vijana hawapo.*

Say "where is the cat?".

1. *Paka*, an animal: class 1 agreement.
2. Class 1 before a place ending is *yu-* (lesson 2.5): *yuko*.
3. *Paka yuko wapi?*

## Where it applies

- **Animals in class 9/10**, the commonest case: *paka*, *mbwa*, *simba* "lion", *twiga* "giraffe", *kuku* "chicken", *farasi* "horse", *nyani* "baboon", *mamba* "crocodile", *mbuzi*, *ng'ombe*, *punda*, *tembo*, *samaki*, *nyoka*.
- **Insects and small creatures**: *nyuki* "bee", *nzige* "locust", *kipepeo* "butterfly"; and the class 1/2 general words *mnyama* "animal", *mdudu* "insect" (Almasi and colleagues).
- **People in class 9/10**: kin terms and *rafiki*: *baba*, *mama*, *dada*, *kaka*, *ndugu* "sibling, relative", *askari* "soldier, guard", *polisi* "police officer".
- **People and animals in class 7/8**: *kijana* "young person", *kipofu* "blind person", *kiongozi* "leader", *kifaru*, *kiboko* "hippo".
- **People in class 5/6**: *daktari*, *rais* "president" (plural *marais*), *bwana*, *bibi*.
- **Every kind of agreement you know**: subject prefixes (*paka analala*), negatives (*hawapo*), place forms (*yuko*), adjectives (*mbwa mkali*), predicate adjectives (*rafiki ni mrefu*). Later, object prefixes (lesson 4.8) and demonstratives (lesson 3.12) follow the same rule.
- **Dictionaries.** Wiktionary sometimes labels such nouns by their agreement ("*kipofu* class I, plural *vipofu* class II") and sometimes by form ("*kijana* class VII"). Both mean the same thing.

## Watch out

**Do not give animals the agreement of their form.** *\*Paka inalala* treats the cat as a thing. *Paka analala.*

**Do not use the class 9 adjective for an animal.** *\*Mbwa kali* is the class 9 form. *Mbwa mkali*; plural *mbwa wakali*.

**Do not use *zi-* for several animals.** *\*Mbwa zinabweka* is the thing plural. *Mbwa wanabweka.*

**Do not change the noun's own plural.** Animacy affects only what agrees. *\*Wafaru* and *\*wadaktari* are wrong: *vifaru*, *madaktari*.

**Do not treat meat, or a machine, as an animal.** *Nyama* "meat" is a thing: *nyama inaoza* "the meat is going off", not *\*nyama anaoza*. *Ndege* as a plane is a thing: *ndege kubwa inaruka*.

**Do not use class 7 agreement for a class 7 person.** *\*Kijana kinasoma* gives a person the agreement of an object. *Kijana anasoma.*

**But do not "correct" diminutives.** When a noun is made small with *ki-* (*kitoto* "little child", from *mtoto*), Almasi and colleagues report that it takes class 7 agreement even for a person or animal, often with a fond or scornful tone: *kitoto kizuri*. Lesson 5.11 covers these.

**Do not assume the rule reaches every agreeing word.** Thompson and Schleicher say animate nouns take class 1 agreement "in most grammatical contexts". The main exception is the possessive with kin terms and *rafiki*: "my friend" is *rafiki yangu*, with class 9 *y-*. Lesson 3.10 explains it.

**Do not be surprised by group nouns.** Thompson and Schleicher note that *familia* "family" can agree as a class 9 thing or as a group of people in class 2. Both are correct.

## Connections

Spanish has a small version of this: a noun's gender can be overridden by the sex of the person meant, as in *el guía* and *la guía*. Swahili's rule is broader and simpler: anything alive takes the people classes, whatever the noun looks like. Japanese splits existence the same way, いる for people and animals and ある for things, so a Japanese speaker already sorts the world along this line.

## Exercises

1. Say "the lion is sleeping" and "the lions are sleeping".
2. Say "a small chicken" and "small chickens".
3. Take apart *Hawapo* in *Askari hawapo* and translate.
4. Correct *\*Twiga ndefu inakimbia.*
5. Correct *\*Wafaru wanakimbia.*
6. Translate *Kiongozi mpya anafika* (*-pya* "new").
7. *Kiboko kiko hapa* and *Kiboko yuko hapa*: what is each about?
8. Say "the bees are flying".
9. Why is "the meat is rotten" *nyama ni mbovu* but "the animal is fierce" *mnyama ni mkali*?
10. Say "the president is tall, but the leaders are short".

## Worked answers

1. *Simba* is an animal: class 1 *a-*, class 2 *wa-*. *Simba analala. Simba wanalala.*
2. *Kuku mdogo* and *kuku wadogo*: the noun stays the same; the adjective takes class 1 *m-* and class 2 *wa-*.
3. *Ha-*: not. *-wa-*: class 2 subject. *-po*: here. "The guards aren't here." *Askari* is a class 9/10 noun for people, so class 2 agreement.
4. A giraffe is an animal: adjective *mrefu*, verb *a-*. *Twiga mrefu anakimbia.*
5. The noun keeps its own plural: *Vifaru wanakimbia.*
6. "A new leader is arriving." *Kiongozi* is a person in class 7, so the adjective is class 1 (*mpya*) and the verb *a-*.
7. *Kiboko kiko hapa*, with class 7 *ki-*: "the whip is here". *Kiboko yuko hapa*, with class 1 *yu-*: "the hippo is here".
8. *Nyuki wanaruka.* Class 2 for several insects.
9. *Nyama* is a thing, class 9: class 9 *-bovu* is *mbovu*. *Mnyama* is an animal, class 1: *mkali*.
10. *Rais ni mrefu, lakini viongozi ni wafupi.* Both are people: class 1 *m-* and class 2 *wa-*, though *rais* is class 5 and *viongozi* class 8 in form.
