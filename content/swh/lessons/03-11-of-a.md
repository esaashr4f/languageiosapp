## The idea

English links two nouns with "of" or *'s*: "the teacher's book", "a cup of tea". Spanish and French use one invariable word, *de*. Swahili uses a small word built like a possessive: an agreement prefix plus *-a*. Grammars call it the **-a of association** (Polomé calls it the connective particle). *Kitabu cha mwalimu* "the teacher's book": *ch-* agrees with *kitabu*, *-a* links it to *mwalimu*.

The order is the reverse of English *'s*. The thing comes first, then *-a*, then the owner or description: "book of-teacher". This is the same order as the possessive of lesson 3.10, and the prefix is the same too. Almasi and colleagues build both from one table: *ch-* + *-angu* is *changu* "my", *ch-* + *-a* is *cha* "of". So you already know every form: *cha*, *vya*, *wa*, *ya*, *la*, *za*.

The prefix agrees with the **first** noun, the one being described. *Kitabu cha mtoto* "the child's book", *vitabu vya mtoto* "the child's books", *mtoto wa mwalimu* "the teacher's child". What follows *-a* has no influence.

The *-a* phrase does far more than show ownership. Polomé divides its uses into two kinds. It can show **possession**, as above. Or it can **characterise** the first noun, like an adjective: *maji ya baridi* "cold water" (literally "water of cold"), *kabati la mti* "a wooden cupboard" ("cupboard of wood"), *chumba cha kulala* "bedroom" ("room of sleeping"). Almasi and colleagues count such phrases among the adjectives and call them compound adjectives; lesson 5.2 shows how much describing they do.

In speech the little word leans on what follows. Polomé notes that the *-a* of association is unstressed and is pronounced with the next word: *kitabu cha-mwalimu*. Spelling keeps it separate.

## How it is built

Say "the teacher's books are here".

1. The first noun, the thing: *vitabu*, class 8.
2. Class 8 prefix *vy-* + *-a*: *vya*.
3. The owner after it: *mwalimu*. *Vitabu vya mwalimu*.
4. The verb agrees with *vitabu*: *viko hapa*.
5. *Vitabu vya mwalimu viko hapa.*

Say "a cup of tea".

1. First noun *kikombe*, class 7: *cha*.
2. *Kikombe cha chai.* The *-a* phrase tells you what is in the cup.

Say "the bedroom is small".

1. "Bedroom" is a room for sleeping: *chumba*, class 7, + *cha* + the infinitive *kulala*: *chumba cha kulala*. Wiktionary lists it as a set compound.
2. The adjective agrees with *chumba*: *kidogo*.
3. *Chumba cha kulala ni kidogo.*

**Plurals of compounds.** Only the first noun and the *-a* change: *chumba cha kulala* → *vyumba vya kulala* (Wiktionary). The second noun stays as it is.

**Places.** With a place in *-ni*, the *-a* takes a place prefix: *nyumbani kwa Juma* "at Juma's house". Lesson 3.13 explains *kwa*, *pa* and *mwa*.

## Where it applies

- **Possession**: *kitabu cha mwalimu*, *jina la mtoto* "the child's name", *nyumba ya Juma*.
- **Contents and material**: *kikombe cha chai*, *kabati la mti* "a wooden cupboard", *kiti cha chuma* "an iron chair".
- **Description in place of an adjective**: *maji ya moto* "hot water", *maji ya baridi* "cold water", *mahali pa hatari* "a dangerous place" (Almasi and colleagues).
- **Purpose, with an infinitive**: *chumba cha kulala* "bedroom", *maji ya kunywa* "drinking water". Wiktionary even explains *chakula* "food" as *cha* + *kula* "of eating", fused into one noun.
- **Subject, field or language**: *mwalimu wa Kiswahili*, *gazeti la Kiingereza* "an English newspaper".
- **Origin**: *watu wa Mombasa*, *mwenyeji wa mji* "a native of the town" (Polomé).
- **Time**: *gazeti la leo* "today's paper", *mtu wa juzi* "a newcomer" (Polomé: "a person of the day before yesterday").
- **Fixed compounds and names**: *mpira wa miguu* "football", *Bahari ya Hindi* "the Indian Ocean", *Kiswahili cha Kenya* "Kenyan Swahili".
- **Order numbers**: *kitabu cha tatu* "the third book" (Polomé). Lesson 5.3.
- **With places**: *nyumbani kwa Juma*, and *kwetu* "our place, home" (lesson 3.13).
- **Prepositions built on *ya***: *juu ya* "on top of", *kabla ya* "before". Polomé notes that this *ya* no longer agrees with anything. Lesson 5.8.

## Watch out

**Do not make the prefix agree with the second noun.** *\*Kitabu wa mwalimu* takes *w-* from *mwalimu*. The prefix comes from *kitabu*: *kitabu cha mwalimu.*

**Do not use one form for all.** Spanish *de* and French *de* never change, and *ya* can feel like a general "of". *\*Kitabu ya mtoto* is wrong in the standard: *kitabu cha mtoto*.

**Do not copy English *'s* order.** *\*Mwalimu cha kitabu* means nothing sensible. The thing first: *kitabu cha mwalimu*.

**Do not leave out the *-a* word.** *\*Kikombe chai* runs the nouns together as English "teacup" does. Swahili needs the link: *kikombe cha chai*.

**Do not use *-a* with a pronoun.** *\*Kitabu cha mimi* for "my book" is wrong. Pronouns have possessive forms: *kitabu changu* (lesson 3.10).

**Do not change the second noun in a plural compound.** *Vyumba vya kulala* is right; *\*vyumba vya vilala* is not a word. As the name of the game, *mpira wa miguu* has no plural (Wiktionary); several footballs, the balls themselves, would be *mipira ya miguu*, with *ya* because *mipira* is class 4.

**Do not let the verb agree with the second noun.** *\*Vitabu vya mtoto anasoma* is not "the child's books are read". The subject is *vitabu*: *Vitabu vya mtoto viko hapa.*

**Do not confuse the *-a* words with subject prefixes.** *Wa* "of" (classes 1, 2, 3, 11, 14) stands alone before a noun; class 2 *wa-* starts a verb. *Ya* "of" (classes 4, 6, 9) is a separate word; class 6 *ya-* starts a verb. *Maji ya moto yako wapi?* has both.

**Do not use an adjective form after *-a*.** *\*Maji ya kali* is wrong: after *-a* comes a noun, an infinitive or an adverb, not a bare adjective stem. Adjectives agree directly: *maji safi* "clean water", *chai tamu*.

## Connections

French *une tasse de thé*, Spanish *una taza de té* and Swahili *kikombe cha chai* have the same order: container, link, contents. The difference is that *de* never changes, while *cha* agrees with *kikombe*. Japanese 紅茶のカップ and Korean 차 한 잔 put the parts the other way round. Arabic *kitāb al-muʿallim* "the teacher's book" has the Swahili order with no linking word at all.

## Exercises

1. Say "the child's name" and "the children's names".
2. Say "a cup of water" and "cups of coffee".
3. Take apart *Viti vya chuma viko nje* and translate.
4. Say "the Swahili teachers are arriving".
5. Correct *\*Nyumba wa mwalimu ni kubwa.*
6. Correct *\*Kikombe chai kiko wapi?*
7. Correct *\*Kitabu cha yeye kiko hapa.*
8. Give the plural of *chumba cha kulala* and say why only two words change.
9. Translate *Maji ya baridi yanatosha, lakini maji ya moto hayatoshi.*
10. In *Mtoto wa mwalimu anasoma*, why is the verb *anasoma* and not something agreeing with *mwalimu*?

## Worked answers

1. *Jina la mtoto* (class 5 *la*). *Majina ya watoto* (class 6 *ya*).
2. *Kikombe cha maji.* "Cups of coffee": *vikombe vya kahawa* (class 8 *vya*).
3. *Viti*: chairs (8). *Vya chuma*: of iron. *Viko nje*: are outside. "The iron chairs are outside."
4. *Walimu wa Kiswahili wanafika.* Class 2 *wa*, class 2 *wa-* on the verb.
5. *Nyumba* is class 9: *ya*. *Nyumba ya mwalimu ni kubwa.*
6. The link is needed: *Kikombe cha chai kiko wapi?*
7. Pronouns use possessives, not *-a*: *Kitabu chake kiko hapa.*
8. *Vyumba vya kulala.* The first noun becomes plural (*vyumba*), the *-a* word agrees with it (*vya*), and *kulala*, the infinitive after *-a*, does not agree with anything.
9. "There is enough cold water, but not enough hot water." Both phrases are class 6 because *maji* is class 6: *ya*, *yanatosha*, *hayatoshi*.
10. The subject is *mtoto*, class 1, the first noun. *Wa mwalimu* only describes it.
