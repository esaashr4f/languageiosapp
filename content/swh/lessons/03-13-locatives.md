## The idea

English uses prepositions for place: "in the room", "on the table", "at school". Swahili mostly uses a suffix. Add **-ni** to a noun and it becomes a place: *chumba* "room" → *chumbani* "in the room"; *meza* "table" → *mezani* "on the table"; *shule* → *shuleni* "at school". Almasi and colleagues note that one *-ni* covers English "in", "on" and "at". You have been using such words since lesson 2.5 (*nyumbani*, *darasani*, *sokoni*). This lesson explains them.

A noun with *-ni* is no longer in its old class. It has become a place, and places have their own three classes, which you met inside *-po*, *-ko*, *-mo* and *pana*, *kuna*, *mna*:

- **Class 16** (*pa-*): a definite spot, "at, on". Thompson and Schleicher: places that are specific or close to the speaker.
- **Class 17** (*ku-*): a general area, "around, at, to, from". Places that are general or not close.
- **Class 18** (*mu-*, written *m-* or *mw-*): inside, "in, within".

The same place word can take any of the three, and the agreement says which kind of place you mean. Thompson and Schleicher put it plainly: the three classes look the same on the noun, and other clues in the sentence show the difference. So *chumbani* is "at the room" with *pa-*, "around the room" with *ku-*, "in the room" with *mu-*. *Chumbani mna kitanda* "in the room there is a bed".

Only one ordinary noun belongs to class 16 without *-ni*: *mahali* "place" (Almasi and colleagues; Wiktionary lists the older form *pahali*). It is the one place noun you can describe with an adjective: *mahali pazuri* "a nice place".

Why a suffix and three classes? The sources used here do not trace the history of *-ni*. They do agree on the system: the place classes are ordinary Bantu classes, with numbers like the others, and their prefixes behave like any other agreement prefixes. Swahili treats a place as a noun with its own class, and lets every agreeing word say what kind of place it is.

## How it is built

**Making the place word.** Add *-ni* to the noun. The stress moves to the new second-last syllable (lesson 1.5): *kitábu* → *kitabúni*.

**Agreement with the place.** Everything that refers to the place takes a class 16, 17 or 18 prefix, chosen by meaning. The subject prefixes are *pa-*, *ku-* and *m(u)-*. The possessive prefixes, by the vowel rules of lesson 1.6, are *p-*, *kw-* and *mw-* (*pangu*, *kwangu*, *mwangu* "my"), and the *-a* of association is *pa*, *kwa*, *mwa* (Almasi and colleagues; Wiktionary's table for *-angu*).

Say "my keys are in my bag".

1. *Funguo*, class 10: *zi-*. Inside: *-mo*. *Zimo*.
2. "Bag": *mfuko* → *mfukoni*.
3. "My", inside: class 18 possessive *mw-* + *-angu*: *mwangu*. The possessive agrees with the place, not with *mfuko*.
4. *Funguo zimo mfukoni mwangu.*

Say "at our home there are trees".

1. "Home": *nyumbani*. A general area: class 17.
2. "Our": class 17 *kw-* + *-etu*: *kwetu*. *Nyumbani kwetu*. Thompson and Schleicher note that this, not *\*nyumba yangu*, is the everyday "at my place".
3. "There are": class 17 *kuna*. *Nyumbani kwetu kuna miti.*

Say "on the table there are cups".

1. *Meza* → *mezani*. On a surface: class 16.
2. *Pana vikombe.*
3. *Mezani pana vikombe.*

**With *-a* and demonstratives.** *Nyumbani kwa Juma* "at Juma's"; *huko mjini* "there in town". Thompson and Schleicher note that place demonstratives almost always come before the place noun.

## Where it applies

- **In, on, at**: *chumbani*, *mezani*, *shuleni*. One suffix for all three; the agreement and the context tell you which.
- **To and from, after verbs of motion**: *anarudi nyumbani* "she's going home", *anatoka sokoni* "she's coming from the market". The verb decides the direction.
- **"There is" with a place**: *Chumbani mna kitanda*, *Mezani pana vikombe*, *Sokoni kuna samaki* (lesson 2.6, now with the place named).
- **A place as subject of a verb**: Almasi and colleagues give *mahali pananuka* "the place stinks", and the same with *ku-* and *m-*.
- **Possessives agreeing with the place**: *nyumbani kwangu* "at my home", *mfukoni mwangu* "in my pocket", *kitandani kwako* "on your bed". Polomé: *kwetu* alone means "our place, home", and *hana kwao* "he has no home".
- **"Of" with a place**: *nyumbani kwa Juma* "at Juma's", *nyumbani mwa Juma* "in Juma's house", *mahali pa hatari* "a dangerous place" (Thompson and Schleicher's table).
- **Demonstratives of place**: *hapa*, *huku*, *humu* "here"; *pale*, *kule*, *mle* "there"; *hapo*, *huko*, *humo* "there (mentioned)". Usually before the place noun: *huko mjini*, *humu chumbani*.
- **Adjectives with *mahali***: *mahali pazuri*, *mahali kuzuri*, *mahali mzuri* (Almasi and colleagues' adjective table).
- **Words that are places already** take no *-ni*: town and country names (*Nairobi*, *Tanzania*), *nyumbani* itself, *pwani* "the coast" (Wiktionary: itself a locative), and the adverbs *ndani*, *nje*, *juu* "above", *chini* "below", *mbele* "in front", *nyuma* "behind", *karibu*, *mbali*.

## Watch out

**Do not put an adjective after a *-ni* word.** Polomé notes that locatives in *-ni* can be followed by a possessive, a demonstrative or an *-a* phrase, but not by an adjective. *\*Nyumbani kuzuri* is wrong. Say *mahali pazuri*, or use *katika* "in" with the full phrase (lesson 5.8): Almasi and colleagues give *katika chuo kikuu* "at the university", not *\*chuo kikuuni*.

**Do not keep the old class on agreeing words.** *\*Mfukoni wangu* agrees with *mfuko* (class 3). Once *-ni* is added, the place classes take over: *mfukoni mwangu.*

**Do not add *-ni* to place names.** *\*Nairobini*, *\*Tanzaniani* are wrong (lesson 2.5). *Yuko Nairobi.*

**Do not add a preposition as well.** English "in the room" tempts *\*ndani chumbani*. *Chumbani* already means "in the room". (With *ndani ya*, "inside of", you use the bare noun: *ndani ya chumba*, lesson 5.8.)

**Do not use *-ni* for people.** "At the doctor's", "to my friend's" use *kwa*: Thompson and Schleicher give *ninaenda kwa daktari* "I am going to the doctor's". *\*Daktarini* is wrong. Lesson 5.8.

**Do not assume every *-ni* word is a place.** *Ni* the copula, *-ni* of plural commands (*someni*) and the *-ni* of *jioni* "evening" are different things. A place *-ni* is added to a noun you can find without it.

**Do not worry about choosing exactly.** The three classes overlap in practice, as *-po*, *-ko*, *-mo* did in lesson 2.5. *Ku-* is the safe general choice; use *mu-* when "inside" matters and *pa-* for a definite spot or surface.

**Do not confuse class 18 *m-* with "you (all)".** *Mna* is "there is (inside)" and also "you have" (lesson 2.6); *mwangu* "in my" is not *mwanangu* "my child".

## Connections

Japanese に and で, Korean 에 and 에서 also cover "in", "on" and "at" with one form each, after the noun, as Swahili *-ni* does. The difference is that Swahili then lets the rest of the sentence agree with the place, choosing between spot, area and inside. Spanish *en* is another single word for "in, on, at": *en la mesa*, *mezani*.

## Exercises

1. Make place words from *kitanda*, *sanduku* and *bustani*.
2. Say "the cups are in the box".
3. Say "there are no cars in town today".
4. Take apart *Chumbani mwake mna picha* and translate.
5. Say "at my place there is a garden".
6. Correct *\*Kitabu kiko mezani yangu.*
7. Correct *\*Wanaishi Mombasani.*
8. Correct *\*Mahali hapa ni kuzuri* if the speaker means one definite spot.
9. Say "we're at the doctor's house" using *nyumbani* and *-a*.
10. Why can you say *mahali pazuri* but not *\*nyumbani pazuri*?

## Worked answers

1. *Kitandani* "on/in the bed", *sandukuni* "in the box", *bustanini* "in the garden". Each is the noun + *-ni*, with the stress moved to the new second-last syllable.
2. *Vikombe vimo sandukuni.* The subject is *vikombe* (class 8); inside is *-mo*.
3. *Mjini hakuna magari leo.* A general area: class 17 *hakuna*.
4. *Chumba-ni*: in the room. *Mw-ake*: class 18 "his/her", agreeing with the inside place. *M-na picha*: there is a picture (inside). "There's a picture in his (or her) room."
5. *Nyumbani kwangu kuna bustani.* Class 17 *kwangu* and *kuna*.
6. The possessive agrees with the place, not with *meza*: *Kitabu kiko mezani kwangu.*
7. Place names take no *-ni*: *Wanaishi Mombasa.*
8. For one definite spot, class 16: *Mahali hapa ni pazuri.*
9. *Tuko nyumbani kwa daktari.* *Kwa* agrees with the place *nyumbani* (class 17).
10. *Mahali* is a noun of class 16 and can take an adjective. A *-ni* place cannot be followed by an adjective (Polomé); it can take a possessive, a demonstrative or an *-a* phrase.
