## The idea

Part 3 has given you, class by class, seven kinds of agreeing word: the adjective, the subject prefix and its negative, the possessive, the *-a* of "of", the three demonstratives, and the short reference forms (*nacho*, *ninayo*). Laid out in full that is well over a hundred forms. This lesson puts them in one map and shows that you do not have to learn them one by one. Almost all of them follow from two facts about each class.

**Fact 1: the noun prefix.** Adjectives copy it (Thompson and Schleicher's rule of thumb from lesson 3.3). Polomé calls this the **nominal** agreement: the prefixes that look like the noun's own.

**Fact 2: the subject prefix.** Everything else is built on it: possessives, the *-a* of association, demonstratives, the reference forms. Polomé calls the agreement of these words **pronominal**, and lists the verb's own prefixes as a third, **verbal** set, nearly the same in shape. The East African school names of lesson 3.1 (KI-VI, LI-YA, U-I, I-ZI) are lists of exactly these prefixes, which is why schools name the classes by them: they predict the most forms.

So for any class you need two prefixes, and the rules of lesson 1.6 do the rest. *Kitabu*: noun prefix *ki-*, subject prefix *ki-*. Then the adjective is *kikubwa*; "my" is *ch-* + *-angu*; "of" is *ch-* + *-a*; "this" is *h-i-ki*; "that" is *ki-le*; "that one" is *hi-cho*; "with it" is *na-cho*.

## How it is built

**From the subject prefix to the possessive and "of".** The possessive stems and *-a* begin with a vowel, so the subject prefix meets a vowel and changes as in lesson 1.6:

- *ki-* → *ch-*, *vi-* → *vy-* (the *i* becomes a glide and fuses);
- *li-* → *l-*, *zi-* → *z-*, *i-* → *y-* (the *i* becomes a glide or drops);
- *u-* → *w-*, *ku-* → *kw-*, *mu-* → *mw-* (the *u* becomes *w*);
- *ya-* → *y-*, *wa-* → *w-*, *pa-* → *p-* (the *a* merges with the next *a*).

Class 1 is the one class you must learn separately: subject *a-*, but possessive *w-* (*wangu*, *wa*) as if it were *u-*.

**From the subject prefix to the demonstratives.** "This" is *h-* + the prefix's vowel + the prefix (*h-a-ya* → *haya*); "that" is prefix + *-le* (*yale*); "that one" is "this" with its last vowel replaced by *-o* (*hayo*). Class 1 uses *yu-*: *huyu*, *yule*, *huyo* (Almasi and colleagues; Thompson and Schleicher; Polomé).

**From the subject prefix to the reference forms.** Prefix + *-o*, with the same sound changes: *ki* + *o* → *cho*, *vi* + *o* → *vyo*, *li* + *o* → *lo*, *i* + *o* → *yo*, *zi* + *o* → *zo*, *u* + *o* → *o*, *ya* + *o* → *yo*, *pa* + *o* → *po*, *ku* + *o* → *ko*, *mu* + *o* → *mo*. Class 1 has *-ye*, class 2 *-o*. These are the endings of *nacho*, *ninayo*, *hicho*, and of the place forms *-po*, *-ko*, *-mo* you have used since lesson 2.5. Polomé analyses all of them as a prefix plus the referential particle *-o*.

**Where the adjective departs from the noun prefix.** Four places to remember:

1. Class 5 nouns may have *ji-*, but adjectives have nothing: *jiwe zito* (lesson 3.5).
2. Classes 9 and 10 adjectives take the nasal by the adjective's first sound: *nzuri*, *mbaya*, *ndefu*, *kubwa* (lesson 3.6).
3. Classes 11 and 14 nouns have *u-*, but adjectives have *m-*: *ukuta mrefu* (lesson 3.7).
4. People and animals take class 1/2 adjectives whatever the noun: *paka mdogo* (lesson 3.9).

**Overriding the class.** Animate agreement (lesson 3.9) switches the whole pronominal row to classes 1 and 2, except possessives with kin terms (*rafiki yangu*, lesson 3.10). The place classes 16 to 18 take over as soon as *-ni* is added (lesson 3.13).

**The chain for any sentence.** Say "those big trees of ours are falling".

1. *Miti*, class 4. Noun prefix *mi-*, subject prefix *i-*.
2. "Our": *i-* + *-etu* → *yetu*. Straight after the noun.
3. "Those (over there)": *i-* + *-le* → *ile*.
4. "Big": copy the noun prefix: *mikubwa*.
5. Verb: *inaanguka*.
6. *Miti yetu ile mikubwa inaanguka.* (Polomé puts the demonstrative after the possessive and before the adjective; lesson 3.12.)

## Where it applies

- **Every agreeing word in parts 2 and 3**: subject prefixes, negative subject prefixes, adjectives, possessives, *-a*, demonstratives, place forms, short forms with *na*, short answers with "have".
- **Later agreeing words** use the same two rows: numbers take the nominal (adjective) prefixes (*vitabu vitatu*, lesson 5.3); object prefixes (lesson 4.8) and relative markers (part 6) take the pronominal ones (*-ki-*, *-cho-*); *-ote* "all" and *-enye* "having" take the possessive shape (*vyote*, *chenye*; Almasi and colleagues).
- **Reading.** When you meet an unknown noun, the agreeing words tell you its class: *... zile ndefu ziko ...* can only be class 10.
- **Dictionaries and the school names.** Wiktionary's class labels and the school names (A-WA, U-I, LI-YA, KI-VI, I-ZI, U-ZI, U-U, KU-KU, PA, KU, MU) can both be read off this map.

## Watch out

**Do not build adjectives on the subject prefix.** *\*Mti ukubwa* uses the verb's *u-*. Adjectives copy the noun prefix: *mti mkubwa*.

**Do not build possessives or demonstratives on the noun prefix.** *\*Mti mwangu* and *\*mti muu* copy the noun's *m-*. Pronominal forms come from the subject prefix *u-*: *mti wangu*, *mti huu*.

**Do not forget class 1's three faces.** Subject *a-* (*anacheza*), but *yu-* before place endings and in demonstratives (*yuko*, *huyu*, *yule*), and *w-* in possessives and *-a* (*wangu*, *wa*). The reference form is *-ye* (*naye*).

**Do not trust the noun alone for classes 9, 10 and 5.** These nouns often show no prefix. Read the agreeing words.

**Do not apply the animate override to the possessive of kin terms.** *\*Rafiki wangu* follows the animate rule too far: *rafiki yangu*. Everything else is class 1/2.

**Do not treat the map as a list to memorise.** If you know *mti*, *u-* and the vowel rules, you can work out *wangu*, *wa*, *huu*, *ule*, *huo*, *nao*. Use the table to check, not to learn.

## Connections

The class numbers are shared across the Bantu family (Wikipedia's grammar article), so a map like this one is how other Bantu languages are laid out too. Nothing similar exists in Spanish or French, whose agreement fits in a few lines; the nearest European experience is learning a full table of Latin or Greek adjective endings, with the difference that here the endings are at the front.

## Exercises

1. *Ufunguo* is class 11. Without the table, give "my key", "this key", "that key over there" and "the key of the door".
2. *Kitunguu* is class 7. Give "these onions", "your onions" and "the onions are enough".
3. Which two prefixes do you need to know for a class to predict all its agreeing forms? Give them for class 5.
4. Say "my heavy pot is in the kitchen" (*sufuria*, class 9).
5. Correct *\*Mti mwangu ukubwa unaanguka.*
6. Correct *\*Rafiki wangu wazuri wanafika* (meant: "my good friends are arriving").
7. Explain why "that one (class 7)" is *hicho*, not *\*hiko*.
8. In *\_\_ zile ndefu ziko wapi?* the noun is missing. Name two nouns that could fill the gap.
9. Build "those apples of yours are sweet" (*tufaha*, class 5/6).
10. Give the class 18 forms of the possessive "my" and of "that one", and explain where the *mw-* comes from.

## Worked answers

1. Subject prefix *u-*. "My key" *ufunguo wangu* (*u-* → *w-*); "this key" *ufunguo huu* (*h-u-u*); "that key" *ufunguo ule* (*u-* + *-le*); "the key of the door" *ufunguo wa mlango*.
2. Plural class 8, subject *vi-*, noun *vi-*. "These onions" *vitunguu hivi*; "your onions" *vitunguu vyako* (*vi-* → *vy-*); *Vitunguu vinatosha.*
3. The noun prefix (for adjectives) and the subject prefix (for everything else). Class 5: noun prefix zero (or *ji-*), subject prefix *li-*.
4. *Sufuria yangu nzito iko jikoni.* Class 9: *y-angu*, *n-zito*, *i-ko*; "in the kitchen" *jikoni*.
5. Possessive from the subject prefix: *wangu*; adjective from the noun prefix: *mkubwa*. *Mti wangu mkubwa unaanguka.*
6. The kin-type possessive is class 10: *zangu*. *Rafiki zangu wazuri wanafika.*
7. The referring form replaces the last vowel of *hiki* with *-o*, and before *o* the *ki* becomes *cho*, as *ki-* + *-umba* became *chumba* (lesson 1.6).
8. Any class 10 noun with a consonant-stem adjective *-refu*: *barua zile ndefu*, *nyumba zile ndefu*, *kuta zile ndefu*, *funguo zile ndefu*.
9. *Matufaha yako yale ni matamu.* Class 6: possessive *y-ako*, demonstrative *yale*, predicate adjective *matamu*.
10. *Mwangu* and *humo* (or *mle*, *humu*). The class 18 subject prefix is *mu-*; before the vowel of *-angu* its *u* becomes *w*: *mw-angu*.
