## Before you start

This review teaches nothing new. It covers all of part 3: the idea of a class (lesson 3.1), the class pairs 7/8 (3.2), 3/4 (3.4), 5/6 (3.5), 9/10 (3.6) and 11/14 (3.7), adjective agreement (3.3), animate agreement (3.9), possessives (3.10), the *-a* of association (3.11), demonstratives (3.12), place classes and *-ni* (3.13), pronouns, word order and the short forms with *na* (3.14), and the agreement map (3.15). Part 2 is assumed throughout.

The passage is a short letter written by a girl to a new pen friend, constructed for this review. It ends with a real proverb. For each noun, decide its class before you read the words that agree with it; then check yourself against the glosses.

## Exercises

1. Translate the whole letter into natural English without looking at the translation lines.
2. List every noun in the letter with its class, and say whether its agreement is the class's own or class 1/2.
3. In line 3, *kubwa*, *nzuri* and *ndogo* are all class 9. Why do they look so different?
4. In line 4, why is it *mwangu* and not *changu*?
5. In line 5, explain *vya* and *ya*.
6. In line 8, why is it *jina lake* and not *jina wake*?
7. In line 9, why *huyo* and not *huyu* or *hii*?
8. Answer Amina's question *Una paka?* with "yes, I have one" using a reference form, and with "no, I haven't".
9. Make line 3 about several houses: "our houses aren't big, but they're nice".
10. Say "these knives of mine are sharp".
11. Say "that tall tree over there is falling".
12. Say "the keys are in my bag" and "my bag is in the room".
13. Say "the doctor's car is on the road" (*barabarani*).
14. Say "the lion is fierce, but the lions are not fierce".
15. Correct: *\*Kitabu yangu kiko mezani.*
16. Correct: *\*Mtoto mzuri hii anasoma.*
17. Correct: *\*Meza refu ziko chumbani.* (meant: "the long tables are in the room")
18. Correct: *\*Kisu cha mimi ni kikali.*
19. Correct: *\*Nyumbani kuzuri kwetu.*
20. Correct: *\*Juma na mimi wanasoma.*
21. Correct: *\*Ukuta urefu wangu unaanguka.*
22. Correct: *\*Paka yangu inalala.*
23. *Una kalamu?* Answer "yes, I have (it)" and explain the ending.
24. Fill in the gaps for class 3 *mto* "river": "this river", "that river (over there)", "that river (we mentioned)", "my river", "the river of the town".
25. Explain in two sentences what the noun prefix predicts and what the subject prefix predicts.

## Worked answers

1. "My new friend, my name is Amina. I live in Mombasa with my family. Our house isn't big, but it's nice, and it has a small garden. In my room there's a bed, a table and a chair. On my table there are Swahili books and a picture of my grandmother. My father is a doctor and my mother is an English teacher. My brothers are students; they study at college. We have a little black cat; his name is Simba! That cat sleeps on my bed every day. Mum says: 'Like father, like son!' (literally 'the child of a snake is a snake') And you? Have you got a cat? Are there any animals at your place?" (Lessons 3.1 to 3.14.)
2. *Rafiki* 9 (class 1/2 agreement, but class 9 possessive); *jina* 5; *familia* 9; *nyumba* 9; *bustani* 9; *chumbani* 18 (from *chumba*, 7); *kitanda* 7; *meza* 9; *kiti* 7; *mezani* 17 (from *meza*); *vitabu* 8; *Kiswahili* 7; *picha* 9; *bibi* 5 (class 1/2 agreement, class 9 possessive); *baba*, *mama* 9 (kin: class 9 possessive, class 1 agreement); *daktari* 5 (class 1 agreement); *mwalimu* 1; *Kiingereza* 7; *kaka* 10 (kin); *wanafunzi* 2; *chuoni* 17 (from *chuo*, 7); *paka* 9 (class 1 agreement); *kitandani* 17; *siku* 9; *mtoto* 1; *nyoka* 9; *wanyama* 2. Lessons 3.2 to 3.13.
3. The class 9 adjective prefix is a nasal that adapts to the adjective's first sound: nothing before *k* (*kubwa*), *n* before *z* (*nzuri*) and before *d* (*ndogo*). Lesson 3.6.
4. *Chumbani* is a place: inside the room is class 18, so "my" is *mw-* + *-angu*. *Changu* would agree with *chumba* as a thing. Lesson 3.13.
5. *Vya* agrees with *vitabu* (class 8) and *ya* with *picha* (class 9): the *-a* word agrees with the first noun. Lesson 3.11.
6. The possessive agrees with *jina*, the thing owned (class 5: *l-*), not with the cat. Lesson 3.10.
7. The cat has just been mentioned, so the referring form *huyo*; and an animal takes class 1, not class 9 *hii*/*hiyo*. Lessons 3.9 and 3.12.
8. *Ndiyo, ninaye.* A cat is an animal: the class 1 reference form *-ye*. *Hapana, sina* (or *sinaye*). Lessons 3.9 and 3.14.
9. *Nyumba zetu si kubwa, lakini ni nzuri.* Only the possessive shows the plural (*zetu*). Lessons 3.6 and 3.10.
10. *Visu vyangu hivi ni vikali.* Class 8: possessive *vy-*, demonstrative *hivi*, adjective *vi-*. Lessons 3.2, 3.10, 3.12.
11. *Mti ule mrefu unaanguka.* Lessons 3.4 and 3.12.
12. *Funguo zimo mfukoni mwangu.* *Mfuko wangu umo chumbani.* In the first, *mwangu* agrees with the place; in the second, *wangu* with *mfuko* (class 3). Lessons 3.7, 3.10, 3.13.
13. *Gari la daktari liko barabarani.* *La* agrees with *gari* (class 5). Lessons 3.5, 3.11, 3.13.
14. *Simba ni mkali, lakini simba si wakali.* Class 1 and class 2 adjectives for an animal. Lesson 3.9.
15. *Kitabu* is class 7: *Kitabu changu kiko mezani.* Lesson 3.10.
16. A person takes *huyu*, and the demonstrative follows the noun: *Mtoto huyu mzuri anasoma* (or *mtoto mzuri huyu*). Lessons 3.3 and 3.12.
17. Class 10 adjective in *r*: *ndefu*. *Meza ndefu ziko chumbani.* Lesson 3.6.
18. Pronouns use possessives, not *-a*: *Kisu changu ni kikali.* Lesson 3.11.
19. A *-ni* place cannot be followed by an adjective (Polomé). Use *mahali*, which can: *Mahali petu ni pazuri* "our place is nice"; or describe what is there: *Nyumbani kwetu kuna bustani nzuri*. Lesson 3.13.
20. "Juma and I" is "we": *Juma na mimi tunasoma.* Lesson 3.14.
21. Possessive first, adjective *m-* for class 11: *Ukuta wangu mrefu unaanguka.* Lessons 3.7 and 3.10.
22. A cat takes class 1 agreement, and a pet's possessive is *w-* in the singular: *Paka wangu analala.* Lessons 3.9 and 3.10.
23. *Ndiyo, ninayo.* *Nina* "I have" + *-yo*, the class 9 reference form pointing back to *kalamu*. Lesson 3.14.
24. *Mto huu*, *mto ule*, *mto huo*, *mto wangu*, *mto wa mji*. All built on the class 3 subject prefix *u-*. Lesson 3.15.
25. The noun prefix predicts the adjective prefix (with the class 5, 9/10 and 11/14 adjustments). The subject prefix predicts the possessive, the *-a* word, the demonstratives and the reference forms, by the vowel rules of lesson 1.6. Lesson 3.15.
