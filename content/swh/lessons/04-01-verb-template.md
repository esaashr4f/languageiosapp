## The idea

Part 3 was about nouns and the prefixes that echo them. Part 4 turns to the other half of the language: the verb. Part 0 described the Swahili verb as a **template**, a fixed row of positions, each filled by one kind of piece. You have been using that row since lesson 2.2 without seeing all of it. This lesson lays the whole row out, puts every piece you know in its place, and marks the empty positions that the rest of part 4 will fill.

Why think in slots at all? Because Swahili does. A verb like *ninasoma* is not memorised as a word; it is assembled. Every finite verb (a verb with a subject and a time) is built from the same positions in the same order, and each position takes only one kind of piece. Once you know the row, a long verb stops being a blur. You read it left to right and ask of each piece: which slot is this?

Here is the row, using the names this guide will keep:

1. **negative prefix** *ha-* (only in some negatives);
2. **subject prefix**: who does it (*ni-*, *u-*, *a-*, *ki-*...);
3. **negative marker** *-si-* (only in some other negatives, from lesson 4.11);
4. **tense marker**: when, or how (*-na-*, and the others of this part);
5. **relative marker**: "who, which" inside the verb (part 6);
6. **object prefix**: whom or what (lessons 4.7 and 4.8);
7. **root**: the meaning (*-som-* "read");
8. **extension**: a suffix that changes the meaning of the root (part 7);
9. **final vowel**: *-a*, or a replacement for it (*-i*, *-e*).

After the final vowel one more piece can come: the *-ni* of "you all" (*someni*).

This is not the guide's invention. Polomé, the structural sketch this guide uses most, lists the same row in nine positions: a "preinitial", an "initial", a "postinitial", a "marker", an "infix", the root, "suffixes", a "final" and a "postfinal". His names are technical; ours are plain, but the positions match one for one. Wiktionary's verb appendix gives the core of it as subject prefix + tense marker + (object prefix) + stem.

Learner books call the pieces in the middle of the verb "infixes", because they sit inside the word. Linguists use "infix" for something else (a piece pushed into the middle of a root), so this guide says **marker** or **prefix** instead. When you meet "object infix" in another book, it means the object prefix.

Not every slot is filled in every verb. Most verbs use four: subject, tense, root, final vowel. Some slots exclude each other: the two negative slots never appear together, Polomé notes, and a verb with the negative prefix *ha-* cannot take *-si-*. But the order never changes. No Swahili verb puts the tense before the subject, or the object after the root.

## How it is built

Take a verb you already know, *hatuuzi* "we don't sell", and file each piece.

1. *ha-*: the negative prefix, slot 1.
2. *-tu-*: the subject prefix "we", slot 2.
3. Slot 4, the tense, is empty. Lesson 2.3 showed that the negative present has no tense marker.
4. *-uz-*: the root "sell", slot 7.
5. *-i*: the negative final vowel, slot 9, replacing *-a*.

Now build in the other direction. You want "the children don't use the knife".

1. Is it negative? Yes: slot 1 gets *ha-*.
2. Who? *Watoto*, class 2: slot 2 gets *wa-*. Together *hawa-*.
3. Tense: negative present, so slot 4 stays empty.
4. Root: *-tumia* "use", root *-tumi-*.
5. Final vowel: negative present, *-i*.
6. *Hawatumii.* The noun object follows: *Watoto hawatumii kisu.*

The same reasoning handles every verb in this part. Each new lesson gives you a new filler for one slot, usually the tense slot, and tells you what it does to its neighbours. Some fillers have side effects: the negative present changes the final vowel; lesson 4.4 shows a tense slot that cannot carry stress and so pulls in an extra *ku-*; lesson 4.10 shows a mood that changes *-a* to *-e*. Ask of each new piece which slot it fills and what it does next door.

Three forms you know already have empty slots by design.

- **The command** (lesson 2.7) has an empty subject slot and an empty tense slot: *tumia!* "use!". The plural adds the *-ni* that comes after the final vowel: *tumieni!*.
- **The infinitive** (lesson 2.2) has *ku-* at the front and no tense: *kutumia* "to use". Polomé describes this *ku-* as the prefix of class 15, standing where a subject prefix would stand. Lesson 4.12 takes this up.
- **Loan verbs** have no final vowel slot. *-jibu*, *-fikiri*, *-rudi* end in a vowel that never changes (lesson 2.2), so whatever another verb does in slot 9, a loan verb does nothing there.

## Where it applies

- **Every finite verb**, positive or negative, in every class: *ninalima*, *kinaanguka*, *hatuuzi*. The subject slot accepts all the subject prefixes of part 3, including the place classes (*kuna*, lesson 2.6, is the class 17 subject prefix *ku-* with *na* "with").
- **Every tense of this part.** Past *-li-*, future *-ta-*, perfect *-me-*, "not yet" *-ja-*, *-ki-*, *-ka-* and the rest all go in slot 4. The negative past puts *-ku-* there (lesson 4.2).
- **Verbs with objects.** The object prefix goes in slot 6, after the tense, never after the root: *ninakiona* "I see it" (lesson 4.8).
- **Relative verbs.** "Who reads" can be said inside the verb with a relative marker in slot 5 (part 6). The slot sits between tense and object.
- **Derived verbs.** Extensions such as the applicative *-i-* in *-tumia* (historically "send" + *-i-*, says Wiktionary) fill slot 8. Until part 7, treat them as part of the stem you learn.
- **Commands, infinitives and loan verbs**: the same row with some slots empty, as above.
- **Reading.** When a long verb appears, segment it from the left: subject or negative first, then tense, then anything before the root.

## Watch out

**The tense never goes first.** English speakers sometimes build *\*Nanisoma*, putting the time before the person. Slot 2 precedes slot 4: *Ninasoma*.

**The negative present has no *-na-*.** *\*Hatunasoma* fills slot 4 where the negative present leaves it empty. Say *Hatusomi*. (Other negatives do keep a tense marker: lesson 4.2.)

**The final *-i* belongs to the negative only.** *\*Ninasomi* mixes a positive front with a negative end. Slot 9 and slot 1 must agree: *ninasoma* or *sisomi*.

**A loan verb has no slot 9.** *\*Hatujibi* changes a vowel that is not a final vowel. Say *Hatujibu*.

**Objects do not follow the root inside the verb.** Spanish puts object pronouns after an infinitive (*comerlo*), and French puts them before the verb but outside it (*je le vois*). In Swahili the object is a prefix inside the verb, before the root: lesson 4.7.

**One piece, several jobs.** The same shape can fill different slots. *wa-* is the class 2 subject prefix in slot 2; lesson 4.7 shows *-wa-* as an object prefix in slot 6. *Ku-* is the infinitive prefix, but lesson 4.2 puts a negative *-ku-* in slot 4, and lesson 4.7 an object *-ku-* "you" in slot 6. Position tells you which one you are looking at.

**Do not stop at the first match.** *Hapokei* is not *ha-* + *po-*. The root is *-poke-* "receive", and *ha-* here is the fused class 1 negative. Try each split against the slots.

## Connections

Japanese and Korean verbs also string pieces together, but after the root (食べ-させ-られ-なかった). Swahili puts most of its pieces before the root. Bantu languages share this row: Polomé's names were made for Bantu verbs in general, and the same slots appear across the family.

## Exercises

1. Segment *hawalipi* and name the slot of each piece.
2. Segment *ninabeba* and *tunatazama*. Which slots are filled, which empty?
3. Build "the farmer isn't selling the goat", naming each slot as you fill it.
4. Build "they are building houses" and "they aren't building houses". Which slots change?
5. Which slots does *fagia!* leave empty? And *fagieni!*?
6. Correct *\*Ninapokei* (meant: "I'm not receiving").
7. Correct *\*Hawasafiri* if it is meant as "they don't travel". Is anything wrong?
8. In *nilimwona* (example 10), find the subject prefix, the root and the final vowel. Two pieces are left. In which slots do they sit?

## Worked answers

1. *Ha-wa-lip-i*: *ha-* negative prefix (slot 1), *wa-* class 2 subject (slot 2), slot 4 empty, *-lip-* root "pay" (slot 7), *-i* negative final vowel (slot 9). "They don't pay."
2. *Ni-na-beb-a*, *tu-na-tazam-a*: slots 2, 4, 7 and 9 filled; slots 1, 3, 5, 6 and 8 empty. "I am carrying", "we are watching."
3. Negative: slot 1 *ha-*. Subject *mkulima*, class 1: fused with slot 1 as *ha-*. Tense: none. Root *-uz-*, final *-i*: *Mkulima hauzi mbuzi.*
4. *Wanajenga nyumba* / *Hawajengi nyumba.* Slot 1 gains *ha-*, slot 4 loses *-na-*, slot 9 changes from *-a* to *-i*.
5. *Fagia!* fills only the root and the final vowel: slots 1 to 6 and 8 are empty. *Fagieni!* also changes the final vowel to *-e* and adds *-ni* after it.
6. The front is positive and the end negative. "I'm not receiving" needs the fused negative *si-*, no tense, and *-i*: *Sipokei.*
7. Nothing is wrong. *-safiri* is a loan verb: no slot 9, so the negative shows only at the front: *hawasafiri* "they don't travel". (The positive is *wanasafiri*.)
8. *ni-* subject (slot 2), *-on-* root (slot 7), *-a* final vowel (slot 9). *-li-* sits in the tense slot (4) and *-mw-* in the object slot (6).
