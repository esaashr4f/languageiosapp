## The idea

So far every Swahili verb you have built has been in the present. To move it into the past you change one slot: the tense marker. *Ninasoma* "I am reading" becomes *nilisoma* "I read, I was reading", with *-li-* in place of *-na-*. Nothing else moves. The subject prefix, the root and the final *-a* stay as they were. Almasi and colleagues stress this: *-li-* is the same for every subject and every class.

This is the **simple past**: it places an action before now, and says nothing more. Polomé describes *-li-* as "merely situating" the action in the past. It does not tell you whether the action is finished and still matters (that is the job of *-me-*, lesson 4.5) or whether it was going on at the time (lesson 4.16). It is the form for telling what happened: yesterday, last year, long ago, a minute ago.

The negative is where the past differs from the present. In the negative present, the tense slot was empty and the final vowel became *-i* (lesson 2.3). In the negative past, the tense slot is not empty: *-li-* is replaced by a different marker, *-ku-*. The final vowel stays *-a*. "I didn't read" is *sikusoma*: negative *si-*, negative past *-ku-*, root, *-a*.

So the past marks "not" once at the front, as every negative does, and once in the tense slot, with its own negative marker. Polomé lists *-ku-* among the markers found only in negative forms, and describes its meaning as stating that an action did not happen at the time in question. Thompson and Schleicher give the formula as negative prefix + subject prefix + *-ku-* + verb stem.

Where does *-li-* come from? Wiktionary's etymology traces it to an older past marker *-a-* followed by *-li* "be", a verb root that survives in modern Swahili only in a few forms of "be" (part 6 meets it in *aliye* "who is"). So the past was once, roughly, "was" + verb. For the negative *-ku-*, the sources used here give no origin, and this guide will not guess. What matters for building is that *-ku-* now has a third job. You know *ku-* as the infinitive prefix; in slot 4, after a negative subject, it is the negative past. Position tells them apart.

## How it is built

Say "the farmers cultivated the field".

1. Root: *-lima* "cultivate", root *-lim-*.
2. Subject: *wakulima*, class 2: *wa-*.
3. Past: tense marker *-li-*.
4. Final vowel unchanged: *-a*.
5. *Wa-li-lim-a*: *Wakulima walilima shamba.*

Now make it negative: "the farmers did not cultivate the field".

1. Negative subject prefix: *ha-* + *wa-* = *hawa-*.
2. Tense: the negative past marker *-ku-* replaces *-li-*.
3. Root and final vowel unchanged: *-lima*.
4. *Ha-wa-ku-lim-a*: *Wakulima hawakulima shamba.*

The fused negative prefixes of lesson 2.3 work here exactly as in the present: *si-* "I", *hu-* "you", *ha-* "he, she". So "I didn't pay" is *sikulipa*, "you didn't pay" is *hukulipa*, "she didn't pay" is *hakulipa*. Thompson and Schleicher show both *hukulima* "you didn't farm" and *hakuzunguka* "he didn't wander around" built this way.

Loan verbs follow the same pattern and, as always, keep their vowel: *nilijibu* "I answered", *sikujibu* "I didn't answer" (Almasi and colleagues: *walikubali*, *hawakukubali*).

Short verbs (*-la*, *-nywa*, *-ja*) do something extra in the positive past. *-li-* cannot carry the stress, so a one-syllable root keeps its *ku-*: *nilikula* "I ate". In the negative, the *-ku-* of the negative past can carry the stress, so nothing extra is needed: *sikula* "I didn't eat". Almasi and colleagues point out that the *-ku-* of *sikula* is the negative marker, not the *ku-* of *kula*. Lesson 4.4 explains this fully.

## Where it applies

- **Any completed event in the past**, near or far: *Basi liliondoka* (a minute ago or last year). Wpgrammar describes *-li-* as covering the near and the distant past alike; Swahili has no separate "recent past".
- **A past action that was going on**, when nothing hangs on its duration: *Nilisoma jana* can be "I read" or "I was reading yesterday". To stress "was in the middle of", use the compound forms of lesson 4.16 (*nilikuwa nikisoma*).
- **Past habits** in a plain statement: *Zamani tulilima shamba* "in the old days we farmed". (Lesson 4.16 adds *-likuwa* + *-ki-* for "used to".)
- **Every class and person**: *kikombe kilianguka* "the cup fell", *miti ilianguka* "the trees fell", *mvua ilinyesha*.
- **Questions**: the same form with question intonation or *je* (lesson 2.4): *Je, ulilipa?* "Did you pay?" Answer *Ndiyo, nililipa* or *Hapana, sikulipa*.
- **"Was" and "had" with nouns and places.** *Ni*, *-ko* and *-na* "have" have no past forms of their own. The past is built on the verb *kuwa* "be": *nilikuwa mwalimu* "I was a teacher", *nilikuwa nyumbani* "I was at home", *nilikuwa na gari* "I had a car", *kulikuwa na watu* "there were people". *Kuwa* is a short verb: lesson 4.4 explains its *ku-*, and lesson 4.16 builds on it.
- **Negative answers about the past** use *-ku-* even when the question used the perfect: Polomé gives *Umeandika barua? Sikuandika* "Have you written the letter? I have not" (for "not yet", lesson 4.5).

## Watch out

**No *-li-* in the negative.** *\*Silisoma* and *\*Hatulisoma* keep the positive tense marker. The negative past replaces it: *Sikusoma*, *Hatukusoma*.

**No final *-i* in the negative past.** *\*Sikusomi* carries over the ending of the negative present. Only the negative present changes the final vowel. Say *Sikusoma*.

**Do not mix up the fused prefixes.** *Hakulipa* is "he or she didn't pay". "You didn't pay" is *hukulipa*, with the 2SG negative *hu-*. The difference is one vowel at the front.

**Do not put *-na-* with *jana*.** A Spanish speaker may reach for a present with a past time word, as in a historic present (*ayer voy al mercado y...*). *\*Jana ninasoma* is not Swahili for "yesterday I read". Say *Jana nilisoma.* (Inside a clause that follows a past verb, *-na-* can describe what was happening at that moment; Polomé gives "the animals realised that the savannah *was* burning" with *-na-*. That is part 6 material.)

**Do not build "was" with *ni*.** *\*Mimi ni mwalimu jana* puts a present copula in a past sentence. Use *kuwa*: *Nilikuwa mwalimu.*

**English "did" is not a separate verb.** *\*Nilifanya kusoma* for "I did read" doubles the verb. Swahili has no emphatic "did"; Thompson and Schleicher note the absence of a past emphatic. Say *Nilisoma*, and add stress with tone of voice or *kweli* "truly".

**Do not confuse negative *-ku-* with "you" or the infinitive.** In *hukulipa*, *hu-* is "you (not)" and *-ku-* is the negative past. In *kulipa*, *ku-* is the infinitive. Lesson 4.7 adds an object *-ku-* "you": *sikukulipa* "I didn't pay you" has two *ku*s in two slots.

***Hakuna* is not a past.** *Hakuna* "there isn't" is the negative of *kuna*: *ha-* + class 17 *ku-* + *na*. The *ku* there is a subject prefix. "There wasn't" is *hakukuwa na* (lesson 4.16).

## Connections

Spanish has two pasts, *leí* and *leía*; Swahili's *-li-* covers both, and uses compound forms when the "was doing" sense must be explicit. Japanese *-ta* (読んだ) is closer: one plain past for completed and ongoing events. Its negative, 読まなかった, also marks "not" and "past" in separate pieces, as *si-ku-soma* does.

## Exercises

1. Put into the past: *Ninapika*, *Tunaoga*, *Wanapanda basi*, *Kitabu kinaanguka*.
2. Make each sentence of exercise 1 negative in the past.
3. Translate: *Wageni hawakufika jana.*
4. Say "you (one person) didn't win" and "he didn't win". Which piece shows the difference?
5. Say "the meeting didn't happen" (*mkutano*, class 3).
6. Correct *\*Hatukulipi.*
7. Correct *\*Mimi ni mgonjwa jana* (meant: "I was ill yesterday"). Give two ways.
8. Take apart *hamkushuka* and translate it.

## Worked answers

1. Replace *-na-* by *-li-*: *Nilipika*, *Tulioga*, *Walipanda basi*, *Kitabu kilianguka*.
2. Negative subject prefix, *-ku-* for *-li-*, final *-a* kept: *Sikupika*, *Hatukuoga*, *Hawakupanda basi*, *Kitabu hakikuanguka*.
3. *Ha-wa-ku-fik-a*: class 2 negative, negative past, "arrive". "The guests didn't arrive yesterday."
4. *Hukushinda* and *hakushinda*. Only the fused negative prefix differs: *hu-* "you", *ha-* "he, she".
5. Class 3 subject *u-*, negative *hau-*: *Mkutano haukutokea.* (Wiktionary's table of negative prefixes gives *hau-* for class 3.)
6. The negative past keeps *-a*: *Hatukulipa* "we didn't pay".
7. The copula has no past; use *kuwa*. *Nilikuwa mgonjwa jana* "I was a sick person yesterday", or with the verb *-ugua*: *Niliugua jana* "I was ill yesterday".
8. *Ha-m-ku-shuk-a*: *ha-* negative, *m-* "you (several)", *-ku-* negative past, *-shuk-* "get off", *-a*. "You (all) didn't get off."
