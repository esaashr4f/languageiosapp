## The idea

The future is the easiest tense in Swahili. Put *-ta-* in the tense slot: *nitasoma* "I will read". For the negative, change the subject prefix to its negative form and keep everything else, *-ta-* included: *sitasoma* "I won't read". Almasi and colleagues sum it up: the only change in the negative future is the subject prefix. The final vowel stays *-a*.

Compare the three negatives you now know. The negative present empties the tense slot and changes the final vowel (*sisomi*). The negative past swaps the tense marker for *-ku-* (*sikusoma*). The negative future keeps its marker and its vowel (*sitasoma*). In all three, the front of the verb carries "not". So the reliable signal of a negative verb is its first syllable: *si-*, *hu-*, *ha-*.

Where does *-ta-* come from? Wiktionary derives it from the verb *-taka* "want". The path from "want to read" to "will read" is a familiar one: English *will* itself first meant "want". The longer verb still lives beside its short descendant, and this lesson's Where it applies shows *-taka* followed by an infinitive meaning "be about to".

The future in Swahili, as in English, covers more than prediction. Polomé describes *-ta-* as placing an action after the time of reference, with nothing added. In practice that covers plans (*nitasafiri kesho*), promises (*nitarudi*), predictions (*mvua itanyesha*) and announcements.

In speech you will hear a second negative future: *-to-* in place of *-ta-*. *Hatokuja* "he won't come" stands beside the standard *hatakuja*. Wpgrammar reports that "a number of speakers" use it, suggests that it was copied from the *-to-* of the negative infinitive (lesson 4.12), and points to its usefulness: *hatakuja* "he won't come" differs from *atakuja* "he will come" only by its *h*, and second-language speakers in many areas, it notes, have trouble saying and hearing *h*. The *o* keeps the negative audible. This guide writes *-ta-*, the standard form, and labels *-to-* as colloquial.

## How it is built

Say "we will move to Mombasa next year".

1. Root: *-hamia* "move to", root *-hami-*.
2. Subject "we": *tu-*.
3. Future: *-ta-*.
4. Final *-a*: *tu-ta-hami-a*, *tutahamia*.
5. Add the place and time: *Tutahamia Mombasa mwakani.*

Now "the teacher won't come back next week".

1. Root: *-rudi* "come back", a loan verb: no final vowel to change.
2. Subject *mwalimu*, class 1; negative *ha-*.
3. Future *-ta-*, kept in the negative.
4. *Ha-ta-rudi*: *Mwalimu hatarudi wiki ijayo.*

Short verbs keep *ku-* in the future, positive and negative alike, because *-ta-* cannot carry the stress: *nitakula* "I will eat", *sitakula* "I won't eat" (Almasi and colleagues; Thompson and Schleicher give *hamtakula* "you all won't eat"). Lesson 4.4 explains why. Contrast the negative past *sikula*, where *-ku-* can carry the stress.

## Where it applies

- **Plans and arrangements**: *Tutahamia Mombasa mwakani.* Swahili uses the future where English often uses "is going to" or a present (*I'm leaving tomorrow* = *Nitaondoka kesho*).
- **Predictions**: *Mvua itanyesha baadaye.*
- **Promises and refusals**: *Nitarudi.* *Sitasahau.* *Sitalipa!* "I won't pay!"
- **Questions about the future**: *Utarudi kesho?* "Will you come back tomorrow?"
- **Every class**: *Gari litapita hapa* "the car will come by here"; *Kiti kitaanguka* "the chair will fall".
- **The main clause of an "if" sentence**: "if it rains, we won't go". The "if" half has its own form (lesson 4.14); the result half is an ordinary future.
- **"About to"**: *-taka* + infinitive, as in example 9. With a person as subject the same words can mean "want": *Anataka kuondoka* "she wants to leave". Context decides.
- **Inside relative verbs** the future marker has a longer shape, *-taka-* (part 6): *atakayekuja* "who will come". Do not use that shape outside relatives.

## Watch out

**No final *-i* in the negative future.** *\*Sitasomi* borrows the ending of the negative present. Say *Sitasoma.*

**Do not drop *-ta-* in the negative.** *\*Sisoma* and *\*Hatusoma* are not words. The negative future is *sitasoma*, *hatutasoma*.

**"Will" asking a favour is not a future.** English "Will you close the door?" is a request. *Utafunga mlango?* asks whether the listener is going to close it. For a request, use *tafadhali* with a command, or the subjunctive (lesson 4.10).

**Spanish *ir a* + infinitive is not *-enda* + infinitive.** "I'm going to read" is *Nitasoma*, not *\*Ninakwenda kusoma*, which means "I am going (somewhere) to read".

**Know *-to-* when you hear it, write *-ta-*.** *Sitokula*, *hatokuja* are common in speech. In writing and in this guide, *sitakula*, *hatakuja*.

**The *h* matters.** *Atakuja* "she will come" and *hatakuja* "she won't come" differ only in *h*. Pronounce it, and listen for it.

**Do not use *-taka* for a plain future.** *Ninataka kusafiri* is "I want to travel", not "I will travel".

## Connections

The path from "want" to "will" is common: English *will* first meant "want", and Wiktionary derives Swahili *-ta-* from *-taka* "want". Spanish built its future differently, from Latin "have to" (*cantar he* > *cantaré*). Japanese has no future form at all: 明日行きます uses the plain non-past.

## Exercises

1. Put into the future: *Ninalipa*, *Wanaoa* ("they are getting married", said of men), *Kitabu kinaanguka*, *Tunaendelea*.
2. Make each answer of exercise 1 negative.
3. Translate: *Hatutaondoka kesho kutwa; tutasubiri.*
4. Say "you (several) will pass the exam" and "you (several) won't pass the exam".
5. Correct *\*Hatutasafiri* if meant as "we didn't travel".
6. Correct *\*Sitasomi kesho.*
7. What does *Mvua inataka kunyesha* mean? Why not "the rain wants to rain"?
8. Someone says *Hatokuja*. Give the standard spelling and the meaning.

## Worked answers

1. Replace *-na-* by *-ta-*: *Nitalipa*, *Wataoa*, *Kitabu kitaanguka*, *Tutaendelea*.
2. Negative prefix, *-ta-* kept, *-a* kept: *Sitalipa*, *Hawataoa*, *Kitabu hakitaanguka*, *Hatutaendelea*.
3. *Ha-tu-ta-ondok-a*: "we won't leave"; *tu-ta-subiri*: "we will wait". "We won't leave the day after tomorrow; we'll wait."
4. *Mtafaulu mtihani.* *Hamtafaulu mtihani.* The 2PL negative prefix is *ham-*.
5. "Didn't" is the negative past with *-ku-*: *Hatukusafiri.* *Hatutasafiri* means "we won't travel".
6. The negative future keeps the final *-a*; *-i* belongs only to the negative present. *Sitasoma kesho* "I won't read tomorrow".
7. "It's about to rain." *-taka* + infinitive can mean "be about to" (Wiktionary's verb appendix lists this use), and with a subject that cannot want anything that is the only sensible reading.
8. *Hatakuja*, "he (or she) won't come". The *-to-* is a spoken variant of the negative future *-ta-*.
