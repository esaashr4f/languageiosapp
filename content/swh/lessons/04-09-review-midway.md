## Before you start

This review teaches nothing new. It covers the first half of part 4: the verb template (lesson 4.1), the past with *-li-* and *-ku-* (4.2), the future *-ta-* (4.3), short verbs and their *ku-* (4.4), the perfect *-me-* and "not yet" *-ja-* (4.5), states in the perfect (4.6), and object prefixes for persons (4.7), for classes and for "self" (4.8). Parts 2 and 3 are assumed throughout.

The passage is a short diary entry, constructed for this review, written by a student called Juma. For each verb, find the slots from the left before you read the gloss: negative or subject, tense, object, root, final vowel.

## Exercises

1. Translate the whole diary entry into natural English without looking at the translation lines.
2. Name the slot of every piece in *halikunisubiri* (line 4).
3. In line 1, why *nilichelewa* and not *nimechelewa*? In line 5, why *nimeamka* and not *niliamka*?
4. In line 3, why does *nilikunywa* have *ku* while *sikula* has only one *ku*?
5. In line 8, explain *sijaiona*: the tense, the object prefix and why the object prefix is there.
6. In line 2, why *-m-* in *sikumsikia* but *-mw-* in *nitamwuliza* (line 10)?
7. Say "the guests haven't arrived yet; we are waiting for them".
8. Say "Did you see the teacher? No, I didn't see him."
9. Say "I'll give you the key" (*ufunguo*, class 11), then "I'll give it to you".
10. Say "the children are asleep and the food is cooked".
11. Say "we'll go to the market tomorrow, but we won't take the bus".
12. Say "this letter, have you read it?"
13. Say "she saw herself in the window".
14. Say "I've never been to Nairobi".
15. Change *Nitakusaidia* so that "you" means several people, in two ways.
16. Explain the two *ku*s of *sikukupenda*.
17. Correct *\*Sijamwona jana* (meant: "I didn't see him yesterday").
18. Correct *\*Ninaona wewe.*
19. Correct *\*Hatukuli.*
20. Correct *\*Ninachoka sana* meant as "I'm very tired".
21. Correct *\*Hawatasomi kesho.*
22. Correct *\*Alikuwapa pesa* (meant: "he gave them money").
23. Translate *Mzee ameketi; amevaa koti na kofia.*
24. Put the object first: "The shirts, I've washed them" (*mashati*).

## Worked answers

1. "Yesterday I woke up early, but I was late. Mum called me, but I didn't hear her. I drank some tea quickly, but I didn't eat. The bus didn't wait for me! Today I've got up early, washed and eaten. My friend Amina has arrived in town; I'm waiting for her here. We'll go to the market together; I'll buy fruit and beans. But I haven't seen my hat since yesterday! Maybe I've lost it, or maybe my sister has taken it. I'll ask her." (Lessons 4.2 to 4.8.)
2. *ha-* negative prefix (slot 1), *-li-* class 5 subject, agreeing with *basi* (slot 2), *-ku-* negative past (slot 4), *-ni-* object "me" (slot 6), *-subiri* root (slot 7); no final vowel slot, since *-subiri* is a loan verb. Lessons 4.1, 4.2, 4.7.
3. Yesterday's lateness is a past event, told as such: *-li-*. Today's waking has a result that holds now (Juma is up): *-me-*. Lessons 4.2, 4.5, 4.6.
4. *-li-* cannot carry stress, so the short verb keeps *ku-*. In *sikula* the one *ku* is the negative past marker, which can carry stress, so no second one is added. Lesson 4.4.
5. *Si-ja-i-on-a*: "not yet" *-ja-* (the hat may still turn up), object prefix *-i-* for *kofia* (class 9). The object prefix is needed because the object has been put first. Lessons 4.5, 4.8.
6. *-m-* before a consonant (*-sikia*), *-mw-* before a vowel (*-uliza*). Lesson 4.7.
7. *Wageni hawajafika bado; tunawasubiri.* "Not yet" with *-ja-*; *-wa-* for the guests. Lessons 4.5, 4.7.
8. *Ulimwona mwalimu? Hapana, sikumwona.* Lessons 4.2, 4.7.
9. *Nitakupa ufunguo.* With only one object slot, the person takes it (Almasi and colleagues), so "it" stays a noun or is left out: *Nitakupa* (or *Nitakupa ufunguo huo*). Lessons 4.7, 4.8.
10. *Watoto wamelala na chakula kimeiva.* States through the perfect. Lesson 4.6.
11. *Tutakwenda sokoni kesho, lakini hatutapanda basi.* Lessons 4.3, 4.4.
12. *Barua hii umeisoma?* Object first, class 9 object *-i-*. Lesson 4.8.
13. *Alijiona dirishani.* Reflexive *-ji-*. Lesson 4.8.
14. *Sijawahi kwenda Nairobi.* Lesson 4.5.
15. *Nitawasaidieni* (-wa- + -eni) or *Nitawasaidia ninyi*. Lesson 4.7.
16. The first is the negative past marker (slot 4); the second is the object "you" (slot 6). "I didn't love you." Lessons 4.2, 4.7.
17. *Jana* closes the matter: use the negative past, *Sikumwona jana*. *-ja-* means "not yet". Lesson 4.5.
18. The object goes in the verb as *-ku-*: *Ninakuona.* Lesson 4.7.
19. "We didn't eat" is *Hatukula* (negative past, final *-a*); "we don't eat" is *Hatuli*. *\*Hatukuli* mixes the two. Lessons 2.3, 4.2, 4.4.
20. *Ninachoka* is "I'm getting tired". The state is *Nimechoka sana.* Lesson 4.6.
21. The negative future keeps *-a*: *Hawatasoma kesho.* Lesson 4.3.
22. The object prefix "them" is *-wa-*, and *-pa* never takes *ku-*: *Aliwapa pesa.* Lessons 4.4, 4.7.
23. "The old man is sitting down; he is wearing a coat and a hat." Both are states through the perfect. Lesson 4.6.
24. *Mashati nimeyaosha.* Class 6 object *-ya-*. Lesson 4.8.
