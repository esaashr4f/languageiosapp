## The idea

You have used *kusoma*, *kula*, *kwenda* since lesson 2.2: the form that names an action, quoted in every word list. This lesson looks at it properly. It is called the **infinitive**: the form of a verb with no subject and no tense, naming the action itself.

The infinitive is half verb and half noun. Thompson and Schleicher call it a "verbal noun" and point out that Swahili does not distinguish English "to read" and "reading": *kusoma* is both. They also note that infinitives are nouns of class 15. Wpgrammar says the same: class 15 is "reserved specifically for infinitives", and an infinitive "may occur in the same contexts as other nouns".

**As a verb** it keeps the verb's inside. It can take an object prefix: *kukuona* "to see you" (lesson 4.7). It can have objects after it: *kusoma kitabu*. It can be negative. What it cannot take are a subject prefix or a tense marker: wpgrammar notes that infinitives "cannot take subject, relative or TAM prefixes". The *ku-* stands in the subject slot (lesson 4.1), and there is no tense slot.

**As a noun** it belongs to class 15, with *ku-* as its noun prefix. Like any noun it can be a subject, an object, or the second noun after *-a* "of", and words that agree with it take class 15 prefixes: subject prefix *ku-*, possessive *kw-* (*kusoma kwako* "your reading"), adjective *ku-*, *kw-* before a vowel. Polomé gives *kuimba kwetu* "our singing" and *kusoma kwema* "good reading". Thompson and Schleicher give a sentence with an infinitive subject: *Kuona kifaru kunavutia* "seeing a rhino is interesting", where the verb has class 15 *ku-*.

**The negative** adds *-to-* after *ku-*: *kutosoma* "not to read, not reading" (Wiktionary; Thompson and Schleicher). Wiktionary derives *kuto-* from *kutoa* "to lack"; wpgrammar from the same verb in the sense "take away". With short verbs, Wiktionary says *kuto-* is put in front of the whole positive infinitive: *kutokula* "not to eat". Thompson and Schleicher also give a longer form for all verbs, *kutokufanya* beside *kutofanya*; Wiktionary calls such forms older or dialectal. This guide writes *kuto-* + stem, and *kutoku-* only for short verbs.

## How it is built

Say "I can't come tomorrow".

1. Main verb: *-weza* "be able". Negative present "I": *siwezi*.
2. What I can't do: "come", an infinitive: *kuja*.
3. *Siwezi kuja kesho.*

Now "not smoking is good for you", with the infinitive as subject.

1. "Smoke": *-vuta sigara* "draw on a cigarette". Negative infinitive: *kutovuta sigara*.
2. It is the subject: class 15. With *ni* and an adjective, the adjective agrees in class 15: *ni kuzuri*.
3. *Kutovuta sigara ni kuzuri.*

And "they stood and watched".

1. First verb: *walisimama*.
2. Polomé notes that when two actions go closely together, the second can be an infinitive joined by *na*: *na kutazama*.
3. *Walisimama na kutazama.*

## Where it applies

- **After verbs that take an action as object**, with the same subject: *-penda* "like", *-taka* "want", *-weza* "can", *-anza* "begin", *-jaribu* "try", *-acha* "stop", *-endelea* "go on", *-sahau* "forget", *-kumbuka* "remember", *-zoea* "get used to", *-furahia* "enjoy", *-epuka* "avoid", *-jitahidi* "make an effort". Thompson and Schleicher: the infinitive's main use is to complete the meaning of the conjugated verb.
- **After verbs of going and coming, for purpose**: *nilikuja kusoma* "I came to study" (Almasi and colleagues; lesson 4.11).
- **As subject**: *Kuuliza si ujinga*; *Kusoma kunasaidia.*
- **As object**: Thompson and Schleicher's *Sisomi kuandika kwake* "I don't read his writing".
- **After *-a* "of"**: *chumba cha kulala*, *maji ya kunywa*, *mashine ya kuosha* "washing machine".
- **After *ni* + adjective**: *ni vigumu kutocheka*, *ni rahisi kuogelea*, *ni muhimu kusoma*.
- **A second, closely linked action with *na***: *walisimama na kutazama* (Polomé).
- **With object prefixes and *-ji-***: *kukuona*, *kujifunza* "to learn".
- **Negative**: *kutosoma*, *kutokula*, *kutokwenda*.
- **"Have" as an infinitive**: *kuwa na* "to have": *Ninapenda kuwa na rafiki* "I like having friends".

## Watch out

**No subject or tense in the infinitive.** *\*Ninataka ninaogelea* and *\*Ninataka nitaogelea* are wrong. The second verb is *kuogelea* (or a subjunctive with a different subject: lesson 4.11).

**Do not use the infinitive for a different subject.** *\*Ninataka wewe kuogelea* fails. Say *Ninataka uogelee.*

**Agreement with an infinitive is class 15, not class 9 or 1.** *\*Kusoma inasaidia* and *\*Kusoma anasaidia* use the wrong prefix. Say *Kusoma kunasaidia.*

**Negative with *-to-*, not *si*.** *\*Kusisoma* and *\*Si kusoma* do not negate an infinitive. Say *kutosoma*. (*Si* is the negative copula: *kusoma si kugumu* "reading is not hard".)

**Short verbs keep *ku-* inside the negative.** *\*Kutola* is not "not to eat": the negative is built on the whole infinitive, *kutokula*.

**Do not confuse three *ku*s.** In *kukuona* the first is the infinitive, the second is "you". In *hakuona* "he didn't see", *-ku-* is the negative past. In *kula* it is the infinitive kept for stress. Slots decide.

**English "-ing" is not always an infinitive.** "I am reading" is *ninasoma*, a tensed verb. "He was walking" is a compound tense (lesson 4.16). "Walking to school, she saw a snake" uses *-ki-* (lesson 4.14). Thompson and Schleicher give a chart of these: only the "-ing" that names an action as a noun is *ku-*.

## Connections

Spanish *el leer* "reading" and Japanese 読むこと show the same idea: an infinitive or verb form treated as a noun. Swahili goes further and gives it a full noun class with its own agreement. Polomé traces the class 15 prefix *ku-* back to proto-Bantu *\*ko*, and notes that in Swahili the class is used for nothing but infinitives.

## Exercises

1. Say "she can't swim" and "she doesn't like swimming".
2. Say "we'll begin to cook later" and "they've stopped smoking".
3. Translate *Kutosoma ni hasara.*
4. Make *kusoma* the subject: "reading is important" and "reading helps children".
5. Say "your (several people's) singing is beautiful".
6. Correct *\*Kuogelea inafurahisha.* (*-furahisha* "please, delight")
7. Correct *\*Ninapenda ninaimba.*
8. Give the negative infinitive of *-la*, *-andika* and *-jibu*.

## Worked answers

1. *Hawezi kuogelea.* *Hapendi kuogelea.* Negative of the main verb; the infinitive does not change.
2. *Tutaanza kupika baadaye.* *Wameacha kuvuta sigara.*
3. *Ku-to-som-a*: "not reading"; *ni hasara*: "is a loss". "Not reading is a loss."
4. *Kusoma ni muhimu.* *Kusoma kunawasaidia watoto* (object prefix *-wa-* for the children, lesson 4.7).
5. *Kuimba kwenu ni kuzuri.* Class 15 possessive *kw-* + *-enu*, adjective *ku-*.
6. An infinitive subject takes class 15 agreement: *Kuogelea kunafurahisha* "swimming is delightful".
7. Same subject: the second verb is an infinitive: *Ninapenda kuimba.*
8. *Kutokula* (short verb: *kuto-* + *kula*), *kutoandika*, *kutojibu*.
