## The idea

Tell a story in English and you chain the events with "and" and "then": "I came home, and then I ate, and then I slept." Swahili builds the "and then" into the verb. The first event is told with *-li-*; each following event takes *-ka-* in the tense slot: *Nilifika nyumbani, nikala, nikalala.* The *-ka-* says: this happened next.

This is the **consecutive** tense (Polomé says "subsecutive"; wpgrammar and Almasi and colleagues "narrative"): a tense marking an event as following the one before. Thompson and Schleicher describe it as an action occurring after the action of the verb before it, and note that "the verb itself incorporates the sense of both the conjunction *and* and the adverb *then*". Almasi and colleagues make the practical point: you do not need *na* "and" between *-ka-* verbs.

*-ka-* has no time of its own. It takes its time from the story. That is why it normally comes after a *-li-* verb: the *-li-* sets the scene in the past, and the *-ka-* verbs follow on. Once the story is clearly in the past, wpgrammar notes, a sentence can even begin with *-ka-*. Long folk tales run for pages on *-ka-* after one opening *-li-*.

If you want to stress the order of events, you can add *halafu* or *kisha* "then": *Alisoma barua, halafu akamwonyesha mama yake* (Almasi and colleagues' example, with *-onyesha* "show").

*-ka-* can carry stress, so short verbs and *-enda* take no *ku-* or *kw-*: *nikala* "and I ate", *akaenda* "and he went" (Almasi and colleagues; Thompson and Schleicher: "monosyllabic verbs drop the infinitive prefix *ku-*"). Object prefixes are allowed: *tukazipika* "and we cooked them".

**The negative.** There is no negative *-ka-*. Wpgrammar says the negative subjunctive may "occasionally" fill the gap, and Almasi and colleagues give it as the regular way: *Niliwaita, wasije* "I called them, but they didn't come". Lesson 4.11 met the same pattern as a failed attempt.

### *-ka-* with the subjunctive: "go and do"

*-ka-* has a second use. Combined with the subjunctive ending *-e*, it means "go and do": *Nenda ukasome* "go and read". Almasi and colleagues call it the "consecutive subjunctive" and say it carries the meaning of going to do something; wpgrammar calls it the "expeditive". Polomé's example is *katazame* "go and see", with no subject prefix: the command form of lesson 4.10 with *-ka-* inside. Almasi and colleagues add that it is not used with verbs of coming closer, since it implies going away.

### A note on headlines

Newspapers use *ka-* with no subject prefix in headlines about one person: Almasi and colleagues give a headline of the type "man steals the president's watch" with *kaiba* "stole". Wpgrammar reports a related colloquial *ka-* for *a-me-* (*kapotea* "he's got lost"). Neither is the narrative *-ka-*: lesson 7.12 returns to them.

## How it is built

Tell a short sequence: "She took the bucket, went to the well and drew water."

1. First event, past: *a-li-chuku-a ndoo*, *alichukua ndoo*.
2. Second event: *-ka-* with the same subject: *a-ka-end-a kisimani*. No *kw-* after *-ka-*.
3. Third event: *a-ka-tek-a maji*.
4. *Alichukua ndoo, akaenda kisimani, akateka maji.*

Now "go and buy bread" to one person.

1. "Go": *nenda*.
2. "And buy": subjunctive with *-ka-*: *u-ka-nunu-e*.
3. *Nenda ukanunue mkate.*

## Where it applies

- **A chain of past events** after an opening *-li-*: *Nilifika nyumbani, nikala, nikalala.* This is the normal way to narrate in Swahili.
- **Continuing a story** without a new *-li-*: *Akamdanganya... Fisi akakasirika.* Folk tales and news reports use it page after page.
- **With a change of subject** inside the chain: each *-ka-* verb carries its own subject prefix.
- **With object prefixes**: *tukazipika*, *akamsalimu*.
- **"And then" after a past *-ki-* or other clause**: Polomé's *nilikwenda mkutanoni nikamwona Hemedi nikamwambia aje* "I went to the meeting, saw Hemedi and told him to come".
- **"Go and do"** with the subjunctive: *ukanunue*, *tukamtembelee*, *wakalime* "let them go and cultivate" (Almasi and colleagues).
- **As a command**: *kamwite*, *katazame*.
- **Negative continuation**: negative subjunctive, *wasije*.

## Watch out

**Do not repeat *-li-* with *na* for every event.** *Nilifika nyumbani na nilikula na nililala* is understandable but heavy, a word-for-word copy of English. Swahili narrative uses *-ka-*: *Nilifika nyumbani, nikala, nikalala.*

**No *ku-* after *-ka-*.** *\*Nikakula*, *\*akakwenda* keep a stress-bearer that *-ka-* does not need: *nikala*, *akaenda*.

**Do not start a conversation with *-ka-*.** *-ka-* needs an established past. Out of the blue, *\*Nikala* is odd; say *Nilikula*.

**Do not use *-ka-* for the present or future chain.** "I'll go and buy bread" (a plan) is *Nitakwenda kununua mkate*; the indicative *-ka-* of narrative is a past device. (The subjunctive *-ka-* is fine for requests and plans: *nikanunue*.)

**No negative *-ka-*.** *\*Hakakuja* does not exist. Use the negative subjunctive: *asije*.

**Do not use "go and" *-ka-* with coming verbs.** Almasi and colleagues note that it implies movement away, so *\*Njoo ukasome* "come and read" is wrong. Use a plain subjunctive: *Njoo usome.*

**Do not confuse *-ka-* with *-kaa* "sit".** *Akakaa* is "and he sat": *a-ka-ka-a*, with the tense and then the root.

## Connections

Biblical Hebrew narrative works the same way: one opening verb, then a long chain of "and"-forms (*wayyiqtol*) that take their time from the story. Arabic story-telling chains with فـ and ثم, as Swahili adds *halafu*. The hare is the trickster of East African tales; part 8 reads a nineteenth-century Zanzibar story in which a hare outwits a lion.

## Exercises

1. Continue the chain: *Juma aliamka...* "and washed, and dressed, and went out".
2. Tell in Swahili: "We went to the market, bought fish and came back home."
3. Translate: *Kobe alitembea polepole, akafika kisimani, akanywa maji.*
4. Say "go and wash your hands" (*-nawa*, *mikono*).
5. Say "let's go and see the cave".
6. Say "I looked for my key, but I didn't find it" (*ufunguo*, -ona).
7. Correct *\*Tulikwenda sokoni, tukakula.* Is anything wrong?
8. Correct *\*Alifika, akakwenda nyumbani.*

## Worked answers

1. *Juma aliamka, akaoga, akavaa, akatoka.* Each next event has *a-ka-*.
2. *Tulikwenda sokoni, tukanunua samaki, tukarudi nyumbani.*
3. "The tortoise walked slowly, reached the well and drank water." *A-ka-nyw-a*: no *ku-* after *-ka-*.
4. *Nenda ukanawe mikono.* (Or *Kanawe mikono.*)
5. *Twende tukaone pango.* *Tu-ka-on-e*: *-ka-* with the subjunctive.
6. *Niliutafuta ufunguo wangu, nisiuone.* The negative subjunctive continues the past chain; *-u-* is the class 11 object prefix.
7. *Tukakula* keeps *ku-*, which *-ka-* does not need: *Tulikwenda sokoni, tukala.*
8. *-enda* takes no *kw-* after *-ka-*: *Alifika, akaenda nyumbani.*
