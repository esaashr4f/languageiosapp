## The idea

The one-word tenses each do one job. *-li-* places an event in the past; *-me-* gives a completed action with a present result; *-ki-* and *-na-* show an action under way. What if you want an action under way *in the past* ("I was reading"), or a result that held *in the past* ("they had arrived", "they were asleep")? One verb has only one tense slot, so it cannot say both "past" and "under way".

Swahili solves this with two verbs. The first is *kuwa* "be", inflected for the time: *nilikuwa* "I was", *nitakuwa* "I will be", *nimekuwa* "I have been". The second is the main verb, inflected for the kind of action: *-ki-* or *-na-* for "in progress", *-me-* for "completed, with its result". *Nilikuwa nikisoma* "I was reading": literally "I was, I reading".

Thompson and Schleicher call these **compound tenses**: tenses made of two verbs, an auxiliary and a main verb. (An **auxiliary** is a helping verb that carries tense for another verb.) Swahili names them *tensi za maneno mawili* "two-word tenses". Polomé describes the pattern the same way: a tense of *kuwa* followed by the main verb in the *-na-*, *-ki-* or *-me-* tense, nothing else. Wpgrammar stresses that both verbs are full verbs, so the subject prefix appears twice.

Two families result.

**Ongoing action** (English "was doing", "will be doing"): *kuwa* + *-ki-* or *-na-*.
- *Nilikuwa nikisoma* or *nilikuwa ninasoma* "I was reading" (wpgrammar gives both, *walikuwa wakila* and *walikuwa wanakula*).
- *Nitakuwa nikisoma* "I will be reading".
- *Nimekuwa nikisoma* "I have been reading".
Polomé glosses *mtoto alikuwa akicheza mlangoni* as "the child used to play at the door": the same form serves for a past habit, English "used to".

**Completed action with its result** (English "had done", "will have done"): *kuwa* + *-me-*.
- *Walikuwa wamefika* "they had arrived".
- *Watakuwa wamefika* "they will have arrived".
With the change-of-state verbs of lesson 4.6 this gives past and future states: *walikuwa wamelala* "they were asleep", *nitakuwa nimechoka* "I'll be tired" (wpgrammar).

Notice that English "had done" and "will have done", which feel like simple tenses to an English speaker, are two-word forms in Swahili. Thompson and Schleicher make exactly this point.

Even longer chains exist: *nilikuwa nimekuwa nikienda* "I had been going" (Thompson and Schleicher's three-word tense). You will rarely need them.

### Where the "not" goes

Polomé and wpgrammar describe the negatives.

- **With *-na-*, negate the main verb**: *ulikuwa husomi* "you weren't reading" (wpgrammar), with the negative present.
- **With *-ki-*, negate *kuwa***: *hakuwa akisoma* "he wasn't reading". *-ki-* itself has no negative.
- **With *-me-*, usually negate the main verb with *-ja-***: *alikuwa hajavaa viatu* "he hadn't (yet) put his shoes on" (wpgrammar). Negating *kuwa* instead, *hakuwa amevaa viatu*, is used to contradict an assumption.

### "There was", "I had"

Lesson 4.2 noted that *ni*, *-ko* and *-na* have no past. Their past and future are built on *kuwa* too: *kulikuwa na watu* "there were people", *nilikuwa na gari* "I had a car", *nitakuwa nyumbani* "I'll be at home". These are not compound tenses (there is only one inflected verb) but they use the same auxiliary.

## How it is built

Say "when we arrived, they were eating" (two sentences for now: "we arrived; they were eating").

1. The background action is in progress in the past.
2. Past of *kuwa* for "they": *walikuwa*.
3. "Eating" in progress: *wakila* (or *wanakula*).
4. *Tulifika. Walikuwa wakila.*

Now "by tomorrow the guests will have arrived".

1. Future time: *watakuwa*.
2. Completed, with result: *wamefika*.
3. *Kesho wageni watakuwa wamefika.*

And "the child wasn't asleep".

1. A past state: *alikuwa* + perfect of *-lala*.
2. Negative: put *-ja-* on the main verb: *hajalala*.
3. *Mtoto alikuwa hajalala.*

## Where it applies

- **Past progressive**: *nilikuwa nikisoma*, *walikuwa wanakula*.
- **Past habit, "used to"**: *alikuwa akicheza nje*; Almasi and colleagues: *nilikuwa ninapenda kuona jua likichwa* "I used to like watching the sun set".
- **Future progressive**: *nitakuwa nikisafiri*; Thompson and Schleicher: *mtakuwa mkila* "you'll be eating".
- **Perfect progressive**: *nimekuwa nikikusubiri*; wpgrammar: *wamekuwa wakijaribu* "they have been trying".
- **Past perfect**: *tulikuwa tumesoma*, *walikuwa wamefika*.
- **Future perfect**: *watakuwa wamefika*.
- **Past and future states** of change-of-state verbs: *walikuwa wamelala*, *nitakuwa nimeshiba*.
- **Negatives**: *ulikuwa husomi*, *hakuwa akisoma*, *alikuwa hajavaa*, *hakuwa amevaa* (contradicting).
- **Past and future of "be", "have", "there is"**: *nilikuwa mgonjwa*, *nilikuwa na gari*, *kulikuwa na watu*, *nitakuwa nyumbani*.
- **With *huwa***: Polomé lists *huwa* + main verb among these forms; lesson 4.13 used *huwa tunakula*.
- **Inside the narrative chain**: *akawa* "and he became": Polomé's *hata akawa analala mezani* "until he fell asleep at the table".

## Watch out

**Do not use one verb for "was doing".** *\*Nilisoma nikisoma* and *\*Nilikisoma* are not progressive pasts. Use *kuwa*: *Nilikuwa nikisoma.*

**Repeat the subject prefix.** *\*Nilikuwa kusoma* uses an infinitive. The main verb is a full verb: *Nilikuwa nikisoma.*

**Do not put the past on the main verb.** *\*Nilikuwa nilisoma* doubles the past. The main verb carries only the kind of action: *-ki-*, *-na-* or *-me-*.

**Not *\*Walilala* for "they were asleep".** That is "they went to sleep". Use *walikuwa wamelala.*

**English "had" is not *-li-*.** *\*Walifika* for "they had arrived (before I came)" loses the "before". Use *walikuwa wamefika*. (Often the plain past is enough in narrative; use the compound when the order matters.)

**Do not negate *-ki-*.** *\*Alikuwa hakisoma* is not a word. Negate *kuwa*: *Hakuwa akisoma.*

**Short verb *-wa* keeps *ku-* after *-li-*, *-ta-* and *-me-*.** *\*Nilwa*, *\*nitawa* fail: *nilikuwa*, *nitakuwa*, *nimekuwa*. After the negative past it is *hakuwa* (lesson 4.4).

## Connections

English builds the same forms with "be" and "have" (*was reading*, *had read*), and Spanish with *estar* and *haber* (*estaba leyendo*, *había leído*). Swahili uses one auxiliary, *kuwa*, for both families, and inflects the main verb fully instead of using a participle. Japanese 読んでいた "was reading" also stacks a "be" verb on the main verb.

## Exercises

1. Say "we were listening to the radio".
2. Say "they had already eaten" (use plain *-me-* for "had eaten").
3. Translate *Nilipofika, mtoto alikuwa amelala.* (*nilipofika* "when I arrived")
4. Say "at nine o'clock I'll be working" (use *saa tatu* "nine o'clock").
5. Say "he wasn't watching television" in the two possible ways.
6. Say "I had a computer" and "there will be music".
7. Correct *\*Tulikuwa tulisoma.*
8. Correct *\*Wageni walikuwa hawakufika.* (meant: "the guests hadn't arrived yet")

## Worked answers

1. *Tulikuwa tukisikiliza redio* (or *tunasikiliza redio*).
2. *Walikuwa wamekula.* Past of *kuwa* + perfect of a short verb (*ku-* stays after *-me-*).
3. "When I arrived, the child was asleep." A past state.
4. *Saa tatu nitakuwa nikifanya kazi.* (Swahili counts hours from dawn: lesson 5.4.)
5. *Hakuwa akitazama televisheni* (negative on *kuwa* with *-ki-*) or *Alikuwa hatazami televisheni* (negative present on the main verb with *-na-*).
6. *Nilikuwa na kompyuta.* *Kutakuwa na muziki.*
7. The main verb takes the kind of action, not the past: *Tulikuwa tumesoma* "we had studied" or *Tulikuwa tukisoma* "we were studying".
8. "Not yet" with *-me-* is *-ja-* on the main verb: *Wageni walikuwa hawajafika.*
