## The idea

"Already" and "still" are about expectations. "She has already left" says the leaving happened sooner than you might think. "She is still here" says a state goes on longer than you might think. Swahili has verb pieces for both, built from verbs you know.

### Already: -mesha-

"I have already eaten" is *nimeshakula*. Between the perfect *-me-* and the root sits *-sha-*. Almasi and colleagues explain the form: it is *-me-* + *-isha* "finish", contracted. *Nimeshakula* is in origin "I have finished eating". Wiktionary gives the same etymology and an older, uncontracted form *-mekwisha-*; Polomé shows the full two-verb construction it grew from, *amekwisha kwenda* "he has already gone", with *kwisha* followed by an infinitive or a bare stem. Wpgrammar calls *-sha-* "relatively new", derived from *kwisha*. This guide glosses it ANT, for **anterior**: a marker saying an action is complete before the time in question.

So *-sha-* is a second marker in the tense area, after the first. Lesson 4.1's template had one tense slot; *-sha-* shows that a tense slot can hold two pieces, *-me-sha-*. Wpgrammar puts it exactly so: *-sha-* sits "after [other markers] within the TAM slot".

What does it add to *-me-*? Wiktionary calls it a perfect that "indicates that an action has taken place even though one might have expected otherwise"; wpgrammar says it "often imparts the meaning of 'already', emphasising the completeness of the action". The classic use is polite refusal: *Asante, nimeshakula* "thank you, I've already eaten" (wpgrammar).

Almasi and colleagues say short verbs keep *ku-* after *-mesha-*: *nimeshakula*, and their example *jua limeshakuchwa* "the sun has already set".

The negative is the "not yet" of lesson 4.5: *Sijala* "I haven't eaten yet" (Almasi and colleagues).

*-sha-* also combines with other markers. Wpgrammar notes it often follows *-ki-*: *ukishapata kazi* "once you've got a job". In compound tenses it gives "had already": *walikuwa wameshafika* "they had already arrived". On its own, without *-me-* (*nishakula*), it is common in speech but, wpgrammar says, may not be regarded as standard.

### Still: -ngali and bado

"Still" with a noun, an adjective or a place ("I'm still a child", "it's still raw", "she's still here") uses a special form of "be": the subject prefix + *-ngali*. *Ningali mtoto* "I'm still a child", *angali mgonjwa* "she's still ill". Wpgrammar calls it the **continuative** and gives the full set: *ningali, ungali, angali, tungali, mngali, wangali*, and for classes *ungali, ingali, lingali, kingali, vingali, zingali*. It has no tense and no negative. Wiktionary records the proverb *Samaki mkunje angali mbichi* "bend the fish while it is still fresh": teach good habits while children are young. Wiktionary also lists the variant *yungali*, with the class 1 *yu-* of *yuko*.

"Still" with a verb is the adverb *bado* (lesson 2.4) with a positive verb: *Bado ninasoma* "I'm still studying". With *-ja-*, *bado* means "not yet" (lesson 4.5). And "not any more" is a negative verb with *tena* (lesson 2.3): *Sisomi tena* "I don't study any more".

So the four corners are:

- *Nimeshamaliza* "I've already finished";
- *Sijamaliza (bado)* "I haven't finished yet";
- *Bado ninasoma* "I'm still studying";
- *Sisomi tena* "I don't study any more".

## How it is built

Say "the bus has already left".

1. Subject *basi*, class 5: *li-*.
2. Perfect *-me-* + anterior *-sha-*: *-mesha-*.
3. Root *-ondok-*, final *-a*: *li-me-sha-ondok-a*.
4. *Basi limeshaondoka.*

Now "grandfather is still healthy".

1. "Still" with an adjective: the continuative.
2. Subject class 1: *a-ngali*.
3. The adjective *-zima* "whole, healthy" (lesson 5.1) in class 1: *mzima*.
4. *Babu angali mzima.*

And "once you've finished, rest".

1. A condition already fulfilled: *-ki-* + *-sha-*: *u-ki-sha-maliz-a*.
2. Result as a command: *pumzika*.
3. *Ukishamaliza, pumzika.*

## Where it applies

- **"Already" with a present result**: *nimeshakula*, *basi limeshaondoka*, *imeshanyesha* "it has already rained" (Almasi and colleagues).
- **Polite refusals**: *Asante, nimeshashiba* "thanks, I'm already full".
- **"Once, as soon as"** with *-ki-sha-*: *ukishamaliza*, *ukishapata kazi* (wpgrammar).
- **"Had already", "will already have"** with *kuwa*: *walikuwa wameshafika*, *watakuwa wameshaondoka*; wpgrammar: *mtakuwa mmeshazaa watoto* "you will already have had children".
- **The full form** *-mekwisha-* (Wiktionary) and the two-verb *amekwisha kwenda* (Polomé) in writing.
- **"Still" with nouns, adjectives, places**: *ningali mwanafunzi*, *angali mgonjwa*, *mzee angali shambani*, and with classes: *kingali*, *zingali* (wpgrammar's table).
- **"Still" with verbs**: *bado ninasoma*, *bado anafundisha* (lesson 2.4).
- **"Not yet"**: *-ja-* (+ *bado*).
- **"Not any more"**: negative + *tena*: *sisomi tena*, *hakuja tena* "he didn't come again".

## Watch out

**No *-sha-* in the negative.** *\*Sijashakula* and *\*Hatumeshafika* combine a negative with "already". The negative is plain "not yet": *Sijala*, *Hatujafika*.

**Not *\*Nimeshala*.** Short verbs keep *ku-* after *-mesha-*: *nimeshakula* (Almasi and colleagues; wpgrammar).

**Do not drop *-me-* in writing.** *Nishakula* is common speech; wpgrammar says it may not count as standard. Write *nimeshakula*.

**Do not use *-ngali* with a verb.** *\*Ningali ninasoma* is not the standard way to say "I'm still studying". Use *bado*: *Bado ninasoma.* *-ngali* goes with nouns, adjectives and places.

**Do not negate *-ngali*.** *\*Singali mtoto* is not "I'm no longer a child". Say *Mimi si mtoto tena.*

**Not *\*bado* + *-me-* for "not yet".** *Bado nimekula* is not "I haven't eaten yet". "Not yet" needs *-ja-*: *Sijala bado.*

**Do not use *tena* alone for "no longer" with a positive verb.** *Ninasoma tena* means "I'm studying again". "Any more" needs the negative: *Sisomi tena.*

## Connections

German *schon* and *noch*, French *déjà* and *encore*, Spanish *ya* and *todavía* are separate words; Swahili builds "already" into the verb. Japanese もう and まだ match *-sha-* and *bado* closely, including まだ + negative for "not yet" (*bado* + *-ja-*). The growth of a "finish" verb into an "already" marker is common: Wiktionary's history of *-me-* (lesson 4.5) shows Swahili doing it twice.

## Exercises

1. Say "they've already gone home".
2. Say "I haven't paid the rent yet".
3. Say "once you've finished, wash the plates".
4. Say "we are still students" and "the guests are still here".
5. Say "she doesn't work at the bank any more".
6. Translate *Babu ameshastaafu, lakini bado anafanya kazi shambani.*
7. Correct *\*Hajashafika.*
8. Correct *\*Bado nimesoma kitabu hiki* (meant: "I haven't read this book yet").

## Worked answers

1. *Wameshakwenda nyumbani.* *-enda* keeps *kw-* after *-mesha-*, as short verbs keep *ku-* (Almasi and colleagues; Polomé on *kw-*).
2. *Sijalipa kodi bado.*
3. *Ukishamaliza, osha sahani.* *-ki-sha-* "once", then a plain command.
4. *Tungali wanafunzi.* *Wageni wangali hapa.* Subject prefix + *-ngali*.
5. *Hafanyi kazi benki tena.* Negative present + *tena*.
6. "Grandfather has already retired, but he still works on the farm." *-mesha-* "already"; *bado* + positive verb "still".
7. No "already" in the negative: *Hajafika* "he hasn't arrived yet".
8. "Not yet" is *-ja-*: *Sijakisoma kitabu hiki bado.*
