## Before you start

This review teaches nothing new. It covers all of part 4: the template (lesson 4.1), *-li-* and *-ku-* (4.2), *-ta-* (4.3), short verbs (4.4), *-me-* and *-ja-* (4.5), states (4.6), object prefixes and *-ji-* (4.7, 4.8), the subjunctive and its negative (4.10, 4.11), the infinitive (4.12), *hu-* (4.13), *-ki-* (4.14), *-ka-* (4.15), compound tenses (4.16), *-sha-*, *-ngali* and *bado* (4.17), and choosing among them (4.18). Parts 2 and 3 are assumed.

The passage is a short animal tale, constructed for this review in the style of East African trickster stories. It ends with a real saying. Before reading each gloss, ask of every verb: stated or wanted? What kind of action? What time?

## Exercises

1. Translate the tale into natural English without looking at the translation lines.
2. List every verb in lines 1 to 10 with its tense or mood, and say why that one was chosen.
3. In line 2, why *kilikuwa kimekwisha* and not *kilikwisha*?
4. In line 3, explain *twende* and *tukatafute*.
5. In line 6, why *akala* but in line 1 *walikuwa*?
6. In line 8, explain *alikuwa ameshiba*. Why not *alishiba*?
7. In line 10, what does *huwa* add?
8. In the saying, why is it *tenda* but *nenda*?
9. Retell lines 6 and 7 in the future, as a warning: "the hyena will go into the farm, eat bananas, and the farmer will catch him".
10. Say "if the farmer sees you, he will catch you".
11. Say "I want you to come with me, but I don't want the hyena to come".
12. Say "Don't steal! It's better to work."
13. Say "the hare is still hiding; he hasn't come out yet".
14. Say "the hyena used to eat at the farmer's every day".
15. Say "we have been looking for you!"
16. Say "once you've eaten, let's go".
17. Say "the hyena hurt himself".
18. Correct *\*Fisi alikula, alishiba, alilala.* Is it wrong? How would a storyteller say it?
19. Correct *\*Sungura alishauri fisi kutoiba.* (meant: "the hare advised the hyena not to steal")
20. Correct *\*Usikule ndizi!*
21. Correct *\*Fisi hajaweza kukimbia jana.*
22. Correct *\*Mkulima anahukamata wezi.*
23. Correct *\*Kuiba ni mbaya* if it is meant as "stealing is bad" with class 15 agreement.
24. Build slot by slot, naming the slots: "they hadn't caught him yet".

## Worked answers

1. "Once upon a time the hare and the hyena were friends. In the dry season the food had run out. The hyena said to the hare, 'Let's go and look for food.' As they walked along the road, they came upon a banana farm. The hare said, 'Let's not steal! The farmer will catch us.' But the hyena didn't listen to him; he went into the farm, ate bananas and stuffed himself. The farmer arrived and saw the hyena. The hyena was so full he couldn't run. The hare had hidden behind a tree, and got away. Since that day the hyena has always been afraid of farms." (Lessons 4.2 to 4.17.)
2. *walikuwa* (past of *kuwa*: a past state, 4.16); *kilikuwa kimekwisha* (past state, 4.16); *akamwambia* (next event, 4.15); *twende* (proposal, 4.10); *tukatafute* ("go and do", 4.15); *wakitembea* (background, 4.14); *walikuta* (past event, 4.2); *akasema* (4.15); *tusiibe* (negative "let's", 4.11); *atatukamata* (future, 4.3); *hakumsikiliza* (negative past, 4.2); *akaingia*, *akala*, *akashiba*, *akafika*, *akamwona* (chain, 4.15); *alikuwa ameshiba* (past state, 4.6, 4.16); *hakuweza* (negative past, 4.2) + *kukimbia* (infinitive, 4.12); *alikuwa amejificha* (past perfect, 4.16); *akatoroka* (4.15); *huwa anaogopa* (habit, 4.13).
3. The food had run out before the story starts and stayed gone: a past state, time + result, so *kuwa* + *-me-*. *Kilikwisha* would report the running out as an event. Lessons 4.6, 4.16.
4. *Twende*: subjunctive of *-enda* with *tu-* (written *tw-*), "let's go", no *kw-*. *Tukatafute*: *-ka-* + subjunctive *-e*, "and look for", the "go and do" use. Lessons 4.10, 4.15.
5. *-ka-* can carry stress, so the short verb drops *ku-*; *-li-* cannot, so *kuwa* keeps it. Lessons 4.4, 4.15.
6. A past state: he had got full and was full at that moment. *Alishiba* is "he got full", an event. Lessons 4.6, 4.16.
7. "Usually, habitually": *huwa* + *-na-* gives a lasting habit where *anaogopa* alone could be one occasion. Lesson 4.13.
8. *-tenda* is regular: the bare stem is the command. *-enda* is one of the three irregular commands: *nenda*. Lesson 2.7.
9. *Fisi ataingia shambani, atakula ndizi, na mkulima atamkamata.* *-ka-* is for past stories; a future sequence uses *-ta-* (with *ku-* on the short verb). Lessons 4.3, 4.4, 4.15.
10. *Mkulima akikuona, atakukamata.* Lessons 4.7, 4.14.
11. *Ninataka uje nami, lakini sitaki fisi aje.* Different subjects: subjunctive. Lessons 4.11, 3.14.
12. *Usiibe! Afadhali kufanya kazi* (or *ufanye kazi*). Lesson 4.11.
13. *Sungura bado amejificha; hajatoka bado.* "Still" with a verb is *bado*; *-ngali* goes with nouns, adjectives and places. Lessons 4.5, 4.6, 4.17.
14. *Fisi alikuwa akila kwa mkulima kila siku.* *Kuwa* + *-ki-* for a past habit (*kwa* "at someone's" is lesson 5.8). Lesson 4.16.
15. *Tumekuwa tukikutafuta!* Perfect of *kuwa* + *-ki-*. Lesson 4.16.
16. *Ukishakula, twende.* *-ki-sha-* "once", then a proposal. Lessons 4.10, 4.17.
17. *Fisi alijiumiza.* Reflexive *-ji-*. Lesson 4.8.
18. Not wrong, but flat. A storyteller chains with *-ka-*: *Fisi alikula, akashiba, akalala.* Lesson 4.15.
19. A different subject after *-shauri*: object prefix on the first verb, negative subjunctive on the second: *Sungura alimshauri fisi asiibe.* Lessons 4.7, 4.11.
20. No *ku-* in the subjunctive: *Usile ndizi!* Lessons 4.4, 4.11.
21. *Jana* closes the chance: negative past. *Fisi hakuweza kukimbia jana.* Lesson 4.5.
22. The habitual has no subject prefix and no *-na-*: *Mkulima huwakamata wezi* (or *Mkulima anawakamata wezi*). Lesson 4.13.
23. An infinitive subject takes class 15 agreement: *Kuiba ni kubaya.* Lesson 4.12.
24. Past state, not yet: *walikuwa* + *-ja-*. Main verb: *ha-* (1), *-wa-* "they" (2), *-ja-* (4), *-m-* "him" (6), *-kamat-* (7), *-a* (9): *Walikuwa hawajamkamata.* Lessons 4.1, 4.5, 4.7, 4.16.
