## The idea

Lesson 3.3 promised that every adjective until now would start with a consonant. That kept the prefixes clean: *ki-* + *-kubwa* is *kikubwa*, *wa-* + *-zuri* is *wazuri*. About a dozen common adjectives start with a vowel instead: *-ema* "good, kind", *-ingi* "many, much", *-ekundu* "red", *-eupe* "white", *-eusi* "black". When the prefix meets them, two vowels touch.

Swahili does not always tolerate that. Polomé describes what happens (§2.2.1): the vowel of the prefix and the vowel of the stem either **merge** into one vowel, or the prefix vowel **turns into a consonant sound** (y or w) and leans on the stem. You have seen both already. *Wangu* "my" (lesson 3.10) is *wa-* + *-angu* with nothing changed; *changu* is *ki-* + *-angu*, where the *i* became a glide and *ky* became *ch*; *letu* "our" is *li-* + *-etu*, where the *i* vanished. Vowel-stem adjectives follow the same sound rules, so they look irregular only until you see the rules.

A second group is awkward for another reason. *-pya* "new" has a stem of one syllable, and Swahili does not like a one-syllable word. So where other adjectives drop their prefix (class 5, class 9), *-pya* keeps one: *jipya*, *mpya*.

A third group never agrees at all. *Safi* "clean" and *ghali* "expensive" (lesson 3.3 mentioned them) are **invariable adjectives**: adjectives that keep one form whatever the noun. Almasi and colleagues note that most are borrowed, many from Arabic; Wiktionary's etymologies confirm it word by word (*maskini* from Arabic *miskīn*, *karimu* from *karīm*). A borrowed word arrives without a Bantu prefix slot, and it stays that way.

## How it is built

### The vowel rules

Take the stem *-ingi* "many" and walk through the prefixes.

1. **a + i, a + e give e.** *wa-* + *-ingi* is *wengi* "many (people)"; *ma-* + *-ingi* is *mengi*; *wa-* + *-ema* is *wema*; *ma-* + *-ekundu* is *mekundu*. Polomé gives *wageni wengi* "many strangers" and *mawe mekundu* "red stones".
2. **i + i gives i.** *ki-* + *-ingi* is *kingi*; *vi-* + *-ingi* is *vingi*; *mi-* + *-ingi* is *mingi* (Polomé: *miwa mingi* "many sugar canes").
3. **i before e becomes y, and ky becomes ch.** *vi-* + *-ema* is *vyema*; *mi-* + *-ema* is *myema* (Polomé: *miti myema* "good trees"); *ki-* + *-ema* is *chema*, exactly as *ki-* + *-angu* is *changu*.
4. **m before a vowel becomes mw.** Classes 1, 3, 11 and 18: *mwema*, *mwekundu*, *mwingi*. You know this from nouns: *mwalimu*, *mwaka*.
5. **Class 9 and 10: ny- before the vowel.** *nyekundu*, *nyeupe*, *nyingi*. This is the nasal of lesson 3.6 meeting a vowel (Polomé §2.2.2.2).
6. **Class 5: j- before the vowel.** *jekundu*, *jingi*, *jema*. Polomé traces this *j-* to the old class 5 prefix that also gives *jicho* "eye".
7. **a + o does not merge.** *-ovu* "evil" gives *waovu* "evil people" and *maovu*; Polomé's example is *maisha maovu* "an evil life" (§2.2.1.1), and he also gives class 3 *mji mwovu* "a wicked town". (The one exception, *wote* "all", is lesson 5.5.) The sources disagree on how far *-ovu* goes: Wikipedia's "Swahili grammar" and Wiktionary say it has only the class 1/2 forms *mwovu*, *waovu*, because it describes only people, yet Wiktionary's own examples include class 5 *jicho ovu* "evil eye" (without *j-*). Use *mwovu* and *waovu* freely; treat other classes as possible but less common.

**The one true exception.** By rule 5, *-ema* in class 9 should be *nyema*. It is *njema*: *safari njema* "a good journey". Polomé says plainly that this exception "cannot be accounted for". Learn it as a single fact.

### One-syllable stems

*-pya* "new" follows lesson 3.3 in most classes: *mpya* (class 1), *wapya*, *kipya*, *vipya*, *mapya*. Two classes differ, both checked in Wiktionary's table:

- class 5: *jipya*, not *pya*. Polomé (note 31) states the rule: one-syllable stems take *ji-* in class 5, as one-syllable nouns do (*jicho*, *jino*).
- classes 9 and 10: *mpya*, not *pya*. The nasal surfaces as *m* before *p*. Almasi and colleagues list it among the special forms; it is the same holding-on to a prefix, though the sources consulted state the reason only for class 5.

### Invariable adjectives

Nothing to build. Put the word after the noun: *kisu safi*, *nyumba ghali*, *watu maskini*. With *ni* it is the same word: *Nyumba hii ni ghali.* Thompson and Schleicher give the test (p. 117): a dictionary writes an agreeing adjective with a hyphen (*-dogo*) and an invariable one without (*bora*).

### Chain of thought

Say "the red cups are on the table".

1. "Cups": *vikombe*, class 8.
2. "Red": *-ekundu*, a vowel stem. Class 8 *vi-* + *e*: the *i* becomes *y*: *vyekundu*.
3. Subject prefix class 8 *vi-*, place *-ko*, "on the table" *mezani*.
4. *Vikombe vyekundu viko mezani.*

## Where it applies

- **Every vowel-stem adjective in every class**, by the seven rules: *-ema*, *-ingi*, *-ekundu*, *-eupe*, *-eusi*, *-embamba* "thin, narrow", *-epesi* "light, easy", *-erevu* "clever, cunning", *-ovu* "evil". The table below gives the full set for three of them.
- **The colour words that agree** are exactly the three vowel stems *-eupe*, *-eusi*, *-ekundu*. Other colours are nouns joined with *-a* (*-a kijani* "green", Wiktionary), which lesson 5.2 teaches.
- **Animate nouns** take class 1/2 forms of these adjectives as of any other: *ndege mweupe* "a white bird" but *ndege nyeupe* "a white plane" (lesson 3.9).
- **Classes 11 and 14** take the *mw-* form: *ukuta mweupe* "a white wall"; the plural *kuta* is class 10: *kuta nyeupe*.
- **The place classes**: *mahali pema* "a good place" (Wiktionary's class 16 form *pema*).
- **Adjectives standing for a noun**, as in lesson 3.3: *Wengi wamekuja* "many (people) have come"; *Ninataka nyeupe* "I want white ones (clothes)". Polomé's *lete vyeupe vitano* "bring five white ones" means cups.
- **Adjectives that are nouns too.** *Mkuu* (class 1 of *-kuu* "great, chief") means "head, chief, leader". *Mgeni* "guest, stranger" (lesson 2.1) is class 1 of *-geni* "foreign, strange": *lugha ngeni* "a foreign language". *Wema* is also a class 14 noun "goodness", the *u-ema* of *tenda wema* (lesson 4.19).
- **-chache "few"** is plural by meaning, so Wiktionary gives no singular forms: *watu wachache* "few people", *siku chache* "a few days".
- **-zima** means "whole" of things and "healthy, alive and well" of people. Wiktionary gives "well" as its sense in greetings.
- **-vivu "lazy"** has class 9 *mvivu*: the nasal is *m* before *v*, as before *b* in *mbaya* (Wiktionary table).
- **Invariable adjectives** anywhere an adjective goes: after the noun, after *ni*, standing alone (*Wewe ni hodari* "you are capable"). The common ones: *safi*, *ghali*, *bora*, *rahisi*, *tayari* (known); *maskini* "poor", *tajiri* "rich", *hodari* "able, brave", *karimu* "generous", *imara* "strong, firm", *wazi* "open, clear", *hai* "alive", *kamili* "complete" (also "o'clock exactly", lesson 5.4).
- **Several adjectives on one noun.** The adjective most tightly bound to the noun comes first. Polomé's example: *nguo nyekundu ndefu* "red dresses that are long", but *nguo ndefu nyekundu* "long dresses that are red".

## Watch out

**Do not leave the two vowels side by side.** *\*Waingi*, *\*maema*, *\*viema* are not words. Apply the rules: *wengi*, *mema*, *vyema*.

**Do not glide where Swahili merges, or merge where it glides.** *\*Myingi* is wrong for class 4: *i + i* merges, *mingi*. But *\*miema* is also wrong: before *e* the *i* glides, *myema*.

**Do not write *\*kiema* or *\*kiekundu*.** Class 7 *ki-* before *e* becomes *ch-*: *chema*, *chekundu*. The verb prefix stays *ki-* before a consonant: *Kikombe chekundu kimeanguka*.

**Do not regularise *njema*.** *\*Safari nyema* follows the rule and is still wrong. *Safari njema.* Only *-ema* does this; *nyeupe*, *nyingi*, *nyekundu* are regular.

**Do not drop the prefix of *-pya*.** *\*Gari pya* and *\*nyumba pya* copy *gari zuri* and *nyumba kubwa*. One-syllable *-pya* keeps a prefix: *gari jipya*, *nyumba mpya*.

**Do not contract a + o.** *\*Wovu* for "evil people" by analogy with *wengi* is wrong: *waovu*.

**Do not put a prefix on a borrowed adjective.** *\*Mmaskini*, *\*kisafi*, *\*mtajiri* (as an adjective) are wrong. *Mtoto maskini*, *kisu safi*. (*Tajiri* is also a noun "rich person", class 5/6 *matajiri* in Wiktionary; that is a noun, not agreement.)

**Do not give a person class 9 colour forms.** English and Spanish do not mark this. *\*Ndege nyeusi* for a black bird describes a black aeroplane. A creature takes class 1: *ndege mweusi*.

**Do not use *-chache* for "a little" of one thing.** *\*Maji machache* is odd for "a little water"; *-chache* counts. Use *kidogo* (lesson 2.7): *maji kidogo*.

**Do not confuse *-ingi* with *sana*.** *Sana* "very, a lot" goes with verbs and adjectives: *anasoma sana* "she studies a lot". *-ingi* agrees with a noun: *vitabu vingi*. *\*Vitabu sana* is wrong.

## Connections

Arabic speakers will recognise *maskini* (*miskīn*), *karimu* (*karīm*), *kamili* (*kāmil*) and *hai* (*ḥayy*), and Spanish keeps the first of these as *mezquino*. Spanish also shows why a borrowed adjective can stay invariable: *rosa* and *naranja* as colours often do not agree (*camisas rosa*), because they entered as nouns.

## Exercises

1. Say "many trees" and "many children".
2. Say "a white cup" and "white cups".
3. Say "the red car is new".
4. Translate *Watoto wachache wanasoma Kiarabu.*
5. Correct *\*Nina habari nyema.*
6. Correct *\*Nyumba pya ni kubwa.*
7. Correct *\*Huyu ni mtu mmaskini.*
8. Say "this road is narrow" (*njia*, class 9).
9. Why is "many" *mingi* in class 4 but "good" *myema*?
10. Say "a black dog" and explain your prefix.

## Worked answers

1. *Mti* is class 3; the plural *miti* is class 4: *mi-* + *-ingi*, *i + i* merge: *miti mingi*. *Watoto* is class 2: *wa-* + *-ingi* gives *wengi*: *watoto wengi*.
2. *Kikombe* is class 7: *ki-* + *-eupe* gives *cheupe*: *kikombe cheupe*. Class 8: *vi-* + *e* gives *vy-*: *vikombe vyeupe*.
3. *Gari* is class 5. "Red": *j-* + *-ekundu*, *jekundu*. "New": one-syllable stem, class 5 *jipya*. *Gari jekundu ni jipya.*
4. *Wachache*: class 2 of *-chache* "few". "Few children are studying Arabic" (or "a few children are studying Arabic").
5. *Habari* is class 9 and *-ema* has the exceptional class 9 form *njema*: *Nina habari njema* "I have good news".
6. Class 9 of *-pya* is *mpya*: *Nyumba mpya ni kubwa.*
7. *Maskini* is invariable: *Huyu ni mtu maskini.*
8. Class 9 *ny-* before the vowel: *Njia hii ni nyembamba.*
9. Two different rules. *i + i* merge into one *i* (*mingi*); *i* before *e* turns into *y* (*myema*). Polomé gives both, *miwa mingi* and *miti myema*.
10. *Mbwa* is class 9/10 but an animal, so class 1 agreement: *mbwa mweusi*, with *m-* becoming *mw-* before the vowel. *\*Mbwa nyeusi* would treat the dog as a thing.
