## The idea

Count the agreeing adjectives you now know: about forty stems, and the grammars consulted list few more. Wikipedia's grammar article speaks of a "paucity of true adjectives". English has thousands. So how does Swahili say "strong", "clever", "modern", "green", "dangerous", "left-handed"?

It describes a thing by what it **has** or by what **kind** it is. "A strong man" is "a man having strength", *mtu mwenye nguvu*. "A modern phone" is "a phone of the present style", *simu ya kisasa*. "Green" is "of the leaf", *-a kijani*. You met the second pattern in lesson 3.11 (*maji ya moto* "hot water"). This lesson adds the first, and shows how far both reach.

Almasi and colleagues treat both as adjectives: for them, any word or phrase that describes a noun is an adjective, and they call the *-a* type "compound adjectives". Whatever you call them, they behave the same way: they come after the noun, and their first piece agrees with it.

**-enye** is a stem meaning "having, possessing, with" (Wiktionary calls it the **ornative** particle; *ornative* means "furnishing with something"). It is old: Wiktionary derives it from a Common Bantu *\*-jéné*. It cannot stand alone. It needs a prefix in front and a noun after: *-enye nguvu* "having strength".

## How it is built

### The prefix of -enye

*-enye* takes the same prefixes as the *-a* of lesson 3.11, and the vowels meet by the rules of lesson 5.1: *wa* + *enye* gives *wenye*, *cha* + *enye* gives *chenye*, *za* + *enye* gives *zenye*. There is one surprise. Class 1 is *mwenye*, not *wenye*: it takes the *mw-* of an adjective (*mwema*), while class 2 is *wenye*. Almasi and colleagues point this out: apart from class 1, "-enye" follows the *-a* forms exactly.

So, beside *-a*:

- class 7: *cha* → *chenye*; class 8: *vya* → *vyenye*;
- class 5: *la* → *lenye*; class 6: *ya* → *yenye*;
- class 9: *ya* → *yenye*; class 10: *za* → *zenye*;
- class 3 and 11: *wa* → *wenye*; class 4: *ya* → *yenye*;
- class 1: *wa* but *mwenye*; class 2: *wa* → *wenye*;
- places: *penye*, *kwenye*, *mwenye*.

### Chain of thought with -enye

Say "I want a room with a big window".

1. The thing described: *chumba*, class 7.
2. "With, having": class 7 *chenye*.
3. What it has: *dirisha kubwa*. *Dirisha* is class 5, so its own adjective is class 5: *kubwa*.
4. *Ninataka chumba chenye dirisha kubwa.*

Notice the two agreements. *Chenye* agrees with *chumba*; *kubwa* agrees with *dirisha*. Each word looks to its own noun.

### Chain of thought with -a

Say "her phone is modern".

1. *Simu yake*, class 9.
2. "Modern" is "of the present style": *ya* (class 9) + *kisasa*.
3. As a predicate, put *ni* before the phrase: *Simu yake ni ya kisasa.*

*Kisasa* is *sasa* "now" with a *ki-* in front. Wiktionary calls this *ki-* "adverbial": it turns a word into "in the manner of". Wikipedia's grammar notes the same *ki-* in learned words like *-a kimataifa* "international". So *-a* + *ki-* word means "of the X kind": *-a kitoto* "childish", *-a kike* "female, for women", *-a kiume* "male, for men" (Almasi and colleagues, Wiktionary).

### Which one?

Ask: does the noun **have** the thing, or **belong to a kind**? A bag with money in it is *mfuko wenye pesa*; a money bag, whatever is inside, is *mfuko wa pesa*. A man with strength is *mtu mwenye nguvu*; there is no "strength kind" of man.

## Where it applies

**-enye + noun:**

- **Qualities people have**: *mwenye nguvu* "strong", *mwenye akili* "clever", *mwenye busara* "wise", *mwenye elimu* "educated". Wikipedia's grammar gives *-enye nywele fupi* "short-haired".
- **Parts and contents of things**: *chumba chenye dirisha*, *mfuko wenye pesa*, *maji yenye chumvi* "salty water", *chakula chenye ladha nzuri* "food with a good flavour".
- **The noun after -enye may carry its own words**: *chumba chenye dirisha kubwa*, *mtu mwenye nywele ndefu* (*nywele* is class 10, so *ndefu*).
- **As a noun, "owner, the one with"**: *mwenye duka* "shopkeeper", *mwenye nyumba* "landlord", plural *wenye*: *wenye magari* "car owners". The proverb *Mwenye kisima hapangi foleni*.
- **Place forms**: *penye* "where there is" in the proverb. *Kwenye* became a preposition "at, on, in" (lesson 5.8).
- **With an infinitive**: Almasi and colleagues allow *-enye* + infinitive, as in their *helikopta yenye kufika* "the arriving helicopter". It is rare; a relative verb (part 6) is the normal way.

**-a + word:**

- **-a + noun of a quality**: *maji ya moto*, *mahali pa hatari*, *chakula cha bure* "free food" (Almasi and colleagues: *bure* alone is "useless", *-a bure* "free of charge").
- **-a + ki- word, "of the X kind"**: *-a kisasa* "modern", *-a kitoto* "childish", *-a kike*, *-a kiume*. Languages work the same way: *-a Kiswahili*.
- **Colours other than red, white, black**: *-a kijani* "green", *-a kijivu* "grey" (Wiktionary: from *jivu* "ash"). Borrowed colour words vary. *Kahawia* "brown" (from *kahawa* "coffee") is an invariable adjective; "blue" takes *-a*: Wiktionary lists *-a bluu*, and Thompson and Schleicher print *shati la buluu* (p. 117).
- **-a + place or direction word**: *mlango wa mbele* "front door", *chumba cha nyuma* "back room", *mkono wa kulia* "right hand", *upande wa kushoto* "left side". Wiktionary explains *kulia* "right" as coming from *mkono wa kulia*, the hand you eat with.
- **-a + infinitive, "for doing"**: *maji ya kunywa*, *chumba cha kulala* (lesson 3.11).
- **-a + time word**: *gazeti la leo*, *-a zamani* "old, former" (lesson 3.11).
- **As a predicate**, with *ni* and *si*: *Simu yake ni ya kisasa*, *Chakula hiki si cha bure.*
- **Standing alone for the noun**: *Ninataka ya kijani* "I want the green one" (a class 9 thing understood), as adjectives do (lesson 5.1).

## Watch out

**Do not use *wenye* for one person.** *\*Mtu wenye nguvu* follows the *-a* pattern (*mtu wa*) too far. Class 1 is *mwenye*: *mtu mwenye nguvu*.

**Do not make -enye agree with the noun after it.** *\*Chumba lenye dirisha* takes *l-* from *dirisha*. *-enye* looks back: *chumba chenye dirisha*.

**Do not leave -enye without a noun.** *\*Mtu huyu ni mwenye* says "this man is having". Something must follow: *Mtu huyu ni mwenye nguvu* "this man is strong".

**Do not put an adjective stem after -enye or -a.** *\*Mtu mwenye mrefu* and *\*mtu wa mrefu* try to say "a tall man". An adjective agrees directly: *mtu mrefu*. *-enye* and *-a* take nouns (and *-a* also infinitives and place words).

**Do not use -a for what a thing contains.** *\*Chupa ya maji* is fine for "a water bottle", but if you mean "a bottle with water in it now", say *chupa yenye maji*. The *-a* phrase names the kind; *-enye* names the contents.

**Do not use a bare colour noun.** *\*Shati kijani* puts two nouns side by side. Join them: *shati la kijani*. Only the invariable colour words go bare: *shati kahawia*.

**Do not translate English "with" by *-enye* for company or tools.** *-enye* is "having as a feature". "I went with my brother" and "write with a pen" use *na* and *kwa* (lessons 3.14 and 5.8).

**Do not confuse *mwenye* "owner" with *mwenyewe* "himself, the owner himself".** Almasi and colleagues note that *mwenyewe* can also mean "owner"; the "self" sense is lesson 5.5. *Mwenye duka* is "the shopkeeper"; *mwenye duka mwenyewe* "the shopkeeper himself".

## Connections

French and Spanish do much the same with *de*: *une femme de caractère*, *un hombre de confianza*, *agua de coco*. Swahili's *-a* is that *de*, but it agrees. *-enye* has no exact match in French or Spanish; the nearest is Arabic *ḏū* "possessor of" (*ḏū māl* "rich", literally "having wealth"), which, like *mwenye*, also works as a noun "owner". Japanese *no* links nouns the same way (*ki no ha* "leaf of a tree"), but puts the describer first.

## Exercises

1. Say "a clever child" with *-enye*.
2. Say "a bag with money (in it)" and "a money bag".
3. Say "the red shirt" and "the green shirt".
4. Translate *Mgeni mwenye busara alisikiliza.*
5. Translate *Wenye magari wanalipa kodi.*
6. Correct *\*Kitabu yenye picha kiko wapi?*
7. Correct *\*Mwalimu wenye nguvu.*
8. Say "the right hand is dirty".
9. Explain the two agreements in *nyumba yenye milango mipya*.
10. Why can you say *mtu mrefu* but not *\*mtu mwenye mrefu*?

## Worked answers

1. *Mtoto* is class 1: *mwenye*. *Mtoto mwenye akili.*
2. Contents: *mfuko wenye pesa* (*mfuko* class 3, *wenye*). Kind: *mfuko wa pesa*.
3. Red is an agreeing adjective (lesson 5.1); *shati* is class 5, so *jekundu*: *shati jekundu*. Green is an *-a* phrase, class 5 *la*: *shati la kijani*.
4. *Mgeni* class 1, *mwenye busara* "having wisdom". "The wise guest listened."
5. *Wenye* as a noun, class 2: "car owners". "Car owners pay tax."
6. *Kitabu* is class 7: *chenye*. *Kitabu chenye picha kiko wapi?* "Where is the book with pictures?"
7. One person: *mwalimu mwenye nguvu*.
8. *Mkono* is class 3: *wa kulia*. The adjective and the verb are class 3 too: *Mkono wa kulia ni mchafu.*
9. *Yenye* is class 9, agreeing with *nyumba*. *Mipya* is class 4, agreeing with *milango*. Each word agrees with its own noun.
10. *-refu* is an adjective stem, which agrees directly with the noun. *-enye* must be followed by a noun, a thing possessed. Lessons 3.3 and 5.2.
