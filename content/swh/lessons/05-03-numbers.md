## The idea

Swahili numbers come in two layers, and the layers explain the grammar.

The old Bantu numbers are **adjective stems**. *-moja* "one", *-wili* "two", *-tatu* "three", *-nne* "four", *-tano* "five" and *-nane* "eight" take the adjective prefix of the noun they count, like *-kubwa*: *vitabu vitatu* "three books", *watoto watatu* "three children". Polomé lists them among the adjectives.

The others do not agree. *Sita* "six", *saba* "seven" and *tisa* "nine" are borrowed from Arabic (*sitta*, *sabʕa*, *tisʕa*; Wiktionary), and like the borrowed adjectives of lesson 5.1 they never take a prefix: *vitabu sita*, *watoto sita*. Wiktionary notes that *sita* and *saba* displaced older native words, and Polomé still records an old *kenda* beside *tisa* for "nine". *Kumi* "ten" is native, but it was a noun, "a ten" (Wiktionary still lists *kumi*, plural *makumi*), and a noun does not take an adjective prefix. So it is invariable too.

That gives the rule Almasi and colleagues state: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 8 agree; 6, 7, 9 and 10 do not.

## How it is built

### Counting aloud

When you count with no noun (*moja, mbili, tatu, nne, tano, sita, saba, nane, tisa, kumi*), you use the forms with no visible prefix. These are the class 9 forms: Wiktionary lists *mbili* as the class 9/10 form of *-wili*, and *tatu*, *nne*, *tano*, *nane* also as class 9 nouns. The same bare forms are used for telephone numbers, prices and sums. "Zero" is *sifuri*, from Arabic *ṣifr* (Wiktionary).

### Why "two" is *mbili*

The stem is *-wili*. In class 9 and 10 the nasal prefix meets the *w*, and *n* + *w* becomes *mb* (Polomé §2.2.2.2: the same change gives *mbili* and *mbati* "hut poles" from *uwati*). In every other class you see *-wili*: *wawili*, *miwili*, *mawili*, *viwili*.

### Counting a noun

Say "the farmer has three goats".

1. *Mkulima ana* "the farmer has" (lesson 2.6).
2. "Goats": *mbuzi*, class 10, but animals take class 1/2 agreement (lesson 3.9).
3. "Three": *-tatu*, an agreeing number, class 2: *watatu*.
4. The number follows the noun: *mbuzi watatu*.
5. *Mkulima ana mbuzi watatu.*

Polomé (§2.6.1.1) gives the same rule with a number: *mitume wawili walikufa* "two apostles died", class 4 noun, class 2 number.

Now "seven goats": *saba* is invariable. *Mkulima ana mbuzi saba.* Nothing else to do.

### Singular and plural

*-moja* goes with singular nouns, so it has singular prefixes: *mtu mmoja*, *kitu kimoja*, *jicho moja* (class 5, no prefix, like *zuri*). The others go with plurals: *watu wawili*, *vitu vitano*. *-moja* also means "same" (Wiktionary): *Tunaishi mji mmoja* "we live in the same town", or "in one town", from context.

### First, second, third

Swahili has no separate ordinal words. It uses the *-a* of lesson 3.11 with a number: *mtoto wa tano* "the fifth child", *kitabu cha tatu* "the third book" (Polomé §2.5.2.1). Two of them are special: "first" is *-a kwanza*, with *kwanza* "first, to begin" (lesson 4.18), not *moja*; "second" is *-a pili*, with *pili*, a second word for "two" that Wiktionary describes as inherited and used only as an abstract value, never as an agreeing adjective. From "third" on, the counting form follows *-a*: *-a tatu*, *-a nne*, *-a sita*.

### Order in the noun phrase

The number usually comes after the adjectives: *nguo nyekundu mbili* "two red dresses". Polomé (§2.6.2.1) explains why: "red" defines the dresses closely, "two" counts the red dresses. If you want "the two dresses, and red ones", the order flips: *nguo mbili nyekundu*. A possessive comes first: *nguo zake nyekundu mbili* "her two red dresses".

## Where it applies

- **Counting objects in every class**, with the adjective prefixes of lesson 3.15: *mtu mmoja*, *watu watatu*, *mti mmoja*, *miti mitano*, *jicho moja*, *macho mawili*, *kiti kimoja*, *viti vinane*, *nyumba moja*, *nyumba nne* (Almasi and colleagues, table 18.1).
- **People and animals in any class** take class 1/2 forms: *mbuzi watatu*, *rafiki wawili*. (The sources disagree here. Almasi and colleagues print *rafiki thelathini na nne* "thirty-four friends" and say *nne* has no prefix because *rafiki* is a class 9/10 noun. Polomé's rule, with *mitume wawili*, gives class 2: *rafiki wanne*. This guide follows Polomé; expect both in texts.)
- **Class 11 singulars**: Wiktionary gives *mmoja* (*ukuta mmoja*), as for adjectives; Almasi and colleagues print *unywele moja*. Use *mmoja*; their plurals are class 10, *kuta mbili*.
- **Places**: *mahali pamoja* "one place". *Pamoja* "together" (lesson 3.14) is this class 16 form, "in one place". Almasi and colleagues also give *mahali pawili*, *kuwili*, *mwili* for two places.
- **Infinitives as nouns**: *kusoma kumoja* "one reading" (Almasi and colleagues).
- **Numbers standing alone for a noun**: *Wawili wamefika* "two (people) have arrived"; *Ninataka vitatu* "I want three (of the class 8 things)". Polomé gives *lete vyeupe vitano* "bring five white ones".
- **Counting aloud, sums, phone numbers, prices**: the bare forms *moja, mbili, tatu*. *Sifuri* for zero.
- **Ordinals** with *-a*: *-a kwanza*, *-a pili*, *-a tatu* ... *-a kumi*. "Last" is *-a mwisho* (*mwisho* "end", lesson 4.2).
- **Times**: *mara moja* "once" (and "at once"), *mara mbili* "twice", *mara ya kwanza* "the first time".
- **Numbers above ten** add the units with *na*: *kumi na moja* "eleven", and an agreeing unit still agrees: Polomé's *visu kumi na kimoja* "eleven knives". Lesson 5.4 continues.
- **"How many?"** is *-ngapi*, which agrees like *-tatu*: lesson 5.10.

## Watch out

**Do not use the counting form with a noun that needs agreement.** *\*Watoto mbili* uses the class 10 form for class 2. Say *watoto wawili*. *Mbili* is right only with class 9/10 things: *kalamu mbili*.

**Do not give 6, 7, 9 or 10 a prefix.** *\*Vitabu visita* and *\*watu wasaba* treat Arabic loans as Bantu stems. *Vitabu sita*, *watu saba*, *watu kumi*.

**Do not put the number first.** English, Spanish and French say "two children", *dos niños*. *\*Wawili watoto* is wrong. *Watoto wawili.*

**Do not give a person or animal the class 10 form.** *\*Mbwa mbili* counts dogs as if they were pens. *Mbwa wawili.*

**Do not put a prefix on class 5 "one".** *\*Jicho jimoja* or *\*jicho limoja* copy *jipya* or the verb prefix *li-*. *-moja* has two syllables and behaves like *-zuri*: *jicho moja*.

**Do not use *moja* or *mbili* for "first" and "second".** *\*Siku ya moja* and *\*mwezi wa mbili* are wrong. *Siku ya kwanza*, *mwezi wa pili*.

**Do not use *pili* to count.** *\*Mara pili* for "twice" is wrong; *pili* never counts a noun (Wiktionary: use *-wili* for that); you will meet it in *-a pili*. *Mara mbili* "twice", *mara ya pili* "the second time".

**Do not agree the ordinal with the number.** *\*Kitabu cha vitatu* puts a class 8 prefix on the number. After *-a* the number is in its bare form: *kitabu cha tatu*.

**Do not read *mwili* as a number by default.** *Mwili* "body" (lesson 1.6) is a noun; *mwili* "two (inside places)" is a rare class 18 form. Context tells them apart.

## Connections

Arabic readers will see *sita*, *saba*, *tisa* and *sifuri* at once, and Spanish *cifra* and French *chiffre* come from the same *ṣifr*. The Bantu layer is shared across the family: Wiktionary traces *kumi* to Proto-Bantu *\*ìkʊ́mì* and *-moja* to *\*-mòì*. Japanese also has two layers of numbers, native *hitotsu, futatsu* and Chinese *ichi, ni*, but there both layers cover one to ten side by side; Swahili kept native 1 to 5, 8 and 10 and filled only the gaps with loans.

## Exercises

1. Say "three cups" and "five chairs".
2. Say "two trees" and "two houses".
3. Say "I have seven brothers" (*kaka*, animate class 9/10).
4. Translate *Wanafunzi wanane wamefaulu.*
5. Say "the second question was difficult".
6. Correct *\*Nilinunua machungwa masita.*
7. Correct *\*Watoto mbili wanacheza.*
8. Correct *\*Hii ni mara ya mbili.*
9. Say "one egg" and "four eggs" and explain the difference in prefixes.
10. Put in order: *mbili*, *nyeupe*, *yangu*, *kalamu* ("my two white pens").

## Worked answers

1. *Kikombe* class 7: plural *vikombe vitatu*. *Kiti* class 7: *viti vitano*.
2. *Miti miwili* (class 4 *mi-* on *-wili*). *Nyumba mbili* (class 10, *n* + *w* gives *mb*).
3. *Saba* is invariable, so no animate prefix is needed: *Nina kaka saba.*
4. *Wanane*: class 2 "eight". "Eight students have passed."
5. *Swali* class 5: *la pili*. *Swali la pili lilikuwa gumu.* (*Gumu*: class 5 adjective, no prefix.)
6. *Sita* is invariable: *Nilinunua machungwa sita.*
7. People take class 2: *Watoto wawili wanacheza.*
8. "Second" is *pili*: *Hii ni mara ya pili.*
9. *Yai* is class 5, plural *mayai* class 6. "One" is singular and class 5 has no adjective prefix: *yai moja*. "Four" is plural: *mayai manne*.
10. Possessive, then adjective, then number: *kalamu zangu nyeupe mbili*.
