## The idea

Three things in this lesson look odd from outside and are completely regular from inside.

**Numbers above ten are built with *na* "and, with".** Eleven is *kumi na moja* "ten and one". The tens from twenty up, and the words for hundred and thousand, are Arabic (Polomé: "the tens are all Arabic"), so they are invariable like *sita*. But the unit at the end is still a Swahili number, and if it is one of the agreeing ones it still agrees: *vitabu kumi na kimoja* "eleven books".

**The clock starts at dawn.** Almasi and colleagues explain it by geography. Near the equator the sun rises at about six and sets at about six all year. So the day's first hour ends at seven in the morning, and seven o'clock is *saa moja* "hour one". Counting goes on to *saa kumi na mbili* "hour twelve" at six in the evening; then the night's hours start again at *saa moja*. Swahili time is English time minus six: *saa mbili* is eight o'clock, *saa sita* is noon or midnight.

**The week starts on Saturday.** *Jumamosi* "Saturday" is *juma* "week" + *mosi*, an inherited Bantu word for "one" that survives beside *-moja* (Wiktionary). Sunday is "week two", *Jumapili*, Monday "week three", *Jumatatu*, and so on to Wednesday, *Jumatano*. Thursday and Friday keep their Arabic names, *Alhamisi* (*al-ḵamīs* "the fifth") and *Ijumaa* (*al-jumʕa*, the day of congregation). *Juma* itself is from Arabic *jumʕa* (Wiktionary), the same word as *Ijumaa*.

## How it is built

### Numbers from 11 to a million

1. Tens: *kumi* 10, *ishirini* 20, *thelathini* 30, *arobaini* 40, *hamsini* 50, *sitini* 60, *sabini* 70, *themanini* 80, *tisini* 90.
2. Add a unit with *na*: *ishirini na tano* 25, *tisini na tisa* 99.
3. Hundreds and thousands: *mia* "hundred" and *elfu* "thousand" come first, and the number of them follows, because they were nouns, "a hundred, a thousand" (Wiktionary lists *mia*, plural *mamia*, and *elfu*, plural *maelfu*): *mia tano* 500 is "hundreds five", *elfu tatu* 3,000.
4. Join the parts, largest first, with *na* before the last part: *mia moja na tisa* 109; *elfu mbili na ishirini na sita* 2,026.
5. *Laki* is 100,000 (from Hindi *lākh* through Arabic, Wiktionary), *milioni* a million.

Almasi and colleagues point out a trap: *elfu kumi na nne* can mean 10,004 ("thousands ten, and four") or 14,000 ("thousands fourteen"). Many speakers now avoid it by putting the count first, *kumi na nne elfu* 14,000, like English.

Polomé adds that the Arabic teens *edashara* 11 and *thenashara* 12 are sometimes heard. *Kumi na moja* is the normal form.

### Agreement in big numbers

Only the last unit can agree, and only if it is 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 or 8. Say "thirty-five mangoes": *maembe* is class 6; *thelathini* stays; *-tano* takes class 6: *maembe thelathini na matano* (Polomé's example).

### Telling the time

Say "it is 8.30 in the morning".

1. 8 minus 6 is 2: *saa mbili*.
2. Half: *na nusu* "and a half".
3. Which part of the day: *asubuhi*.
4. "It is": *ni*. *Ni saa mbili na nusu asubuhi.*

Minutes and quarters are added with *na* or taken off the next hour with *kasoro* "less" (Almasi and colleagues): *saa tatu na robo* 9.15, *saa nne kasoro robo* 9.45, *saa nne kasoro dakika tano* 9.55. Almasi and colleagues add that *kasoro robo* is often contracted to one word, *kasorobo*: *saa sita kasorobo usiku* 11.45 p.m. *Kamili* "complete" (lesson 5.1) gives "exactly": *saa tano kamili* 11 o'clock sharp.

The part of the day replaces a.m. and p.m. Almasi and colleagues give: *asubuhi* from 6 to noon, *mchana* from noon to 5, *jioni* from 5 to 7, *usiku* from 7 at night to 6 in the morning.

### At a time, for a time

*Saa* answers "at what time?" with no preposition: *Nitaondoka saa mbili* "I'll leave at eight". The same words can also mean "two hours". To measure a stretch of time, say *kwa muda wa* "for a period of": *kwa muda wa saa mbili* "for two hours" (Almasi and colleagues). *Kwa* is taught in lesson 5.8. Note the order with fractions: *nusu saa* "half an hour" puts *nusu* first, but *saa mbili na nusu* "half past eight" puts it last.

### Days, months, years

Days and months are class 9 nouns and need no preposition: *Ijumaa nitasafiri* "on Friday I'll travel"; *Ijumaa hii* "this Friday". Months are English loans (*Januari*, *Februari* ... *Desemba*) or counted: *mwezi wa nne* "the fourth month, April" (Almasi and colleagues). Dates use *tarehe* "date" with the bare number or the ordinal: *tarehe tano* or *tarehe ya tano* "the fifth". A year is *mwaka* + the number: *mwaka elfu moja, mia tisa na tisini* "1990".

## Where it applies

- **Counting past ten** with any noun: *watu kumi na wawili* "twelve people", *magari tisini na manane* "ninety-eight cars", *vitabu mia moja na kimoja* "101 books" (Almasi and colleagues). The last unit agrees; everything else is invariable.
- **Ordinals above ten** use the counting forms, even for 1 and 2: *nyumba ya ishirini na moja* "the twenty-first house", *kisu cha ishirini na mbili* "the twenty-second knife" (Almasi and colleagues). *Kwanza* and *pili* are only "first" and "second" on their own.
- **Prices and money**: *shilingi* "shilling" (class 9/10) with bare numbers: *shilingi mia tano*.
- **Clock times** with *saa*, the part of the day, *na*, *kasoro*, *robo*, *nusu*, *dakika*, *kamili*. Almasi and colleagues allow both *saa nne kasoro dakika tano* and *saa tatu na dakika hamsini na tano* for 9.55.
- **Approximate times**: *karibu saa mbili* "almost eight" and *kama saa kumi na mbili* "about six" (Almasi and colleagues; *kama* "about" with numbers).
- **Times of day with no clock**: *alfajiri* "dawn", *usiku wa manane* "the dead of night" (Almasi and colleagues; several such words come from the Muslim prayer times).
- **Durations**: *kwa muda wa saa tatu* "for three hours", *nusu saa* "half an hour", *robo saa* "a quarter of an hour", *dakika tano na sekunde kumi*.
- **Days of the week**, alone or with demonstratives: *Jumatatu*, *Jumatatu hii*, *Jumatatu ijayo* "next Monday" (like *wiki ijayo*, lesson 4.3), *siku ya Jumatatu*.
- **Relative days** with no preposition: *leo*, *jana*, *juzi*, *kesho*, *kesho kutwa* (known); Almasi and colleagues add *mtondo* "in three days".
- **Months**: *Aprili*, *mwezi wa Aprili*, *mwezi wa nne*. With *mwezi* in front, agreement goes with *mwezi*, class 3 (Almasi and colleagues).
- **Dates**: *tarehe mbili* or *tarehe ya pili* "the second"; for the first, *tarehe moja* or *tarehe ya kwanza* (Almasi and colleagues). Wiktionary also lists *mosi* as an invariable adjective "first", which you may meet in dates. Order: day, month, year.
- **Fractions**: *nusu*, *theluthi* "a third", *robo*: *robo tatu* "three quarters" (Almasi and colleagues).

## Watch out

**Do not add six; subtract it.** *\*Saa nane* for 8 o'clock gives two in the afternoon. 8 − 6 = 2: *saa mbili*. Quick check: *saa sita* must be noon or midnight.

**Do not use *saa moja* for 1 o'clock.** *Saa moja* is seven. One in the afternoon is *saa saba mchana*.

**Do not forget the part of the day.** *Saa mbili* alone can be 8 a.m. or 8 p.m. Add *asubuhi* or *usiku*.

**Do not put a preposition before a time or a day.** English "at eight", "on Friday" and Spanish *a las ocho*, *el viernes* invite *\*katika saa mbili* or *\*kwa Ijumaa*. Say *saa mbili*, *Ijumaa*.

**Do not read a duration as a clock time.** *Nilisoma saa mbili* means "I studied at eight". For "for two hours" use *kwa muda wa saa mbili*.

**Do not agree the tens.** *\*Wanafunzi waishirini* is wrong: *ishirini* is Arabic and invariable. Only the final unit agrees: *wanafunzi ishirini na wawili*.

**Do not put the count before *mia* and *elfu*** in the standard order. *\*Tano mia* copies English. Say *mia tano*. (For thousands, *kumi na nne elfu* is a modern exception that avoids ambiguity.)

**Do not use *kwanza* or *pili* inside a bigger ordinal.** *\*Siku ya ishirini na kwanza* is wrong: *siku ya ishirini na moja*.

**Do not count the week from Monday.** *Jumatatu* has "three" in it but is Monday, because the count starts on Saturday. *Jumapili* "week two" is Sunday.

**Do not say *\*saa mbili na nusu* for "two and a half hours" without *kwa muda wa*.** It is a clock time, 8.30. For the duration: *kwa muda wa saa mbili na nusu*.

## Connections

Arabic speakers will hear *ʕišrīn*, *thalāthīn*, *arbaʕīn*, *khamsīn* in the tens and *miʔa*, *ʔalf* in *mia*, *elfu*. Japanese and Korean count days of the week by the planets; Portuguese, like Swahili, numbers them (*segunda-feira* "second day" is Monday), but from Sunday.

## Exercises

1. Say "fourteen children" and "fourteen chairs".
2. Give the Swahili for 7 a.m., 1 p.m., 6 p.m. and 11 p.m.
3. Say "the meeting will start at a quarter past three in the afternoon".
4. Translate *Basi liliondoka saa moja kasoro dakika kumi asubuhi.*
5. Say "I worked for three hours".
6. Write 2,026 and 15,300 in words.
7. Correct *\*Nitarudi katika saa nane asubuhi* (meaning 8 a.m.).
8. Correct *\*Kuna wanafunzi wathelathini.*
9. Say "on Sunday the fourth of May".
10. Why is Monday *Jumatatu*, with *tatu* "three"?

## Worked answers

1. The final unit is *-nne*, which agrees: *watoto kumi na wanne*; class 8 *viti kumi na vinne*.
2. Subtract six: 7 a.m. *saa moja asubuhi*; 1 p.m. *saa saba mchana*; 6 p.m. *saa kumi na mbili jioni*; 11 p.m. *saa tano usiku*.
3. 3 p.m. is *saa tisa*; a quarter past is *na robo*: *Mkutano utaanza saa tisa na robo mchana.*
4. *Saa moja* is 7; *kasoro dakika kumi* takes ten minutes off. "The bus left at ten to seven in the morning."
5. A duration: *Nilifanya kazi kwa muda wa saa tatu.* Without *kwa muda wa* it would mean "at nine o'clock".
6. *Elfu mbili na ishirini na sita*. 15,300: *elfu kumi na tano, mia tatu* (or *kumi na tano elfu, mia tatu*, the order many speakers now use to avoid ambiguity).
7. No preposition, and 8 − 6 = 2: *Nitarudi saa mbili asubuhi.*
8. Tens never agree: *Kuna wanafunzi thelathini.*
9. *Jumapili, tarehe nne Mei* (or *tarehe ya nne*).
10. The week is counted from Saturday, *Jumamosi* "week one", so Monday is the third day. Lesson 5.4.
