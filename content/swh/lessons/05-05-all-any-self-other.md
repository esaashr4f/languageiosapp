## The idea

Lesson 3.15 split agreement into two rows. Adjectives and numbers take the **nominal** prefixes, close to the noun's own (*mi-ti mi-refu*). Possessives, demonstratives and the *-a* of association take the **pronominal** prefixes, close to the verb's subject prefixes (*mi-ti y-angu*, *mi-ti hii*). Every new agreeing word belongs to one row or the other.

This lesson's words split the same way:

- *-ote* "all", *-o-ote* "any", *-enyewe* "self" and *-pi* "which?" take the **pronominal** row. Wikipedia's grammar says they follow "the verbal inflection pattern": *miti yote* "all the trees", like *miti yangu*, not *\*mizote*.
- *-ingine* "other" is an ordinary vowel-stem **adjective** (lesson 5.1): *miti mingine* "other trees", like *mingi*.

Why the difference? Wiktionary's etymology of *-ote* says its *-o-* "comes from the pronominal stem": the word is built on the same *-o* that gives *huo* and *hayo* (lesson 3.12), and that *-o* always takes the pronominal prefixes. *-enyewe* is "from the same root as *-enye*" (Wiktionary), so it takes *-enye*'s prefixes (lesson 5.2), *mwenyewe* in class 1 included. *-ingine* has no such history; it is an adjective. So the row you need follows from what the word is made of.

## How it is built

### -ote "all, whole"

Take the pronominal prefix (the consonant of *-a*: *cha*, *vya*, *za*...) and add *-ote*. The vowels meet by the rules of lesson 5.1: *vy-ote*, *z-ote*, *l-ote*, *y-ote*, *ch-ote*; class 2 *wa* + *ote* gives *wote* (Polomé notes that *a* + *o* merges only after *wa*, here and in *hao*).

In the plural, *-ote* means "all": *vitabu vyote* "all the books". In the singular it means "the whole": Polomé's *daraja yote imechukuliwa na maji* "the whole bridge was swept away". Wikipedia's grammar gives class 1 no form at all: you do not say "the whole person".

**Persons.** "All of us" is *sote* and "all of you" *nyote*, with or without the pronoun: *(sisi) sote*, *(ninyi) nyote* (Polomé; Wikipedia's grammar). "They all" is *wote*. And here is the point of this lesson's title: *sisi wote*, with the class 2 form *wote* after "we", means "**both** of us" (Wikipedia's grammar). *Sote* is "all of us"; *sisi wote* is "the two of us". Numbers make it explicit: *sote watatu* "the three of us" (Polomé), *wote wawili* "both of them".

### -o-ote "any, whatever"

Double the pronominal piece: prefix + *o*, then prefix + *ote*. Class 7: *cho* + *chote* = *chochote*; class 4 and 9: *yoyote*; class 10: *zozote*; class 5: *lolote*. Class 1 is *yeyote* (Polomé: *ye yote* in the standard). Older texts and Almasi and colleagues write it as two words, *cho chote*; Wikipedia's grammar notes it is now more often written as one. This guide writes one word.

With a negative verb, *-o-ote* gives "no ... at all": *Sitaki kitu chochote* "I don't want anything at all".

### -enyewe "self, itself"

Prefix as for *-enye*, with *mw-* in class 1: *mwenyewe*, *wenyewe*, *chenyewe*, *yenyewe*. It follows a noun or pronoun and stresses that very one: *mimi mwenyewe* "I myself", *kisu chenyewe* "the knife itself" (Polomé). After a verb it means "by oneself": *Alifanya mwenyewe* "he did it himself" (Almasi and colleagues).

### -ingine "other, another"

An adjective: *mtu mwingine* "another person", *watu wengine* "other people, some people", *kitabu kingine*, *nyumba nyingine*. Polomé records *lingine* beside *jingine* in class 5. *Wengine* alone means "others, some (people)", and *pengine*, the class 16 form, has become an adverb "sometimes, perhaps" (Wiktionary).

### -pi "which?"

Pronominal prefix + *-pi*: *kipi*, *vipi*, *lipi*, *ipi*, *zipi*. Class 1 is *yupi*; class 2 is *wepi*, to keep it apart from *wapi* "where?" (Polomé §2.4.3.4, who adds that *wepi* is not common in speech). *-pi* asks for a choice among things you can see or have mentioned: *Unataka kitabu kipi?* "which book do you want?". Lesson 5.10 sets it among the other question words.

### Order

These words come late in the noun phrase. Almasi and colleagues: *-ote* and *-o-ote* follow the descriptive adjectives (*vitabu vikubwa vyekundu vyote*), and *-enye* and *-enyewe* come after those. *-pi* usually comes last of all (Polomé: *watoto watatu wepi?* "which three children?").

## Where it applies

- **-ote with plurals: "all"**: *watu wote*, *vitu vyote*, *nyumba zote*, *maji yote* "all the water".
- **-ote with singulars: "whole"**: *mkate wote*, *siku yote* "all day", *usiku wote* "all night" (class 11 *wote*).
- **Places**: *pote* "everywhere" (Wiktionary), *kote* "everywhere around", *mote* "all through inside".
- **Persons**: *sote*, *nyote*, *wote*; *sisi wote* and *wote wawili* "both"; *sote watatu* "the three of us".
- **-ote standing alone**: *Wote wamefika* "they have all arrived"; Polomé's *chukua hii yote* "carry all these" (loads).
- **-o-ote, "any, whichever"**: *mtu yeyote* "anyone", *kitu chochote* "anything", *wakati wowote* "any time", *popote* "anywhere" (Wiktionary also "wherever, everywhere"), *kokote*. Polomé's *sitaki kuona kifaru yeyote* "I don't want to see any rhino at all" uses class 1 for an animal, as animate agreement predicts.
- **-o-ote with a negative**: "no ... at all", "nothing", "nobody": *Sikumwona mtu yeyote* "I didn't see anybody".
- **-enyewe for emphasis on persons**: *mimi mwenyewe*, *sisi wenyewe*, *ninyi wenyewe* "you yourselves" (Polomé).
- **-enyewe with things: "the actual one, itself"**: *kisu chenyewe*, *nyumba yenyewe*.
- **-enyewe after a verb: "by oneself, unaided"**: *Alifanya mwenyewe.*
- **Mwenyewe as a noun "owner"** (Wiktionary, Almasi and colleagues), beside *mwenye* (lesson 5.2).
- **-ingine "other, another, some"**: *siku nyingine* "another day, some day", *mara nyingine* "another time, sometimes", *wengine* "some people", *mahali pengine* "somewhere else".
- **-ingine with -o, "and other such"**: Polomé describes *wengineo*, *zinginezo* at the end of a list: *wakulima, wachunga na wengineo* "farmers, herders and others like them". Recognise it.
- **-pi "which?"** after a noun or alone: *Kipi?* "Which one?" (class 7 thing understood).
- **Vipi**: the class 8 form also means "how?" (Wikipedia's grammar; Wiktionary), and colloquial *Vipi?* is a greeting, "how's it going?". Lesson 5.10.

## Watch out

**Do not give -ote, -o-ote, -enyewe or -pi the adjective prefix.** *\*Miti mizote* and *\*vitu vichote* follow *-zuri*. These words take the pronominal row: *miti yote*, *vitu vyote*, as in *miti yangu*, *vitu vyangu*.

**Do not give -ingine the pronominal prefix.** The opposite mistake. *\*Miti yingine* copies *yote*. *-ingine* is an adjective: *miti mingine*. (You will hear *zingine*, *jengine* in Kenya; the standard is *nyingine*, *jingine*.)

**Do not say *sisi wote* for "all of us" in a big group.** It means "both of us". For three or more: *sote*.

**Do not use *-ote* for "every".** *\*Siku zote* is "all the days, always", not "each day". "Every day" is *kila siku* (lesson 3.1): *kila* comes before the noun and the noun is singular.

**Do not use *-ote* in class 1 for "the whole person".** *\*Mtoto wote* is wrong. For "the whole child, the child in one piece" use *-zima*: *mtoto mzima*.

**Do not translate "any" in a positive question with *-o-ote* by reflex.** *Una swali lolote?* "Do you have any question (whatever)?" is fine, but English "any" in "do you have any bread?" is usually just *Una mkate?* Use *-o-ote* when you mean "whichever, no matter which".

**Do not use *mwenyewe* as a reflexive object.** "He saw himself" is *alijiona* with *-ji-* (lesson 4.8). *\*Alimwona mwenyewe* means "he saw him, the owner" or "he himself saw him". *Mwenyewe* adds emphasis; it does not fill the object slot.

**Do not confuse *peke* and *-enyewe*.** *Peke yangu* (lesson 3.14) is "alone, without company"; *mimi mwenyewe* is "I myself, not someone else". *Nilikwenda peke yangu* "I went alone"; *Nilikwenda mwenyewe* "I went in person".

**Do not use *wapi* for "which people".** *\*Watoto wapi?* asks "children where?". "Which children?" is *watoto wepi?* (Polomé), or more usually *watoto gani?* (lesson 5.10).

## Connections

Spanish *todo* also shifts meaning with number: *todos los libros* "all the books", *todo el pan* "the whole loaf", exactly like *vitabu vyote* and *mkate wote*. And Spanish *mismo* covers both *-enyewe* (*yo mismo* "I myself") and *-moja* "same" (lesson 5.3), which Swahili keeps apart. Arabic *nafs* in *anā nafsī* "I myself" is the closest match to *mimi mwenyewe*.

## Exercises

1. Say "all the books" and "the whole book".
2. Say "all of us are tired" and "both of us are tired".
3. Translate *Wageni wote wameondoka.*
4. Say "I didn't buy anything at all".
5. Say "she wrote the letter herself".
6. Say "another day" and "other houses".
7. Correct *\*Viti vizote viko nje.*
8. Correct *\*Ninataka machungwa yengine.*
9. Say "which chair?" and "which chairs?".
10. Why is "all" in class 4 *yote*, but "many" in class 4 *mingi*?

## Worked answers

1. *Vitabu vyote* (plural: "all"). *Kitabu chote* (singular: "the whole").
2. *Sote tumechoka* (1PL *sote* + the perfect of *-choka*, lesson 4.6). *Sisi wote tumechoka* "both of us are tired".
3. *Wote*: class 2 "all". "All the guests have left."
4. *-o-ote* with a negative verb: *Sikununua kitu chochote.*
5. *Mwenyewe* after the verb phrase: *Aliandika barua mwenyewe.*
6. *Siku nyingine* (class 9 adjective *nyingine*). *Nyumba nyingine* (class 10, the same form).
7. *-ote* takes the pronominal prefix: *Viti vyote viko nje.*
8. *-ingine* is an adjective; class 6 *ma-* + *i* gives *me-*: *Ninataka machungwa mengine.*
9. Class 7: *kiti kipi?*; class 8: *viti vipi?*. (Context shows that *vipi* is "which" here, not "how".)
10. *-ote* takes the pronominal prefixes (*miti yangu*, *miti yote*) because it is built on the pronominal *-o*; *-ingi* is an adjective and takes the nominal *mi-* (*miti mingi*). Lessons 3.15, 5.1, 5.5.
