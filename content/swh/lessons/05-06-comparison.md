## The idea

English changes the adjective: big, bigger, biggest. Spanish and French add a word: *más grande*, *plus grand*. Swahili does neither. The adjective stays exactly as it is, *mkubwa*, and the comparison is made by what follows it: a word meaning "compared with where B is", "more", or a verb meaning "beat, pass".

So "Juma is taller than Ali" is in Swahili "Juma is tall **where Ali is**", "Juma is tall, **beating** Ali", or "Juma is tall **more than** Ali". Thompson and Schleicher (p. 378) list the four comparison words: *kuliko*, *zaidi ya*, *kupita* and *kushinda*.

**Kuliko** is the commonest. Wiktionary explains it as a frozen verb: the place prefix *ku-*, the "be" root *-li-* and a place-relative *-ko*, literally "where there is". You will build such forms yourself in part 6. Here, treat *kuliko* as one word, "than". Almasi and colleagues note that it can still be read literally: *mahali kuliko na watu wengi* "a place where there are many people".

**Zaidi** "more" is from Arabic *zāʔid* (Wiktionary), and the verb *-zidi* "increase, exceed" is its relative.

**Kushinda** and **kupita** are the infinitives of *-shinda* "beat, win" and *-pita* "pass" (lesson 4.2, 3.4). Used after an adjective they mean "beating", "surpassing". Because they are still verbs, Thompson and Schleicher point out (p. 380), they take an **object prefix** when the thing beaten is a person: *kuwashinda wengine* "beating the others".

There is no special superlative either. "The tallest" is "tall than all": *mrefu kuliko wote*.

## How it is built

### Comparative with kuliko

Say "this house is bigger than that one".

1. "This house": *nyumba hii*, class 9.
2. "Is big": *ni kubwa* (class 9 adjective, no prefix).
3. "Than that one": *kuliko ile*.
4. *Nyumba hii ni kubwa kuliko ile.*

The adjective agrees with the subject, as always. Nothing agrees with the thing compared.

### Superlative

Compare with "all" or "any" (lesson 5.5). Say "Amina is the cleverest student".

1. *Amina ni mwanafunzi hodari* "Amina is a capable student" (lesson 5.1).
2. Add *kuliko wote* "than all (of them)": *Amina ni mwanafunzi hodari kuliko wote.*

*Kuliko wote* and *kuliko yeyote* "than anyone" both work (Almasi and colleagues). Thompson and Schleicher's pattern for "the shortest teacher" is the same: noun + adjective + *kuliko wote*.

### With verbs

*Kuliko* also compares actions and preferences: *Ninapenda chai kuliko kahawa* "I like tea more than coffee". With *bora*, *afadhali* or *heri* "better" (lessons 4.10, 2.8), you get "it is better to X than to Y": Almasi and colleagues' *Afadhali kuchelewa kuliko kukosa* "better late than to miss it".

### zaidi and zaidi ya

*Zaidi* alone is "more" when there is no second term: *Nina vitabu vingi zaidi* "I have more books" (Thompson and Schleicher). *Zaidi ya* + noun is "more than": *mpana zaidi ya mfuko wake* "wider than his bag" (Almasi and colleagues). With numbers it is the only choice: *zaidi ya watu mia* "more than a hundred people".

### kushinda, kupita, -zidi

*Kitambaa hiki kina rangi nyingi kupita kile* "this cloth has more colours than that one" (Thompson and Schleicher's pattern). With a person, an object prefix: *Ali ni mrefu kumshinda Juma* "Ali is taller than Juma". *-zidi* works as a full verb: *Bei ya sukari imezidi bei ya chumvi* "the price of sugar has gone above the price of salt".

### Equal and less

"As ... as" uses *kama* "like" (lesson 4.14): *mrefu kama baba yake* "as tall as his father". *Sawa na* is "equal to, the same as": *Kitabu changu ni sawa na chako.* *-fanana* "resemble" and *-lingana* "be equal, match" are verbs (Almasi and colleagues).

"Less ... than" has no common single word. Say "not as ... as": *Gari hili si ghali kama lile* "this car is not as expensive as that one". Almasi and colleagues also give *duni ya* "inferior to" and the verb *-pungua* "decrease"; both are formal or specific.

## Where it applies

- **Adjective + kuliko + noun or pronoun**: *mkubwa kuliko mimi* "bigger than me" (independent pronoun after *kuliko*), *ghali kuliko ile*.
- **Invariable adjectives** work the same: *safi kuliko*, *ghali kuliko*, *bora kuliko*.
- **-a and -enye phrases** too: *simu yake ni ya kisasa kuliko yangu* "her phone is more modern than mine".
- **Verbs**: *anasoma kuliko wewe* "she studies more than you"; *ninapenda X kuliko Y*.
- **Preferences**: *bora*, *afadhali*, *heri* + infinitive + *kuliko* + infinitive.
- **Superlative of people**: *kuliko wote*, *kuliko yeyote*, *kuwashinda wote*.
- **Superlative of things**: *kuliko vyote*, *kupita zote*, *zaidi ya mifuko yote* (Almasi and colleagues).
- **Quantities**: *zaidi ya* with numbers and amounts; *vingi zaidi* "more (of them)"; *zaidi* after a verb: *Soma zaidi!* "Read more!".
- **Comparing by verb alone**: *-shinda* "be better than, beat", *-pita* "pass, exceed", *-zidi* "exceed": *Bei imezidi* "the price has gone up (too much)".
- **Equality**: *kama* "as ... as"; *sawa na*; *sawasawa* "equally, the same" (Wiktionary, a doubled *sawa*); *-fanana*, *-lingana*.
- **Contrast by juxtaposition**: Almasi and colleagues note that you can name two people and then say which has the quality: *Huyu na yule, yule ni mwembamba* "this one and that one: that one is thinner".
- **"Not as ... as"** for "less than": *si ghali kama*.
- **Comparing measures with a quality noun**: *urefu* "height", *uzito* "weight", *umri* "age": *Wana umri sawa* "they are the same age".

## Watch out

**Do not look for a comparative ending.** *\*Mkubwazaidi* or a form like *-kubwa-er* does not exist. The adjective stays as it is: *mkubwa kuliko*.

**Do not use *zaidi* where *kuliko* is needed with a noun.** *\*Mkubwa zaidi Juma* drops the link. Either *mkubwa kuliko Juma* or *mkubwa zaidi ya Juma*.

**Do not put *zaidi* in front like Spanish *más*.** *\*Ni zaidi mkubwa* copies *es más grande*. *Zaidi* follows: *ni mkubwa zaidi*.

**Do not drop the object prefix with *kushinda* and a person.** *\*Ali ni mrefu kushinda Juma* treats *kushinda* as a plain preposition. Thompson and Schleicher: these verbs "must take object markers" with people. *Kumshinda Juma.*

**Do not use the subject form of a pronoun after kuliko wrongly.** English "taller than I" and "than me" both exist; Swahili uses the independent pronoun: *kuliko mimi*, *kuliko wao*. *\*Kuliko ni* is wrong (*ni* is the copula).

**Do not make the superlative with *sana*.** *Mrefu sana* is "very tall", not "the tallest". The superlative needs the comparison: *mrefu kuliko wote*.

**Do not agree "all" with the wrong group.** In *Amina ni hodari kuliko wote*, *wote* is the other students (class 2). Comparing things in class 7: *kuliko vyote*.

**Do not use *kama* for "than".** Spanish *como* and French *comme* mean "as", and so does *kama*. *\*Mrefu kama Juma* means "as tall as Juma", not "taller".

**Do not translate "less" by *kidogo*.** *\*Ghali kidogo kuliko* is "a little more expensive than". For "less expensive": *si ghali kama* or *rahisi kuliko* "cheaper than".

## Connections

Many languages compare with a word for "pass" or "exceed": Japanese uses *yori* "from", Korean *boda* "than (seeing)", and Swahili *kupita* "passing". Arabic is the opposite: it has a real comparative form, *akbar* "bigger" from *kabīr*, and Swahili borrowed none of that system, only words like *zaidi* (*zāʔid* "exceeding").

## Exercises

1. Say "the cow is bigger than the goat".
2. Say "this is the cheapest shop" (*rahisi* "cheap").
3. Translate *Kiswahili ni rahisi kuliko Kiarabu.*
4. Say "she is older than me".
5. Say "more than twenty students did not pass".
6. Say "better to eat now than later" (*baadaye*).
7. Correct *\*Ali ni mrefu kushinda mimi.*
8. Correct *\*Chai ni zaidi tamu kuliko kahawa.*
9. Say "my phone is the same as yours".
10. What is the difference between *mrefu sana* and *mrefu kuliko wote*?

## Worked answers

1. The cow is animate: class 1 adjective *mkubwa*. *Ng'ombe ni mkubwa kuliko mbuzi.*
2. *Duka* class 5: *Hili ni duka rahisi kuliko yote* (*yote*, class 6, "all the shops").
3. "Swahili is easier than Arabic."
4. *Mkubwa* also means "older" of people (lesson 3.3): *Yeye ni mkubwa kuliko mimi.*
5. *Zaidi ya* with a number: *Zaidi ya wanafunzi ishirini hawakufaulu.*
6. *Afadhali kula sasa kuliko baadaye.*
7. *Kushinda* takes an object prefix for a person, here "me": *Ali ni mrefu kunishinda mimi* (or *mrefu kuliko mimi*).
8. *Zaidi* never goes before the adjective. Either *Chai ni tamu kuliko kahawa* or *Chai ni tamu zaidi ya kahawa.*
9. *Sawa na* with a possessive standing for the noun: *Simu yangu ni sawa na yako.*
10. *Mrefu sana* is "very tall", with no comparison. *Mrefu kuliko wote* is "taller than all", the superlative. Lesson 5.6.
