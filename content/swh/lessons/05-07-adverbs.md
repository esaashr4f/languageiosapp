## The idea

An **adverb** is a word that says how, when, where or how much something happens: "well", "quickly", "here", "often", "very". English makes many of them by adding *-ly* to an adjective. Swahili has no such ending. It has four ways instead, and you already know the raw material for all of them.

**1. An adjective in class 8.** *Vizuri* "well" is *-zuri* with the class 8 prefix; *vibaya* "badly" is *-baya*. Wiktionary describes *vizuri* as the class 8 form "and adverbial form" of *-zuri*. Lesson 3.3 showed *kidogo* "a little", the class 7 form of *-dogo*, used the same way. An adjective describing an action, not a noun, takes class 8 (sometimes class 7). The grammars consulted describe the pattern without explaining why these classes were chosen; Almasi and colleagues add that for some words *ki-* and *vi-* are both used with no difference in meaning.

**2. A ki- word "in the manner of".** This is the *ki-* of *Kiswahili*, "the Swahili way" (lesson 3.2), and of *-a kisasa* (lesson 5.2). Almasi and colleagues list *kizee* "like an old man", *kiaskari* "like a soldier", *kizungu* "European-style". Used after a verb it says how: *Anavaa kisasa* "she dresses in a modern way".

**3. *kwa* + noun.** *Kwa haraka* "with haste, quickly", *kwa sauti* "aloud", *kwa siri* "in secret". *Kwa* is the class 15/17 form of the *-a* of association (Wiktionary), and it has become a general "with, by, in the manner of". Lesson 5.8 gives its other uses.

**4. Reduplication.** **Reduplication** means saying a word twice to make a new word. *Polepole* "slowly" (lesson 2.7) is *pole* doubled; *sawasawa* "equally" is *sawa* doubled (Wiktionary); *mbalimbali* "various" doubles *mbali* "far" (Wiktionary). Almasi and colleagues list *kisirisiri* "secretly" and *mojamoja* "one by one". Doubling a noun or adverb often gives a manner or a repetition: *haraka haraka* "in a rush, hurriedly", the start of a well-known proverb.

A fifth group is simply adverbs by birth: *sana* "very, a lot", *tena* "again", *kabisa* "completely", *sasa* "now", *ghafla* "suddenly", *upesi* "quickly", *mno* "too much".

## How it is built

### Chain of thought

Say "the children sang well".

1. "The children sang": *Watoto waliimba* (past, class 2).
2. "Well": *-zuri* as an adverb takes class 8: *vizuri*.
3. The adverb follows the verb: *Watoto waliimba vizuri.*

Say "he answered quickly".

1. *Alijibu* (*-jibu* is a loan verb).
2. "Quickly": *kwa haraka*, or *upesi*, or *haraka* alone (lesson 2.6 lists it as an adverb too).
3. *Alijibu kwa haraka.*

### Where adverbs go

Manner adverbs follow the verb, and the object if there is one: *Anaandika barua vizuri* "she writes letters well". Time and frequency adverbs go at the start or the end: *Kesho nitasafiri* or *Nitasafiri kesho*. Almasi and colleagues add that days and times need no preposition (lesson 5.4).

Degree adverbs follow what they modify: *ghali sana*, *ghali mno* "far too expensive", *kidogo* after a verb or adjective: *Nimechoka kidogo* "I'm a bit tired".

## Where it applies

- **Class 8 adverbs from adjectives**: *vizuri*, *vibaya*, *vyema* "well, properly" (Wiktionary), *vigumu* "with difficulty" (lesson 4.12 used it in *ni vigumu* "it is hard"), *vingine* "otherwise, in another way". Any adjective that makes sense: *virefu*, *vipya* are possible but rare.
- **Class 7 *kidogo*** "a little, slightly": *Subiri kidogo* "wait a bit".
- **ki- manner words**: *kisasa*, *kizee*, *kiaskari*; and languages: *Anaongea Kiswahili* "she speaks Swahili" is the same kind of word as object.
- **kwa + noun**: *kwa haraka*, *kwa sauti*, *kwa siri*, *kwa bahati* "luckily", *kwa nguvu* "by force", *kwa shida* "with difficulty", *kwa makusudi* "on purpose" (Almasi and colleagues list these).
- **u- adverbs**: *upesi* "quickly", *upya* "anew" (Almasi and colleagues).
- **Reduplicated words**: *polepole*, *sawasawa*, *haraka haraka*, *kidogo kidogo* "little by little", *mbalimbali*, *mara kwa mara*.
- **Plain adverbs of time**: *sasa*, *leo*, *jana*, *kesho*, *zamani*, *baadaye*, *mapema* (all known); *mnamo* "at about": *mnamo saa moja* "at about seven" (Almasi and colleagues).
- **Frequency**: *mara moja*, *mara nyingi* "often", *mara chache* "rarely", *tena* "again", *mara kwa mara*.
- **Degree**: *sana*, *mno*, *kabisa*, *kidogo*, *kiasi* "somewhat, moderately" (Wiktionary), *hasa* "especially, exactly", *zaidi* (lesson 5.6).
- **Place**: *hapa*, *pale*, *huku*, *kule*, *humu*, *mle* (lesson 3.12), *ndani*, *nje*, *juu*, *chini*, *mbele*, *nyuma*, *karibu*, *mbali*, *katikati* "in the middle". Lesson 5.8 turns many of them into prepositions with *ya* or *na*.
- **Imitative words** (often called ideophones) that intensify one verb: Almasi and colleagues give *kufa fofofo* "stone dead" and *pumzika fofofo* "rest soundly". Recognise them; they are fixed to their verbs.
- **-a + adverb** makes an adjective phrase: *-a ghafla* "sudden" (Wiktionary), *-a kweli* "real, true" (Wiktionary).

## Watch out

**Do not use an agreeing adjective after a verb.** *\*Anaimba mzuri* gives "sings" a class 1 adjective, as if it meant "he, being good, sings". A verb takes the class 8 adverb: *Anaimba vizuri.*

**Do not use *nzuri* for "well".** *\*Ameandika nzuri* copies the greeting reply *nzuri* (class 9, agreeing with *habari*). With a verb: *ameandika vizuri*.

**Do not put *kwa* before a class 8 adverb.** *\*Kwa vizuri* doubles the marking. *Kwa* goes before nouns: *kwa haraka*, *kwa nguvu*.

**Do not put *sana* before the verb or adjective.** *\*Sana napenda* and *\*sana ghali* follow English "really". *Ninapenda sana*, *ghali sana*.

**Do not put a manner adverb between the verb and its object.** *\*Anaandika vizuri barua* splits verb and object, the way French *il écrit bien les lettres* does. Swahili prefers *Anaandika barua vizuri*.

**Do not use *mno* as a compliment.** *Mzuri mno* can be "very beautiful", but *mno* often means "too much": *Ni ghali mno* is a complaint. Use *sana* for plain "very".

**Do not translate "really" by *kweli* before an adjective.** *\*Kweli mzuri* is not Swahili. "Really good" is *mzuri kweli* or *mzuri sana*; *kweli* follows, or stands alone as "really?".

## Connections

French *bien* and Spanish *bien*, like *vizuri*, are separate words rather than *bon* or *bueno* + an ending, and Spanish *a escondidas* "secretly" is built like *kwa siri*. Japanese makes adverbs from adjectives with a fixed form (*hayai* → *hayaku*), close to Swahili's fixed class 8 *vi-*. Doubling for manner is widespread: Japanese *dondon*, *yukkuri*, and Indonesian *pelan-pelan* "slowly", exactly *polepole*.

## Exercises

1. Say "she cooks well".
2. Say "they spoke in secret".
3. Translate *Wageni walifika ghafla.*
4. Say "read slowly!" to one person and to several.
5. Say "we went to the market early".
6. Correct *\*Anaimba nzuri sana.*
7. Correct *\*Alijibu kwa vizuri.*
8. Correct *\*Sana ninapenda chai.*
9. Translate *Kidogo kidogo mtoto alianza kutembea.*
10. Why is "well" *vizuri*, in class 8, and not in the class of the subject?

## Worked answers

1. *Anapika vizuri.* Class 8 adverb after the verb.
2. *Walizungumza kwa siri* (or *walisema kwa siri*). *Kwa* + noun.
3. "The guests arrived suddenly."
4. *Soma polepole!* To several, the plural command (lesson 2.7): *Someni polepole!* The adverb does not change.
5. *Tulikwenda sokoni mapema.* A time adverb at the end.
6. A verb takes the class 8 adverb: *Anaimba vizuri sana.*
7. No *kwa* before a class 8 adverb: *Alijibu vizuri* (or *kwa haraka*, if "quickly" was meant).
8. *Sana* follows: *Ninapenda chai sana.*
9. "Little by little the child began to walk."
10. The adverb describes the action, not a noun, so it does not take the subject's class; adjectives used as adverbs take class 8 whatever the subject. Lesson 5.7.
