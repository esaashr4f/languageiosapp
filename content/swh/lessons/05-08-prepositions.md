## The idea

A **preposition** is a small word before a noun that says how the noun relates to the rest of the sentence: "on the table", "with a knife", "without sugar". English has dozens. Swahili has very few true ones, and you know most of them already, because they grew out of things you have learnt.

- **na** "with, and" (lessons 2.6, 3.14).
- **kwa** is the *-a* of association in class 15/17 (Wiktionary; Wikipedia's grammar). When it agrees with no noun, it relates to the whole action: "by, with, at, for". Lesson 5.7 used it for manner.
- **kwenye** is *-enye* "having" in class 17 (lesson 5.2), "a place having", hence "at, on, in".
- **katika** "in, at, on, during" is *kati* "middle" fused with an old *-a* form *ka* of a class that Swahili has almost lost (class 12): Wiktionary and Polomé (§2.4.5.1) give the same origin.

A few others are Arabic: *bila* "without" (*bilā*), *kabla* "before" (*qabla*), *badala* "in place of" (*badal*), *dhidi* "against" (*ḍidd*) (all Wiktionary).

Everything else is built. Most English prepositions of place are, in Swahili, a place word followed by *ya* "of": *juu ya meza* "the top of the table", that is, "on the table". You met *juu*, *chini*, *ndani*, *nje*, *mbele*, *nyuma* as adverbs (lessons 2.5, 3.13). Add *ya* and a noun, and they become prepositions. Polomé notes that this *ya* no longer agrees with anything (lesson 3.11): it is *ya* whatever the noun.

A few place words take *na* "with" instead: *karibu na* "near (with)", *mbali na* "far from", *pamoja na* "together with". Almasi and colleagues list them: they are the words of nearness, distance, company and sameness (*sawa na*, lesson 5.6).

## How it is built

### Place: four choices

Say "the book is on the table".

1. *Kitabu kiko* (lesson 2.5).
2. "On": the top of: *juu ya*.
3. *Kitabu kiko juu ya meza.*

You could also say *Kitabu kiko mezani*: the *-ni* place (lesson 3.13) covers "on, in, at" without saying which. So for a place you have:

1. **-ni**: *sokoni* "at/to/in the market". Short, but the noun cannot take an adjective (lesson 3.13).
2. **katika** + noun: *katika soko* "in the market"; and *katika soko kubwa* "in the big market", which *-ni* cannot do (Almasi and colleagues).
3. **kwenye** + noun: *kwenye soko kubwa*. Wikipedia's grammar says modern Swahili often prefers *kwenye* to *-ni* exactly when the noun has an adjective or an *-a* phrase, because the noun then keeps its own class and agreement.
4. **A place word + ya/na**: *juu ya*, *chini ya*, *ndani ya*, *karibu na*, when the exact position matters.

### Places that are people: kwa

"At my aunt's", "to the doctor's". A person is not a place, so *-ni* is impossible. Swahili uses *kwa* + the person: *kwa shangazi* "at/to my aunt's", *kwa daktari* "to the doctor's". Wikipedia's grammar states the division: in the standard, *kwa* marks a place associated with a person or animal, *kwenye* a place associated with a thing. Your own home is *kwangu* "my place" (lesson 3.13).

### Means and manner: kwa

*Kwa basi* "by bus", *kwa miguu* "on foot", *kwa kisu* "with a knife", *kwa haraka* "quickly" (lesson 5.7). *Kwa* is the "by means of" word.

### Company: na

*Na* is "with" for company: *Nilikwenda na rafiki yangu* "I went with my friend". With a pronoun, the short forms: *nami*, *nawe*, *naye* (lesson 3.14).

### Pronouns after the compounds

After a *ya*-compound, a pronoun becomes a possessive with *y-*: *juu yake* "on it, on him", *badala yangu* "instead of me", *nyuma yetu* "behind us" (Almasi and colleagues). After a *na*-compound, the *na* form: *karibu nami* "near me", *pamoja nao* "together with them".

## Where it applies

- **ya-compounds of place**: *juu ya* "on, above", *chini ya* "under", *ndani ya* "inside" (with the bare noun: *ndani ya chumba*, lesson 3.13), *nje ya* "outside", *mbele ya* "in front of", *nyuma ya* "behind" (lesson 4.19), *kando ya* "beside", *katikati ya* "in the middle of, among" (lesson 5.7). Almasi and colleagues add *mahali pa* "in place of".
- **ya-compounds of time and other relations**: *kabla ya* "before", *baada ya* "after" (*baada*, lesson 1.4), *badala ya* "instead of", *zaidi ya* "more than" (lesson 5.6), *dhidi ya* "against".
- **na-compounds**: *karibu na*, *mbali na*, *pamoja na*, *sawa na* (Almasi and colleagues).
- **katika**: place (*katika soko*), time "during" (*katika safari yetu* "during our journey", the use Almasi and colleagues show), "among" (Wiktionary), and the formal written style generally.
- **kwenye**: place with things, especially with a modified noun: *kwenye soko kubwa la mboga* (Wikipedia's grammar).
- **kwa + person: "at/to someone's"**: *kwa shangazi*, *kwa daktari*, *kwa Juma*; *Alirudi kwa mama yake* "she went back to her mother's".
- **kwa for means, instrument, manner**: *kwa basi*, *kwa miguu*, *kwa kisu*, *kwa simu* "by phone", *kwa Kiswahili* "in Swahili".
- **kwa in fixed phrases**: *kwa heri* "goodbye" (lesson 2.8), *kwa muda wa* (lesson 5.4), *mara kwa mara* (lesson 5.7).
- **na for company**: *na rafiki yangu*, *nami*; and for "have" (*-wa na*, lesson 4.16). The passive agent "by" is lesson 7.4.
- **bila "without"**, with a noun or an infinitive: *bila sukari*, *bila kulipa* "without paying".
- **kutoka "from"**: *kutoka Nairobi*, *kutoka kwa mama*. Almasi and colleagues give *toka* and *tokea* as shorter variants.
- **kuhusu "about"**: *kitabu kuhusu historia*.
- **miongoni mwa "among"** (Wiktionary): *miongoni mwa wanafunzi* "among the students". *Mwa* is the class 18 *-a*.
- **Pronouns**: *juu yake*, *badala yetu*, *karibu nawe*, *pamoja nasi*.

## Watch out

**Do not leave out *ya*.** *\*Juu meza* copies English "on table". *Juu* alone is an adverb "up, above": *juu ya meza*.

**Do not make the *ya* agree.** *\*Chini cha kitanda* treats it as an *-a* of association. In the compound it is always *ya*: *chini ya kitanda.*

**Do not use *ya* after words of nearness.** *\*Karibu ya kanisa* follows *juu ya* too far. Almasi and colleagues list *karibu na*: *karibu na kanisa*.

**Do not double the place marking.** *\*Katika sokoni* and *\*kwenye mezani* say "in" twice. Choose: *sokoni*, *katika soko*, *kwenye meza*.

**Do not add an adjective to a *-ni* noun.** *\*Sokoni kubwa* fails (lesson 3.13). Use *katika soko kubwa* or *kwenye soko kubwa*.

**Do not use *-ni* or *kwenye* with a person.** *\*Daktarini* and *\*kwenye daktari* treat the doctor as a place. *Kwa daktari.*

**Do not use *kwa* + person for company.** *Nilikwenda kwa rafiki* means "I went to my friend's". "With my friend" is *na rafiki*.

**Do not use *na* for means.** Spanish *con* and French *avec* cover both "with a friend" and "with a knife". Swahili splits them: *na* for company, *kwa* for tools and transport: *alikata kwa kisu*, *tulisafiri kwa basi*.

**Do not put an independent pronoun after a *ya*-compound.** *\*Juu ya yeye*, *\*badala ya mimi* are wrong. Use the possessive: *juu yake*, *badala yangu*.

**Do not use *kutoka* with *-toka*.** *\*Ametoka kutoka Nairobi* says "from" twice; *-toka* already means "come from": *Ametoka Nairobi*.

**Do not use a preposition before days and times.** *\*Katika Jumatatu* is wrong (lesson 5.4): *Jumatatu*. *Katika* goes with periods: *katika mwezi wa Aprili* "in (the month of) April", which Almasi and colleagues allow.

## Connections

French *au-dessus de*, *à côté de*, *en face de* and Spanish *encima de*, *debajo de*, *cerca de* are built exactly like *juu ya*, *kando ya*: a place word plus "of". French *chez* is a close match for *kwa* + person: *chez le médecin* is *kwa daktari*. Japanese *tsukue no ue ni* "on the desk's top" is *juu ya meza* in reverse order.

## Exercises

1. Say "the cat is behind the door".
2. Say "the children are playing in the big garden" (two ways).
3. Say "I'm going to the doctor's".
4. Say "she cut the bread with a knife".
5. Translate *Nyumba yetu iko mbali na mji.*
6. Say "he sat near me".
7. Correct *\*Alikwenda Mombasa na basi.*
8. Correct *\*Kitabu kiko chini cha kiti.*
9. Correct *\*Ninaishi katika mjini.*
10. Why is it *kwa shangazi* but *kwenye duka*?

## Worked answers

1. *Mlango* class 3, but the compound keeps *ya*: *Paka yuko nyuma ya mlango.* (Animate: *yuko*.)
2. *Watoto wanacheza katika bustani kubwa* or *kwenye bustani kubwa*. Not *\*bustanini kubwa*, because a *-ni* noun takes no adjective.
3. A person as a place: *Ninakwenda kwa daktari.*
4. A tool takes *kwa*: *Alikata mkate kwa kisu.*
5. "Our house is far from town." *Mbali* takes *na*.
6. *Karibu na* + the *na*-form of "me": *Alikaa karibu nami* (or *karibu na mimi*).
7. Transport takes *kwa*: *Alikwenda Mombasa kwa basi.* *Na basi* would mean "together with a bus".
8. The compound never agrees: *chini ya kiti*.
9. Either *mjini* or *katika mji*, not both: *Ninaishi mjini.*
10. *Shangazi* is a person, so the place is "at her home": *kwa*. *Duka* is a thing: *kwenye* (or *dukani*). Lesson 5.8, after Wikipedia's grammar.
