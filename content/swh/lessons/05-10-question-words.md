## The idea

Lesson 2.4 taught yes-no questions: say the sentence, add *je* in front if you like, and let the voice rise. This lesson teaches **content questions**, the ones that ask "who?", "what?", "where?", "when?", "how many?".

English moves the question word to the front and often rearranges the rest: "You want tea" becomes "**What** do you want?". Swahili does not need to move anything. You put the question word **where the answer would go**:

- *Unataka chai.* "You want tea." → *Unataka nini?* "You want what?"
- *Ulifika jana.* "You arrived yesterday." → *Ulifika lini?* "You arrived when?"
- *Kitabu ni cha Juma.* → *Kitabu ni cha nani?* "The book is whose?"

The verb, its prefixes and its tense stay exactly as in the statement. Almasi and colleagues add that most question words can also stand at the start or end for emphasis (*Lini utasoma Kifaransa?*), with one exception: *gani* never comes first.

So the whole skill is knowing which word replaces which part of the sentence, and how the agreeing ones agree.

- **nani** "who?" replaces a person. Wiktionary gives it as a pronoun, so it agrees as a person: class 1 on the verb (*Nani amekuja?*).
- **nini** "what?" replaces a thing.
- **wapi** "where?" replaces a place (lesson 2.5); **lini** "when?" a time.
- **-ngapi** "how many?" replaces a number, and agrees like *-tatu* (lesson 5.3), with plural prefixes only.
- **gani** "what kind of, which?" (lesson 2.8) replaces an adjective; it is invariable.
- **-pi** "which (of these)?" (lesson 5.5) picks from a known set; it takes pronominal prefixes.
- **vipi** "how?" replaces a manner adverb (lesson 5.7).

Polomé (§2.5.7) describes three question words that attach to the end of the verb itself: *-je* "how?", *-ni* "what (for)?", *-pi* "where?". *Ulifikaje?* "how did you get here?", *Unachekani?* "what are you laughing at?".

## How it is built

### Chain of thought

Say "how many children do you have?".

1. Start from the statement: *Una watoto watatu* "you have three children".
2. Replace the number with *-ngapi*, agreeing with *watoto* (class 2): *wangapi*.
3. *Una watoto wangapi?*

Say "whose car is this?".

1. Statement: *Hili ni gari la Juma* "this is Juma's car".
2. Replace the owner with *nani*: *la nani*.
3. *Hili ni gari la nani?*

Say "who is looking for me?" and "who are you looking for?".

1. Subject: *Nani ananitafuta?* *Nani* takes the subject slot; the verb takes the class 1 prefix *a-*.
2. Object: *Unamtafuta nani?* *Nani* takes the object position after the verb, and the verb takes the class 1 object prefix *-m-* as it would for any person (lesson 4.7). Polomé's *unamwita nani?* "whom are you calling?" has the same shape.

### Names

Swahili asks for a name with "who", not "what": *Jina lako nani?* "What is your name?", the form Almasi and colleagues use in a dialogue. The name stands for a person.

### Why? How?

"Why" is *kwa nini*, literally "for what". Wiktionary also lists *kwani* (lesson 5.9) and *mbona*. "For what reason" is *kwa sababu gani* (Almasi and colleagues). "How" is *vipi*, *kwa namna gani*, or *-je* on the verb.

### The verb endings

*-je* "how?" goes after the final vowel: *u-li-fik-a-je*. *-ni* "what?, what for?" does the same: Polomé's example is *unaliani?* "what are you crying for?". These are short, spoken forms of *vipi* and *nini*; the full words are always correct.

## Where it applies

- **nani** for any person, as subject (*Nani anapika?*), object (*Ulimwona nani?*), after *ni* (*Yule ni nani?*, lesson 3.12), after *-a* "whose" (*cha nani*), after *kwa* "at whose place" (*Alilala kwa nani?*, Almasi and colleagues), after *na* "with whom" (*Ulikwenda na nani?*). Names: *Jina lako nani?*
- **nini** for things: *Unafanya nini?*, *Hiki ni nini?*. As a subject Almasi and colleagues print *Nini ilianguka?* "what fell?", with a class 9 verb; a speaker who knows the class usually names the thing instead: *Kitu gani kilianguka?*
- **wapi** for places, with the place verbs (*Uko wapi?*) and with motion verbs (*Unakwenda wapi?*); *kutoka wapi?* "from where?". Polomé gives a verb ending *-pi* "where?": *wanakwendapi?* "where are they going?".
- **lini** for times: *Utarudi lini?*. For clock time, *saa ngapi?* (lesson 5.4); for dates, *tarehe ngapi?*.
- **-ngapi** with plural prefixes only (Almasi and colleagues): *watu wangapi*, *miti mingapi*, *mayai mangapi*, *vitu vingapi*, *nyumba ngapi* (class 10, no prefix), *mara ngapi?* "how many times?". Alone, *Wangapi?* "how many (people)?".
- **gani** after any noun: *habari gani?*, *rangi gani?* "what colour?", *aina gani?* "what kind?", *bei gani?* "what price?", *mtu gani?* "what sort of person?".
- **-pi** for a choice among known things: *kitabu kipi?*, *mtu yupi?*, *watu wepi?* (lesson 5.5).
- **How**: *vipi*, *kwa namna gani*, *-je*: *Umelalaje?* "how did you sleep?" (lesson 2.8 greeting).
- **Why**: *kwa nini*, *kwani*, *mbona*; *kwa sababu gani*.
- **What for**: *-ni* on the verb, or *kwa ajili ya nini* (lesson 6.10 teaches *kwa ajili ya*).
- **Several question words in one sentence**: each in its own place: *Nani alifika lini?* "who arrived when?".
- **Asking for details**: *Unaishi mtaa gani?* "which street do you live in?"; *Anwani yako ni ipi?* "what is your address?" (*-pi*: one address among possible ones); *Una uhakika?* "are you sure?".
- **Echo questions**: *Ulisema nini?* "you said what?" works exactly like English.
- **Indirect questions** ("I asked him where he lives") are lesson 6.9.

## Watch out

**Do not move the question word to the front by habit.** English and French train you to say *Nini unataka?*. Some question words can go first for emphasis (Almasi and colleagues give *Lini utasoma Kifaransa?*), but the neutral place is where the answer would go: *Unataka nini?*

**Never put *gani* first.** *\*Gani chakula unapenda?* is wrong (Almasi and colleagues: "never at the beginning"). *Unapenda chakula gani?*

**Do not use the counting form of *-ngapi* for agreeing nouns.** *\*Watoto ngapi?* leaves out the prefix. *Watoto wangapi?* (but *nyumba ngapi?*, class 10).

**Do not ask "how many" with a singular prefix.** *\*Mtoto mngapi?* is impossible: you count plurals. *Watoto wangapi?*

**Do not ask for a name with *nini*.** *Jina lako ni nini?* translates English word for word. Swahili treats a name as a person: *Jina lako nani?*, as in Almasi and colleagues' dialogue.

**Do not confuse *kwa nini* and *kwa nani*.** *Kwa nini?* "why?"; *Kwa nani?* "at whose place?, to whom?". One letter apart.

**Do not translate "whose" as *nani* alone.** *\*Nani kitabu?* is meaningless. Use *-a nani* agreeing with the owned thing: *kitabu cha nani?*

**Do not forget the object prefix with *nani*.** A person as object normally gets *-m-* on the verb (lesson 4.7), and *nani* is a person: *Unamtafuta nani?*, as in Polomé's *unamwita nani?*.

**Do not use *-pi* for "what kind".** *Kitabu kipi?* asks "which (of these) book?"; *Kitabu gani?* asks "what book, what sort of book?". In speech *gani* often covers both (Wikipedia's grammar: "colloquially, which").

**Do not add *je* to the end of a non-verb.** *-je* "how" attaches only to verbs. *Je* at the start of a sentence (lesson 2.4), or after a noun as "what about?" (*Na wewe je?* "and you?", Almasi and colleagues), is a separate word.

## Connections

Japanese and Korean also leave question words in place: *nani o tabemasu ka* "what (object) eat?", *mueos-eul meog-eoyo?* — like *unakula nini?*. Arabic, Spanish and French move them to the front (*¿qué quieres?*, *qu'est-ce que tu veux?*), the habit to unlearn here.

## Exercises

1. Turn *Mwalimu anasoma gazeti* into "who is reading the newspaper?" and "what is the teacher reading?".
2. Say "where do you live?".
3. Say "when did they arrive?".
4. Say "how many books do you have?".
5. Say "whose shoes are these?" (*viatu*).
6. Say "what kind of tea do you want?".
7. Say "how did you sleep?" in two ways.
8. Correct *\*Gani kitabu unasoma?*
9. Correct *\*Una kaka ngapi?* (*kaka*: animate class 9/10).
10. Make *Jina lako ni nini?* sound Swahili.

## Worked answers

1. Replace the subject: *Nani anasoma gazeti?* Replace the object: *Mwalimu anasoma nini?*
2. *Unaishi wapi?* (*-ishi* is a loan verb.)
3. *Walifika lini?*
4. *Una vitabu vingapi?* Class 8 prefix on *-ngapi*.
5. *Viatu hivi ni vya nani?* "Whose" is *-a nani*, agreeing with *viatu* (class 8).
6. *Unataka chai gani?* *Gani* after the noun.
7. *Ulilalaje?* or *Ulilala vipi?* (The greeting form is *Umelalaje?*, with the perfect, lesson 2.8.)
8. *Gani* never comes first: *Unasoma kitabu gani?*
9. Brothers are people, so class 2 agreement: *Una kaka wangapi?*
10. A name is asked with "who": *Jina lako nani?* (or *Jina lako ni nani?*).
