## Before you start

This review teaches nothing new. It covers all of part 5: vowel-stem, irregular and invariable adjectives (lesson 5.1), *-enye* and *-a* phrases (5.2), numbers (5.3), time, days and big numbers (5.4), *-ote*, *-o-ote*, *-enyewe*, *-ingine*, *-pi* (5.5), comparison (5.6), adverbs (5.7), prepositions (5.8), conjunctions (5.9), question words (5.10), and derived and shifted nouns (5.11). Parts 2 to 4 are assumed: the verb tenses, especially *-li-* and the narrative *-ka-*, carry the story.

The passage is a short scene at a market, constructed for this review. It ends with a real saying. As you read each line, ask of every agreeing word: which noun does it agree with, and which row of prefixes (adjective or pronominal) does it take?

## Exercises

1. Translate the passage into natural English without looking at the translation lines.
2. In line 2, explain *wengi* and *nyingine*: which noun does each agree with, and why do they look different?
3. In line 3, why *mwenye* and not *wenye*? Why *matamu* and not *tamu*?
4. In line 4, the sister asks the price of one orange. Ask instead "how many oranges do you have?".
5. In line 6, why *mawili*, when line 1 has *mbili*?
6. In lines 7 to 9, why do the verbs about the child have *ki-* and not *a-*?
7. In line 10, what would *Tulikisaidia saa mbili* mean, and why does the passage use *kwa muda wa*?
8. What time is *saa mbili asubuhi* in English? Say "at half past four in the afternoon".
9. Say "this market is bigger than that one".
10. Say "all the oranges are sweet, but the other fruits are not".
11. Say "put the shoes under the bed".
12. Say "who is the writer of this book?".
13. Say "I want a new red car".
14. Say "she speaks Swahili well, because she lived in Mombasa for three years".
15. Say "the shopkeeper himself opened the door".
16. Say "my sister and I are going to my aunt's on Friday".
17. Correct *\*Nina vitabu viingi.*
18. Correct *\*Tulikuwa na safari nyema.*
19. Correct *\*Kitabu kiko juu meza.*
20. Correct *\*Alikwenda kwenye daktari.*
21. Correct *\*Gani kitabu unataka?*
22. Correct *\*Miti mizote imeanguka.*
23. Correct *\*Kijitu mdogo anacheza.*
24. Correct *\*Ali ni mrefu zaidi Juma.*

## Worked answers

1. "On Saturday at eight in the morning my sister and I went to the market. There were more people at the market than on other days. A seller with a loud voice was selling sweet oranges. My sister asked him, 'How much is one orange?' The seller answered, 'Only a hundred shillings.' We bought twelve oranges and then went to the shoe shop. Near that shop we saw a little child crying. We asked it, 'Where's your mum?' The child didn't answer; it was lost. We helped it look for its mother for twenty minutes. At last its mother arrived in a hurry, picked the child up and thanked us warmly. Unity is strength." (Lessons 5.1 to 5.11.)
2. *Wengi* is class 2, with *watu*: *wa-* + *-ingi*, *a + i* merges to *e*. *Nyingine* is class 10, with *siku*: the nasal before a vowel is *ny-*. Both are adjectives with vowel stems. Lessons 5.1, 5.5.
3. *-enye* takes *mw-* in class 1, unlike *-a* (*wa*). *Matamu* is class 6, agreeing with *machungwa*; *tamu* would be class 5 or 9. Lessons 5.2, 3.3.
4. *Una machungwa mangapi?* *-ngapi* agrees with *machungwa* (class 6). Lesson 5.10.
5. *Mbili* is the class 9/10 form (*n* + *w* → *mb*), agreeing with *saa*. *Mawili* is class 6, agreeing with *machungwa*: the final unit of a big number still agrees. Lessons 5.3, 5.4.
6. *Kitoto* is a diminutive, and diminutives take class 7 agreement even for people: *kikilia*, *hakikujibu*, *kilikuwa kimepotea*, and the object prefix *-ki-* in *tukakiuliza*. Lesson 5.11.
7. "We helped it at eight o'clock." *Saa mbili* alone is a clock time; *kwa muda wa* marks a stretch of time. Lesson 5.4.
8. 8 a.m. For 4.30 p.m., count the daylight hours from 7 a.m. (*saa moja*): 4 p.m. is the tenth, *saa kumi*. So *saa kumi na nusu mchana* (*mchana* runs to 5 p.m.). Lesson 5.4.
9. *Soko hili ni kubwa kuliko lile.* Class 5 adjective with no prefix; *lile* stands for *soko lile*. Lessons 5.6, 3.12.
10. *Machungwa yote ni matamu, lakini matunda mengine si matamu.* *Yote* takes the pronominal *y-*, *mengine* the adjective *ma-* + *i* → *me-*. Lessons 5.5, 5.9.
11. *Weka viatu chini ya kitanda.* The compound keeps *ya*. Lesson 5.8.
12. *Mwandishi wa kitabu hiki ni nani?* (*nani* where the answer would be). Lessons 5.10, 5.11.
13. *Ninataka gari jipya jekundu.* Class 5: *jipya* (one-syllable stem keeps *ji-*), *jekundu* (*j-* before the vowel). Lesson 5.1.
14. *Anasema Kiswahili vizuri, kwa sababu aliishi Mombasa kwa muda wa miaka mitatu.* Class 8 adverb, *kwa sababu* + clause, *kwa muda wa* + *miaka mitatu* (class 4). Lessons 5.3, 5.4, 5.7, 5.9.
15. *Mwenye duka mwenyewe alifungua mlango.* *Mwenye duka* "owner of the shop", then *mwenyewe* "himself" in class 1. Lessons 5.2, 5.5.
16. *Mimi na dada yangu tunakwenda kwa shangazi yangu Ijumaa.* Two subjects including "I" take *tu-*; *kwa* + person for "at someone's"; no preposition before *Ijumaa*. Lessons 5.4, 5.8, 5.9.
17. *-ingi* after *vi-*: *i + i* gives *i*: *Nina vitabu vingi.* Lesson 5.1.
18. *-ema* has the exceptional class 9 form *njema*: *Tulikuwa na safari njema.* Lesson 5.1.
19. *Juu* needs *ya* to take a noun: *juu ya meza.* Lesson 5.8.
20. A person as a place takes *kwa*: *Alikwenda kwa daktari.* Lesson 5.8.
21. *Gani* never comes first and follows its noun: *Unataka kitabu gani?* Lesson 5.10.
22. *-ote* takes the pronominal prefix: *Miti yote imeanguka.* Lesson 5.5.
23. A diminutive agrees in class 7: *Kijitu kidogo kinacheza.* Lesson 5.11.
24. *Zaidi* needs *ya* before the compared noun, or use *kuliko*: *Ali ni mrefu kuliko Juma* or *mrefu zaidi ya Juma*. Lesson 5.6.
