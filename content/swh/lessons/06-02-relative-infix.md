## The idea

Lesson 6.1 made relative clauses with a separate word: *mtu ambaye alikuja* "the person who came". Swahili has a shorter way. It takes the o of reference that sat on *amba-* and puts it **inside the verb**: *mtu aliyekuja*. One word now says "who came".

Lesson 4.1 showed the verb template, with a slot after the tense marker kept empty until part 6. This is that slot. The **relative marker** is the o of reference placed in the verb, straight after the tense marker: *a-li-**ye**-ku-ja*. Almasi and colleagues (p. 297) call it the "relative infix"; this guide calls it the relative marker, as it calls the other pieces markers (lesson 4.1).

Why have two ways? They are not quite equal. The relative marker can only follow three tense markers: *-li-* (past), *-na-* (present) and the future, which takes a longer shape *-taka-* in relatives. Almasi and colleagues are firm: relative markers "are NEVER used with the past perfect tense", by which they mean *-me-*. They cannot follow the negative past or future either. *Amba-* has no such limits. So the two systems share the work: the short form where it exists, *amba-* everywhere else.

Why *-taka-* for the future? The sources consulted state the fact but do not explain it, so this guide does not guess. Learn it as the future's relative shape: *atakayekuja* "who will come", with *-taka-* + *-ye-*.

## How it is built

### The slot order

The verb is built left to right:

1. subject prefix: agrees with the subject of the relative clause;
2. tense: *-li-*, *-na-* or *-taka-*;
3. relative marker: the o of reference of the noun described (*-ye-*, *-o-*, *-cho-* ...);
4. *ku-*, if the verb is short;
5. root and final vowel.

### Chain of thought

Say "the person who came yesterday is my friend".

1. The noun: *mtu*, class 1.
2. The relative clause: "he came yesterday". Subject prefix *a-*; past *-li-*.
3. Relative marker for class 1: *-ye-*. So far *a-li-ye-*.
4. *-ja* "come" is a short verb. The relative marker cannot carry stress (Wiktionary's verb appendix lists the markers that can take stress, and the relative marker is not among them), so *ku-* stays: *a-li-ye-ku-ja*.
5. *Mtu aliyekuja jana ni rafiki yangu.*

Say "the next lesson is hard", that is, "the lesson which follows".

1. *Somo*, class 5.
2. "It follows": present *-na-*, subject prefix *li-*: *linafuata*.
3. Relative marker class 5 *-lo-* after the tense: *li-na-lo-fuata*.
4. *Somo linalofuata ni gumu.*

Say "the students who will win will get a prize".

1. *Wanafunzi*, class 2: subject prefix *wa-*, relative *-o-*.
2. Future in a relative: *-taka-*.
3. *Wa-taka-o-shinda*: *watakaoshinda*.
4. *Wanafunzi watakaoshinda watapata tuzo.* The main verb *watapata* has the ordinary *-ta-*: it is not a relative.

### When the subject is not the noun

The subject prefix and the relative marker can belong to different nouns. In *kitabu nitakachosoma* "the book that I will read", *ni-* is "I" (the reader), and *-cho-* is class 7, pointing back to *kitabu*. Lesson 6.3 is about exactly this, and about the object prefix that can join them.

### Persons

"I who", "you who" use the person subject prefix with the relative of a person: *-ye-* for one, *-o-* for several, as with *sisi ambao* in lesson 6.1. *Wewe uliyeshinda* "you who won"; *sisi tuliofika* "we who arrived".

## Where it applies

- **Past, present and future, positive**: *aliyekuja*, *anayekuja*, *atakayekuja*; *kilichoanguka*, *kinachoanguka*, *kitakachoanguka*.
- **Every class**, with the o of reference of lesson 6.1: *waliokuja*, *uliokuja* (class 3), *iliyoanguka* (classes 4 and 9), *lililoanguka*, *yaliyoanguka*, *vilivyoanguka*, *zilizoanguka*. Almasi and colleagues: *fedha zilizoibiwa* "money which was stolen".
- **People in other classes**, with class 1/2 agreement: *fundi aliyekuja*, *rafiki anayeishi Mombasa*.
- **Person subjects**: *mimi niliyekuja*, *wewe uliyeshinda*, *sisi tuliofika*, *ninyi mliokuja*.
- **As a noun, with no head**: *aliyekuja* "the one who came", *waliofika* "those who arrived". After *nani*: *Nani atakayelipa?*; in proverb-like statements, *atakayeshinda atapata tuzo* "whoever wins will get a prize".
- **After a demonstrative**: *yule aliyekuja* "that one who came".
- **With an object on the verb**: the object prefix goes after the relative marker: *aliyekiona* "who saw it" (lesson 6.3).
- **With a different subject**: *kitabu nitakachosoma* (lesson 6.3).
- **For the present, *-na-* relatives can also mean habit**: *watoto wanaocheka sana* "children who laugh a lot" (Almasi and colleagues).

Where it does **not** apply, use *amba-* (lesson 6.1): after *-me-*, *-ja-*, the negative past and future, the compound tenses, and the conditionals. The negative present has its own *-si-* relative (lesson 6.4).

## Watch out

**No relative marker after -me-.** *\*Wageni waliomefika* tries to put *-o-* after *-me-*. Almasi and colleagues: never. Use *amba-*: *wageni ambao wamefika*.

**The future in a relative is -taka-, not -ta-.** *\*Watoto watao-kuja* (*\*wataokuja*) is wrong. *Watoto watakaokuja* "the children who will come".

**Do not use -taka- outside a relative.** *\*Nitakakuja* is not a future. Plain future: *nitakuja*. *-taka-* appears only before a relative marker.

**Keep ku with short verbs.** *\*Aliyeja* drops the ku. The relative marker cannot carry stress: *aliyekuja*, *wanaokula*, *atakayekunywa*.

**Do not put the relative marker at the end as well.** *\*Aliyekujaye* doubles it. The ending position belongs to the tenseless relative of lesson 6.4 (*asemaye*), which has no tense marker at all.

**Do not use a negative verb with the relative marker for the past or future.** *\*Hakuyekuja* is not a word. Almasi and colleagues: past and future relatives are negated only with *amba-*: *mtu ambaye hakuja*, *wasichana ambao hawatapata zawadi*.

**Do not take the relative verb for a sentence.** *Mtu aliyekuja.* is only a phrase, "the person who came". A sentence needs a main verb: *Mtu aliyekuja ameondoka.*

**Do not confuse aliyekuja with aliye.** *Aliyekuja* is past "who came". *Aliye* with no verb root is "who is" (lesson 6.5): *mtu aliye nyumbani* "the person who is at home". Its *-li-* is the old verb "be", not the past.

**Do not let the relative marker agree with the nearest noun.** *\*Kiwanda kinavyotengeneza viatu* takes class 8 from *viatu*. The marker points back to *kiwanda*: *kinachotengeneza*.

**Do not put a relative marker into -ki-, -ka- or hu- verbs.** *\*Akiyekuja* and *\*akayekuja* do not exist. Those forms have no relative slot; use *amba-* with a plain tense.

## Connections

Putting "who" inside the verb has no match in Spanish or French. Japanese and Korean come closer in spirit: a Korean relative is also a verb form, *온 사람* "the person who came", with the relative ending *-(으)ㄴ* on the verb and no separate "who" word. The difference is that Korean puts the clause before the noun and does not agree with it, while the Swahili verb carries the noun's class.

## Exercises

1. Build "the woman who is cooking" (*mwanamke*).
2. Build "the cars which will come" (*magari*).
3. Build "the tree which fell" with the relative marker, then with *amba-*.
4. Translate *Mwigizaji aliyeshinda tuzo atatoa hotuba.*
5. Translate *Kozi inayofuata itaanza Jumatatu.*
6. Why is *\*wageni waliomefika* wrong? Give the right form.
7. Correct *\*Mtalii atayekuja kesho.*
8. Correct *\*Watazamaji waliokula wamefika* if the meaning is "the spectators who are eating have arrived".
9. Translate "who discovered this?" with a headless relative.
10. Take apart *tulichokipendekeza* into its slots (it has an object prefix, lesson 6.3).

## Worked answers

1. *Mwanamke* class 1: *a-na-ye-pika*, *mwanamke anayepika*. Lesson 6.2.
2. *Magari* class 6: subject prefix *ya-*, future *-taka-*, relative *-yo-*, then *ku-* for the short verb: *magari yatakayokuja*.
3. *Mti* class 3: *mti ulioanguka*; *mti ambao ulianguka*. Both are correct (lessons 6.1, 6.2).
4. "The actor who won the prize will give a speech." *Aliyeshinda* is the relative; *atatoa* is the main verb (*-toa* "give out").
5. "The next course will start on Monday." *Kozi* class 9: *i-na-yo-fuata*.
6. The relative marker cannot follow *-me-* (Almasi and colleagues). Use *amba-*: *wageni ambao wamefika*.
7. The future in a relative is *-taka-*: *mtalii atakayekuja kesho*.
8. "Who are eating" is present: *wanaokula*. *Watazamaji wanaokula wamefika.* (*Waliokula* is "who ate".)
9. *Nani aliyegundua hiki?* "Who is the one who discovered this?" Class 1 relative because the question is about a person.
10. *tu-li-cho-ki-pendekez-a*: *tu-* "we" (subject), *-li-* past, *-cho-* class 7 relative (the thing), *-ki-* class 7 object prefix, *-pendekez-a* "propose". "(the thing) which we proposed". Lesson 6.3 explains why *-ki-* appears.
