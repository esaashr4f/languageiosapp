## The idea

"The man **who is** a doctor", "the book **which is** on the table", "people **who have** money". Each of these needs a relative of "be" or "have". But present "be" in Swahili is not a normal verb: it is *ni* (lesson 2.1), or a subject prefix with *-ko*, *-po*, *-mo* (lesson 2.5). Neither has a slot for a relative marker. So Swahili uses a separate form.

That form is old. It is a subject prefix + a verb root **-li** "be" + the o of reference: *a-li-ye* "who is", *ki-li-cho* "which is". Lesson 4.2 told you that the past marker *-li-* goes back to this verb "be", which survives in modern Swahili only in a few forms; this is the main one. Wikipedia's grammar article: present relatives of the copula are "formed with the subject prefix, the stem -li- in the positive and -si- in the negative, and the suffixed relative marker". So:

- *aliye* "who is", *asiye* "who is not";
- *kilicho* "which is", *kisicho* "which is not";
- *walio* "who are", *wasio* "who are not".

Because "have" in Swahili is "be with" (*nina* "I am with", lesson 2.6), the same form makes "who has": *aliye na* "who is with", that is, "who has". *Walio na pesa* "those who have money".

## How it is built

### "Who is"

Say "a doctor who is also a writer". (Wikipedia's grammar uses this phrase.)

1. *Daktari*, a person: class 1 agreement.
2. Subject prefix *a-*, root *-li*, relative *-ye*: *aliye*.
3. What he is: *pia mwandishi* "also a writer".
4. *Daktari aliye pia mwandishi.*

The form works before anything *ni* could take: a noun, an adjective, an *-a* phrase, a place. For place, it may add the place suffix *-ko*, *-po*, *-mo* of lesson 2.5: Wikipedia's grammar gives *watu wote waliomo gerezani* "all the people who are in prison" (*wa-li-o-mo*). A plain *walio* before a place is also normal: Almasi and colleagues' exercise has *wanyama walio katika zu* "animals that are in the zoo".

### "Who has"

Say "students who have a ticket".

1. *Wanafunzi*, class 2: *walio*.
2. "Have" is "be with": add *na*. *Walio na.*
3. *Wanafunzi walio na tiketi.*

### "Which I have": the object of "have"

Say "the money I have". Now the noun described is what is had, and the one who has is "I".

1. Subject prefix for the haver: *ni-*.
2. Root *-li*, relative marker pointing to the money: *pesa* is class 10, *-zo*. *Ni-li-zo.*
3. "With it": *na* + the class 10 reference form *-zo*: *nazo*.
4. *Pesa nilizo nazo.* Wikipedia's grammar gives *Hizi ni pesa zote nilizo nazo* "this is all the money I have".

The noun's class appears twice, as in the *naye* of lesson 6.3: once in the relative, once on *na*.

### Other tenses

*-li* "be" has only this present relative. For other times, use the relative of *kuwa* "be" (lesson 6.2): *aliyekuwa* "who was", *atakayekuwa* "who will be", and with *na*: *aliyekuwa na* "who had". Wikipedia's grammar: "In other tenses, relatives are formed regularly from the word *kuwa*." For *-me-* and the negatives of other tenses, *amba-*: *ambaye amekuwa*, *ambaye hakuwa na*.

### kuliko, taken apart

Lesson 5.6 taught *kuliko* "than" as one word and promised to take it apart. It is this form for class 17: *ku-li-ko*, "where there is". Wikipedia's grammar table lists *kuliko* as the class 17 (and 15) "which is, where there is", and Almasi and colleagues note the literal use *mahali kuliko na watu wengi* "a place where there are many people". *Juma ni mrefu kuliko Ali* is, word for word, "Juma is tall where Ali is".

## Where it applies

- **"Who is / which is" + noun**: *aliye pia mwandishi*, *walio wanachama* "who are members".
- **+ adjective**: *kitu kilicho kizuri* "a thing which is good" (a relative is not needed here, since *kitu kizuri* says the same, but it adds weight); Wikipedia's *nyumba zisizo za kawaida*.
- **+ -a phrase or -enye phrase**: *zisizo za kawaida*, *kilicho cha zamani*.
- **+ place**: *kilicho mezani*, *walio katika zu*, and with the place suffixes *waliopo*, *waliomo*, *walioko*.
- **Headless**: *aliye kando*; Wikipedia's grammar gives *Zilizomo* "(the things) which are inside", the usual heading for a table of contents.
- **"Who has", "which has"**: *walio na*, *kilicho na*, *asiye na* "who has no".
- **"Which I have / which they have"**: *nilizo nazo*, *alicho nacho* "which he has", *walivyo navyo* "which they have" (class 8).
- **Persons**: *mimi niliye*, *wewe uliye*, *sisi tulio*, *ninyi mlio*; negative *nisiye*, *usiye*, *tusio*, *msio* (Wikipedia's grammar table).
- **Other tenses** through *kuwa*: *aliyekuwa*, *atakayekuwa*; with *amba-* for the rest.
- **Inside fixed words**: *kuliko* "than"; *palipo* and *pasipo* "where there is (not)", lesson 6.6.

## Watch out

**Do not read aliye as a past.** *Aliye nyumbani* is "who **is** at home", now. "Who was at home" is *aliyekuwa nyumbani*.

**Do not put ni after aliye.** *\*Aliye ni daktari* says "be" twice. *Aliye* already contains "is": *aliye daktari*.

**Do not drop na for "have".** *\*Walio pesa* means "who are money". "Who have money": *walio na pesa*.

**Do not forget the second reference form.** *\*Pesa nilizo na* leaves *na* without its object. *Pesa nilizo nazo.*

**Do not use the relative marker of the haver.** *\*Pesa niliye nazo* uses *-ye* for "I". The relative points to the money: *nilizo*.

**Do not add ku.** *\*Alikuye* is not a word: *aliye* has no tense marker and no *ku*.

**Do not use aliye for "who has been" or a future.** *\*Aliye mwalimu mwaka ujao* puts a present form on a future fact. Use *atakayekuwa mwalimu*.

**Do not confuse kilicho with kilichokuwa.** *Kitabu kilicho hapa* "the book which is here"; *kitabu kilichokuwa hapa* "the book which was here".

**About ambaye ni.** You will hear *mtu ambaye ni daktari* "a person who is a doctor". It is common, but the grammars consulted do not discuss it; *aliye daktari* is the form they teach.

## Connections

The *-li* "be" root is a fossil, like the English "-s" of "he is" against "be": an old verb surviving only in a corner of the system. Spanish speakers can think of *el que está en la mesa* "the one who is on the table": Swahili *kilicho mezani* packs *el que está* into one word. Arabic *allaḏī ʕinda-hu māl* "who has money", literally "who with-him money", has the same "be with" idea of "have" as *walio na pesa*.

## Exercises

1. Say "the children who are at school".
2. Say "a woman who has a licence".
3. Say "the certificates I have".
4. Translate *Wakazi wasio na maji wanalalamika.* (*-lalamika* "complain".)
5. Translate *Hakimu aliyekuwa hapa jana ni rafiki wa mwanasheria.*
6. Correct *\*Mwananchi aliye ni mwanachama.*
7. Correct *\*Kadi niliyo na.*
8. Say "who was a member" and "who is a member".
9. Take *kuliko* apart and say why it can mean "than".

## Worked answers

1. *Watoto walio shuleni* (or *waliopo shuleni*). Class 2 *wa-li-o*. Lesson 6.5.
2. *Mwanamke aliye na leseni.* "Has" is "is with".
3. *Vyeti nilivyo navyo.* Class 8 *-vyo* twice: in the relative and on *na*.
4. "The residents who have no water are complaining." *Wasio na* "who are not with".
5. "The magistrate who was here yesterday is the lawyer's friend." *Aliyekuwa* is the past relative of *kuwa*; *hakimu* takes class 1 agreement.
6. *Aliye* already means "who is": *mwananchi aliye mwanachama*.
7. *Kadi* class 9: *kadi niliyo nayo*. *Na* needs its reference form.
8. *Aliyekuwa mwanachama* (past, *kuwa*); *aliye mwanachama* (present, *-li*).
9. *Ku-li-ko*: class 17 subject prefix, root *-li* "be", class 17 relative *-ko*: "where there is". "Tall where Ali is" came to mean "taller than Ali". Lessons 5.6 and 6.5.
