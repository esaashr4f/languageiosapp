## The idea

The relatives so far have pointed back to a person or a thing. Swahili uses the same slot to point to a **place**, a **time** and a **manner**. It does this with the relative markers of the place classes and of class 8:

- **-po-** (class 16) "when" and "where (at that spot)": *alipofika* "when he arrived";
- **-ko-** (class 17) "where (around there, to there)": *alikokwenda* "where he went";
- **-mo-** (class 18) "where (inside)": *ninamoishi* "in which I live";
- **-vyo-** (class 8) "as, how, the way that": *anavyosema* "as he says".

Why these classes? The place classes 16, 17 and 18 already mean "at", "around" and "in" (lesson 3.13). A relative with their marker means "at which", "around which", "in which": a place. Time borrows from place, as it does in *hapo* "there; then" (lesson 3.12): the definite spot *-po-* is also the definite moment. And class 8 *-vyo-* is the class of *hivyo* "like that" and *vyema* "well" (lessons 3.12, 5.1): the plural of things serves as "ways, manners". This guide glosses them `C16.REL`, `C17.REL`, `C18.REL` and `C8.REL`.

Almasi and colleagues (pp. 305–311) treat these as one family and give the rules that hold for all of them. They take the relative marker after *-li-*, *-na-* and *-taka-*, and "NEVER" after *-me-*. In the present, the marker can also go at the end with *-na-* dropped, like the general relative of lesson 6.4: *asomavyo* "as he reads", *kipoteapo* "when it gets lost".

These clauses carry no noun in front of them; the marker is enough. But you can add one: *wakati* "time", *mahali* "place", *jinsi* "manner", which makes the meaning plain and gives you a place to hang *amba-* when the relative marker cannot be used.

## How it is built

### When

Say "when the guests arrived, we greeted them".

1. The time clause: "the guests arrived". Subject *wageni*, class 2: *wa-*. Past *-li-*.
2. "When": *-po-* after the tense. *Wa-li-po-fika.*
3. Main clause: *tuliwasalimu* "we greeted them".
4. *Wageni walipofika, tuliwasalimu.* (Almasi and colleagues.)

For a future "when", Swahili uses the future relative *-takapo-*, where English uses a present: *Pumzika utakapomaliza kazi* "rest when you finish work" (Almasi and colleagues), literally "when you will finish".

### Where

Say "the police know where he went".

1. "He went": *a-li-kw-enda*.
2. "Where (to)": general place *-ko-*. It cannot carry stress, so *kw-* stays: *a-li-ko-kw-enda*, *alikokwenda*.
3. *Polisi wanajua alikokwenda.* (Almasi and colleagues.)

Use *-po-* for a definite spot, *-ko-* for a general area or a direction, *-mo-* for inside, as with *-po*, *-ko*, *-mo* in lesson 2.5.

### How, as

Say "do as I said".

1. "I said": *ni-li-sema*.
2. "As, the way that": *-vyo-*: *ni-li-vyo-sema*.
3. Almasi and colleagues list three words that often stand before it: *kama* "as, like", *jinsi* "how, the manner in which", *kadiri* "as far as, as much as".
4. *Fanya kama nilivyosema.*

### The negative

For a negative "when" in the present, use *-si-* + the marker, as in lesson 6.4: *usipocheka* "when you don't laugh". For a negative "when" in the past and future, and for *-me-*, Almasi and colleagues use *amba-* after *wakati*: *wakati ambapo hakulalamika* "at the time when he didn't complain". For a negative "where" they use *mahali* + *amba-* in every tense, the present included: *mahali ambapo hainyeshi mvua* "where it does not rain", *mahali ambako hakwenda* "the place where he didn't go" (pp. 309–311).

## Where it applies

**-po- "when":**

- **Past, present, future**: *alipofika*, *anapofika*, *atakapofika*.
- **Whenever**, with *kila*: *kila unapocheka*.
- **Present at the end of the verb**: *kipoteapo* "when it gets lost", *ufanyikapo* (Almasi and colleagues).
- **After *wakati***: *wakati alipofika* "at the time when he arrived"; *wakati ambapo* + any tense.
- **Inside compound tenses**: *nilipokuwa mtoto* "when I was a child" (*kuwa* takes the relative like any verb).
- **Negative present**: *-sipo-*, which also means "if not" (lesson 6.12).

**-po-, -ko-, -mo- "where":**

- **A definite spot**: *alipokaa* "where he sat". Almasi and colleagues: *inaponyesha mvua, watu wanaishi* "where it rains, people live".
- **A direction or area**: *alikokwenda*, *wanakotoka* "where they come from".
- **Inside**: *ninamoishi*, Almasi and colleagues' *atakamoingia mwizi* "where the thief will enter".
- **After *mahali***: *mahali alipozaliwa* "the place where he was born"; *mahali ambapo, ambako, ambamo* for the negatives.
- **As the object of "know", "show", "ask"**: *Sijui walikoficha hazina.*

**-vyo- "as, how":**

- **After *kama*, *jinsi*, *kadiri***: *kama nilivyosema*, *jinsi alivyopika*, *kadiri tutakavyoweza*.
- **Alone**: *Tunaendesha gari atakavyoendesha* "we drive the way he will drive" (Almasi and colleagues).
- **"As you know", "as I said"**: *kama unavyojua*, *kama nilivyosema*.
- **Indirect "how"**: *Sijui alivyofanya* "I don't know how he did it".
- **With *amba-***: *jinsi ambavyo* + any tense (Almasi and colleagues: *jinsi ambavyo mama anafunga*).

## Watch out

**Do not use a present for a future "when".** *\*Pumzika unapomaliza kazi* is a habit ("whenever you finish"). For one future moment: *utakapomaliza*.

**Do not use -po- with -me-.** *\*Alipomefika*: Almasi and colleagues' rule holds. Use *wakati ambapo amefika*, or *alipofika*.

**Do not drop ku.** *\*Alipoja* and *\*alikoenda* (in the standard written form): *alipokuja*, *alikokwenda*. The marker cannot carry stress.

**Prefer the relative to wapi or lini inside a statement.** *Sijui wapi alikwenda* copies English "where" word for word. The relative is the usual way: *Sijui alikokwenda.* Almasi and colleagues (p. 403) do report questions with the question word kept (*alitaka kujua lini tulikwenda*), so the word-for-word version is heard; lesson 6.9 returns to reported questions.

**Do not use -po- for "where to".** *Alipokwenda* is possible for a definite spot, but "where he went (off to)" is more naturally *-ko-*: *alikokwenda*.

**Do not translate "how" in "I know how he did it" with vipi.** *\*Najua vipi alifanya.* Use *-vyo-*: *Ninajua alivyofanya* or *jinsi alivyofanya*.

**Do not mix -vyo- with the class of a noun.** *-vyo-* in *alivyopika* is "the way", not a class 8 thing. If a class 8 noun is meant, the sentence has one: *vitabu alivyovinunua* "the books which he bought".

**Do not negate the -vyo- clause to negate the sentence.** Almasi and colleagues: in *Hataimba kama tutakavyoimba* "he won't sing as we will sing", the main verb is negated and the *-vyo-* clause stays positive.

**Do not confuse -po- "when" with -po- "is here".** *Yupo* "he is here" (lesson 2.5) has no tense; *alipo* "where he is" has *-li-* "be" (lesson 6.5); *alipofika* "when he arrived" has the past *-li-* and a root.

## Connections

French *où* means both "where" and "when" (*le jour où il est arrivé*), the same blend of place and time as *-po-*. Spanish *donde*, *cuando*, *como* are three separate words; Swahili makes all three from one slot by changing the class. Japanese uses nouns for the same job, *toki* "time" and *tokoro* "place" after a verb, which is close to Swahili *wakati alipofika*, *mahali alipozaliwa*.

## Exercises

1. Say "when I was a child".
2. Say "when you arrive, call me" with a future relative.
3. Say "I know where they live" (general area).
4. Say "do as the teacher says".
5. Translate *Mbu walikuwa wengi mahali alipolala.*
6. Translate *Gari lilipowasha taa, tuliona njia.*
7. Rephrase with a relative: *Sijui wapi umeweka tochi.*
8. Correct *\*Utakapofika, ninakupigia simu* (meaning "when you arrive, I'll call you").
9. Correct *\*Alieleza vipi alipika.*
10. Say "the place where we didn't sleep" with *amba-*.

## Worked answers

1. *Nilipokuwa mtoto.* The relative of *kuwa*: *ni-li-po-ku-wa*. Lesson 6.6.
2. *Utakapofika, nipigie simu.* Future *-takapo-*; the request is a subjunctive with the object prefix (lesson 4.10).
3. *Ninajua wanakoishi.* *-ko-* for a general place.
4. *Fanya kama mwalimu anavyosema* (or *asemavyo*).
5. "There were many mosquitoes where he slept." *Alipolala*: class 16 *-po-*, a definite spot. *Mbu* takes class 2 agreement: *wengi*.
6. "When the car switched on its lights, we saw the road." *Gari* class 5: *li-li-po-washa*.
7. A statement normally takes a relative rather than *wapi*; and *-me-* takes no relative, so use the past: *Sijui ulikoweka tochi* (or *mahali ambapo umeweka tochi*).
8. The main verb is a future: *nitakupigia simu*. *-na-* would be "I am calling you".
9. *Alieleza jinsi alivyopika.* "How" in a statement is *-vyo-* (with *jinsi*).
10. *Mahali ambapo hatukulala.* A negative past "where" needs *amba-* (Almasi and colleagues).
