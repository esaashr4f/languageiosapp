## The idea

"**Juma** broke the window" can be said with stress on *Juma*: it was Juma, not someone else. English does it with the voice, or with "it was Juma who broke the window". Swahili has a word for it: **ndi-** + the reference form of the noun, *ndiye* "it is he", *ndicho* "it is that (thing)". Wikipedia's grammar article calls *ndi-* the **emphatic** or **focusing copula**: it "places its subject in focus, emphasising that it is *that particular referent* and not another". **Focus** is the name for singling out one part of a sentence as the new or contrasting part.

You have seen *ndi-* once already. *Ndiyo* "yes" (lesson 2.4) is *ndi-* + *-yo*, "it is (so)", as Wiktionary explains it. *Siyo* and *sivyo* "it is not so" (lesson 2.4) are its negatives.

The pieces are all known:

- *ndi-* "it is (precisely)", or its negative *si-* "it is not", the same *si* as the negative copula of lesson 2.1;
- the **reference form**: the same short piece that follows *na* (*naye*, *nacho*, lesson 3.14), which is also the o of reference of lesson 6.1. For persons, *-mi*, *-we*, *-ye*, *-si*, *-nyi*, *-o*.

So *ndi-ye* "it is he", *ndi-cho* "it is it (class 7)", *si-ye* "it is not he", *si-cho* "it is not it".

Focus words are naturally followed by a relative clause, since "it is X **who** did it" needs a "who". Here everything from lessons 6.1 to 6.6 comes together. Wikipedia's grammar gives *Mimi ndiye niliyewaona kwanza* "I'm the one who saw them first", with the plain *Niliwaona kwanza* beside it.

## How it is built

### Chain of thought

Say "it was Juma who broke the window".

1. The focused person: *Juma*, class 1 agreement.
2. "It is he": *ndi-* + *-ye*: *ndiye*.
3. "Who broke the window": a subject relative, lesson 6.2: *a-li-ye-vunja dirisha*.
4. *Juma ndiye aliyevunja dirisha.*

The focused noun comes first; *ndiye* agrees with it; the relative clause follows and agrees with it too.

Say "this is the book I want".

1. *Kitabu hiki*, class 7.
2. "It is it": *ndicho*.
3. "Which I want": object relative, *ni-na-cho-taka*.
4. *Kitabu hiki ndicho ninachotaka.* "This book is the one I want."

Say "it isn't the teacher who said that".

1. *Mwalimu*, class 1. Negative: *si-ye*, *siye*.
2. "Who said that": *aliyesema hivyo*.
3. *Mwalimu siye aliyesema hivyo.*

### Persons

The emphatic copula for "I", "you" and "we" has its own forms, *ndimi* "it is I", *ndiwe* "it is you", *ndisi* "it is we", *ndinyi* "it is you (all)". Wikipedia's grammar notes that the third-person forms are "frequently used" instead: *mimi ndiye*, *sisi ndio*. That is what its example does: *Mimi ndiye niliyewaona*.

### Place and manner

The place and manner classes make some of the most useful focus words:

- *ndipo* "it is there (that)"; *ndiko* "it is (over) there"; *ndimo* "it is in there";
- *ndivyo* "it is so, that's how it is"; *sivyo* "it is not so".

*Ndivyo ilivyo* "that is how it is", with the *-vyo-* "how" of lesson 6.6 and the relative "be" of lesson 6.5, is an everyday phrase.

## Where it applies

- **"It is X who / which ..."**: *Juma ndiye aliyevunja*, *kitabu hiki ndicho ninachotaka*.
- **Persons**: *mimi ndiye*, *wewe ndiye*, *sisi ndio*; or the person forms *ndimi*, *ndiwe*, *ndisi*, *ndinyi* (Wikipedia's grammar).
- **Identifying "the one"**: *Hili ndilo tatizo* "this is the problem"; *Wao ndio wahusika.* Wikipedia's grammar notes that focus forms are "frequently equivalent to a definite phrase in translation": they do part of the work of English "the".
- **Answers to "who?" and "which?"**: *Nani amekuja? — Juma ndiye.* "Who's come? — Juma (is the one)."
- **Negative focus**: *siye*, *sicho*, *silo*, *sio*, *siyo*, *sizo*: "it is not X (but someone else)", often followed by *bali* or *ni* (lesson 5.9): *Si mimi, ni yeye.*
- **Place**: *ndipo*, *ndiko*, *ndimo* + a "where" relative.
- **Manner**: *ndivyo* "that's how", *sivyo* "it isn't so"; *ndivyo ilivyo*.
- **Yes and no**: *ndiyo*, *siyo* are the class 9 forms, used as answers.

## Watch out

**Do not use ni for "it is he who".** *Juma ni aliyevunja dirisha* is understood but loses the contrast. The focus word is *ndiye*.

**Do not make ndi- agree with the wrong noun.** *\*Kitabu hiki ndiye ninachotaka* uses the person form for a book. Class 7: *ndicho*.

**Do not attach ndi- to a full pronoun.** *\*Ndiyeye* and *\*ndimimi* are wrong. *Ndi-* takes the short reference form: *ndiye*, *ndimi*.

**Do not drop the relative in the clause after ndi-.** *\*Juma ndiye alivunja dirisha* puts a plain verb after the focus word. The sentence means "it is Juma **who** broke it": *aliyevunja*.

**Do not mix the two negatives.** *Si* alone is the negative copula "is not" (*Mimi si mwalimu*). *Siye*, *sicho* deny a focus: "it is not he (who...)". *\*Mwalimu si aliyesema* misses the reference form: *Mwalimu siye aliyesema.*

**Do not read sisi as "it is not we".** Wikipedia's grammar points out that the expected form is the same as the pronoun *sisi* "we", so speakers use *sio* instead: *Sio sisi* "it isn't us".

**Do not confuse ndio and ndiyo.** *Ndio* is class 2, 3, 11 or 14 (*wao ndio*); *ndiyo* is class 4, 6 or 9, and the word for "yes". In speech and casual writing, "yes" is often spelt *ndio*; the standard spelling is *ndiyo*.

**Do not stack ndi- on a verb.** *\*Ndialivunja* is not a word. *Ndi-* stands before the relative verb as a separate word: *ndiye aliyevunja*.

## Connections

French *c'est lui qui l'a cassé* and Spanish *fue Juan quien rompió la ventana* build focus with "it is" + a relative, exactly the Swahili frame *Juma ndiye aliyevunja*. The difference is that Swahili's "it is" agrees with the focused noun. Japanese marks a focused subject with *ga* (*Jon ga watta* "it was John who broke it"), and Korean with *-i/-ga*: a particle rather than a copula, but the same job.

## Exercises

1. Say "it is she who cooked".
2. Say "this is the house where I was born" (*-zaliwa*, lesson 4.6 vocabulary).
3. Say "it wasn't the suspect who stole the money".
4. Translate *Hili ndilo lengo letu.*
5. Translate *Shahidi ndiye aliyethibitisha ushahidi huu.*
6. Correct *\*Kalamu hii ndiye niliyoinunua.*
7. Correct *\*Rais ndiye alisema hivyo.*
8. Answer *Nani amekiri kosa?* with "the driver (is the one)".
9. Explain why *ndiyo* means "yes".

## Worked answers

1. *Yeye ndiye aliyepika.* *Ndiye* + subject relative. Lesson 6.7.
2. *Hii ndiyo nyumba nilimozaliwa* ("in which", *-mo-*) or *nilipozaliwa*. *Ndiyo* is class 9 for *nyumba*. Lesson 6.6 for the relative.
3. *Mshukiwa siye aliyeiba pesa.* *Siye* denies the focus.
4. "This is our goal." *Lengo* class 5: *hili*, *ndilo*, *letu*.
5. "It was the witness who confirmed this evidence." *Shahidi* takes class 1 agreement.
6. *Kalamu* is class 9: *Kalamu hii ndiyo niliyoinunua.*
7. After a focus word, the verb is a relative: *Rais ndiye aliyesema hivyo.*
8. *Dereva ndiye.* (Or in full: *Dereva ndiye aliyekiri kosa.*)
9. *Ndi-* "it is" + class 9 *-yo*: "it is (so)". The class 9 form is the one used for a whole statement, as in *hiyo* "that (matter)". Wiktionary gives the analysis; lessons 2.4 and 6.7.
