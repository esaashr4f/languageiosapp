## The idea

"She said **that she is cooking**." "I know **that you are tired**." "I don't know **whether he will come**." The clause in bold is the object of *say*, *know*, *ask*: a whole clause standing where a noun would stand. This guide calls it a **that-clause** (grammars say "complement clause"): a clause that completes a verb of saying, thinking or knowing.

**Reported speech** is the use of such clauses to pass on what someone said, in your own words, instead of quoting them. Almasi and colleagues (ch. 39) call quoting *usemi halisi* "actual speech" and reporting *usemi wa taarifa* "speech of report".

Swahili introduces that-clauses with a small set of words that Almasi and colleagues (p. 401) call "speech indicators":

- **kwamba** and **kuwa** "that", for statements; also *ya kwamba*;
- **kama** "whether, if", for yes-no questions;
- **eti** "supposedly, they say", when the reporter doubts or is surprised by what was said.

Look at the first two. *Kwamba* is an infinitive: Wiktionary lists it as the verbal noun of a verb *-amba* "say", the same *amba-* that, with a relative marker, made "which" in lesson 6.1. *Kuwa* is the infinitive "to be". So *Alisema kwamba anapika* is, at bottom, "he said, saying: she is cooking". The "that" is optional: Almasi and colleagues often leave it out (*Baba alisema alisoma kitabu*).

The real work of reporting is done by the subject prefixes. Swahili has no separate "she said she" ambiguity, Almasi and colleagues point out, because the prefix shows whose point of view the clause takes. *Natasha alisema, ninapenda kula* can only mean "Natasha said **I** (the speaker) like to eat"; for Natasha herself you say *anapenda*.

## How it is built

### Statements

Report *"Ninapika,"* Anna alisema ("I'm cooking," Anna said), to someone else.

1. The reporting verb: *Anna alisema*.
2. "That": *kwamba* (or *kuwa*, or nothing).
3. Turn the persons round: Anna's "I" is "she" for you: *ni-* → *a-*. *Anapika.*
4. *Anna alisema kwamba anapika.*

If you report it to Anna herself, her "I" becomes "you": *Ulisema kwamba unapika*. If Anna reports it back, it stays "I": *Nilisema kwamba ninapika*. Almasi and colleagues set out every combination (pp. 399–400). Possessives and pronouns shift the same way: *chakula changu* → *chakula chake* (pp. 406–407).

### The tense

Almasi and colleagues: if the action is still going on or still to come when you report it, the tense **does not change**. *Nyanya alisema kwamba wataondoka sasa hivi* "Grandmother said they would leave right now", with *-ta-* kept. If the time has passed, the tense can shift back ("backshifting"): *-na-* becomes *-li-*, *-li-* becomes *-likuwa -me-*. Their table also turns *-ta-* into *-nge-*, the "would" of lesson 6.13. With backshifting, *leo* "today" becomes *siku ile* "that day", *kesho* becomes *siku iliyofuata* "the day that followed", and *hili* "this" becomes *lile* "that".

### Yes-no questions: kama

Report *"Unakwenda shuleni?"* mwalimu aliuliza.

1. Reporting verb: *Mwalimu aliuliza*, or *Mwalimu alitaka kujua* "the teacher wanted to know" (Almasi and colleagues' usual phrase).
2. "Whether": *kama*.
3. Shift the person: if the question was to me, *ni-*.
4. *Mwalimu aliuliza kama ninakwenda shuleni.*

### Question-word questions

Keep the question word, or use a relative (lesson 6.6). Almasi and colleagues: *alitaka kujua lini tulikwenda* "he wanted to know when we had gone". The relative version, *alitaka kujua tulipokwenda*, is also correct.

### Commands

A command is reported as a subjunctive (lesson 4.11), with the person shifted: *"Soma!"* → *Aliniambia nisome* "he told me to read". *Kwamba* may stand before it: *Alisema kwamba tuondoke* "he said we should leave".

## Where it applies

- **After verbs of saying**: *-sema*, *-ambia* (with a person object), *-eleza*, *-jibu*, *-ahidi*, *-fahamisha* "inform", *-sisitiza* "insist", *-nong'ona* "whisper" (Almasi and colleagues' list includes *-eleza*, *-fahamisha*, *-nong'ona*).
- **After verbs of thinking and knowing**: *-jua*, *-fikiri*, *-dhani*, *-amini*, *-tambua*, *-hisi* "feel", *-sikia* "hear (that)", *-kumbuka*, *-sahau*.
- **After nouns of saying**: *habari kwamba...* "the news that ...", *taarifa kwamba* "the report that", *uvumi kwamba* "the rumour that".
- **Yes-no questions**: *-uliza kama*, *-taka kujua kama*, *sijui kama*.
- **Question-word questions**: with the question word or a relative (*-po-*, *-ko-*, *-vyo-*, *-cho-*): *Sijui alichosema* "I don't know what he said".
- **Commands and requests**: *-ambia*, *-omba*, *-agiza*, *-shauri* + subjunctive (lesson 4.11).
- **Doubt**: *eti*, alone or after the verb: *Anasema eti hana pesa* "he says (supposedly) he has no money".
- **With no that-word**: *Alisema alisoma kitabu* (Almasi and colleagues). Leaving it out is normal in speech.

## Watch out

**Do not keep the speaker's "I" when you mean the speaker.** *Juma alisema ninakwenda* (without quotation marks) means "Juma said **I** (the reporter) am going". For Juma: *Juma alisema anakwenda.*

**Do not use a relative for "that" after a verb.** *\*Alisema ambacho anapika* confuses the relative "which" with the "that" of saying. Use *kwamba* or *kuwa*.

**Do not use kwamba for "whether".** *\*Aliuliza kwamba ninakwenda* reports a question as a statement. Yes-no questions take *kama*.

**Do not use kama for a reported statement.** *Kama* in *Alisema kama atakuja* is read as "whether" or "if". For "that", Almasi and colleagues do list *kama*, but *kwamba* is the safe choice.

**Do not report a command with the indicative.** *\*Aliniambia ninapumzika* reports a statement ("he told me I am resting"). A command needs the subjunctive: *nipumzike.*

**Do not use the infinitive for a reported command to someone else.** *\*Aliniambia kupumzika* copies English "told me to rest". Swahili gives the second verb its own subject: *nipumzike.*

**Do not backshift what is still true.** If the vehicles are still here and the trip is still coming, keep the tense: *Alisema wataondoka kesho.*

**Do not mistake kuwa "that" for kuwa "be".** In *Ninajua kuwa umechoka*, *kuwa* introduces the clause; it is not "to be tired". The verb of the clause is *umechoka*.

## Connections

Spanish and French make the same choices: *dijo que* "said that", *preguntó si* "asked whether", *me dijo que descansara* "told me to rest" with a subjunctive. Swahili *kwamba*, *kama* and the subjunctive line up with *que*, *si* and the Spanish subjunctive almost one for one. Japanese marks reported words with *to* (*kuru to itta* "said he would come"), and like Swahili it shifts the point of view of the clause rather than the tense.

## Exercises

1. Report *"Nimechoka,"* Amina alisema, to someone else.
2. Report the same words back to Amina.
3. Report *"Utakuja kesho?"* mama aliuliza, a question put to you.
4. Report *"Funga mlango!"* baba aliniambia.
5. Report *"Usiende!"* walituambia.
6. Translate *Sijui kama wanajua kwamba tumefika.*
7. Translate *Eti hakuiba pesa!*
8. Correct *\*Aliuliza kwamba nina pesa.*
9. Correct *\*Mwalimu alituambia kusoma.*
10. Say "I don't know what he wants".

## Worked answers

1. *Amina alisema kwamba amechoka.* Her "I" becomes *a-*. Lesson 6.9.
2. *Ulisema kwamba umechoka.* Reporting to her, her "I" becomes "you".
3. *Mama aliuliza kama nitakuja kesho.* Yes-no question: *kama*; her "you" is me. If tomorrow has passed, Almasi and colleagues would shift the future (lesson 6.13).
4. *Baba aliniambia nifunge mlango.* Command → subjunctive, person shifted. Lessons 4.11, 6.9.
5. *Walituambia tusiende.* Negative subjunctive.
6. "I don't know whether they know that we have arrived." *Kama* for the question, *kwamba* for the statement inside it.
7. "Supposedly he didn't steal the money!" *Eti* shows the reporter's doubt.
8. A question takes *kama*: *Aliuliza kama nina pesa.*
9. A command to someone else takes the subjunctive: *Mwalimu alituambia tusome.*
10. *Sijui anachotaka.* A headless class 7 relative for "what". Lessons 6.3, 6.9.
