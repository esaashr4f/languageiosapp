## The idea

A **purpose clause** says what an action is for: "I saved money **so that I could travel**", "she came **to help**". Lesson 4.11 gave you the core of it: *ili* + subjunctive, *Nimejificha ili wasinione* "I've hidden so that they don't see me". This lesson maps the whole territory: when to use the subjunctive, when an infinitive, and when a noun phrase with *kwa ajili ya* "for the sake of".

Why the subjunctive? A purpose is not yet a fact. It is something wished for, exactly like the "let me", "that you should" of lesson 4.10. Spanish and French make the same choice: *para que vengas*, *pour que tu viennes*. And because a subjunctive carries its own subject prefix, the purpose clause can have a **different subject** from the main clause: *Nilimpa pesa ili anunue chakula* "I gave him money so that he could buy food".

When the purpose has the **same subject** as the main verb, Swahili can also use the infinitive, as English does with "to": Almasi and colleagues' narrative text has *ili kuwaburudisha watalii* "in order to entertain the tourists". After verbs of going and coming the infinitive alone is enough (lesson 4.11: *alirudi kucheza*).

**Kwa ajili ya** takes a **noun**, not a clause. *Ajili* is a noun (Wiktionary: class 9); the phrase is "for the sake of": *kwa ajili ya sikukuu* "for the holiday" (Almasi and colleagues' exercise). It is also used for a cause, "because of": Almasi and colleagues gloss *kwa ajili* "for, because", and use it in *wananchi wametaabika kwa ajili ya kushuka kwa uchumi* "citizens have suffered because of the decline of the economy".

## How it is built

### ili + subjunctive

Say "I saved money so that I could travel".

1. Main clause: *Niliweka akiba* "I put by savings".
2. "So that": *ili*.
3. The purpose: "I travel", subjunctive *nisafiri* (a loan verb: no change of final vowel, lesson 4.10).
4. *Niliweka akiba ili nisafiri.*

The subjunctive has no tense, so it serves for past, present and future main clauses alike. Almasi and colleagues also give *ili kwamba* with the same meaning.

### Negative purpose: "so as not to"

Put *-si-* in the subjunctive: *ili wasinione* "so that they don't see me"; *Nilifunga mlango ili mbwa asiingie* "I shut the door so that the dog wouldn't come in".

### ili + infinitive

Same subject only: *Alikwenda sokoni ili kununua mboga* "she went to the market to buy vegetables". *Ili anunue* is equally correct.

### kwa ajili ya + noun or infinitive

1. "For the guests": *kwa ajili ya wageni*.
2. "For cooking": the infinitive is a class 15 noun, so *kwa ajili ya kupika*.

## Where it applies

- **Different subject**: *ili* + subjunctive only: *Nilimpa pesa ili anunue chakula.*
- **Same subject**: *ili* + subjunctive or *ili* + infinitive: *ili nisafiri*, *ili kusafiri*.
- **After going and coming**: the infinitive alone: *Alikuja kunisalimu* "he came to greet me" (lesson 4.11).
- **Negative purpose**: *ili* + negative subjunctive: *ili asiingie*. With the infinitive, *ili kutochelewa* "so as not to be late" uses the negative infinitive of lesson 4.12.
- **ili kwamba**: same as *ili* (Almasi and colleagues).
- **Purpose of a thing**: *kwa ajili ya* + noun or infinitive: *sufuria kwa ajili ya kupika*; also *-a* + infinitive (*maji ya kunywa*, lesson 3.11).
- **"For" a person's benefit**: *kwa ajili ya wageni*, *kwa ajili yako* "for your sake" (possessive after *ya*).
- **Cause**: *kwa ajili ya ukame* "because of the drought"; *kwa ajili ya hayo* "therefore, for those reasons" (Almasi and colleagues).
- **"What for?"**: *kwa ajili ya nini?* (lesson 5.10 promised this), or *-ni* on the verb.

## Watch out

**Do not use the infinitive with a new subject.** *\*Nilimpa pesa ili kununua chakula* leaves unclear who buys; it reads as "I gave him money in order (for me) to buy food". Different subject: *ili anunue*.

**Do not use the indicative after ili.** *\*Ili ananunua* states a fact. *Ili* needs the subjunctive: *ili anunue*.

**Do not use kwa or kwa ajili ya before a clause.** *\*Kwa ajili ya ninasafiri* puts a clause after a noun phrase. Use *ili nisafiri*.

**Do not drop ya.** *\*Kwa ajili wageni* is incomplete: *kwa ajili ya wageni*.

**Do not use kwa + infinitive for purpose.** *\*Nilikuja kwa kusoma* (lesson 4.11): *Nilikuja kusoma* or *ili nisome*.

**Do not use English "for" + -ing as a person's purpose.** "Thanks for coming" is *Asante kwa kuja*, with *kwa* "by, for" (lesson 5.8), not purpose. Purpose is about what is wanted next; *kwa kuja* is about what has happened.

**Do not add ku to a short verb in the subjunctive.** *\*Ili ukule*: the subjunctive takes no *ku*: *ili ule*.

## Exercises

1. Say "I'm studying so that I pass the exam".
2. Say "she called me so that I would help her".
3. Say "we hurried so as not to be late" (*-fanya haraka*, *-chelewa*).
4. Translate *Walilima shamba ili kupata mavuno mengi.*
5. Translate *Kitabu hiki ni kwa ajili yako.*
6. Correct *\*Nilikupigia simu ili kuja.* ("I phoned you so that you would come.")
7. Correct *\*Alifanya kazi kwa ajili ya apate pesa.*
8. Say "for whom did you cook?" with *kwa ajili ya*.

## Worked answers

1. *Ninasoma ili nifaulu mtihani* (or *ili kufaulu*). Same subject: subjunctive or infinitive. Lesson 6.10.
2. *Alinipigia simu ili nimsaidie.* Different subject: subjunctive; *-m-* is "her". Lessons 4.10, 6.10.
3. *Tulifanya haraka ili tusichelewe.* Negative subjunctive.
4. "They farmed the field to get a big harvest." *Ili* + infinitive, same subject.
5. "This book is for you." *Kwa ajili* + *ya* + possessive *-ako* in class 9: *yako*.
6. Different subject: *Nilikupigia simu ili uje.*
7. A clause after "for" takes *ili*: *Alifanya kazi ili apate pesa.* *Kwa ajili ya* takes a noun: *kwa ajili ya pesa*.
8. *Ulipika kwa ajili ya nani?* *Nani* stands where the answer would. Lessons 5.10, 6.10.
