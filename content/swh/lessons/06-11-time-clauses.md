## The idea

A **time clause** places one event in time against another: "**when** I arrived", "**before** we eat", "**after** she left", "**until** he comes", "**since** they moved", "**while** I was reading".

English uses one kind of tool for all of these: a conjunction followed by an ordinary clause. Swahili spreads the work over three tools you already have, and this lesson puts them side by side:

1. **The relative marker -po-** (lesson 6.6) for "when": *alipofika*.
2. **Prepositions with an infinitive**: *baada ya* "after" and *kabla ya* "before" take a noun (lesson 5.8), and an infinitive is a noun (lesson 4.12). So "after eating" is *baada ya kula*, "before sleeping" is *kabla ya kulala*.
3. **Words that take the subjunctive**: Almasi and colleagues (p. 383) list *mpaka* and *hadi* "until", *tangu* "since", *hata* "even, until" among the words after which a subjunctive is used: *Winchi isitengenezwe hadi nije* "the winch should not be repaired until I come".
4. **-ki-** (lesson 4.14) for "while", the background situation: *Nilimwona akipika* "I saw her cooking".

Why an infinitive after *kabla ya* and *baada ya*? Because they are nouns of time with *ya* "of": *baada* "after(ness)", *kabla* "before(ness)". "Before of eating" is a noun phrase, and the infinitive is how Swahili turns a verb into a noun. This also explains a limit: the infinitive has no subject prefix, so the subject is understood from the main clause or the context.

## How it is built

### After, before

Say "after eating, we rested".

1. "After": *baada ya*.
2. The event as a noun: *kula*.
3. Main clause: *tulipumzika*.
4. *Baada ya kula, tulipumzika.*

Say "brush your teeth before you go to bed". (Almasi and colleagues have *Nipige mswaki kabla ya kulala!*)

1. Main clause: *Piga mswaki*.
2. "Before": *kabla ya* + infinitive *kulala*.
3. *Piga mswaki kabla ya kulala.*

If the two events have different subjects, use a "when" relative instead, or put a noun after *baada ya*: *baada ya wageni kuondoka* "after the guests left", literally "after the guests' leaving". The noun stands before the infinitive.

### Until

*Mpaka* and *hadi* mean "up to, as far as, until". Before a noun or a time: *mpaka jioni* "until evening" (Almasi and colleagues: *Alisoma mpaka jioni*). Before a clause about something still to come, a subjunctive: *Sitamkopesha mpaka akulipe* "I won't lend to him until he pays you" (Almasi and colleagues). Before a clause about something that did happen, an ordinary past: Almasi and colleagues' *tulipiga ngoma mpaka mikono yetu ilitoka damu* "we drummed until our hands bled".

### Since

*Tangu* "since, from" (lesson 4.5) before a noun: *tangu mwaka jana*. Before a clause, the "when" relative is natural: *tangu alipofika* "since (the time) he arrived". Almasi and colleagues also list *tangu* among the words taking a subjunctive.

### While

The *-ki-* form for the same subject: *Akitembea, aliimba* "while walking, she sang". For two subjects at the same time, *-po-* or *wakati*: *Nilipokuwa nikisoma, watoto walicheza* "while I was reading, the children played" (compound tense, lesson 4.16).

## Where it applies

- **"When"**: *-po-* in any of the three relative tenses (lesson 6.6); *wakati* + *-po-*; *wakati ambapo* + any tense.
- **"After"**: *baada ya* + infinitive (same subject) or + noun + infinitive (new subject); *baada ya* + noun (*baada ya chakula*, lesson 5.8).
- **"Before"**: *kabla ya* + infinitive or + noun. Almasi and colleagues: *Tulifikiria sana jambo hili kabla ya kufanya uamuzi* "we thought about this a lot before making a decision". You will also meet *kabla* followed directly by a negative *-ja-* verb, "before he had (yet) come"; the sources consulted for this lesson do not describe it, so recognise it only (unverified).
- **"Until"**: *mpaka*, *hadi* + noun, time or place (*mpaka hapa* "as far as here"); + subjunctive for what is still to come; + past for what happened.
- **"Since"**: *tangu* + noun or time (*tangu mwaka jana*), + *-po-* relative.
- **"From ... until"**: *tangu ... mpaka/hadi*, *kutoka ... mpaka*.
- **"While"**: *-ki-* (same subject); *-po-* + compound tense or *wakati* (different subjects).
- **"As soon as"**: *mara* "time" + *-po-*: *mara alipofika* "the moment he arrived".

## Watch out

**Do not put a tensed verb after baada ya or kabla ya.** *\*Baada ya tulikula* copies English "after we ate". Use the infinitive: *baada ya kula*.

**Do not drop ya.** *\*Kabla kulala* is heard in speech, but the written standard keeps *ya*: *kabla ya kulala*.

**Do not use a future after mpaka for something still to come.** *\*Usiondoke mpaka nitarudi* is not the pattern Almasi and colleagues teach. Use the subjunctive: *mpaka nirudi*.

**Do not use -ki- for different subjects.** *\*Nikisoma, watoto walicheza* reads as "if I read, the children played". Use *nilipokuwa nikisoma*.

**Do not use tangu with an English present perfect only.** English says "I have lived here since 2010"; Swahili usually uses the present: *Ninaishi hapa tangu 2010*, because the state still holds (lesson 4.5).

**Do not confuse mpaka "until" with mpaka "border".** Wiktionary lists both: the noun *mpaka* (class 3/4, pl. *mipaka*) means "border, boundary". "Until" never takes a plural or an adjective.

**Do not use -po- with -me-.** *\*Alipomefika*: lesson 6.6.

## Exercises

1. Say "after reading, I slept".
2. Say "before eating, wash your hands" (*-nawa*).
3. Say "wait until I come".
4. Say "since I arrived, it has been raining" (*-nyesha*, present).
5. Translate *Baada ya mvua kunyesha, tulipanda mbegu.*
6. Translate *Walichimba hadi magharibi.*
7. Correct *\*Kabla ya tulikula, tulinawa mikono.*
8. Correct *\*Nikipika, mume wangu alisoma gazeti.*

## Worked answers

1. *Baada ya kusoma, nililala.* Lesson 6.11.
2. *Kabla ya kula, nawa mikono.* *-nawa* as a command.
3. *Ngoja mpaka nije.* Subjunctive of *-ja*: *nije* (no *ku*). Almasi and colleagues' *hadi nije*.
4. *Tangu nilipofika, mvua inanyesha.* The rain still goes on: present.
5. "After the rain had fallen, we planted the seeds." A noun subject before the infinitive.
6. "They dug until sunset."
7. An infinitive after *kabla ya*: *Kabla ya kula, tulinawa mikono.*
8. Different subjects: *Nilipokuwa nikipika, mume wangu alisoma gazeti.* *-ki-* alone needs the same subject. Lessons 4.14, 6.11.
