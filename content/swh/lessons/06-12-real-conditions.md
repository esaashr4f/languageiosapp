## The idea

A **condition** is an "if" clause: one event depends on another. A **real** (or open) condition is one that may well happen: "if it rains, we'll stay in". The next lesson deals with unreal conditions, "if I were rich", "if you had come".

Lesson 4.14 gave you the main tool: the *-ki-* form before a future or a command, *Ukija kesho, utamwona* "if you come tomorrow, you'll see him". It also previewed the negative, *usipokuja* "if you don't come", and two if-words, *kama* and *ikiwa*. With the relatives of this part behind you, that preview can now be explained, and the pieces put in order.

**-sipo-** is not a new tense. It is the negative relative of lesson 6.4 (*-si-* + relative marker) with the class 16 marker *-po-* of lesson 6.6: *u-si-po-kuja*, literally "at the time when you don't come". Since *-po-* means "when", the negative "when not" slides into "if not, unless". Wikipedia's grammar calls it "quite close in meaning to a negative equivalent of *-ki-*", and notes that, like all relative markers, *-po-* cannot carry stress, so short verbs keep *ku*. A well-known proverb uses it:

*Usipoziba ufa utajenga ukuta* "if you don't fill the crack, you'll be building a wall" (Wiktionary: "a stitch in time saves nine").

**The if-words** stand in front of a clause and make the condition explicit:

- *kama* "if" (also "as, like" and "whether");
- *ikiwa* "if", literally "it being", the *-ki-* form of *kuwa* (lesson 4.14);
- *iwapo* "if, supposing that" (Wiktionary);
- *endapo* "if it should happen" (Wiktionary: from *-enda* "go" + *-po*, literally "when it goes").

Notice that *iwapo* and *endapo* are themselves frozen *-po-* relatives, like *kuliko* in lesson 6.5.

## How it is built

### -ki- for "if"

Say "if you see him, tell him".

1. Condition: *u-ki-mw-ona*: *ukimwona*.
2. Result: a command, *mwambie*.
3. *Ukimwona, mwambie.* (Lesson 4.14.)

### -sipo- for "if not, unless"

Say "if you don't fill the crack, you'll build a wall".

1. Subject: "you", *u-*.
2. Negative condition: *-si-po-*.
3. Root: *-ziba* "fill (a hole)": *u-si-po-ziba*.
4. Result in the future: *utajenga ukuta*.
5. *Usipoziba ufa utajenga ukuta.*

With a short verb, keep *ku*: *asipokuja* "if he doesn't come".

### kama and ikiwa

*Kama* takes an ordinary verb in the tense the meaning needs:

- present: *Kama sisomi, sitafaulu* "if I don't study, I won't pass" (Almasi and colleagues, as in lesson 4.14);
- past: *Kama alikuja jana, hakuniona* "if he came yesterday, he didn't see me" (the speaker does not know);
- with *-ki-*, doubling the "if" for emphasis: *Kama ukija...* (Wikipedia's grammar).

*Ikiwa* and *iwapo* work the same way: *Ikiwa mvua itanyesha, tutakaa nyumbani* "if it rains, we'll stay at home".

### The result clause

After a real condition about the future, the result is a future or a command: *utajenga*, *mwambie*. Almasi and colleagues describe conditional *-ki-* as "invariably followed by a future tense or an imperative". For a general truth, the result is a present or a habitual: *Ukichanganya rangi hizi, unapata kijani* "if you mix these colours you get green".

## Where it applies

- **-ki- + future or command**: the everyday "if": *ukija, utamwona*; *ukimwona, mwambie*.
- **-ki- + present or habitual**: general truths, "whenever": *ukichanganya..., unapata*.
- **-sipo- for "if not", "unless"**: *usipoziba*, *asipokuja*, *usipolipa*. With every person and class: *isiponyesha* "if it doesn't rain" (Almasi and colleagues: *Mvua isiponyesha*).
- **kama + any tense**: present, past, future, negative: the only way to state a condition about the past or a present fact you are unsure of.
- **kama + -ki-, ikiwa + -ki-**: an emphatic doubled "if" (Wikipedia's grammar).
- **ikiwa, iwapo, endapo** + ordinary tenses: more formal "if, supposing"; *endapo* "in the event that".
- **-sipo- for "when not"**: Almasi and colleagues' *Kila usipocheka* "whenever you don't laugh" (lesson 6.6).
- **Unreal conditions** ("if I were", "if I had") are lesson 6.13.

## Watch out

**Do not use -ki- with a past result.** *\*Ukija jana, ulimwona* mixes an open "if" with a fact already past. For a past you are unsure of: *Kama ulikuja jana, ulimwona.* For an unreal past: lesson 6.13.

**Do not make -ki- negative.** *\*Usikija*, *\*ukihuji* are not words. Use *-sipo-*: *usipokuja*; or *kama* + negative: *kama huji*.

**Do not drop ku after -sipo-.** *\*Asipoja*: *asipokuja*.

**Do not put -sipo- in the result.** *\*Ukija, hautamwona* is fine, but *\*Ukija, usipomwona* has two conditions and no result.

**Do not use kama + -ki- + kama.** Pick one "if" (or the emphatic pair once): *Kama ukija, utamwona.*

**Do not confuse "if" kama with "whether" kama.** After *-uliza*, *-jua*, *sijui*, *kama* is "whether" (lesson 6.9): *Sijui kama atakuja.* At the head of a condition with a result, it is "if".

**Do not use -po- relative for a doubtful "if".** *Utakapofika* "when you arrive" assumes you will; *ukifika* "if you arrive" leaves it open. Choose by how sure you are.

## Exercises

1. Say "if you are hungry, eat".
2. Say "if he doesn't come, we'll leave".
3. Say "unless it rains, the harvest will be small" (*-nyesha*, *mavuno*).
4. Translate *Usipofunga mlango, mbwa ataingia.*
5. Translate *Endapo kutakuwa na mgomo, hatutafanya kazi.*
6. Correct *\*Usikija, nitakasirika.*
7. Correct *\*Ukikosea jana, mwalimu alikusahihisha.*
8. Explain why *usipokuja* has *ku* and *ukija* does not.

## Worked answers

1. *Kama una njaa, kula.* "Have hunger" with *kama* + the present of *-wa na* (lesson 2.6). Lesson 6.12.
2. *Asipokuja, tutaondoka.* *-sipo-* + *ku*.
3. *Mvua isiponyesha, mavuno yatakuwa madogo.* Class 9 *i-si-po-*; *mavuno* class 6.
4. "If you don't shut the door, the dog will come in." *-sipo-* + future.
5. "If there is a strike, we won't work." *Endapo* + future; *kutakuwa na* "there will be" (lesson 2.6, 4.3).
6. There is no negative *-ki-*: *Usipokuja, nitakasirika.*
7. A past event: *Kama ulikosea jana, mwalimu alikusahihisha.* (Or, if it surely happened: *Ulipokosea jana...*) Lessons 6.6, 6.12.
8. *-ki-* can carry the stress; the relative marker *-po-* cannot, so the short verb keeps *ku* (lesson 4.4; Wikipedia's grammar).
