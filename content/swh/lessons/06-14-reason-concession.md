## The idea

Two kinds of clause are left. A **reason clause** says why: "**because** it rained". A **concession** is a clause that admits a fact which might have stopped the main event, but did not: "**although** it rained, we went out". Concession is the opposite of a reason: the expected link between the two events fails.

Lesson 5.9 taught *kwa sababu* "because" and *kwani* "for". This lesson adds the rest of the reason words and the concession words.

**Reason.** *Kwa kuwa* "because, since" is *kwa* "by, with" + *kuwa* "being": "by (its) being (so)". Wiktionary lists it as a conjunction "because"; Almasi and colleagues gloss it "for, because, the reason being". *Kwa vile* is also used; Almasi and colleagues gloss it "because of this", and their example is *Waya wa kompyuta umelegea, kwa vile kompyuta haichaji* "the computer cable has come loose; because of this the computer is not charging". *Maadamu* "since, given that" is a loan from Arabic *mā dāma* "as long as" (Wiktionary).

**Concession.** The two main words, *ingawa* and *ijapokuwa*, are fossils of verb forms. Almasi and colleagues (p. 349) explain that older Swahili had tense markers *-nga-* and *-japo-* meaning "though, even if", which "are not used as regular tense markers in today's Swahili, except for the specific construction of *ingawa* and *ijapokuwa*". Wiktionary analyses *ingawa* as *i-* (class 9 subject) + *-nga-* "even if, though" + *-wa* "be": literally "though it be". *Ijapokuwa* is *i-* + *-japo-* + *kuwa*, the same idea. Both are now invariable words: you learn them whole.

**Even if.** *Hata* "even" (lesson 3.14) + *kama* "if" makes *hata kama* "even if". It joins a concession to a condition: the result holds whether or not the condition is met.

## How it is built

### Because

Say "since you are late, we'll start without you".

1. Reason: *umechelewa* "you are late" (lesson 4.6).
2. "Since, because": *kwa kuwa*. It often comes first, like English "since".
3. Main clause: *tutaanza bila wewe*.
4. *Kwa kuwa umechelewa, tutaanza bila wewe.*

### Although

Say "although the tree fell, it didn't hurt me". (Almasi and colleagues' exercise.)

1. "Although": *ingawa*.
2. The admitted fact: *mti ulianguka*.
3. The result that goes against expectation: *haukuniumiza*.
4. *Ingawa mti ulianguka, haukuniumiza.*

*Ingawa* and *ijapokuwa* themselves never change. Almasi and colleagues: they "cannot themselves be negated; instead the rest of the sentence can be negated": *Ingawa hamnidharau, siwaheshimu* "even though you don't despise me, I don't respect you".

### Even if

Say "even if it rains, we'll go".

1. *Hata kama* + the condition: *mvua itanyesha* (an ordinary future), or the *-ki-* form: *hata kama mvua ikinyesha*.
2. Main clause: *tutakwenda*.
3. *Hata kama mvua itanyesha, tutakwenda.*

Almasi and colleagues also use *ingawa* for "even if" with a future: *Ingawa utampa mshahara mkubwa, ataacha kazi* "even if you give him a big salary, he will leave the job".

### "That's why"

*Ndiyo maana* "that is the reason" uses the focus copula of lesson 6.7 with *maana* "meaning; reason": *Nilikuwa mgonjwa; ndiyo maana sikuja* "I was ill; that's why I didn't come".

## Where it applies

**Reason:**

- *kwa sababu* + clause, *kwa sababu ya* + noun (lesson 5.9).
- *kwa kuwa* + clause, before or after the main clause.
- *kwa vile* + clause, "because of this" (Almasi and colleagues' gloss).
- *kwani* "for" after a statement (lesson 5.9).
- *maadamu* "since, given that".
- *kwa ajili ya* + noun "because of" (lesson 6.10).
- *ndiyo maana* "that's why" pointing back to a reason already given.

**Concession:**

- *ingawa*, *ijapokuwa* + any tense, positive or negative: past (*ingawa mti ulianguka*), present (*ingawa mnanidharau*), perfect (*ijapokuwa hajamaliza*), future as "even if" (*ingawa utampa*).
- *japo*, *ijapo*: shorter forms that Almasi and colleagues list beside *ijapokuwa*.
- *hata kama* + any tense or + *-ki-*: "even if".
- *hata hivyo* "even so, nevertheless" at the start of the second clause (lesson 5.9): *Mvua ilinyesha; hata hivyo, tulikwenda.*
- *lakini* "but" as the simplest alternative: *Mvua ilinyesha, lakini tulikwenda.*

## Watch out

**Do not try to inflect ingawa.** *\*Ingawi*, *\*hangawa* are not words. Almasi and colleagues: negate the clause, not *ingawa*: *Ingawa hamnidharau*.

**Do not use ingawa and lakini together.** *\*Ingawa mvua ilinyesha, lakini tulikwenda* says the contrast twice, as English "although... but". Choose one.

**Do not put kwa kuwa in front of a noun.** *\*Kwa kuwa mvua* is incomplete. Before a noun, use *kwa sababu ya mvua* or *kwa ajili ya mvua*.

**Do not confuse kwa kuwa "because" with kwa kuwa "by being".** *Alifaulu kwa kuwa na bidii* "she succeeded by being diligent" is *kwa* + the infinitive *kuwa na* "having". Context, and the clause that follows, tell you which.

**Do not use kama for "even if" without hata.** *Kama mvua itanyesha, tutakwenda* says "if it rains, we'll go", the opposite of the intended concession. Add *hata*.

**Do not use the subjunctive after ingawa.** *\*Ingawa aende* has no meaning here. Concessions take ordinary tenses.

**Do not confuse ndiyo maana with ndiyo "yes".** *Ndiyo* alone answers a question; *ndiyo maana* begins a sentence: "that's why".

## Connections

Spanish *aunque* and French *bien que* differ in a way Swahili does not mirror: *bien que* demands the subjunctive, while *ingawa* takes ordinary tenses, like Spanish *aunque* for facts. *Maadamu* is Arabic *mā dāma* "as long as", and *kwani* (lesson 5.9) sits beside it. Japanese *-noni* "although" and *-kara* "because" attach to the verb of the clause; Swahili once had a verb form for "though" too, *-nga-*, now frozen inside *ingawa*.

## Exercises

1. Say "because I'm tired, I'll sleep early" with *kwa kuwa*.
2. Say "although she is rich, she lives in a small house".
3. Say "even if you don't help me, I'll finish".
4. Translate *Ijapokuwa kulikuwa na tetemeko la ardhi, watu hawakufa.*
5. Translate *Kampuni ilifungwa kwa kuwa haikuwa na wateja.*
6. Correct *\*Ingawa alikuwa mgonjwa, lakini alikwenda kazini.*
7. Correct *\*Hatangawa mgonjwa...* (meaning "although he isn't ill").
8. Join with *ndiyo maana*: "The wire was loose. The computer didn't charge."

## Worked answers

1. *Kwa kuwa nimechoka, nitalala mapema.* *Nimechoka* "I am tired" (lesson 4.6). Lesson 6.14.
2. *Ingawa ni tajiri, anaishi katika nyumba ndogo.*
3. *Hata kama hutanisaidia, nitamaliza.* *Hata kama* + the negative future (*hu-* is the 2SG negative prefix).
4. "Although there was an earthquake, people did not die." (Almasi and colleagues' exercise has *ingawa* and *watu wengi hawakufa*.)
5. "The company was closed because it had no customers." *Kwa kuwa* + clause.
6. Drop *lakini*: *Ingawa alikuwa mgonjwa, alikwenda kazini.*
7. *Ingawa* never changes; negate the clause: *Ingawa si mgonjwa...*
8. *Waya ulikuwa umelegea; ndiyo maana kompyuta haikuchaji.* *-chaji* "charge" is a loan (Almasi and colleagues' *haichaji*). Lessons 6.7, 6.14.
