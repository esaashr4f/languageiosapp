## The idea

Some words only make sense with a negative. English "at all", "any more", "yet" and "ever" (in "I haven't ever...") need a "not" or a question to lean on: "I don't like it at all", but not "\*I like it at all". Grammarians call such words **negative polarity** items: words that need a negative (or a question) in the same sentence.

Swahili has a set of them, and you already know several:

- *kabisa* "completely", with a negative "at all" (lesson 2.3);
- *tena* "again", with a negative "any more" (lesson 2.3);
- *bado* "still", with *-ja-* "not yet" (lesson 4.5);
- *-o-ote* "any", with a negative "no ... at all" (lesson 5.5).

This lesson adds three more and puts them in order:

**kamwe** "never, not at all". Wiktionary gives a striking history: *kamwe* is built from a fossil prefix of class 12 (*ka-*, the class that survives only in traces, lesson 0.2's note on class 12) and an older form of "one", related to *moja*: roughly "not one bit". It is used "in a negative sentence" as "never, not at all", and in a question as "ever". Wiktionary lists *hata kidogo* "not even a little" and *kabisa* as synonyms.

**-wahi** "get the chance to, be in time" (lesson 4.5). In the perfect it asks about experience: *Umewahi kwenda Mombasa?* "have you ever been to Mombasa?". With *-ja-* it gives "never (so far)": *Sijawahi kwenda* "I have never been". Wiktionary traces the verb to Arabic *waḥā* "hurry".

**wala** "and not, nor". Wiktionary: borrowed from Arabic *wa-lā* "and not"; in a negative sentence it means "and" or "or". It is the word lesson 5.9 promised for "neither ... nor": after one negative, *wala* adds the next thing denied: *Sina pesa wala kazi* "I have neither money nor work".

The second half of the lesson is about **negation across clauses**: which clause takes the "not" when a sentence has several. The short answer: Swahili negates the verb that is false, in whatever form that clause uses (a negative prefix, *-si-*, *-sipo-*, *-singe-*, *kuto-*), and does not move the negative to another clause.

## How it is built

### Never, ever

Say "I have never seen a lion".

1. "Never (so far)": the *-ja-* form of *-wahi*: *si-ja-wahi*.
2. + infinitive: *kuona simba*.
3. *Sijawahi kuona simba.*

Say "I will never go back there".

1. A negative future: *sitarudi*.
2. "Never": *kamwe*, after the verb or at the front for emphasis.
3. *Sitarudi huko kamwe.* Or *Kamwe sitarudi huko.*

### Neither ... nor

Say "he didn't eat or drink" ("he neither ate nor drank").

1. First verb, negative: *hakula*.
2. "Nor": *wala*.
3. Second verb, also negative: *hakunywa*.
4. *Hakula wala hakunywa.*

With nouns after one negative verb, *wala* alone joins them: *Sina pesa wala kazi.* With a subjunctive prohibition: *Usiseme, wala usicheke* "don't speak, and don't laugh".

### Not even one

*Hata* "even" + a negative: *Sina hata shilingi moja* "I don't have even one shilling". *Hakuna hata mtu mmoja* "there isn't even one person".

## Where it applies

**Words that need a negative:**

- *kamwe*, *katu* "never, not at all" (Wiktionary lists *katu* as an interjection "never!"), *hata kidogo* "at all", *kabisa* "at all".
- *tena* "any more": *Sisomi tena.*
- *bado* + *-ja-* "not yet": *Bado hajafika.*
- *-o-ote* "any": *Sikuona kitu chochote* "I saw nothing at all".
- *hata* + one: *hata mmoja*, *hata shilingi moja*.
- *ng'o*, a colloquial interjection that "emphasises a negative statement" (Wiktionary): *Sitakupa ng'o!* "I won't give you a thing!"

**In questions** some of them mean "ever": *Umewahi...?*, and Wiktionary notes *kamwe* "(in a question) ever".

**-wahi:**

- Perfect: *Umewahi...?* "have you ever...?"; *Nimewahi...* "I have (once)...".
- *-ja-*: *Sijawahi...* "I have never...".
- Past: *aliwahi kuwa* "he was once" (see the unverified note above).
- Its own meaning, "be in time, get the chance": *Sikuwahi kumwona* "I didn't get the chance to see him".

**wala:**

- Two negative verbs: *hakula wala hakunywa*.
- Nouns after one negative verb: *sina pesa wala kazi*.
- Negative commands: *usiseme, wala usicheke*.
- *Wala ... wala* before each item, for emphasis: *Sina wala pesa wala kazi.*
- At the start of a clause, "and not, nor": *Hakuja, wala hakupiga simu* "he didn't come, nor did he phone".

**Negation across clauses** (a summary of part 6):

- Main verb: the negative prefixes of lessons 2.3 and 4.2.
- Relative clauses: *-si-* (lesson 6.4) or *amba-* + negative verb (lesson 6.1).
- Conditions: *-sipo-* or *kama* + negative (lesson 6.12); *-singe-*, *-singali-* (lesson 6.13).
- Purpose and commands: negative subjunctive (lessons 4.11, 6.10).
- Infinitives: *kuto-* (lesson 4.12): *Nimeamua kutorudi* "I've decided not to go back".
- *Ingawa*, *kwamba*, *kama* never take the negative themselves; their clause does (lesson 6.14).

## Watch out

**Do not use kamwe with a positive verb.** *\*Nitarudi kamwe* means nothing. *Kamwe* needs a negative: *Sitarudi kamwe.*

**Do not use au after a negative for "nor".** *Sina pesa au kazi* is understood, but the standard way to deny both is *wala* (lesson 5.9): *Sina pesa wala kazi.*

**Do not make the second verb positive after wala.** *\*Hakula wala alikunywa* reads as "he didn't eat, and not he drank". Both verbs are negative: *hakunywa*.

**Do not use -wahi + -li- for "have you ever".** *Uliwahi kwenda Mombasa?* asks about one past occasion ("did you get to go?"). For lifetime experience, use the perfect: *Umewahi...?*

**Do not use -me- with wahi for "never".** *\*Nimewahi kamwe* mixes positive and negative. "Never" is *Sijawahi*.

**Do not double the negative in a that-clause.** *\*Sidhani kwamba hatakuja* says "I don't think he won't come". One negative, on the verb you mean.

**Do not use hakuna mtu with a negative relative.** *\*Hakuna mtu asiyejua* means "there is nobody who doesn't know", that is, everybody knows. For "nobody knew": *Hakuna mtu aliyejua.*

**Do not confuse wala with wala "eaters".** Wiktionary lists a second *wala*, a noun form: the class 2 plural of *mla* "eater" (from *-la*). In a sentence, the conjunction always stands between two negatives or nouns.

## Connections

*Wala* is Arabic *wa-lā* "and not", used exactly as in Arabic *lā māl wa-lā ʕamal* "neither money nor work". Spanish *ni ... ni* and French *ni ... ni* behave the same way, and French *jamais* is like *kamwe*: it needs *ne* in standard writing. Japanese *zenzen* "at all" also needs a negative (*zenzen wakaranai* "I don't understand at all"), just like *hata kidogo*.

## Exercises

1. Say "have you ever been to Zanzibar?" (*Zanzibar*).
2. Say "I have never eaten cassava".
3. Say "I will never forget".
4. Say "she has neither a car nor a bicycle".
5. Say "he didn't read and he didn't write".
6. Translate *Hakuna mtu yeyote aliyethubutu kusema.*
7. Translate *Usijali hata kidogo.*
8. Correct *\*Sijawahi kamwe nilikwenda.*
9. Correct *\*Hakuimba wala alicheza.*
10. Say "I've decided not to go back" with *kuto-*.

## Worked answers

1. *Umewahi kwenda Zanzibar?* The perfect of *-wahi*. Lessons 4.5, 6.15.
2. *Sijawahi kula muhogo.*
3. *Sitasahau kamwe.* (*Kamwe sitasahau* for emphasis.)
4. *Hana gari wala baiskeli.* One negative verb, then *wala*.
5. *Hakusoma wala hakuandika.* Both verbs negative.
6. "Nobody at all dared to speak." Negative existential + positive relative.
7. "Don't worry at all." Negative subjunctive + *hata kidogo*.
8. *Sijawahi* already means "never"; follow it with an infinitive: *Sijawahi kwenda.*
9. The second verb must be negative too: *Hakuimba wala hakucheza.*
10. *Nimeamua kutorudi.* The negative infinitive (lesson 4.12).
