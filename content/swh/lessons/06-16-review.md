## Before you start

This review teaches nothing new. It covers all of part 6, with the weight on its second half: relatives with *amba-* and inside the verb (lessons 6.1, 6.2), object relatives (6.3), general and negative relatives (6.4), relatives of "be" and "have" (6.5), "when, where, how" (6.6), focus with *ndi-* (6.7), that-clauses and reported speech (6.9), purpose (6.10), time clauses (6.11), real conditions (6.12), hypothetical conditions (6.13), reason and concession (6.14), and "ever, never, neither ... nor" (6.15). Parts 2 to 5 are assumed.

The passage is a short account of a conversation, constructed for this review. Before you read a line, find its verbs and ask of each: is it a main verb, or does it belong to a relative, a condition, a purpose, a time clause or a report? Then ask which piece tells you so.

## Exercises

1. Translate the passage into natural English without looking at the translation lines.
2. In line 1, why does *nilipokuwa* keep *ku*, and what does *-po-* mean here?
3. In line 1, rewrite *aliyeishi* with *amba-*.
4. In line 2, the fisherman's own words were *"Nimefanya kazi baharini tangu nilipokuwa mtoto."* Explain the change of persons.
5. In line 3, give the fisherman's question in direct speech.
6. In line 4, change the sentence to the past: "if I had been afraid of it, I couldn't have done this work".
7. In line 5, replace *ingawa* with *ijapokuwa*. What else changes?
8. In line 6, why *nijue* and not *ninajua*?
9. In line 7, what would *upepo ukivuma sana* mean?
10. In line 8, what would *nilielewa kwa nini* change?
11. Say "the fish which we caught yesterday were big" (use *-kamata* "catch").
12. Say "it was the old man who taught me to swim".
13. Say "people who don't know the sea are afraid of it" with a negative relative.
14. Say "I'll wait until you come back".
15. Say "if you don't come, I'll go alone".
16. Say "she said that we should rest".
17. Say "I have never been to Lamu" (*Lamu*).
18. Say "although he is rich, he has neither a car nor a house".
19. Correct *\*Kitabu ambacho nimekisoma kilikuwa kizuri* if the meaning is "the book I read (yesterday)" and you want the relative inside the verb.
20. Correct *\*Ningekuwa tajiri, nitajenga nyumba.*
21. Correct *\*Baada ya tulikula, tulilala.*
22. Correct *\*Ingawa alichelewa, lakini alifika.*
23. Correct *\*Mtu aliyeye nyumbani ni baba yangu.*
24. Correct *\*Nilimpa pesa ili kununua chakula* (he is the one who buys).

## Worked answers

1. "Last year, when I was in Mombasa, I met an old fisherman who lived near the sea. He told me that he had worked at sea since he was a child. I asked him whether he was afraid of the sea. He answered, 'If I were afraid of it, I couldn't do this work. Although the sea is dangerous, I have never gone without food or money. Before going out to sea, I look at the wind so that I know whether it is safe. If the wind is strong, I don't go out; if it isn't blowing hard, I leave at dawn.' After talking with him, I understood how fishermen respect the sea." (Lessons 6.1 to 6.15.)
2. *-wa* is a short verb, and the relative marker *-po-* cannot carry stress, so *ku* stays. *-po-* here is "when". Lessons 4.4, 6.6.
3. *mvuvi mzee ambaye aliishi karibu na bahari.* Lesson 6.1.
4. His "I" becomes "he" for the reporter: *nimefanya* → *amefanya*, *nilipokuwa* → *alipokuwa*. The tense is kept: the reporter does not backshift. Lesson 6.9.
5. *"Je, unaiogopa bahari?"* The reporter's *kama* and *a-* become a direct question with *u-*. Lessons 2.4, 6.9.
6. *Ningaliiogopa, nisingaliweza kufanya kazi hii.* Past irrealis in both clauses. Lesson 6.13.
7. *Ijapokuwa bahari ni hatari, ...* Nothing else changes: both words are invariable. Lesson 6.14.
8. Purpose with *ili* takes the subjunctive; *ninajua* would state a fact. Lesson 6.10.
9. "If the wind blows hard": the positive *-ki-* condition. Lesson 6.12.
10. "I understood **why** fishermen respect the sea", and the relative *-vyo-* would give way to an ordinary verb: *kwa nini wavuvi wanaiheshimu bahari*. Lessons 5.10, 6.6, 6.9.
11. *Samaki tuliowakamata jana walikuwa wakubwa.* Fish are animals: class 2 relative *-o-*, object prefix *-wa-*, class 2 adjective. Lessons 3.9, 6.3.
12. *Mzee ndiye aliyenifundisha kuogelea.* Focus + subject relative with object prefix *-ni-*. Lesson 6.7.
13. *Watu wasioijua bahari huiogopa* (or *wanaiogopa*). Negative relative *wa-si-o-*; object prefix *-i-* for *bahari*. Lesson 6.4.
14. *Nitasubiri mpaka urudi.* *Mpaka* + subjunctive (a loan verb, so *urudi*). Lesson 6.11.
15. *Usipokuja, nitakwenda peke yangu.* *-sipo-* + *ku*. Lesson 6.12.
16. *Alisema kwamba tupumzike.* A reported command takes the subjunctive. Lesson 6.9.
17. *Sijawahi kwenda Lamu.* Lesson 6.15.
18. *Ingawa ni tajiri, hana gari wala nyumba.* Lessons 6.14, 6.15.
19. The relative marker cannot follow *-me-*; for a past event use *-li-*: *Kitabu nilichokisoma jana kilikuwa kizuri.* Lessons 6.2, 6.3.
20. The result must also be irrealis: *ningejenga nyumba.* Lesson 6.13.
21. *Baada ya* takes an infinitive: *Baada ya kula, tulilala.* Lesson 6.11.
22. Drop *lakini*: *Ingawa alichelewa, alifika.* Lesson 6.14.
23. "Who is" is *aliye*: *Mtu aliye nyumbani ni baba yangu.* Lesson 6.5.
24. A different subject needs the subjunctive: *Nilimpa pesa ili anunue chakula.* Lesson 6.10.
