## The idea

A **passive** verb makes the thing acted on the subject, and lets the doer drop out or come back as an optional extra: "the house was built (by my uncle)". Swahili forms it with the extension *-w-* (*-liw-*, *-lew-* after a vowel): *-jenga* "build", *-jengwa* "be built".

Part 6 has already used it four times as a preview: *kimefungwa* "it has been closed", *isiyofungwa*, *utaadhibiwa* "you will be punished", *haangukiwi* in a proverb. This lesson teaches the forms properly.

Why use it? For the same reasons as in English, and some more. The doer may be unknown (*gari langu limeibiwa* "my car has been stolen"), unimportant (*kitabu kipya kimetolewa* "a new book has been published"), or better left unsaid. And Swahili has a reason English lacks: the subject prefix agrees with the subject, so whatever you make the subject controls the agreement of the whole verb. The passive is a way to choose which noun the sentence is about.

The passive differs from the stative of lesson 7.9. Wpgrammar puts it this way: a passive verb "does explicitly allude to the existence of an agent", even when no agent is named. *Kikombe kilivunjwa* "the cup was broken" implies someone broke it; the stative *kikombe kilivunjika* "the cup broke" does not.

## How it is built

### Step 1: turn the object into the subject

Start from the active sentence: *Mjomba alijenga nyumba hii* "my uncle built this house".

1. The object *nyumba* becomes the subject: class 9, subject prefix *i-*.
2. The tense stays: past *-li-*.
3. The verb takes the passive: *jeng-w-a*.
4. *Nyumba hii ilijengwa.* "This house was built."

### Step 2: choose the passive shape

The shape depends on how the root ends (wpgrammar "Passive"; Almasi and colleagues, ch. 23):

1. **Consonant-final root**: add *-w-*. *jeng-w-a*, *sem-w-a*, *pig-w-a*, *kat-w-a*, *kamat-w-a*.
2. **Root ending in a, u, o (verbs in -aa, -ua, -oa)**: add *-liw-* after a, u and *-lew-* after o: *nunu-liw-a*, *ka-liw-a* "be sat on", *va-liw-a* "be worn", *to-lew-a* "be given out, published", *bomo-lew-a* "be demolished". Why the l? It is the lost l of lesson 7.1. Wpgrammar adds a reason for using it here: after o and u a plain *-wa* would be inaudible (*-nunuwa* would sound like *-nunua*). It also notes that the Zanzibar-based standard uses *-liwa* for the *-aa* verbs too, where *-awa* would be audible: *-zaliwa* "be born", from *-zaa*.
3. **Root ending in e, i (verbs in -ea, -ia)**: usually add plain *-w-*: *-ambiwa* "be told", *-zuiwa* "be prevented", *-shambuliwa* "be attacked" (all Wiktionary). A few take *-liw-* or *-lew-*: *-tiliwa* "be put in" (Wiktionary also gives *-tiwa*), *-lelewa* "be brought up". Almasi and colleagues teach *-liw-* for all *-ia* verbs, wpgrammar plain *-w-* for most; check each verb.
4. **Root ending in l, w or y**: add *-iw-* or *-ew-* by harmony: *tawal-iw-a* "be ruled", *gaw-iw-a* "be shared out". The letters ny count as one consonant: *-danganywa* "be deceived" (wpgrammar).
5. **Loan verbs**: in *-i* or *-e*, add *-wa*: *-kubaliwa*, *-samehewa*, *-jeruhiwa*. In consonant + *-u*, the u becomes i: *-jibiwa*, *-jaribiwa*, *-laumiwa*. In *-au*, add *-liwa*: *-sahauliwa*, *-dharauliwa* (wpgrammar; Almasi and colleagues).
6. **One irregular verb**: *-ua* "kill" has *-uawa* "be killed" (Almasi and colleagues; Wiktionary), not the expected *-uliwa*.

Short verbs (*-la*, *-nywa*, *-pa*) have their own passives; lesson 7.5 covers them with the passives of applicatives.

### Step 3: add the agent if you want it

The doer, if named, follows the verb with *na* "by": *Nyumba hii ilijengwa na mjomba* "this house was built by my uncle". Almasi and colleagues make a distinction: *na* for a living agent, *kwa* for a tool. *Mti ulikatwa kwa shoka* "the tree was cut with an axe"; *mti ulikatwa na mkulima* "the tree was cut down by the farmer".

## Where it applies

- **Every tense and form**: *ilijengwa*, *inajengwa* "is being built", *itajengwa*, *imejengwa* "has been built", *ijengwe* "let it be built" (subjunctive), *ikijengwa* "if it is built". The extension sits next to the root; the tense slot is untouched.
- **Relatives**: *mtu aliyejeruhiwa*, *fedha zilizoibiwa* "money which was stolen" (Almasi and colleagues, quoted in lesson 6.2), *kitabu kinachosomwa sana* "a book which is read a lot".
- **The infinitive**: *kupendwa* "to be loved", *anataka kuchaguliwa* "he wants to be elected" (from *-chagua*).
- **With no agent**: the most frequent use. A news sentence such as *watu watatu wamejeruhiwa* "three people have been injured" names no one who did the injuring.
- **With an agent**: *na* for people and animals (*na mfalme*, *na wanakijiji*); *kwa* for tools (*kwa shoka*).
- **Verbs of perception and speech**: *-onwa* "be seen", *-semwa* "be said", *-ulizwa* "be asked" (all Wiktionary).
- **Fixed passive verbs** met earlier: *-zaliwa* "be born", *-olewa* "be married (of a woman)" (from *-oa*), both learnt whole in part 4. Wpgrammar points out that *-toa* "remove, give out" gives *-tolewa* with the same double meaning.

## Watch out

**Do not use the verb "be" plus the verb, as in Spanish or French.** *\*Nyumba ilikuwa ilijenga* copies *la casa fue construida*. Swahili has no participle for this: the passive is one verb, *ilijengwa*.

**Agree with the new subject.** *\*Nyumba alijengwa na mjomba* keeps the class 1 prefix of the old subject. The house is now the subject: *ilijengwa*.

**na, not kwa, for a person.** *\*Ilijengwa kwa mjomba* would mean something like "it was built at my uncle's" (lesson 5.8). The agent takes *na*: *ilijengwa na mjomba*.

**kwa, not na, for a tool.** *\*Ulikatwa na shoka* makes the axe the doer, as if it acted alone. Use *kwa shoka* (Almasi and colleagues).

**Vowel-final roots do not take plain -w-.** *\*Ilinunuwa* is wrong: *ilinunuliwa*. *\*Kimetowa* is wrong: *kimetolewa*.

**-ua is irregular.** *\*Aliuliwa* is the form you would build; the standard is *aliuawa* (wpgrammar notes *-uliwa* only as occasional).

**A verb ending in -wa is not always passive.** *-gawa* "share out", *-nawa* "wash (hands)", *-chelewa* "be late" and *-lewa* "get drunk" are active verbs. Their passives are *-gawiwa*, *-nawiwa* (Almasi and colleagues). *-zaliwa* and *-olewa* are real passives.

**Do not passivise a verb with no object.** *\*Imefikwa* from *-fika* "arrive" has nothing to promote. A passive needs a verb that takes an object (or an applicative that gives it one: lesson 7.5).

**Do not read -iw- as the applicative plus -w-.** In *-tawaliwa*, the i is the passive's own vowel after l. The applicative passive is lesson 7.5's *-andikiwa* "be written to", which carries a real applicative.

## Connections

Japanese is the nearest match: its passive is a suffix too, 建てられた *taterareta* "was built", and Japanese also uses it freely with no agent. Arabic shows the same economy by a vowel change inside the verb (*kataba* "he wrote", *kutiba* "it was written"). Spanish and French build the passive from "be" plus a participle, which is why their speakers' first attempt in Swahili is often the wrong *\*ilikuwa ilijenga*.

## Worked answers

1. *Gari lilinunuliwa na mjomba* and *Gari lilinunuliwa.* *Gari* is class 5 (*li-*); *nunu-* is vowel-final, so *-liw-*. Lesson 7.4.
2. *-teuliwa* (Wiktionary): *teu-* ends in u, so *-liw-*. "The minister has been appointed." Lesson 7.4.
3. *Mti ulikatwa na mkulima kwa shoka.* The person takes *na*, the tool *kwa*. *Mti* is class 3 (*u-*). Lesson 7.4.
4. A person agent takes *na*: *Barua ilisomwa na mwalimu.* Lesson 7.4.
5. *-tawaliwa* (root ends in l: *-iw-*); *-jibiwa* (loan in consonant + u: u becomes i); *-tolewa* (vowel root in o: *-lew-*). Lesson 7.4.
6. "This building will be demolished next year." *Jengo* class 5: *li-ta-bomo-lew-a*. Lesson 7.4.
7. *-fika* "arrive" takes no object, so there is nothing to make the subject. Lesson 7.4.
8. "The government is relied on by all the citizens." *-tegemea* ends in *-ea*, and wpgrammar's rule for most *-ea*, *-ia* verbs is plain *-w-*: *-tegemewa*. Wpgrammar gives this very form; it is not a dictionary headword in Wiktionary. Lesson 7.4.
