## The idea

Lesson 7.6 built causatives by adding a suffix. Some of the commonest causatives were not built that way. *-amka* "wake up" has the causative *-amsha* "wake (someone)"; *-jaa* "become full" has *-jaza* "fill"; *-ona* "see" has *-onya* "warn". No *-ish-* in sight: the last consonant of the root has changed instead.

Why? Because an older causative suffix, a single sound *-y-*, merged with the consonant before it. Wiktionary's etymologies trace *-chemsha*, *-onya*, *-ponya*, *-laza* and *-kataza* to a Proto-Sabaki or Proto-Bantu causative *-ya* (Proto-Bantu *-ia*). Wpgrammar describes the result: the *-y-* "combined with consonants and changed their pronunciation, palatalising or 'softening' them". **Palatalisation** is this kind of sound change: a consonant pulled towards a y-sound, so that k becomes sh, l becomes z.

So the sound-change causatives are fossils of the same system. They are no longer formed freely, but once you know the changes you can recognise them and find the plain verb inside.

The second half of the lesson goes the other way: the living suffix *-ish-*/*-esh-* applied not to verbs but to adjectives and nouns. *safi* "clean" gives *-safisha* "make clean"; *sababu* "reason" gives *-sababisha* "cause". Wiktionary's entry for *-isha* states both uses: added to an adjective, "to cause to become, to make"; added to a noun, "to give a characteristic of".

## How it is built

### The sound changes

Wpgrammar's table of the softened consonants:

- t, k → **sh**: *-pata* "get" → *-pasha* "cause to get; inform" (Wiktionary, "an irregular development"); *-amka* → *-amsha*; *-waka* "burn" → *-washa* "light, switch on"; *-choka* "get tired" → *-chosha* "tire, bore"; *-kumbuka* "remember" → *-kumbusha* "remind"; *-chemka* "boil" (of water) → *-chemsha* "boil (something)".
- l → **z**: *-lala* "lie down" → *-laza* "lay down; admit to hospital" (Wiktionary).
- n → **ny**: *-ona* → *-onya* "warn"; *-pona* "recover" → *-ponya* "heal".
- p → **fy**: *-ogopa* "fear" → *-ogofya* "frighten".
- w → **vy**: *-lewa* "get drunk" → *-levya* "intoxicate" (wpgrammar).

Wpgrammar adds that *-ka* verbs "very frequently" change to *-sha*, but much less often when the syllable before *-ka* has e or i: *-andika* → *-andikisha*, *-cheka* → *-chekesha* (lesson 7.6), not *\*-andisha*, *\*-chesha*. In *-chemka*, the m is syllabic (it was once *mu*), so the e does not count.

### Vowel stems take -za

Verbs that end in two vowels (*-jaa*, *-kataa*, *-tembea*, *-kimbia*, *-kua*) lost an l between them long ago (lesson 7.1). Wpgrammar explains their causative: the old l had already been softened to z by the *-y-* causative, and "the -l- was subsequently lost but the -z- was not". So:

- *-jaa* "become full" → *-jaza* "fill";
- *-kataa* "refuse" → *-kataza* "forbid";
- *-tembea* "walk" → *-tembeza* "show around, walk (a dog)";
- *-kimbia* "run" → *-kimbiza* "chase";
- *-kua* "grow" → *-kuza* "enlarge, raise";
- *-ingia* "enter" → *-ingiza* "let in; import" (Almasi and colleagues, p. 269).

You learnt *-jaza*, *-kataza* and *-tangaza* "announce" (from *-tangaa* "become known", wpgrammar) whole in parts 4 and 6. Now you can see their shape.

### Two causatives from one verb

Many verbs have both an old and a new causative, sometimes with a difference in meaning (wpgrammar; Almasi and colleagues):

- *-pita* "pass": *-pisha* "give way" and *-pitisha* "make pass, approve" (lesson 7.6);
- *-lala*: *-laza* "lay down, admit to hospital" and *-lalisha* "put to bed";
- *-ogopa*: *-ogofya* and *-ogopesha*, both "frighten" (wpgrammar: no difference);
- *-ona*: *-onya* "warn" and *-onyesha* "show", which stacks the new suffix on the old one.

### Verbs from adjectives and nouns

Take the adjective or noun, drop its final vowel, add *-ish-*/*-esh-* by harmony on the last remaining vowel, then *-a*:

1. *safi* "clean" → *saf-* → a takes i → *saf-ish-a* "clean".
2. *-fupi* "short" → *fup-* → *fup-ish-a* "shorten, summarise" (Wiktionary: from *-fupi* + *-isha*).
3. *bora* "better" → *bor-* → o takes e → *bor-esh-a* "improve".
4. *orodha* "list" → *orodh-* → *orodh-esh-a* "list, make a list".

Wpgrammar's list includes *imara* "strong" → *-imarisha* "strengthen", *tayari* "ready" → *-tayarisha* "prepare", *sababu* "reason" → *-sababisha* "cause", *lazima* "obligation" → *-lazimisha* "force", *hakika* "certainty" → *-hakikisha* "make sure" (learnt whole in part 6).

### Glossing

This guide glosses a sound-change causative whole, because the causative piece cannot be cut out: *amsh-a* `wake.CAUS-FV`. A causative from an adjective is segmented: *saf-ish-a* `clean-CAUS-FV`.

## Where it applies

- **k, t → sh**: *-amsha*, *-washa*, *-chosha*, *-kumbusha*, *-chemsha*, *-pasha*.
- **l → z**: *-laza*; **n → ny**: *-onya*, *-ponya*; **p → fy**: *-ogofya*; **w → vy**: *-levya*.
- **Vowel stems → -za**: *-jaza*, *-kataza*, *-tembeza*, *-kimbiza*, *-kuza*, *-ingiza*, *-tangaza*, and *-umiza* "hurt (someone)" (part 4), which Almasi and colleagues derive from *-umia* "get hurt".
- **Old and new side by side**: *-pisha*/*-pitisha*, *-laza*/*-lalisha*, *-ogofya*/*-ogopesha*, *-onya*/*-onyesha*.
- **From adjectives**: *-safisha*, *-fupisha*, *-imarisha*, *-boresha*, *-tayarisha*.
- **From nouns**: *-sababisha*, *-lazimisha*, *-orodhesha*, *-hakikisha*.
- **Every verb form, and further extensions**: passives (*-lazwa*, *-sababishwa*, *-fupishwa*), applicatives (*-amshia* "wake up for someone", Wiktionary), nouns (*kifupisho* "abbreviation", Wiktionary).

## Watch out

**Do not add -ish- where an old causative exists.** *\*Aliniamkisha* is not used for "she woke me": the causative of *-amka* is *-amsha*. Check the dictionary before building *-ish-* on a *-ka* verb.

**Do not apply the sound changes yourself.** They are fossils, not a living rule. *\*-sisha* from *-sikia* "hear" is not a word (its causative is *-sikiliza* "listen", Almasi and colleagues), and *-pisha* does not mean "approve". Use them to recognise words, not to make new ones.

**-ka verbs with e or i usually keep -ish-/-esh-.** *\*-andisha* is wrong: *-andikisha* "make write" (wpgrammar). *\*-chesha*: *-chekesha*.

**-pisha and -pitisha mean different things.** *Alinipisha* "he let me pass, made way for me"; *alipitisha mpango* "he approved the plan".

**Do not confuse -onya and -onyesha.** *Alinionya* "he warned me"; *alinionyesha* "he showed me".

**Plain and causative split the roles.** *Maji yanachemka* (the water boils); *Mama anachemsha maji* (mother boils the water). *\*Mama anachemka maji* uses the plain verb with an object: wrong. *\*Maji yanachemsha* gives the water something to boil.

**From adjectives, drop the agreement prefix and the final vowel.** *\*-kifupisha* or *\*-fupiisha* are wrong. The stem is *-fupi*; drop the i and add the suffix: *-fupisha*.

**Harmony follows the last vowel left.** *bora* → *bor-* → *-boresha*, not *\*-borisha*. *safi* → *saf-* → *-safisha*, not *\*-safesha*.

**Not every -sha, -za verb is a causative.** *-osha* "wash", *-cheza* "play" and *-uza* "sell" are plain verbs. Look for a plain partner (*-amka*/*-amsha*) before taking a verb apart.

## Connections

The k → sh and t → sh changes have a familiar parallel in Japanese, where *s* and *t* soften before *i*: *kas-* gives *kashi* (貸し), *kat-* gives *kachi* (勝ち). The cause is the same kind of y- or i-sound pulling the consonant forward in the mouth. Spanish shows the result in history rather than grammar: Latin *-ti-* before a vowel became *z* in *razón* from *ratio*.

## Exercises

1. Find the plain verb inside *-washa*, *-ponya* and *-jaza*, and say what changed.
2. Translate *Mbwa alituamsha usiku.*
3. Build "the doctor admitted him to hospital" with *-laza*.
4. Build a verb meaning "make ready" from *tayari*, and translate *Tutatayarisha chakula.*
5. Correct *\*Mama alichemka maji.*
6. Choose *-onya* or *-onyesha*: "the guide showed us the way".
7. Build "the dog was chased by the children".
8. Make a verb from *safi* and one from *bora*, and explain the harmony vowel in each.

## Worked answers

1. *-washa* is *-waka* "burn" with k → sh; *-ponya* is *-pona* "recover" with n → ny; *-jaza* is *-jaa* "become full" with the vowel-stem *-za*. Lesson 7.7.
2. "The dog woke us in the night." *-amsha*, the causative of *-amka*, with *-tu-* as the causee. Lesson 7.7.
3. *Daktari alimlaza hospitali.* *-laza* (from *-lala*), with *-m-* for the patient. Lesson 7.7.
4. *tayari* → *tayar-* → a takes i → *-tayarisha*. "We will prepare food." Lesson 7.7.
5. *-chemka* has no object. With mother as the doer, use the causative: *Mama alichemsha maji.* Lesson 7.7.
6. *-onyesha* "show": *Kiongozi alituonyesha njia.* (*-onya* is "warn".) Lesson 7.7.
7. *Mbwa alikimbizwa na watoto.* *-kimbiza* + passive *-w-* (Wiktionary *-kimbizwa*); the dog keeps class 1 agreement as an animal. Lessons 7.4, 7.7.
8. *-safisha*: the last vowel of *saf-* is a, which takes i. *-boresha*: the last vowel of *bor-* is o, which takes e. Lessons 7.1, 7.7.
