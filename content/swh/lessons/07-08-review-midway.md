## Before you start

This review teaches nothing new. It covers the first half of part 7: the extension slot and vowel harmony (lesson 7.1), the applicative for beneficiaries, recipients and places (7.2) and for reason, instrument and direction (7.3), the passive (7.4), the passive of applicatives and of *-pa* (7.5), the causative with *-ish-*, *-esh-*, *-iz-*, *-ez-* (7.6), and the sound-change causatives and verbs from adjectives and nouns (7.7). Everything from parts 2 to 6 is assumed.

The passage is a short account of a day's journey, constructed for this review. For every verb, ask three questions. Is there anything between the root and the final vowel? If so, which extension is it, and which harmony vowel did it take? Who is the subject now, and who has become an object?

## Exercises

1. Translate the passage into natural English without looking at the translation lines.
2. Line 1: give the plain verb inside *aliniamsha* and say what changed.
3. Line 2: in *alinipikia chai*, who benefits and what is cooked? Which piece shows each?
4. Line 2: explain the harmony vowel in *-pikia* and the l-less shape of *-lisha*.
5. Line 3: make the sentence active ("Father took us to the station").
6. Line 4: why *tukaambiwa* and not *\*tukasemwa*?
7. Line 5: take *lilicheleweshwa* apart slot by slot.
8. Line 6: what is the plain verb of *-tembeza*, and why does its causative end in *-za*?
9. Line 7: why is the applicative *-i-* in *-nunulia* preceded by l, and why is it *-e-* in *-letea*?
10. Line 8: make the food the subject instead, using *-tolewa* "be given out".
11. Build "the teacher taught us Swahili" with a causative.
12. Build "the letter was written to my parents" with the parents as subject.
13. Build "the children were bought shoes by their uncle".
14. Correct *\*Mtoto alipikwa chakula na mama* (meaning "the child was cooked food by Mother").
15. Correct *\*Mvua ilifanya sisi kuchelewa.*
16. Correct *\*Tulikupewa zawadi.*
17. Give the passive of *-nunua*, *-toa*, *-tawala* and *-jibu*.
18. Build "please shorten the list" from *-fupi* and *orodha*.

## Worked answers

1. "In the morning Mother woke me early. She made me tea and fed the baby porridge. Then Father took us to the bus station. Our bus was late, and we were told to wait. The driver said the rain had delayed it. In town my aunt welcomed us and showed us round the market. She bought us fruit, and we brought her a present. In the evening we were given a good meal and were very happy." Natural English often turns the passives back into actives.
2. *-amka* "wake up"; k has become sh, the trace of the old causative *-y-* (lesson 7.7).
3. "Me" benefits, shown by the object prefix *-ni-*; *chai* is what is cooked, the second object, with no prefix (lesson 7.2).
4. *Pik-* has i, so the applicative is *-i-* (lesson 7.1). *-lisha* is the causative of the short verb *-la*: short verbs have their causatives learnt one by one (lesson 7.6).
5. *Kisha baba alitupeleka kituoni.* The passive subject *tu-* returns as the object prefix *-tu-*; *baba* becomes the subject (lesson 7.4).
6. "Tell" is *-ambia*, an old applicative whose object is the person told, so its passive makes the person the subject. *-semwa* is "be said, be talked about" (lessons 7.3, 7.5).
7. *li-li-chelew-esh-w-a*: *li-* class 5 subject (*basi*), *-li-* past, *chelew-* "be late", *-esh-* causative (e after e), *-w-* passive (last), *-a* final vowel: "it was made late" (lessons 7.4, 7.6).
8. *-tembea* "walk". It ends in two vowels, so it takes *-za*: the z is the softened form of an l the verb has lost (lesson 7.7).
9. *Nunu-* ends in a vowel, so the old l returns: *-li-*. *Let-* has e, so the applicative is *-e-* (lesson 7.1, 7.2).
10. *Jioni chakula kizuri kilitolewa.* *-pewa* can only have the receiver as subject; for the thing, use another verb (lesson 7.5).
11. *Mwalimu alitusomesha Kiswahili.* The causee *-tu-* first, then the subject studied (lesson 7.6).
12. *Wazazi wangu waliandikiwa barua.* Applicative *-andikia* first, then passive *-w-* (lesson 7.5).
13. *Watoto walinunuliwa viatu na mjomba wao.* *-nunulia* + passive; *viatu* stays as the object; *na* for the agent (lessons 7.4, 7.5).
14. The child is the beneficiary, so the verb needs the applicative: *Mtoto alipikiwa chakula na mama.* *Alipikwa* means "the child was cooked" (lesson 7.5).
15. *Mvua ilituchelewesha.* The causative replaces "make ... be late"; "us" is the object prefix (lesson 7.6).
16. *-pewa* takes no *ku-*: *Tulipewa zawadi.* (*Tulikupewa* is not a word.) (Lesson 7.5.)
17. *-nunuliwa* (vowel root: *-liw-*); *-tolewa* (o: *-lew-*); *-tawaliwa* (l: *-iw-*); *-jibiwa* (loan in consonant + u) (lesson 7.4).
18. *Tafadhali fupisha orodha.* *-fupi* → *fup-* + *-ish-* (the last vowel of *fup-* is u, which takes i) (lesson 7.7).
