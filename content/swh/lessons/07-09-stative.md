## The idea

Three sentences about one window (wpgrammar's examples):

- *Juma alivunja dirisha* "Juma broke the window": an agent and an object;
- *Dirisha lilivunjwa (na Juma)* "the window was broken (by Juma)": passive, the agent pushed out but still implied;
- *Dirisha lilivunjika* "the window broke": no agent at all, not even implied.

The third verb carries the **stative** extension *-ik-*/*-ek-*. It turns a verb with an object into one where the object becomes the subject and no doer is in the picture. Things that "break", "get torn", "come open", "get spoiled" are its natural home.

It has a second use. With many verbs it means "can be done": *-someka* "be legible", *-pikika* "be cookable" (lesson 7.1), *-lika* "be edible" (Wiktionary). Almasi and colleagues (ch. 24, p. 255) sum up both: stative verbs "express a current condition of the subject or the potential of entering that condition".

**About the name.** Learner books, Almasi and colleagues and Wiktionary call this extension "stative". Wpgrammar argues the name is misleading: the verbs are not states but events ("the window broke" is something that happened), and it prefers **mediopassive**, a verb form that promotes the object to subject without implying an agent. This guide keeps "stative" (STAT), the name you will meet, but the wpgrammar point is right and explains a puzzle: *Dirisha limevunjika* reads as "the window is broken" only because of the perfect *-me-* (lesson 4.5), which describes the state that results from an event. Almasi and colleagues make the same observation from the other side: current conditions take *-me-*, possibilities take *-na-*.

## How it is built

### The shape

The harmony of lesson 7.1 again (Almasi and colleagues, pp. 255–258; wpgrammar "Mediopassive"):

1. Consonant-final root with a, i, u: *-ik-*: *vunj-ik-a*, *pik-ik-a*, *pand-ik-a* "be climbable".
2. Consonant-final root with e, o: *-ek-*: *elew-ek-a* "be understood, make sense", *som-ek-a*, *pend-ek-a* "be lovable".
3. Vowel-final root: either a bare *-k-* or the l forms *-lik-*/*-lek-*. *Fungu-a* → *fungu-k-a* "come open"; *siki-a* → *siki-k-a* "be audible" (Almasi and colleagues also give *-sikilika*); *ka-a* → *ka-lik-a* "be habitable"; *ondo-a* "remove" → *ondo-lek-a* "be removable". Which one a verb takes has to be learnt.
4. Loan verbs drop the final vowel and take *-ik-*/*-ek-*: *-haribu* "spoil" → *-haribika* "get spoiled, break down"; *-jibu* → *-jibika* "be answerable"; *-samehe* → *-sameheka* "be forgivable". Loans in *-au* take *-lika*: *-sahaulika* "be forgotten, be forgettable".
5. The short verb *-la* gives *-lika* "be edible" (Wiktionary).

### Chain of thought

Say "the cup broke".

1. Verb: *-vunja* "break (something)".
2. No one did it, or we do not say who: stative. Root *vunj-*, u: *-ik-*. *-vunjika*.
3. Subject: *kikombe*, class 7: *ki-*. Past: *ki-li-vunj-ik-a*.
4. *Kikombe kilivunjika.*

Say "this book is readable".

1. *-soma* → o: *-ek-*: *-someka*.
2. A possibility, so present *-na-* (Almasi and colleagues): *ki-na-som-ek-a*.
3. *Kitabu hiki kinasomeka.*

### Stative or passive?

Ask whether a doer is in the speaker's mind.

- *Mlango ulifunguliwa* "the door was opened": someone opened it.
- *Mlango ulifunguka* "the door came open": it opened, no one is meant.

Only the passive can take an agent with *na*. The stative cannot.

### Fixed -ikana, -ekana verbs

A few very common verbs end in *-ikana*, *-ekana*, *-likana*, which Wiktionary analyses as a suffix meaning "-able" (the stative followed by *-an-*, the reciprocal of lesson 7.10):

- *-onekana* "be visible; seem" (from *-ona*);
- *-patikana* "be available, be found" (from *-pata*);
- *-julikana* "be known, be famous" (from *-jua*);
- *-wezekana* "be possible" (from *-weza*).

Learn them as words: *Inawezekana* "it is possible"; *Haijulikani* "it is not known".

## Where it applies

- **Things breaking, tearing, spoiling, opening by themselves**: *-vunjika*, *-chanika* "be torn" (lesson 7.1), *-pasuka* "split, burst" (Wiktionary, from *-pasua* "split"), *-haribika*, *-funguka*.
- **States, with -me-**: *kimevunjika* "is broken", *limeharibika* "is out of order", *umefunguka* "is open".
- **Possibility, with -na- and other tenses**: *kinasomeka*, *panakalika*, *linajibika*, *halifanyiki*; in the future, *itawezekana* "it will be possible".
- **Abilities in the negative, very often**: *hayaliki* "inedible", *haisomeki* "illegible" (lesson 7.1).
- **Perception**: *-sikika* "be audible", *-onekana* "be visible; seem".
- **Fixed -ikana verbs**: *-patikana*, *-julikana*, *-wezekana*, *-onekana*.
- **From nouns and adjectives**: Almasi and colleagues list *-shughulika* "be busy" (from *shughuli*), *-bahatika* "be lucky" (part 6), *-boreka* "get better" (from *bora*), *-erevuka* "become clever" (from *-erevu*).
- **Further extensions**: *-tumikia* "serve" is the applicative of *-tumika* "be used" (Wiktionary); lesson 7.11 covers stacking.

## Watch out

**No agent with the stative.** *\*Dirisha lilivunjika na Juma* is wrong. If you name the doer, use the passive: *lilivunjwa na Juma*.

**Do not use the stative when someone clearly acted.** *Mwizi alikamatwa* "the thief was arrested", not *\*mwizi alikamatika*: arrest needs an arrester.

**-me- for states, -na- for possibility.** *Kikombe kinavunjika* is "the cup breaks easily, is breakable"; "the cup is broken" is *kikombe kimevunjika*. An English speaker hearing "is" reaches for the present; Swahili uses the perfect (Almasi and colleagues).

**Do not translate "it is possible to" with -weza alone.** *\*Inaweza* is "it can" with a subject that does something. "It is possible" is *inawezekana*.

**Not every -ika, -eka verb is a stative.** *-anika* "spread out to dry", *-funika* "cover", *-weka* "put" and *-bweka* "bark" are plain verbs. Look for a partner without *-ik-* (*-vunja*/*-vunjika*).

**Some verbs were stative in origin and have lost their partner.** Almasi and colleagues list *-lewa* "get drunk" and *-shiba* "get full" as "already in the stative form"; Wiktionary derives *-choka* "get tired" from an old stative. Use them with *-me-* for the state (*nimechoka* "I am tired", lesson 4.6) but do not try to remove a suffix.

**The vowel-final choice is not predictable.** *-funguka*, but *-kalika*; *-sikika* or *-sikilika*. Check the dictionary.

**Do not confuse -pikika with the passive -pikwa.** *Chakula kinapikwa* "the food is being cooked" (someone is cooking it); *chakula hiki kinapikika* "this food can be cooked".

## Connections

Spanish and French express the agentless change with a reflexive: *la ventana se rompió*, *la fenêtre s'est cassée*. That is the closest match to *dirisha lilivunjika*, and it explains why Romance speakers are tempted to add the Swahili reflexive *-ji-*: do not. Japanese has transitive–intransitive pairs that work the same way: 割る *waru* "break (something)", 割れる *wareru* "break, get broken".

## Exercises

1. Make *Mtoto alivunja kikombe* into a stative sentence with no agent.
2. Translate *Barua yako haisomeki.*
3. Build "the meat is not edible" (*nyama* class 9).
4. Choose passive or stative: "the bridge was destroyed by the soldiers" (*-haribu*, *daraja*).
5. Translate *Inawezekana mvua itanyesha kesho.*
6. Correct *\*Gari limeharibika na dereva.*
7. Build "his voice could not be heard" (past negative of *-sikika*).
8. Translate *Dawa hii inapatikana kila duka*, and say which fixed verb it uses.

## Worked answers

1. *Kikombe kilivunjika* "the cup broke", or *kikombe kimevunjika* "the cup is broken". The object becomes the subject (*ki-*); no agent (lesson 7.9).
2. "Your letter is illegible." *ha-i-som-ek-i*: negative present of the stative *-someka* (lesson 7.9).
3. *Nyama hii hailiki.* *-lika* "be edible", negative present with class 9 *ha-i-*, final *-i* (lesson 7.9).
4. Passive, because the agent is named: *Daraja liliharibiwa na askari.* (*-haribiwa*, Wiktionary.) Lessons 7.4, 7.9.
5. "It is possible it will rain tomorrow." *Inawezekana* "it is possible" (lesson 7.9), then an ordinary future clause.
6. A stative takes no agent. Either *Gari limeharibika* (no agent) or *Gari limeharibiwa na dereva* "the car has been damaged by the driver" (lessons 7.4, 7.9).
7. *Sauti yake haikusikika.* Negative past *ha-i-ku-* + *siki-k-a* (lessons 4.2, 7.9).
8. "This medicine is available in every shop." *-patikana* "be available", a fixed *-ikana* verb from *-pata* (lesson 7.9).
