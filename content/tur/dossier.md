Everything a drafting or verifying agent needs to stay consistent is here; where this file and a named source disagree, the source wins and this file gets corrected. ## 1. Variety taught

- **Base:** modern standard Turkish (Türkiye Türkçesi) as written and as spoken by educated speakers in Istanbul. It is the variety described by goksel2025, lewis2000 and kornfilt1997, codified by tdk, and the one wpphon's phonetic sources (zimmerorgun1999) describe as "Istanbul Turkish".
- **Written versus spoken.** Teach the standard spelling, and give the natural spoken form in the note where it differs. Label spoken forms `colloquial` in the example note. The main ones (tur-07-09):
  - Future -(y)AcAK before a vowel-initial person ending loses its first vowel and the ğ: *yapacağım* is said *yapıcam*. Checked: wiktionary entry *yapıcam* "(informal) Spoken variant of yapacağım", with the note that it is "widespread in everyday informal speech" and "inappropriate in written forms". *gelicem* for *geleceğim* follows the same pattern **(unverified: no entry opened)**.
  - -(I)yor loses its r in speech, and 1SG -(I)yorum is often said -(I)yom: *geliyo*, *geliyom*, *napıyosun* for *ne yapıyorsun* **(unverified: no source opened; check goksel2025 ch. 2 or ketrez2012 before stating it as fact; until then present it as "you will hear")**.
  - Formal written forms to label `formal`: -mAktA (*gelmektedir*), -DIr on every statement, the 3PL -lAr on verbs with a plural subject (*doğarlar*), -(y)ArAk and -mAksIzIn chains (tur-07-08).
- **Older and other varieties:** Old Anatolian Turkish appears only in the capstone reading (Yunus Emre, tur-08-08) and Ottoman-era formal vocabulary only in tur-07-11 and the readings tur-08-04 and tur-08-07. Ottoman Turkish (`ota`) has its own guide: cross-link to it, do not teach the Arabic script here. Regional dialects (e.g. the uvular [q] for k before back vowels in the east and southeast, wpphon) and Cypriot and Balkan Turkish are named in tur-07-12 only.
- **Why this variety:** it is the language of Turkish schools, media and almost all writing; every grammar and dictionary listed describes it; it is what a learner hears in Turkey.

## 2. Phoneme inventory

Sources: wpphon (pointer to zimmerorgun1999 and goksel2025, opened); wals 1A–19A (opened: consonant inventory "average", vowels "large (7–14)", "front rounded vowels: high and mid", no tones, no uvulars, no glottalised consonants); tdk for individual long vowels and palatal consonants. zimmerorgun1999 and goksel2025 themselves were not opened.

### Vowels

Eight vowels, which form a cube of three binary choices: front or back, unrounded or rounded, high (close) or low (open). This cube is what vowel harmony works on, so teach it first (tur-01-01).

| Letter | IPA | Front/back | Rounded | High/low | Description | Example (wpphon) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| e | [e] (mid; [ɛ]~[æ] before m, n, l, r in a closed syllable for most speakers) | front | no | low | as in Spanish *mes* | *ses* 'sound' |
| i | [i] | front | no | high | as in Spanish *si* | *dil* 'tongue' |
| ö | [œ] (mid [ø̞]) | front | yes | low | German *ö*, French *eu* | *göz* 'eye' |
| ü | [y] | front | yes | high | German *ü*, French *u* | *güneş* 'sun' |
| a | [a] (central or back) | back | no | low | as in Spanish *casa* | *dal* 'branch' |
| ı | [ɯ] (variously [ɯ], [ɯ̽], [ɨ]) | back | no | high | dotless i: Korean ㅡ *eu* is close | *ılık* 'warm' |
| o | [o] (mid) | back | yes | low | as in Spanish *no* | *yol* 'way' |
| u | [u] | back | yes | high | as in Spanish *tu* | *uçak* 'aeroplane' |

Points to teach:
- **ı is not i.** Dotless ı [ɯ] is a separate vowel and letter; the capital of i is İ, the capital of ı is I (*İstanbul*, *Iğdır*). Korean speakers have the sound in ㅡ.
- **o and ö occur only in the first syllable of native words** (wpphon), which is why no suffix has o or ö, with the one exception of the frozen -(I)yor (wpphon note 3).
- **Vowel length** is not contrastive in native words except where ğ has been lost (*dağ* [daː] 'mountain'). Long vowels occur in Arabic and Persian loans: *tarih* [taːɾih] 'history, date', *hakikat* [hakiːkat] 'truth' (tdk pronunciation notes, checked). Spelling usually does not show them.
- **No diphthongs**: two written vowels are two syllables (*aile* [a.i.le], *laik*) (wpphon). y is a consonant.

Points to teach, each from wpphon unless another id is given:
- **c is [dʒ], ç is [tʃ], ş is [ʃ], j is [ʒ]**: *cam* 'glass', *çay* 'tea', *şey* 'thing', *jilet* 'razor blade' **(example words unverified; check in wiktionary)**. Japanese and Korean readers: c is the j of *jin*, ç the ch of *chi*.
- **k, g and l have front and back versions.** Next to e, i, ö, ü they are palatal [c ɟ] and clear [l]; next to a, ı, o, u they are velar [k ɡ] and dark [ɫ]. In native words this is automatic. In loanwords the front versions can stand next to back vowels, and then the suffixes take front harmony: *hâl* [haːl] 'state', dative *hâle*; *rol* 'role', accusative *rolü* (tdk gives *rolü*, checked; wpphon table). The circumflex marks the palatal k, g, l in some loans: *kâr* [caːɾ] 'profit' vs *kar* 'snow' (tdk: kâr pronounced kâ:rı; kar plain).
- **r** is a tap [ɾ]; at the end of a word and before a voiceless consonant it is often devoiced and fricated, which can sound like [ʃ] to non-native ears (*bir*, *var*).
- **v** is [v], bilabial [β] near rounded vowels for some speakers (*tavuk*).
- **h** is pronounced everywhere, including at the end of a syllable (*Allah*, *sabah*); fronted towards [x] or [ç] at the end of a word.
- **Final devoicing**: b, c, d, g are not written or pronounced at the end of a word in native and long-established words: *kitap* 'book', *kitabı* (tdk taki "bı", checked). A few contrasting monosyllables keep a final voiced letter: *ad* 'name' vs *at* 'horse', *kod* 'code' vs *kot* 'jeans'.
- **Voiceless p, t, k, ç are usually aspirated** at the start of a syllable, as in English.
- **Soft g (ğ)** is not a consonant in standard speech. It never starts a word. Between back vowels it is silent (the vowels run together: *ağaç* [aːtʃ]~[a.atʃ] **(phonetic detail unverified)**); between front vowels it may be a light [j] (*değil* [dejil]); at the end of a syllable it lengthens the vowel before it (*dağ* [daː], *yağmur* [jaːmuɾ]). Whether ğ is still a phoneme is debated (section 6).
- **Assimilation**: a suffix-initial d becomes t after a voiceless consonant (*evde* but *kitapta*); c becomes ç (*gözcü* but *balıkçı* **(unverified examples)**; tables for -DI, -DIK, -DAn, -CA, -DIr checked in wiktionary with "default" and "assimilated" rows). Mnemonic used by Turkish schools: the voiceless consonants are those of the phrase *fıstıkçı şahap* (f, s, t, k, ç, ş, h, p) **(unverified; check before using)**.

### Syllables and stress

- Native syllables are (C)V(C)(C); no word-initial clusters in native words, which is why loans get extra vowels: *kulüp* 'club', *istasyon* 'station' (wpphon).
- **Default stress is on the last syllable**, and it moves rightwards as suffixes are added: *elmá*, *elmalár*, *evlerimdé* (wpphon).
- **Exceptions** (tur-01-07): place names and many loans (*İstánbul*, *Ankára*; the stress stays when suffixes are added: *İstánbul'a*); suffixes that carry stress themselves (-(I)yor: *gelíyor*; -(y)ArAk; -(y)IncA); **pre-accenting** suffixes that put stress on the syllable before them: negative -mA (*gélmedim*, *kórkma*), -lA 'with', -CA, -ki, the particles mI and dA, and the copular markers (*hastáydı*, *çocúklar* 'they are children'); in the aorist negative the stress falls on -mAz (*sönméz*) (all from wpphon).
- Yes-no questions put a high pitch on the syllable before mI: *Bu elmalar taze mi?* (wpphon).
- Do not mark stress in `orig` or `morphemes`. Mark it with an acute only in tur-01-07 and in vocabulary notes for exceptional words, and say there that the acute is the guide's, not Turkish spelling.

## 3. Script and transliteration

### Script

- The Turkish Latin alphabet of 1928: 29 letters, in this order: **a b c ç d e f g ğ h ı i j k l m n o ö p r s ş t u ü v y z**. No q, w, x in native spelling. Upper case: A B C Ç D E F G Ğ H I İ J K L M N O Ö P R S Ş T U Ü V Y Z. Note the two i's: **I ı** and **İ i**.
- The **circumflex** (â, î, û) is not a separate letter. tdk uses it (a) to mark a long vowel in a few loans where it distinguishes words (*âlem*, *hâl* vs *hal*) and (b) to mark palatal k, g, l before a and u (*kâr*, *gâvur*, *hâl*, *mekân*; tdk shows the palatal and the length together, e.g. *hâl* ha:li). Usage is inconsistent in practice and the 2012 tdk spelling guide made it optional in many words **(unverified: the spelling guide was not opened)**. This guide writes the circumflex exactly as tdk's headword does, and copies attested texts as they stand.
- **The apostrophe** separates a suffix from a proper name: *İstanbul'a*, *Ali'nin*, *Türkiye'dir* (wpphon; seen throughout udhr and trwikipedia). It is not used with common nouns.
- **The language reform**: the alphabet replaced the Arabic script in 1928; from 1932 the Turkish Language Association replaced many Arabic and Persian words with Turkish ones (tur-07-11) **(dates from recall: unverified; check glottolog references or a source opened later)**.
- Unicode: use the precomposed letters ç ğ ı İ ö ş ü â î û (U+00E7, U+011F, U+0131, U+0130, U+00F6, U+015F, U+00FC, U+00E2, U+00EE, U+00FB). **ş must be U+015F (s with cedilla), not U+0219 (s with comma below, Romanian)**; same for ç. Files are NFC. Lower-casing *I* must give *ı*, not *i*: any tool that compares Turkish strings case-insensitively must use Turkish casing rules.

### Transliteration

None. `translit_optional` is true: the original is already in the Latin alphabet. The `translit` field is omitted from every example. Pronunciation is given in IPA in part 1 and in vocabulary notes only where spelling misleads (long vowels in loans, palatal k g l, exceptional stress).

## 4. Grammatical and glossing conventions

### Terms

- **Suffix, stem, slot.** Every grammatical unit is a suffix; there are no prefixes (wals 26A "strongly suffixing"). The guide speaks of **slots**: the fixed positions in which suffixes stand (tur-04-01 and the chart in tur-09-01).
- **Vowel harmony**: "two-way" (e/a) and "four-way" (i/ı/u/ü). Turkish school names: *büyük ünlü uyumu* (backness) and *küçük ünlü uyumu* (rounding) (wpphon).
- **Case names**: nominative (bare), accusative, dative, locative, ablative, genitive, and the instrumental-comitative -(y)lA. wiktionary's tables give six cases in the order nominative, definite accusative, dative, locative, ablative, genitive; use that order in tables, with -(y)lA after them in a separate column. wals 49A counts "6–7 cases": say that -(y)lA is sometimes counted (section 6).
- **Tense-aspect names**, matched to wiktionary's conjugation tables so verifiers can compare: -(I)yor "progressive" (wiktionary: "continuous"), aorist (wiktionary: "aorist"), -DI "past" (wiktionary: "past"), -mIş "evidential past" (wiktionary: "inferential"), -(y)AcAK "future", -mAlI "necessitative", -sA "conditional", -(y)A "optative", -mAktA "formal progressive" (wiktionary: "progressive"). Note the clash: what wiktionary calls "progressive" is -mAktA; what this guide calls progressive is wiktionary's "continuous". Say so in tur-07-08.
- **Copular markers**: -(y)DI, -(y)mIş, -(y)sA (written as suffixes, from the old verb *i-* 'be'; may be written as separate words *idi, imiş, ise*) and the personal endings that follow nominal predicates. "Copula" alone means these, plus -DIr.
- **Nominalisation**: subordinate clauses are built by turning the verb into a noun or adjective (-mAk, -mA, -DIK, -(y)AcAK, -(y)An) and giving it case and possessive endings like any noun (wals 62A "possessive-accusative", 125A–128A "deranked"). Call them "clauses as nouns" in titles and use "nominalised clause" in prose after defining it.

### How suffixes are cited: archiphonemes

In **prose, tables and the dossier**, a suffix is cited in the archiphoneme notation of goksel2025 (the notation is widely used in Turkish linguistics; goksel2025 was not opened, so check its exact conventions before citing it as the source):

| Symbol | Stands for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A | e or a (two-way harmony) | -lAr: -ler, -lar |
| I | i, ı, u or ü (four-way harmony) | -(y)I: -i, -ı, -u, -ü, -yi, -yı... |
| D | d or t | -DI: -dı, -di, -du, -dü, -tı, -ti, -tu, -tü |
| C | c or ç | -CI: -cı, -çı... |
| K | k or ğ (at the end of a suffix, before a vowel) | -(y)AcAK: -acak, -acağ-ı |
| (y), (n), (s) | a buffer consonant used only after a vowel | -(y)A: ev-e but araba-ya |
| (I), (A) | a vowel used only after a consonant | -(I)m: ev-im but araba-m |

Write archiphonemes in capitals inside a hyphenated suffix (-DAn) so they can never be mistaken for real letters. Never use them in `orig`, `morphemes` or `:::vocab` headwords.

### Gloss conventions

Segment every suffix with a hyphen. The `morphemes` line shows **surface forms**, exactly as spelt. Abbreviations and the lesson that unlocks each are in `glossary.json`.

- **Buffer consonants** go with the suffix they precede: `araba-yı` `car-ACC`, `araba-sı` `car-3SG.POSS`, `kapı-nın` `door-GEN`, `oku-yacak` `read-FUT`.
- **Pronominal n** (after a 3rd-person possessive, before a case) goes with the case: `ev-i-nde` `house-3SG.POSS-LOC`, `bakım-ı-ndan` `aspect-3SG.POSS-ABL`. Explain it once, in tur-03-06.
- **Stem alternations** are not marked: the stem is shown as it is spelt: `kitab-ı` `book-ACC`, `ağac-ı` `tree-ACC`, `ağz-ı` `mouth-3SG.POSS`, `oğl-u` `son-3SG.POSS`. A stem whose final vowel is dropped before -(I)yor is shown without it: `başl-ıyor` `begin-PROG` (from *başla-*); `di-yor` `say-PROG` and `yi-yor` `eat-PROG` (from *de-*, *ye-*; wiktionary diyor, yiyor) are glossed `say-PROG`, `eat-PROG`.
- **Unmarked categories are not glossed**: nominative, singular, 3SG person on verbs (zero), the bare 2SG imperative, the positive. Do not write `-Ø`.
- **Person endings**: three sets, all glossed the same way (`1SG`, `2SG`, `1PL`, `2PL`, `3PL`):
  - z-set after nouns, -(I)yor, the aorist, the future, -mIş, -mAlI: -(y)Im, -sIn, -, -(y)Iz, -sInIz, -lAr: `gel-iyor-um` `come-PROG-1SG`, `öğretmen-im` `teacher-1SG`.
  - k-set after -DI and -sA: -m, -n, -, -k, -nIz, -lAr: `gel-di-m` `come-PST-1SG`, `gel-di-k` `come-PST-1PL`.
  - optative and imperative sets: `gel-eyim` `come-OPT.1SG`, `gel-elim` `come-OPT.1PL`, `gel-sin` `come-IMP.3SG`, `gel-in` `come-IMP.2PL` (fused person and mood: one piece).
  - 3PL -lAr: gloss `3PL` on verbs and predicates, `PL` on nouns. A noun predicate in the 3PL (*çocuklar* 'they are children') is ambiguous with the plural noun: gloss by meaning and say so.
- **Tense and mood** pieces: `-iyor` PROG; `-ir/-ar/-r` AOR; `-di` PST; `-ecek` FUT; `-meli` NEC; `-ebil` ABIL; `-eme` IMPOT; `-se` COND; `-e` OPT.
- **The negative aorist** -mAz is one piece `NEG.AOR`: `gel-mez` `come-NEG.AOR`. In 1SG and 1PL the z is absent: `gel-me-m` `come-NEG.AOR-1SG`, `gel-me-yiz` `come-NEG.AOR-1PL` (wiktionary gelmem, gelmeyiz, checked). After -(y)AmA the aorist is -z: `gel-eme-z` `come-IMPOT-AOR`.
- **-mIş** is glossed by function, consistently:
  - as the last tense marker on a finite verb: `PST.EVID` (one piece): `gel-miş` `come-PST.EVID` 'he came, apparently', `gel-miş-im` `come-PST.EVID-1SG`.
  - before a copular marker, and as a participle: `PRF`: `gel-miş-ti` `come-PRF-PST` 'he had come'; `kuru-muş ağaç` 'dried-up tree' (`dry-PRF`).
  - as a copular marker after a noun or another tense: `EVID` only (it adds no past): `ev-de-ymiş` `house-LOC-EVID` 'he is at home, apparently'; `gel-iyor-muş` `come-PROG-EVID`.
  This follows Leipzig practice of glossing by function, and keeps EVID for exactly the evidential meaning. Explain the three uses in tur-04-07, tur-04-08 and tur-04-09.
- **Copular -(y)DI** is `PST` and **-(y)sA** is `COND`, the same glosses as verbal -DI and -sA. The lesson text explains the difference (tense on a stem versus copula on a predicate).
- **-DIr** is `COP`: `öğretmen-dir` `teacher-COP`; `gel-miş-tir` `come-PRF-COP` 'he must have come; he has come (formal)'.
- **The question particle** mI is a separate word in spelling, glossed `Q`, with any person ending after it hyphenated: `geliyor mu-sun` `come-PROG Q-2SG`; `geldin mi` `come-PST-2SG Q`.
- **Case**: ACC, DAT, LOC, ABL, GEN; -(y)lA is `INS`. Postpositions written as separate words are glossed in lower case: *için* `for`, *gibi* `like`. The particle *de/da* 'also, too' is a separate word glossed `also`; the conjunction *ki* `that`.
- **Possessive**: `1SG.POSS` etc. The **compound marker** -(s)I in *Türk kahve-si* is glossed `3SG.POSS` (it is the same suffix); tur-03-07 explains the difference in use. 3PL possessive -lArI is one piece `3PL.POSS`: `ev-leri` 'their house'; *evleri* 'his houses' is `ev-ler-i` `house-PL-3SG.POSS`; say the form is ambiguous.
- **Voice**: PASS, CAUS, REFL, RECP, each its own piece: `yap-tır-ıl-dı` `do-CAUS-PASS-PST`.
- **Non-finite**: INF -mAk; VN -mA; NMLZ -DIK; PTCP -(y)An; future nominal and participle -(y)AcAK glossed `FUT` (the possessive after it shows the nominal use): `gel-eceğ-i-ni` `come-FUT-3SG.POSS-ACC`; converb -(y)Ip `CVB`. Other converbs and subordinators are glossed by a lower-case English word joined with full stops: `gel-ince` `come-when`, `gel-ir-ken` `come-AOR-while`, `gel-erek` `come-by`, `gel-dikçe` `come-as.long.as`, `gel-meden` `come-without` (or `come-NEG-ABL` in the lesson that analyses it, tur-06-07), `gel-eli` `come-since`.
- **Derivational suffixes** are glossed in lower case by meaning, inflectional ones in capitals: `tuz-lu` `salt-with`, `tuz-suz` `salt-without`, `göz-lük` `eye-thing.for` (glasses), `balık-çı` `fish-seller`, `Türk-çe` `Turk-language`, `iyi-lik` `good-ness`. Once a derived word is in a `:::vocab` block it may be glossed whole (`gözlük` `glasses`) when its parts are not the point.
- **-ki** is `REL`: `ev-de-ki` `house-LOC-REL` 'the one in the house'; `benim-ki` `1SG.GEN-REL` 'mine'.
- **Pronouns**: `ben` `1SG`, `biz` `1PL`, `bana` `1SG.DAT` (fused: no segment), `benim` `1SG.GEN`, `onu` `3SG-ACC` (segment o-n-u as `on-u`, `3SG-ACC`) **(segmentation is this guide's choice)**. Never gloss "I" as a bare capital.
- **Existentials and negators**: *var* `existent`, *yok* `nonexistent`, *değil* `not`, *hayır* `no`, *evet* `yes`. *var-dı* `existent-PST`, *değil-im* `not-1SG`.
- **Proper names** keep capitals and the apostrophe stays in `orig` but not in `morphemes`: orig *Ali'nin*, morphemes `Ali-nin`, gloss `Ali-GEN`.

### Citation forms

- Nouns: bare form, with the accusative where the stem changes or harmony is irregular: *kitap* (acc. *kitabı*), *ağız* (acc. *ağzı*), *saat* (acc. *saati*), *rol* (acc. *rolü*). This is what wiktionary's headword line and tdk's *taki* field give.
- Verbs: the infinitive in -mAk (*gelmek*), with the 3SG aorist, because the aorist vowel cannot be predicted for monosyllables: *gelmek* (aor. *gelir*), *yapmak* (aor. *yapar*), *gitmek* (aor. *gider*; past *gitti*). This is wiktionary's headword line ("third-person singular simple present gelir"). `:::vocab` notes field: aorist, plus any stem change (*gitmek*: gid-; *etmek*: ed-; *demek*: di-yor, *yemek*: yi-yor).

## 6. Points of scholarly disagreement or uncertainty

Say so in the lesson when one of these comes up.

1. **Stress or pitch accent?** Most grammars call the word accent "stress"; Levi (levi2005) and others find pitch the main cue and compare Turkish with Japanese pitch accent; Kabak (kabak2016) finds no unequivocal evidence for a pitch-accent system (all via wpphon). Teach the position of the accent, which is not disputed, and mention the debate in one sentence (tur-01-07). The Japanese comparison is a fair Connections note if worded as "some researchers".
2. **Is ğ a phoneme?** It has no constant sound, but it blocks the buffer y (*dağ-ın*, not \**dağ-yın*) and lengthens vowels; whether this makes it a phoneme is debated (wpphon). Teach it as a letter with three behaviours.
3. **Zero copula.** In speech the 3SG present of 'be' has no marker (*Ali öğretmen* 'Ali is a teacher'); wals 120A nevertheless codes Turkish as "zero copula impossible" (after the 1967 edition of lewis2000), presumably counting the person endings and -DIr. Teach the facts; mention the classification only if useful.
4. **-mIş: evidential, perfect, or both?** wals 77A–78A (after aksukoc1986) treat -mIş as an indirect evidential in the tense system; wals 68A says Turkish has "no perfect"; yet -mIş is clearly resultative in participles and compound tenses (*gelmişti*, *kurumuş*), which is why this guide glosses it by function (section 4). Mirative use (surprise at something one sees: *Ne güzelmiş!* 'how pretty it is!') is described in the literature **(unverified: no source opened gives the example)**.
5. **The aorist's meaning** (habit, general truth, willingness, prediction, polite requests) is not a tense in the European sense, and the name is a misnomer from Greek grammar. Grammars differ in how they label it (wiktionary "aorist", also "simple present" in its headword lines).
6. **-(I)yor: progressive or imperfective?** It covers ongoing and, increasingly, habitual meaning; goksel2025 describes it as imperfective **(unverified)**. This guide glosses it PROG and explains the wider use in tur-04-16.
7. **Optative.** wals 73A says there is no inflectional optative (after the 1967 edition of lewis2000 and Kononov); wiktionary's tables have an "optative" row (geleyim, gele...). The forms are not in dispute; the label is. Use "optative" and say that its 3rd-person forms are now rare and formal **(rarity unverified)**.
8. **Clitic or suffix?** The question particle mI, the copular markers -(y)DI, -(y)mIş, -(y)sA, the person endings after them, and -DIr are pre-accenting and attach to whole phrases (*güzel ve akıllı-dır*); goksel2025 ch. 11 treats some as clitics. This guide writes them with hyphens like suffixes (following spelling), except mI and de, which are spelt apart.
9. **-DIK: participle or nominaliser?** The same suffix forms "fact" clauses (*geldiğini biliyorum*) and object relative clauses (*geldiğim yer*). Some descriptions call it a participle in both, others a nominaliser **(unverified who says which)**. One gloss, NMLZ, in both uses.
10. **Number of cases**: six or seven, depending on whether -(y)lA counts (wals 49A "6–7").
11. **Origins.** Do not give etymologies of suffixes unless a source opened in the session states them. The origin of -(I)yor in a compound with an old verb *yorı-* 'walk' is stated in wpphon (note 3) and is the standard view **(check wiktionary's etymology of -iyor before stating it)**. The copular markers come from an old verb *i-* 'be', still visible in *idi*, *imiş*, *ise* (spelling evidence, safe to state). Etymologies of loanwords: cite tdk's *lisan* field (e.g. kitap < Arabic kitāb; kâğıt < Persian; rol < French rôle, all checked).
12. **Altaic.** Turkish is Turkic (glottolog, wals "Altaic / Turkic" genus). The Altaic hypothesis linking Turkic, Mongolic, Tungusic, Korean and Japanese is rejected by most specialists (glottolog does not recognise Altaic as a family). Similarities with Korean and Japanese are typological or areal, not proof of common descent: say this every time a Connections note compares them.

## 8. Notes for drafters

- British spelling; second person; short sentences; one idea per paragraph. No "simply", no "just" as filler.
- **The backbone is the slot order.** Every lesson in parts 2 to 4 that adds a suffix says which slot it goes in, and the chain of thought follows the slots left to right: "The stem is *gel-*. Is it negative? Then -mA next. What tense? -DI. Which harmony? The last vowel is e, so -di. Who? I, the k-set ending -m: *gelmedim*." Show the growing word at each step.
- **Harmony is taught before any suffix** (tur-01-04, tur-01-05) and every new suffix is introduced in archiphoneme form with two or three surface examples, one from each harmony class.
- Constructed examples use only words already introduced in a `:::vocab` block and only suffixes already introduced. An example whose gloss needs a category not yet taught carries `preview:` and says so in its note.
- Until tur-03-01, objects in examples are non-specific (no accusative): *kitap okuyorum* 'I am reading (a book / books)'. Until tur-04-03, verbs are in -(I)yor only.
- For every verb used, record in the vocab notes its aorist and any stem change, checked in wiktionary.
- Turkish has no gender: *o* is 'he, she, it'; write "he/she/it" in literal lines when context does not decide.
- Connections worth making for this reader (keep each short and accurate; never imply common descent, section 6 point 12):
  - **Korean and Japanese**: verb-final order, postpositions, case suffixes or particles (Turkish -(y)I like Japanese を but only for specific objects; -DA like に/で, -DAn like から, Korean 에서/에게서), pronoun dropping, no articles, relative clauses before the noun with no relative pronoun (*okuduğum kitap* like 내가 읽은 책 / 私が読んだ本), subordinate clauses built from nominalised verbs (-DIğI like Korean -는 것 / Japanese の・こと), converb chains (-(y)Ip like Japanese て / Korean -고), and a sentence-final question particle (mI like か / -까 in position, though mI attaches to the questioned word). Evidentiality: -mIş resembles Korean -더라/-네 and Japanese らしい・そうだ in meaning only in part; say which part. Korean ㅡ is close to ı.
  - **Spanish and French**: few structural parallels. Loanwords from French are many (*tren*, *istasyon*, *rol*, *kuaför* **(unverified)**); tdk's *lisan* field gives the source. French *tu/vous* matches *sen/siz*.
  - **Arabic**: a large layer of Arabic vocabulary, often through Persian, much of it replaced since the 1930s (*kitap*, *saat*, *hakikat*, *kalp*; tdk lisan, checked). The odd harmony of *saate*, *saati* comes from the Arabic tāʼ marbūṭa (wpphon). Arabic -i of relation is the source of -î (*millî*) **(unverified)**.
  - **Persian**: *ki* 'that' and the izafet (*Türk-i*, in Ottoman and set phrases) are Persian; cross-link Persian (`pes`) and Ottoman (`ota`) in tur-07-11.
  - **Georgian** (in the app): another verb-final language with case suffixes and postpositions; Georgian has many Turkish loanwords **(check in kat dossier or wiktionary before claiming any)**.
- Do not use vulgar proverbs from the wiktionary category. Avoid political material in examples (beyond the fixed readings, which are presented as historical documents).
