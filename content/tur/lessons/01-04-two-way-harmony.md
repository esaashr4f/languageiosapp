## The idea

**Vowel harmony** is the rule that a suffix does not have a fixed vowel. Its vowel is chosen to match the vowel before it. You saw it in lesson 2 of part 0: *evler* "houses" but *arabalar* "cars".

There are two kinds of suffix vowel. This lesson covers the simpler kind, which has two shapes, *e* or *a*. The next lesson covers the other kind, which has four.

The two-way rule asks one question of the three you learnt in lesson 1: **front or back?**

- If the last vowel of the word is front (*e i ö ü*), the suffix takes **e**.
- If the last vowel of the word is back (*a ı o u*), the suffix takes **a**.

Rounding and height do not matter. *Göz* has a rounded vowel and *dil* an unrounded one, but both are front, so both take *e*.

Why *e* and *a*? Look at the cube. *E* and *a* are the two unrounded low vowels, one front and one back. A two-way suffix keeps its vowel low and unrounded and lets the word in front decide only whether it is front or back.

The same preference runs through Turkish roots. Most native words keep all their vowels on one side: *kadın* "woman" is all back, *deniz* "sea" all front. You met the same principle in the consonants, where *k*, *g* and *l* take a front or back form to suit the vowel beside them. Harmony carries that principle across the joins between a word and its suffixes, so that a whole word, however long, is said with the tongue in one region of the mouth.

The system is old. Wiktionary's etymologies trace the "in, at" suffix *-de/-da* to a Proto-Turkic suffix that already had a front form and a back form. Turkish inherited harmony from the parent of its family; it did not invent it.

## How it is built

To add a two-way suffix, take three steps.

1. **Find the last vowel of the word.** Not the first, not the commonest: the last.
2. **Ask whether it is front or back.** Front: *e i ö ü*. Back: *a ı o u*.
3. **Choose the vowel.** Front, *e*. Back, *a*.

Take *göz* "eye" and the plural suffix. The last vowel is *ö*. It is front. So the suffix is *-ler*: *gözler*. Take *yol* "road". The last vowel is *o*, back, so *-lar*: *yollar*.

The plural and the suffix meaning "in, at" are both two-way suffixes. You will learn what they do in part 2. Here they are previews, used only to show harmony at work.

## Watch out

**The last vowel, not the first.** *Elma* starts with a front vowel and *anne* with a back one, and a learner who looks at the start gets both wrong. Some native words, such as *elma*, *anne* and *kardeş* "brother, sister", and very many loanwords, mix front and back vowels inside the root. Harmony between root and suffix still works: the suffix copies the vowel next to it.

**Rounding plays no part here.** *Gözler*, not \**gözlör*. A two-way suffix has only *e* or *a*. It never takes *o* or *ö*, which, as lesson 1 showed, no native suffix contains.

**A word with one vowel is easy; a long word is no harder.** However many suffixes you add, each new one looks at the vowel immediately before it. Since each suffix already agrees with the one before, the whole chain stays on one side. (A few suffixes have a vowel that never changes; each is pointed out when it is taught.)

**A handful of loanwords break the rule.** *Saat* "hour" ends in a back vowel but takes front suffixes: *saatler*. Lesson 8 explains why. For native words and most loans, the rule here holds without exception.

## Exercises

1. Add the plural to *deniz*, *oda*, *gün* and *çocuk*. Show the three steps for one of them.
2. Why is the plural of *göz* not \**gözlör*?
3. Add the plural to *kardeş*. Which vowel did you look at, and which did you ignore?
4. Which vowel would a two-way suffix take after *büyük*? After *kadın*?
5. A learner writes \**annelar*, reasoning that *anne* starts with *a*. Correct the form and the reasoning.
6. *Okulda* means "at school". Predict the form for "at home", using *ev*.
7. Explain in one sentence why harmony keeps even a long Turkish word on one side, front or back.

## Worked answers

1. *Denizler*, *odalar*, *günler*, *çocuklar*. For *çocuk*: the last vowel is *u*; *u* is back; so the suffix is *-lar*.
2. A two-way suffix has only *e* or *a*. *Ö* is front, so it takes *e*: *gözler*. Its rounding does not count, and native suffixes never contain *ö*.
3. *Kardeşler*. The last vowel, *e*, is front. The *a* of the first syllable is ignored.
4. *E* after *büyük* (last vowel *ü*, front). *A* after *kadın* (last vowel *ı*, back).
5. *Anneler*. The suffix looks at the last vowel of the word, not the first. The last vowel of *anne* is *e*, which is front.
6. *Evde*. The last vowel is *e*, front, so the suffix is *-de*.
7. Each suffix copies the front or back quality of the vowel just before it, so once the stem has set the direction, every suffix after it follows in turn.
