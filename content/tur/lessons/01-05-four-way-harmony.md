## The idea

The second kind of suffix vowel is always **high**: one of *i*, *ı*, *u*, *ü*. Which one depends on the vowel before it, and this time the suffix asks two questions, not one.

1. **Front or back?** As in two-way harmony.
2. **Rounded or not?** If the vowel before is rounded (*o ö u ü*), the suffix rounds too.

Two questions with two answers give four results, which is why this is called **four-way harmony**:

- after *e* or *i* (front, unrounded): **i**
- after *a* or *ı* (back, unrounded): **ı**
- after *ö* or *ü* (front, rounded): **ü**
- after *o* or *u* (back, rounded): **u**

Why does a high suffix copy rounding when a low one does not? Look back at lesson 1. In native words, *o* and *ö* stand only in the first syllable. A low suffix vowel that copied rounding would have to be *o* or *ö*, which native words do not allow after the first syllable. High rounded *u* and *ü* have no such limit. The two facts fit together: the two kinds of harmony are one system, with rounding copied wherever the result is a vowel Turkish allows.

## How it is built

To add a four-way suffix, take four steps.

1. **Find the vowel immediately before the suffix.** The last vowel of the word as it stands, including any suffixes already added.
2. **Front or back?** That decides between the pair *i/ü* and the pair *ı/u*.
3. **Rounded or not?** That decides within the pair: unrounded *i* or *ı*, rounded *ü* or *u*.
4. **Write the vowel.**

Take *göz* "eye" and the suffix meaning "my". The vowel before is *ö*: front, rounded. Front and rounded give *ü*: *gözüm* "my eye". Now add the plural first: *gözler*. The vowel before "my" is now the *e* of *-ler*: front, unrounded. So "my" becomes *-im*: *gözlerim* "my eyes". The suffix copies its neighbour, not the stem.

**The notation in capitals.** You can now read the notation this guide uses for suffixes in prose and tables. It follows the reference grammar of Göksel and Kerslake. A capital letter stands for a sound that changes by rule.

- **A** is the two-way vowel, *e* or *a*. The plural is **-lAr**: *-ler*, *-lar*.
- **I** is the four-way vowel, *i*, *ı*, *u* or *ü*. The suffix meaning "my" contains it.
- **D** is *d* or *t*, and **C** is *c* or *ç*. Lesson 6 gives the rule.

Letters in round brackets appear only in some positions. **(I)** is a vowel used after a consonant and dropped after a vowel. So "my" is **-(I)m**: *ev-im* "my house", but *araba-m* "my car", because Turkish does not put two vowels together. **(y)** is a consonant used only after a vowel, as in the suffix **-(y)I**: *-i* after a consonant, *-yi* after a vowel, with the vowel chosen by four-way harmony. Lesson 6 explains these buffer letters.

Read *-(I)m* as an instruction: "after a consonant, add a four-way vowel and m; after a vowel, add m". The capitals never appear in Turkish text. The *morphemes* line of every example shows the real spelling.

## Watch out

**Look at the nearest vowel.** *Gözüm* but *gözlerim*. Once *-ler* is in place, its *e* is the vowel that counts. A learner who looks back at the stem writes \**gözlerüm*.

**Rounding is copied, never started.** A suffix rounds only if the vowel before it is rounded. After *a* or *e* it is never *u* or *ü*: *kızım*, not \**kızum*.

**Do not mix the two notations.** *A* is always two-way, *I* always four-way. The plural is *-lAr*, never \**-lIr*: *yollar*, not \**yollur*. Each suffix belongs to one type for good, and when this guide introduces a suffix the capital letter tells you which.

**Roots can round after a lip consonant.** Inside a root, a rounded vowel sometimes follows an unrounded one after *p*, *b*, *f*, *v* or *m*: *tavuk* "chicken" has *a* then *u*. The rule in this lesson is about suffixes. A suffix after *tavuk* looks at its last vowel, *u*, as usual.

**The particle mI is a separate word in spelling.** It still harmonises with the word before it: *güzel mi*, *büyük mü*. Part 2 teaches where it goes.

## Exercises

1. Add *-(I)m* "my" to *baş*, *yüz*, *diz* and *kuş*. Name the corner of the cube that decides each.
2. Add *-(I)m* to *oda* and explain why no vowel appears.
3. Build "my birds" from *kuş*, the plural *-lAr* and *-(I)m*. Show which vowel each suffix looked at.
4. Why does no suffix contain *o* or *ö*, while many contain *u* and *ü*?
5. Add the particle *mI* to *küçük* and to *soğuk*. Write both.
6. A learner writes \**yollur* for "roads". What has gone wrong?
7. Write out every shape of *-lI* "with", and give one example from this lesson.

## Worked answers

1. *Başım* (after a: back, unrounded, ı). *Yüzüm* (after ü: front, rounded, ü). *Dizim* (after i: front, unrounded, i). *Kuşum* (after u: back, rounded, u).
2. *Odam*. The word ends in a vowel, and the bracketed *(I)* is used only after a consonant. Turkish does not put two vowels side by side.
3. *Kuşlarım*. The plural looked at *u*, which is back, and took *-lar*. "My" then looked at the *a* of *-lar*, back and unrounded, and took *-ım*.
4. A low rounded vowel would be *o* or *ö*, and native words allow those only in the first syllable. A suffix is never the first syllable, so low suffixes stay *e* or *a*. High rounded *u* and *ü* are allowed anywhere, so high suffixes can copy rounding.
5. *Küçük mü* (after ü) and *soğuk mu* (after u).
6. The plural is a two-way suffix, *-lAr*. It takes only *e* or *a*, and after *o*, which is back, it is *-lar*: *yollar*. The learner treated it as four-way.
7. *-li*, *-lı*, *-lu*, *-lü*: after *e/i*, *a/ı*, *o/u*, *ö/ü*. *Tuzlu* "salty" shows *-lu* after *u*.
