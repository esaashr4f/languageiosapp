## The idea

To say "more than one", Turkish adds **-lAr** to the noun: *kitap* "book", *kitaplar* "books"; *ev* "house", *evler* "houses". It is the only plural suffix in the language, and it is completely regular. There are no irregular plurals like English "children" or "mice".

The harder question is not how to form the plural but when to use it. English marks plurality almost every time: "two books", "many books", "books are expensive". Turkish marks it far less.

The reason lies in what the bare noun means. A Turkish noun without *-lAr* is not "singular" in the English sense. Göksel and Kerslake call it **number-neutral**: it names the kind of thing, and leaves the number open unless the context fixes it (goksel2005 §14.3.1.3). *Kitap* can be "the book", "a book" or "books in general". The plural suffix adds the specific information "more than one of them", and Turkish adds it only where nothing else already says so.

So the rule of thumb is: **mark plurality once**. If a number, or a word like "many" or "every", already tells you how many, the noun stays bare: *iki kitap* "two books", *çok kitap* "many books".

The suffix is old but not as old as the family. Wiktionary's etymology traces it to Proto-Common Turkic, a later stage of the family's ancestor, and notes that it is not reconstructed for Proto-Turkic itself. Wiktionary records it in Old Turkic as *-lar*.

## How it is built

The plural is a two-way harmony suffix, so you need only one question.

1. **Find the last vowel of the noun.** *Çiçek* "flower": the last vowel is *e*.
2. **Front or back?** Front vowels (*e i ö ü*) take *-ler*; back vowels (*a ı o u*) take *-lar*: *çiçekler*, *sandalyeler*, *kızlar*, *okullar*.
3. **Write the suffix.** It starts with a consonant, so there is no buffer after a vowel (*araba-lar*) and no consonant change in the stem (*kitap-lar*, *çocuk-lar*). The softening of lesson 6 of part 1 happens only before a vowel.

Loanwords follow the rules of tur-01-08: harmony goes by the last vowel (*otobüsler*), and the few words with front suffixes after a back vowel keep them (*saatler*, *roller*).

**Where *-lAr* goes.** It comes straight after the stem, before any other suffix. Part 0 showed the order: *ev-ler-im-de* "in my houses", with the plural first. A personal ending comes after it.

**Stress.** The plural takes stress, like most suffixes: *kitaplár*, *öğretmenlér*. This separates it from the "they" ending of the last lesson, which has the same letters but takes no stress (goksel2005 §8.4). *Mühendislér* is "engineers"; *Mühendísler* is "they are engineers".

**When to leave it out.** Göksel and Kerslake list the cases (goksel2005 §14.3.1.1).

- **After a number**: *iki kitap*, *beş öğrenci*. A number already gives the count.
- **After words of quantity**: *çok* "many", *birkaç* "a few", *her* "every": *çok insan*, *birkaç soru*, *her öğrenci*.
- **After *bir* "a"**: *bir kitap*.
- **When number does not matter**: the bare noun names the kind. *Kitap pahalı* is a statement about books in general, or about one book; the context decides.

**When to put it in.**

- **More than one particular thing, with no number**: *Kitaplar pahalı* "the books are expensive".
- **After *bazı* "some, certain"**: this word needs the plural, *bazı insanlar* "some people" (goksel2005 §14.3.1.1).
- **After *bütün* "all", when you mean "all the ..."**: *bütün sınıflar* "all the classes". With a bare noun *bütün* means "the whole": *bütün sınıf* "the whole class".

**Counting with *tane*.** Between a number and a noun for a thing, Turkish often puts *tane*, a counting word like Japanese つ or Korean 개: *beş tane elma* "five apples". It is optional, and it is not used for people (goksel2005 §15.8). The noun stays bare after it.

## Where it applies

- **Several particular things or people, with no number**: *kitaplar*, *öğrenciler*.
- **Several kinds of a substance**: *kahveler* can mean "kinds of coffee" (goksel2005 §14.3.1.2).
- **Several portions of a substance**: *çaylar* "the glasses of tea".
- **After *bazı***, which requires it: *bazı öğrenciler*.
- **After *bütün* for "all the ..."**: *bütün öğrenciler* "all the students".
- **On pronouns**: *onlar* "they" is *o* with *-lAr* (and an *n*); *bunlar* "these" comes in part 3.
- **On a person's name, informally**: added to a name, *-lAr* can mean "that person and their family or friends" (goksel2005 §14.3.1.2). You will meet it in conversation.
- **On a predicate, as "they"**: the separate use of the same letters, taught in the last lesson.
- **Before possessive and case suffixes**: the plural always comes first, as in *evlerimde* "in my houses" (part 3).

## Watch out

**Do not add *-lAr* after a number.** Spanish *dos libros* and French *deux livres* tempt *\*iki kitaplar*. The number is enough: *iki kitap*.

**Do not add *-lAr* after *çok*, *birkaç* or *her*.** *\*Çok insanlar*, *\*birkaç sorular* and *\*her öğrenciler* are wrong. Say *çok insan*, *birkaç soru*, *her öğrenci*.

**Do not leave it out after *bazı*.** *\*Bazı öğrenci* is wrong. *Bazı* behaves the opposite way: *bazı öğrenciler*.

**Do not use *bir* with a plural noun.** *\*Bir kitaplar* mixes "a" and "more than one". Say *kitaplar*, or *birkaç kitap* "a few books". (Göksel and Kerslake note a few informal exceptions with "something" and "somewhere"; you will not need them yet.)

**Do not add *-lAr* to the predicate just because the subject is plural.** English "the books are expensive" has a plural verb. In Turkish a plural subject that is a thing usually leaves the predicate bare: *Kitaplar pahalı*, not *Kitaplar pahalılar*, which Göksel and Kerslake describe as unusual for things that are not acting (goksel2005 §12.2.2.1). With people both forms are correct.

**Do not make the predicate noun plural for a group.** "They are students" is *Onlar öğrenci* or *Öğrenciler* said with the stress on *-ci-*. *Onlar öğrenciler*, with the stress on *-ler*, means "they are *the* students", a known group (goksel2005 §22.3).

**Do not use four-way harmony.** The plural is two-way: *\*kuşlur*, *\*gözlür* are wrong. *Kuşlar*, *gözler*.

**Do not soften the stem before *-lAr*.** *\*Kitablar*, *\*çocuğlar*: the softening of lesson 6 of part 1 happens only before a vowel. *Kitaplar*, *çocuklar*.

**Do not reach for *tane* with people.** Göksel and Kerslake describe *tane* as used mainly with things, not people (goksel2005 §15.8). For "two students" say *iki öğrenci*.

## Connections

Japanese and Korean also leave nouns without number most of the time, and both use counting words between a number and a noun (Japanese 三つ, Korean 세 개). Turkish *tane* plays a similar part, but it is optional and there is only one of it for all things. Spanish and French mark the plural on every noun and article, so the main adjustment for this reader is to leave it out.

## Exercises

1. Make *sandalye*, *resim*, *ders*, *otobüs* and *saat* plural.
2. Say "three questions", "many questions" and "the questions".
3. Translate *Bazı insanlar mutlu, bazı insanlar yorgun.*
4. A learner writes *\*dört tane kitaplar*. Correct it and give the rule.
5. Explain the difference between *bütün sınıf* and *bütün sınıflar*.
6. Say "the flowers are beautiful" and "the children are happy". Which predicate may take *-lAr*, and why?
7. How do you tell *Öğrenciler* "they are students" from *öğrenciler* "students" in speech?
8. Why is it *kitaplar* and not *\*kitablar*, when the accusative is *kitabı*?

## Worked answers

1. *Sandalyeler*, *resimler*, *dersler*, *otobüsler*, *saatler*. The last vowel decides each one: *e*, *i*, *e*, *ü* are front. *Saat* has a back vowel but is one of the loans with front suffixes (tur-01-08).
2. *Üç soru*, *çok soru*, *sorular*. After a number or *çok* the noun stays bare. Without either, "the questions" needs *-lar*, back after *u*.
3. "Some people are happy, some people are tired." *Bazı* requires the plural *insanlar*. The predicates take no *-lAr*; with people it would be allowed, but it is not needed when the subject is said.
4. *Dört tane kitap* (or *dört kitap*). After a number the noun stays bare, and *tane* does not change that.
5. With a bare noun *bütün* means "whole": *bütün sınıf* "the whole class", one class. With a plural it means "all": *bütün sınıflar* "all the classes".
6. *Çiçekler güzel*; *Çocuklar mutlu* or *Çocuklar mutlular*. With people the "they" ending is optional. With things such as flowers the predicate is normally left bare.
7. By the stress. The "they" ending is unstressable, so the stress falls before it: *öğrencíler*. The plural suffix takes the stress: *öğrencilér*.
8. The *p* becomes *b* only before a vowel, as in *kitab-ı*. *-lAr* begins with a consonant, so the stem keeps its *p*.
