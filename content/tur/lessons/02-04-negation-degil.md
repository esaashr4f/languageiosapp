## The idea

You can now say "Ali is a teacher" and "I am tired". This lesson says the opposite: "Ali is not a teacher", "I am not tired".

To **negate** a sentence is to turn it into its denial. In English you add "not" to the verb "is". Turkish verbs have a negative suffix of their own (tur-02-09), but a nominal sentence has no verb to carry it. So Turkish uses a separate word, ***değil*** "not", placed after the predicate:

*Ali öğretmen değil.* "Ali teacher not." Ali is not a teacher.

**Değil behaves like a predicate.** It is not a loose particle that floats around the sentence. It stands in the last position, and it takes the personal endings of tur-02-02, so the ending leaves the noun and moves onto *değil*: *Öğretmenim* "I am a teacher" becomes *Öğretmen değilim* "I am not a teacher". Göksel and Kerslake describe *değil* as the normal way to negate an "x is y" sentence, and note that it takes the same suffixes as a noun predicate (goksel2005 §§12.1.1.2, 20.2.1).

**Why it works this way.** The word itself has always meant "is not". Wiktionary traces it to a Proto-Turkic form reconstructed as \*tegül "is not", and records Old Turkic *tügül* in the same sense. So Turkish did not build "is not" from "not" plus "is". It inherited a single word meaning "is not", and that word, like any predicate, takes the ending that names the subject. Korean has the same design: 아니다 is a separate negative "be" (see Connections).

**Two pronunciations.** In careful speech *değil* is [dejil], with the soft g heard as a light y between the two front vowels (tur-01-03). In everyday speech it is often shortened to one long syllable, [diːl] (goksel2005 §1.1.1.1; wiktionary). The spelling stays *değil* either way.

## How it is built

To negate a nominal sentence, take four steps.

1. **Build the positive sentence.** *Yorgunum* "I am tired".
2. **Take the personal ending off the predicate.** *Yorgun.*
3. **Put *değil* after the predicate**, as a separate word: *yorgun değil*.
4. **Put the ending on *değil*.** "I" is *-(y)Im*. *Değil* ends in a consonant, so there is no buffer *y*. Its last vowel is *i*, so harmony gives *-im*: *Yorgun değilim*.

**Harmony follows *değil*, not the predicate.** Once the ending sits on *değil*, it harmonises with the *i* of *değil*. The endings are therefore always the front unrounded set, whatever the predicate: *yorgun değilim*, *hasta değilsin*, *Türk değiliz*, *hazır değilsiniz*.

**The six forms** are *değilim* "I am not", *değilsin* "you are not", *değil* "he, she, it is not", *değiliz* "we are not", *değilsiniz* "you (plural or polite) are not", *değil* or *değiller* "they are not" (wiktionary, *değil*). The rule for *-lAr* is the one from tur-02-02: required for people when no subject word is said, optional when it is: *Evli değiller* "they are not married", *Onlar evli değil(ler)*.

***Bir* stays where it was.** The predicate keeps the shape it had in the positive sentence. *Bu bir kitap* becomes *Bu bir kitap değil* "this is not a book". *Ali öğretmen* (a job, no *bir*) becomes *Ali öğretmen değil*. *Ali iyi bir öğretmen* becomes *Ali iyi bir öğretmen değil*.

**Stress.** Two rules, one inside the word and one in the sentence.

- Inside the word: the personal endings are unstressable (tur-02-02), so the stress falls before them: *değílim*, *değílsiniz*. Göksel and Kerslake mark the stress of *değil* itself on its second syllable (goksel2005 §19.1.1.2); Wiktionary's transcription puts it on the first. In the short form [diːl] there is only one syllable to stress. The acute here is the guide's mark, not Turkish spelling.
- In the sentence: in a positive nominal sentence the main stress falls on the predicate (*Ali öğretMEN*, tur-02-01). In a negative one it moves to *değil*: *Ali öğretmen deĞİL* (goksel2005 §23.1). The new information is the denial, so the denial carries the stress.

**Answering.** The answer words are *evet* "yes" and *hayır* "no". They stand outside the sentence, usually followed by a comma and a full or short answer: *Hayır, öğretmen değilim.* Inside a sentence, "not" is always *değil*, never *hayır*.

**Joining with "but".** *Ama* "but" joins two statements, often a negative one and a positive one: *Zengin değiliz ama mutluyuz* "we are not rich, but we are happy".

## Where it applies

*Değil* negates every sentence of the "x is y" kind taught so far, and some that come later.

- **Jobs, nationality, roles**: *Ali öğretmen değil*, *Siz İspanyol değilsiniz*. No *bir*, as in the positive.
- **Identifying a thing**: *Bu bir kitap değil*. *Bir* stays.
- **Qualities and states**: *Hasta değilim*, *Hava soğuk değil*, *Oda temiz değil*.
- **A noun with its adjective**: *Ali iyi bir öğretmen değil* "Ali is not a good teacher". *Değil* comes after the whole predicate.
- **Every person**: *değilim*, *değilsin*, *değil*, *değiliz*, *değilsiniz*, *değil(ler)*.
- **Plural subjects**: *Kitaplar pahalı değil*; with people, *Öğrenciler hazır değil(ler)*.
- **Denial and correction**: *Ayşe doktor değil, hemşire*. Göksel and Kerslake describe this use, where *değil* stands in for a whole negative clause beside a positive one (goksel2005 §20.2.3). The order can be reversed: *Ayşe hemşire, doktor değil*.
- **With *ama***: *Ev eski ama temiz* "the house is old but clean"; *Zengin değiliz ama mutluyuz*.
- **In answers**: *Hayır, hasta değilim* or, shorter, *Hayır, değilim* "no, I'm not".
- **Where someone is not**: *Ali evde değil* "Ali is not at home" (tur-02-06, where *yok* offers a second way).
- **"Isn't it?"**: *değil* plus the question particle makes a tag, *değil mi?* (tur-02-05).
- **"Was not"**: *değildi* (part 4). The copular markers attach to *değil* as they attach to a noun.
- **Not for verbs, as a rule**: "I am not coming" is built with the verb suffix *-mA* (tur-02-09). *Değil* after a verb exists, but it means "it is not the case that ..." and rejects something the listener assumed (goksel2005 §20.2.2). You will meet it in tur-02-09.

## Watch out

**Do not leave the ending on the predicate.** *\*Öğretmenim değil* tries to keep "I am a teacher" and add "not". The ending must move: *Öğretmen değilim*. There is only one predicate ending per sentence, and in a negative sentence it sits on *değil*.

**Do not put *değil* first.** Spanish *no soy*, French *je ne suis pas* and English "not a teacher" all put the negation early. *\*Ben değil öğretmen* and *\*Değil öğretmen* are wrong. *Değil* comes after the predicate: *Ben öğretmen değilim*.

**Do not put *değil* between an adjective and its noun.** English "not a good teacher" invites *\*Ali değil iyi bir öğretmen*. The whole predicate comes first: *Ali iyi bir öğretmen değil*.

**Do not harmonise the ending with the predicate.** *\*Yorgun değilum* and *\*hasta değilım* take the vowel from the wrong word. The ending attaches to *değil*, so it is always front: *yorgun değilim*, *hasta değilim*.

**Do not add a buffer *y*.** The buffer is for vowel-final words, and *hasta* is no longer the word the ending touches. *\*Hasta değilyim* is wrong: *hasta değilim*.

**Do not write it joined to the predicate.** *Değil* is a separate word in spelling: *\*öğretmendeğil* is wrong.

**Do not use *hayır* for "not".** *Hayır* answers a question; it cannot sit inside a sentence. *\*Ali hayır öğretmen* is wrong: *Ali öğretmen değil*.

**Do not use *değil* for "there is not".** "There is no milk" denies that something exists, not that something is something. *\*Süt değil* is wrong for "there's no milk". Turkish has a separate word, *yok*: *Süt yok* (tur-02-06).

**Do not double the plural.** *\*Evliler değiller* puts *-lAr* on both words. Only the last word of the predicate, *değil*, can take the "they" ending: *Evli değiller*.

**Do not stress the ending.** *\*Değilím* is wrong. The endings are unstressable: *değílim*. And in the sentence, the main stress goes on *değil*, not on the noun before it: *Ali öğretmen deĞİL*, not *Ali öğretMEN değil*, unless you are contrasting that noun with another.

## Connections

Korean and Japanese have the same design: a special negative "be" that replaces the positive one and carries everything the positive one carried. Korean 학생이다 "is a student" becomes 학생이 아니다, Japanese 学生だ becomes 学生ではない; Turkish *öğrenci* becomes *öğrenci değil*. This is a shared type of structure, not evidence that the languages are related. Spanish and French, by contrast, negate the verb *ser* or *être* itself.

## Exercises

1. Negate *Ben doktorum*, *Sen avukatsın* and *Biz hazırız*.
2. Say "the restaurant is not open" using *lokanta*, and "the rooms are not full" using *oda*.
3. Take apart *İngiliz değilsiniz*: which word carries the ending, which person and number is it, and why are both its vowels *i*?
4. Translate *Kelime yanlış değil, doğru.*
5. A learner writes *\*Ben değil Koreliyim* for "I am not Korean". Correct it and give two reasons.
6. Say "Mehmet is not a good driver" and explain where *bir* and *değil* go.
7. Answer the question "Are you tired?" (asked of you) in Turkish with "No, I'm not tired, I'm hungry."
8. In *Ayşe zengin değil*, which word carries the main sentence stress? Compare *Ayşe zengin*.

## Worked answers

1. *Ben doktor değilim*, *Sen avukat değilsin*, *Biz hazır değiliz*. In each, the personal ending leaves the predicate and goes onto *değil*, in its front form: *-im*, *-sin*, *-iz*.
2. *Lokanta açık değil.* *Odalar dolu değil.* *Odalar* is a plural thing, so *değil* takes no *-lAr*, just as *Odalar dolu* would take none (tur-02-03).
3. *Değil* carries the ending *-siniz*: second person plural, "you" to several people or politely to one. Both vowels are *i* because the ending harmonises with *değil*, whose last vowel is *i*, not with *İngiliz*. The sentence means "you are not English".
4. "The word isn't wrong; it's right." *Değil* denies *yanlış*, and *doğru* gives the correct predicate, sharing the subject *kelime*.
5. *(Ben) Koreli değilim.* First, *değil* comes after the predicate, not before it. Second, the ending goes on *değil*, not on *Koreli*: there is one ending, at the end of the predicate.
6. *Mehmet iyi bir şoför değil.* The predicate is built as in the positive sentence, adjective, then *bir*, then the noun (tur-02-01), and *değil* follows the whole of it.
7. *Hayır, yorgun değilim, açım.* *Hayır* stands outside the sentence. The denial puts the ending on *değil*. The correction *açım* takes its ending directly, with no buffer after the consonant ç.
8. In *Ayşe zengin değil* the main stress falls on *değil*: the denial is the news. In *Ayşe zengin* it falls on the predicate *zengin*. Both rules come from goksel2005 §23.1.
