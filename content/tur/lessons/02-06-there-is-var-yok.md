## The idea

This lesson adds two things that work together: a way to say *where*, and a way to say *that something exists*.

**Where: the locative.** A **case** is a suffix on a noun that shows its role in the sentence. Turkish has six; this is the first. The **locative** case, *-DA*, means "in, at, on": *ev* "house", *evde* "at home, in the house"; *masa* "table", *masada* "on the table". English needs three prepositions for this. Turkish has one suffix, and leaves the exact relation to the situation. Göksel and Kerslake describe it as marking physical or abstract location (goksel2005 §14.3.3.4).

A word in the locative can be a predicate, like any noun: *Ali evde* "Ali is at home". This is the nominal sentence of tur-02-01 with a place as the predicate, and it takes personal endings, *değil* and *mI* exactly as *öğretmen* did: *Evdeyim*, *Evde değilim*, *Evde misin?*

**Existence: var and yok.** "There is a book on the table" does not say *where the book is*. It says *that a book exists there*. Turkish has two words for this, ***var*** "existent, there is" and ***yok*** "non-existent, there is not": *Masada bir kitap var* "there is a book on the table"; *Masada kitap yok* "there is no book on the table". Göksel and Kerslake call this an **existential sentence**: a sentence that only asserts that something is present or absent (goksel2005 §12.1.1.2).

Both words are old. Wiktionary traces *var* to Proto-Turkic \*bār "there is" and *yok* to Proto-Turkic \*yōk. Neither is a verb. They are predicate words, like adjectives that can only stand in the predicate. This is why Turkish needs no verb "to be" or "to have" here either.

**Why two patterns?** The two sentences answer different questions. *Kitap masada* "the book is on the table" answers "where is the book?": the book is known, the place is new. *Masada bir kitap var* answers "what is on the table?": the place is known, the thing is new. Göksel and Kerslake make the same distinction (goksel2005 §12.1.1.2), and the word order reflects it: the new information comes just before the predicate (tur-02-07).

## How it is built

**The locative suffix -DA.** Two questions decide its shape (tur-01-04, tur-01-06).

1. **D: is the last sound a voiceless consonant** (p, ç, t, k, f, h, s, ş)? Then *t*, otherwise *d*: *kitap-ta*, *sokak-ta*, *iş-te*, but *ev-de*, *oda-da*, *okul-da*.
2. **A: is the last vowel front or back?** *e* after front vowels, *a* after back: *ev-de*, *okul-da*.

So the four shapes are *-de*, *-da*, *-te*, *-ta*. There is no buffer after a vowel (*oda-da*), and no softening of the stem, because the suffix starts with a consonant (*kitapta*, not \**kitabda*). After a proper name the apostrophe of tur-01-08 appears: *İstanbul'da*, *Türkiye'de*. In this guide's `morphemes` lines the apostrophe is dropped: `İstanbul-da`. The plural comes first: *ev-ler-de* "in the houses".

**A place as predicate**, in three steps.

1. Put the place in the locative: *okul* becomes *okulda*.
2. Add the personal ending, which follows the locative's vowel: *okulda-yım* "I am at school", with the buffer *y* because *-DA* ends in a vowel.
3. Negate with *değil* or ask with *mI* as before: *okulda değilim*, *okulda mısın?*

**An existential sentence**, in three steps.

1. **Say the place first**, in the locative: *mutfakta*.
2. **Then the thing**, usually without *bir* in the negative and often with it in the positive: *mutfakta bir masa*.
3. **End with *var* or *yok***: *Mutfakta bir masa var.* *Mutfakta masa yok.*

Questions put *mI* after *var* or *yok*: *Evde süt var mı?* "Is there milk at home?" A double question is common: *Var mı, yok mu?* "Is there or isn't there?" (goksel2005 §19.1.2).

**Persons with var and yok.** *Yok* takes the personal endings and "they": *yokum* "I'm not there", *yoksun*, *yoklar* (wiktionary, *yok*). *Var* may take first and second person endings, but not the "they" ending (goksel2005 §12.1.1.2). In practice you will mostly use them in the third person.

**Stress.** *-DA* takes stress like most suffixes: *evdé*, *okuldá*. A personal ending after it does not: *evdéyim*. A stem with fixed stress keeps it: *Ánkara'da*. In the sentence, the main stress in a positive existential falls on the thing that exists; in a negative one it falls on *yok* (goksel2005 §23.1.2.2): *Mutfakta bir maSA var*, *Mutfakta masa YOK*.

## Where it applies

**The locative -DA:**

- **Place in, at or on**: *evde*, *masada*, *sokakta*, *otelde*. One suffix covers all three English prepositions.
- **Towns and countries**, with the apostrophe: *Ankara'da*, *Türkiye'de*.
- **A place as predicate**: *Ali evde*, *Evdeyim*, *Evde misin?*, *Evde değilim*.
- **Activities and events treated as places**: *toplantıda* "in a meeting", *derste* "in class", *tatilde* "on holiday", *işte* "at work".
- **Clock times and dates**: *saat beşte* "at five", *1995'te* "in 1995" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1). Not with days of the week (same section).
- **"Per"**: *ayda bir* "once a month", *yılda bir* "once a year" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.3).
- **"Has on him"**: a person in the locative with *var* means the thing is with that person: *Ali'de para var* "Ali has money (on him)" (goksel2005 §12.1.1.2). The full "have" construction is tur-03-09.
- **Action at a place**: *Ali bahçede çalışıyor* "Ali is working in the garden" (tur-02-08). Unlike Japanese and Korean, Turkish uses the same case for where something is and where something happens.

**var and yok:**

- **Something exists somewhere**: *Çantada bir kitap var.*
- **Something is absent**: *Mutfakta peynir yok.*
- **Without a place**: *Süt var* "there is milk"; *Soru var mı?* "Are there any questions?"
- **Questions**: *var mı?*, *yok mu?*, *var mı, yok mu?*
- **With *hiç***: *hiç ... yok* "none at all".
- **A person not there**: *Müdür toplantıda yok*, as an alternative to *değil* (goksel2005 §20.3).
- **As "no"**: in colloquial speech *yok* often answers a question instead of *hayır* (goksel2005 §20.3).
- **Possession**: *Arabam var* "I have a car" (tur-03-09).

## Watch out

**Do not use *var* for "is".** *\*Ali var öğretmen* and *\*Kitap var masada* treat *var* like Spanish *es* or *está*. *Var* is Spanish *hay*: it says that something exists. "The book is on the table" is *Kitap masada*.

**Do not use *değil* for "there is no".** *\*Mutfakta peynir değil* denies the wrong thing. Absence is *yok*: *Mutfakta peynir yok*.

**Do not choose the wrong pattern for a known thing.** *Masada kitap var* means "there is a book (some book) on the table". For a particular book you already know, say where it is: *Kitap masada*. Göksel and Kerslake note the two cannot be swapped (goksel2005 §12.1.1.2).

**Do not put the place after var.** English "there is a book on the table" ends with the place. Turkish puts it first and *var* last: *Masada bir kitap var*, not *\*Bir kitap var masada* as a neutral sentence.

**Do not use *bir* in a plain negative.** *Mutfakta peynir yok* is the normal form, with a bare noun. To stress "not even one", Göksel and Kerslake's example adds *tane* and *bile* "even": *bir tane bile ... yok* (goksel2005 §20.3).

**Do not add *-lAr* to *var*.** *\*Bahçede çocuklar varlar* is wrong: *var* never takes the "they" ending. *Bahçede çocuklar var.*

**Do not use the wrong consonant.** *\*Kitapda*, *\*sokakda* and *\*işde* ignore the voiceless rule: *kitapta*, *sokakta*, *işte*.

**Do not soften the stem.** *\*Kitabta* and *\*sokağda* are wrong: the suffix starts with a consonant, so the stem stays as it is (tur-01-06). *Kitapta*, *sokakta*.

**Do not leave out the apostrophe after a name.** *\*İstanbulda* is wrong in standard spelling: *İstanbul'da*.

**Do not use the locative for days of the week.** Göksel and Kerslake note that days take no locative (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1); you will meet them in part 5.

**Do not use the locative for "to".** *-DA* is location, not movement: "I am going to school" needs the dative, *okula* (tur-03-02). *\*Okulda gidiyorum* means nothing sensible.

## Connections

Spanish *hay* and French *il y a* are the closest match to *var*; *no hay* matches *yok*. Japanese splits location into に (existence) and で (action), and Korean into 에 and 에서; Turkish *-DA* covers both. Japanese ある/ない and Korean 있다/없다 are the closest structural match to *var*/*yok*: a positive and a negative word of existence, also used for "have". This is a shared type, not a sign of common descent.

## Exercises

1. Put into the locative: *otel*, *kütüphane*, *bardak*, *park*, *Türkiye*.
2. Say "I am at the library" and "Are you (one friend) at the hotel?"
3. Say "There are three plates on the table" and "There is no sugar in the glass."
4. Take apart *sinemadayız*: stem, suffixes, person, meaning.
5. A learner writes *\*Anahtar var çantada* for "the key is in the bag". Correct it and say why.
6. Ask "Is there any money in the bag?" and answer "No, there isn't."
7. Give two ways to say "the director is not at the hospital."
8. Why is it *dolapta* but *dolabı*?

## Worked answers

1. *Otelde*, *kütüphanede*, *bardakta*, *parkta*, *Türkiye'de*. *Otel* and *kütüphane* have front last vowels and voiced or vowel endings: *-de*. *Bardak* and *park* end in voiceless *k*, with back vowels: *-ta*. *Türkiye* is a name, so the apostrophe comes before *-de*.
2. *Kütüphanedeyim.* The locative, then *-(y)Im* with the buffer *y* after the vowel of *-de*. *Otelde misin?* *MI* follows the predicate *otelde*, harmonising with its *e*, and takes the ending *-sin* (tur-02-05).
3. *Masada üç tabak var.* The place comes first, the noun after a number stays bare (tur-02-03), *var* comes last. *Bardakta şeker yok.* Absence is *yok*, without *bir*.
4. *Sinema-da-yız*: stem *sinema* "cinema", locative *-da* after the back vowel *a*, buffer *y*, first person plural ending *-ız*. "We are at the cinema."
5. *Anahtar çantada.* The key is a known thing; the sentence says where it is, so the place is the predicate and there is no *var*. *Var* would say that some key exists there, and it would come last: *Çantada bir anahtar var* "there is a key in the bag".
6. *Çantada para var mı?* — *Hayır, yok.* (or colloquially *Yok.*) *MI* follows *var*; the answer repeats the predicate word in the negative.
7. *Müdür hastanede değil* and *Müdür hastanede yok*. Göksel and Kerslake note that with a place predicate *değil* and *yok* are interchangeable (goksel2005 §20.3).
8. The *p* of *dolap* becomes *b* only before a vowel (tur-01-06). The accusative *-(y)I* begins with a vowel: *dolabı*. The locative begins with a consonant: *dolapta*, with the voiceless *t* after *p*.
