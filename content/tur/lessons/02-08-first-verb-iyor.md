## The idea

Every sentence so far has had a noun, an adjective, a place, *var* or *yok* as its predicate, apart from the previews of the last lesson. This lesson builds the first real verb form:

*Ali geliyor.* "Ali is coming."

A Turkish verb is a **stem** followed by suffixes in a fixed order. The stem is the bare verb, *gel-* "come". The dictionary form adds the **infinitive** suffix *-mAk*, "to ...": *gelmek* "to come", *yapmak* "to do". To find the stem, take *-mAk* off: *gel-*, *yap-*, *oku-*.

The first suffix to learn is ***-(I)yor***. It presents an action as going on: *geliyor* "is coming", *okuyor* "is reading". This guide calls it the **progressive**; Göksel and Kerslake call it imperfective, because it also covers habits and lasting states, as Where it applies shows (goksel2005 §21.3.2). Turkish has no separate present-tense marker: a verb with *-(I)yor* and no past marker is, in effect, the present (goksel2005 §21.2.2).

After *-(I)yor* come the same personal endings as after a noun (tur-02-02): *geliyorum* "I am coming", *geliyorsun* "you are coming". You already know them.

**Why -(I)yor looks odd.** It breaks two rules of part 1. Its *o* never harmonises, though *o* otherwise appears only in first syllables of native words (tur-01-01). And it puts the stress before itself instead of on the last syllable: *gelíyor*. Both oddities have the same cause. The suffix was once a separate verb. Wiktionary traces it to Old Anatolian Turkish *yorı-* and Proto-Turkic \*yorï- "walk", used as an auxiliary verb; Göksel and Kerslake describe *-(I)yor* as containing an obsolete verb stem *yor-* (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3). A verb root keeps its own vowel, so *yor* stays *yor*. Only the linking *(I)* in front of it harmonises.

## How it is built

To say what someone is doing, take five steps.

1. **Find the stem.** Drop *-mAk*: *yapmak* → *yap-*, *okumak* → *oku-*, *beklemek* → *bekle-*.
2. **Add -(I)yor.**
   - After a consonant, add *-Iyor*, with *I* by four-way harmony (tur-01-05): *gel-iyor*, *yap-ıyor*, *otur-uyor*, *gör-üyor*.
   - After a high vowel (*i ı u ü*), add *-yor*: *oku-yor*, *uyu-yor*.
   - After *a* or *e*, that vowel itself becomes high and harmonises, and *-yor* follows: *anla-* → *anlıyor*, *bekle-* → *bekliyor*, *iste-* → *istiyor* (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3). This guide shows the stem without its last vowel: `anl-ıyor`, `bekl-iyor`.
   - The two one-letter stems *de-* "say" and *ye-* "eat" write the change too: *diyor*, *yiyor* (goksel2005 §2.6).
3. **Check the stem's last consonant.** *-Iyor* starts with a vowel, so the rules of tur-01-06 apply: *git-* "go" becomes *gid-*: *gidiyor*.
4. **Add the personal ending.** It harmonises with the *o* of *-yor*, so it is always the back rounded set: *-um*, *-sun*, nothing, *-uz*, *-sunuz*, *-lar*. No buffer is needed: *-yor* ends in a consonant.
5. **Put the stress** on the syllable just before *yor*, and leave it there whatever follows: *gelíyorum*, *okúyorsunuz*, *anlíyorlar* (goksel2005 §4.3.1).

A worked chain: "We are waiting." The stem is *bekle-*. It ends in *e*, so *e* becomes *i*: *bekliyor*. "We" is *-(y)Iz*, which after *-yor* is *-uz*: *bekliyoruz*, stressed *beklíyoruz*.

**"They".** The rule of tur-02-02 holds: with no subject word and people meant, *-lAr* is needed: *Geliyorlar*. With a subject word it is optional: *Çocuklar geliyor(lar)*.

**In speech** the *r* of *-yor* is often dropped before a consonant and at the end of a word: *geliyo*, *gidiyosun*. Göksel and Kerslake note that it is kept before a vowel, *seviyorum* (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3); Wiktionary also records spoken *içiyom* "I am drinking". Write *-yor* in full.

**Questions and negatives** with *-(I)yor* come in the next lesson.

## Where it applies

Göksel and Kerslake list the meanings of *-(I)yor* (goksel2005 §§21.2.2, 21.2.3, 21.3.2):

- **An action going on now**: *Ali geliyor*, *Ne yapıyorsun?*
- **A situation in progress around now**, not necessarily at this second: *Türkçe öğreniyorsunuz*, *Mehmet bir bankada çalışıyor*.
- **Habits**: *Genellikle akşam televizyon izliyoruz* "we usually watch television in the evening". The aorist (tur-04-05) also expresses habit, with a different shade.
- **States of mind and feeling**: *biliyorum* "I know", *anlıyorum* "I understand", *istiyorum* "I want", *seviyorum* "I like, love". English does not say "I am knowing"; Turkish uses *-(I)yor* freely here.
- **Physical states**: *uyuyor* "is asleep", *oturuyor* "is sitting". Göksel and Kerslake point out that with *-(I)yor* such verbs describe the state, while the past form describes entering it: *oturdu* "sat down" (goksel2005 §21.3.3).
- **Where someone lives**: *İstanbul'da yaşıyor*, or *İstanbul'da oturuyor*, with *otur-* in its sense "reside".
- **Scheduled future**: *Yarın geliyorlar*. Göksel and Kerslake note that it shows confidence that a plan will run as arranged (goksel2005 §21.2.3).
- **With question words**: *Ne yapıyorsun?*, *Ayşe ne diyor?*
- **With every person**: *-um*, *-sun*, nothing, *-uz*, *-sunuz*, *-lar*.
- **In the past**, *geliyordu* "was coming" (part 4), and with negative *-mA* and questions (tur-02-09).

## Watch out

**Do not add a word for "am".** Spanish *estoy comiendo* and English "I am eating" have a "be" verb. Turkish has only the ending: *\*Ben yiyorum varım* and *\*Ben yiyor öğrenciyim* are nonsense. *(Ben) yiyorum.*

**Do not harmonise the ending with the stem.** *\*Geliyorüm* and *\*geliyorim* take the vowel from *gel-*. The ending follows *-yor*: *geliyorum*.

**Do not keep a stem-final a or e.** *\*Bekleyor*, *\*anlayor* are wrong: *bekliyor*, *anlıyor*.

**Do not add a vowel after a high vowel.** *\*Okuuyor* or *\*okuıyor* are wrong: *okuyor*.

**Do not forget the stem changes.** *\*Gitiyor*: *gidiyor*. *\*Deyor*, *\*yeyor*: *diyor*, *yiyor*.

**Do not harmonise *-yor* itself.** *\*Geliyer* and *\*yapıyar* invent forms. *-yor* is the same after every stem.

**Do not stress the end.** *\*Geliyorúm* follows the last-syllable default, which *-(I)yor* overrides: *gelíyorum*.

**Do not use the infinitive as a finite verb.** *\*Ben okumak kitap* treats *okumak* like an English "I read". *-mAk* is only the dictionary form: *Kitap okuyorum*.

**Do not use *-(I)yor* for "I am hungry".** That is an adjective sentence, *Açım* (tur-02-02). Göksel and Kerslake note that verbs of entering a state, such as *acık-* "get hungry" and *yorul-* "get tired", use the past for the present state (goksel2005 §21.3.3): you will meet *yoruldum* "I'm tired (I have got tired)" in part 4. For now, *yorgunum*.

**Do not write the spoken forms.** *Geliyo* and *napıyosun* are what you hear; write *geliyor* and *ne yapıyorsun*.

## Connections

Japanese 〜ている and Korean -고 있다 grew from a verb plus an auxiliary meaning "be, stay", and both cover ongoing actions and lasting states, much as *-(I)yor* does with its old verb "walk". Like *-(I)yor*, ている also covers states that English expresses without a progressive. Spanish *estar* + gerund is narrower: Spanish says *sé*, not *estoy sabiendo*, where Turkish says *biliyorum*.

## Exercises

1. Give the stems of *gitmek*, *anlamak*, *uyumak*, *istemek*, *demek*.
2. Build "I am going", "you (one friend) understand" and "we want".
3. Build "They are speaking", with no subject word.
4. Take apart *dinliyorsunuz*: stem, suffixes, person, meaning, and where the stress falls.
5. Translate *Ayşe akşam evde film izliyor.*
6. A learner writes *\*Bekleyorüm* for "I am waiting". Find two mistakes.
7. Say "What are you (polite) eating?"
8. Why is the ending in *geliyorsun* *-sun* and not *-sin*, when *gel-* has a front vowel?

## Worked answers

1. *Git-* (with *gid-* before a vowel), *anla-*, *uyu-*, *iste-*, *de-*. Each is the infinitive without *-mAk*.
2. *Gidiyorum*: *git-* becomes *gid-* before the vowel, then *-iyor*, then *-um*. *Anlıyorsun*: the *a* of *anla-* becomes *ı*, then *-yor*, then *-sun*. *İstiyoruz*: the *e* of *iste-* becomes *i*, then *-yor*, then *-uz*.
3. *Konuşuyorlar.* Consonant stem with *u*: *-uyor*. With no subject word and people meant, *-lAr* is required (tur-02-02).
4. *Dinl-iyor-sunuz*: stem *dinle-* "listen", whose *e* becomes *i*; *-yor*; second person plural *-sunuz*. "You are listening" (several people, or politely). Stress on the syllable before *yor*: *dinlíyorsunuz*.
5. "Ayşe watches a film at home in the evening" or "Ayşe is watching a film at home this evening". Time, place, bare object, verb (tur-02-07); *-(I)yor* covers both the habit and the single occasion.
6. *Bekliyorum.* The *e* of *bekle-* becomes *i* before *-yor*; and the ending harmonises with *-yor*, so *-um*, not *-üm*.
7. *Ne yiyorsunuz?* *Ye-* becomes *yi-* before *-yor*; polite "you" takes *-sunuz*; *ne* stands before the verb.
8. The ending attaches to *-yor*, not to *gel-*, and harmonises with the nearest vowel, the *o* of *-yor*. So every ending after *-(I)yor* is from the back rounded set.
