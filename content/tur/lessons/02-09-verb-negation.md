## The idea

A nominal sentence is negated with a separate word, *değil* (tur-02-04). A verb is negated from the inside, with a suffix:

*Geliyor.* "He is coming." *Gelmiyor.* "He isn't coming."

The negative suffix is ***-mA***. It goes straight after the stem, before *-(I)yor*. Göksel and Kerslake call it the primary means of negating a verb (goksel2005 §20.1.1), and place it in a fixed slot: after the stem (and the voice suffixes of part 7), before every tense suffix (goksel2005 §8.2.2).

**Why inside, and why there?** The slot order is the backbone of the guide (tur-00-02). Each suffix adds one meaning to everything on its left. *Gel-me-* is "not-come", a negative action; *-(I)yor* then says that this not-coming is going on; the ending says who. Negation applies to the action itself, so it sits next to the stem, before the suffixes that place the action in time and assign it a person. You will see the same slot used with every tense in part 4: *gelmedi* "didn't come", *gelmeyecek* "won't come".

**Questions** use the same particle *mI* as nominal sentences (tur-02-05). It follows *-(I)yor*, and the personal ending moves onto it: *Geliyor musun?* "Are you coming?"

## How it is built

**The negative**, in five steps.

1. **Find the stem**: *anla-* "understand".
2. **Add -mA.** Before *-(I)yor*, its vowel becomes a high vowel and harmonises four-way with the stem: *gel-mi-*, *yap-mı-*, *otur-mu-*, *gör-mü-*, *anla-mı-* (goksel2005 §8.2.2; wiktionary). The rules of the last lesson for stem-final vowels no longer apply, because the suffix now touches *-mA*, not the stem: *anla-* stays *anla-*, *de-* and *ye-* stay *de-*, *ye-*: *demiyor*, *yemiyor*. *Git-* keeps its *t*, because a consonant follows: *gitmiyor*.
3. **Add -yor.** *-mA* now ends in a vowel, so only *-yor* is added: *anlamıyor*.
4. **Add the personal ending**, the back rounded set as before: *anlamıyorum* "I don't understand".
5. **Move the stress.** *-mA* is unstressable and pulls the stress onto the syllable before it, overriding the stress of *-(I)yor* (goksel2005 §4.3.2.1): *anlíyorum* but *anlámıyorum*; *sevíyor* but *sévmiyor*; *gelíyor* but *gélmiyor*.

This guide segments the negative as `gel-mi-yor`, `come-NEG-PROG`, with the high vowel on the negative, as Göksel and Kerslake do.

**The question**, in three steps.

1. **Build the verb up to -yor**: *geliyor*.
2. **Add *mI* as a separate word.** It harmonises with the *o* of *-yor*, so it is always *mu*: *geliyor mu*.
3. **Put the personal ending on *mu***, with the buffer *y* where needed: *geliyor muyum*, *musun*, *mu*, *muyuz*, *musunuz*. "They" is the exception, as before: *geliyorlar mı* (goksel2005 §11.1.1.5).

The stress stays on the syllable before *yor*: *gelíyor musun*. In a negative question both rules meet: *-mA* wins inside the verb, *gélmiyor musun?* "Aren't you coming?"

**Putting mI on another word.** As in nominal sentences, *mI* can follow the word you are asking about, which then stands before the verb: *Ali mi geliyor?* "Is it Ali who's coming?", *Kitap mı okuyorsun?* "Is it a book you're reading?" The person ending then stays on the verb. Göksel and Kerslake add a subtler use: *mI* before the verb can question the whole sentence when the speaker already suspects the answer from what he sees; *mI* after the verb is the neutral, out-of-the-blue question (goksel2005 §19.1.4). Seeing a book in your friend's hands, you might ask *Kitap mı okuyorsun?*; with no clue at all, *Kitap okuyor musun?*

**Sentence stress.** In a negative verbal sentence, the main stress falls on the verb itself, on the syllable before *-mA*, not on the word before the verb (goksel2005 §23.1): *Ali kitap okúmuyor*, with the main stress on *okú-*.

**Words that need a negative.** *Hiç* "at all, ever" and *kimse* "anyone, no one" only make sense with *-mA*, *değil* or *yok* in a statement; the negative meaning comes from the combination (goksel2005 §20.5): *Hiç anlamıyorum* "I don't understand at all", *Kimse gelmiyor* "no one is coming". In questions they can appear without a negative: *Kimse var mı?* "Is anyone there?". *Artık* with a negative means "no longer": *Artık sigara içmiyor* "he doesn't smoke any more".

## Where it applies

- **Every -(I)yor meaning in the negative**: now (*gelmiyor*), current situations (*çalışmıyor* "isn't working, doesn't have a job"), habits (*et yemiyoruz*), states (*bilmiyorum*, *anlamıyorum*, *tanımıyorum* "I don't know him"), plans (*Yarın gelmiyorum* "I'm not coming tomorrow").
- **Every person**: *gelmiyorum*, *gelmiyorsun*, *gelmiyor*, *gelmiyoruz*, *gelmiyorsunuz*, *gelmiyorlar*.
- **Every later tense**: the same slot after the stem (part 4).
- **Questions about the whole sentence**: *Geliyor musun?*, *Türkçe konuşuyor musunuz?*
- **Questions about one part**: *Ali mi geliyor?*, *Kitap mı okuyorsun?*, *Bugün mü geliyorsun?* "Is it today you're coming?"
- **Negative questions**: *Gelmiyor musun?* "Aren't you coming?"
- **Either-or**: *Geliyor musun, gelmiyor musun?* "Are you coming or not?" (goksel2005 §19.1.2).
- **Tags**: *Geliyorsun, değil mi?* "You're coming, aren't you?"
- **With *hiç*, *kimse*, *artık***: *Hiç anlamıyorum*, *Kimse gelmiyor*, *Artık sigara içmiyor*; and *pek* "very" with a negative: *pek anlamıyorum* "I don't really understand" (goksel2005 §20.5.4). *Asla* "never" adds determination, *Asla sigara içmiyorum* "I never smoke" (goksel2005 §20.5.4.1); *henüz* "yet" mostly appears with the past (part 4).
- **Answers**: *Evet, geliyorum.* *Hayır, gelmiyorum.*
- ***Değil* after a verb**: *Gidiyor değilim* "it's not as if I'm going". Göksel and Kerslake explain that this does not simply deny; it rejects an assumption the listener has made (goksel2005 §20.2.2). Use *-mA* for an ordinary negative.

## Watch out

**Do not negate a verb with *değil*.** *\*Geliyorum değil* or *\*Ben değil geliyorum* copy the nominal pattern or Spanish *no vengo*. The ordinary negative is inside the verb: *Gelmiyorum*.

**Do not put *-mA* after *-(I)yor*.** *\*Geliyormaz*, *\*geliyorme* break the slot order. *-mA* is next to the stem: *gel-mi-yor*.

**Do not keep the vowel *e* or *a* of *-mA*.** *\*Gelmeyor* and *\*yapmayor* are wrong before *-yor*: *gelmiyor*, *yapmıyor*.

**Do not drop the stem vowel.** The last lesson's *anl-ıyor* tempts *\*anlmıyor*. Before *-mA* the stem is whole: *anlamıyor*, *beklemiyor*.

**Do not keep the *di-* and *yi-* of *diyor*, *yiyor*.** *\*Dimiyor*, *\*yimiyor* are wrong: *demiyor*, *yemiyor*.

**Do not soften *git-*.** *\*Gidmiyor* is wrong: before a consonant the *t* stays, *gitmiyor*.

**Do not stress the negative like the positive.** *\*Gelmíyor* keeps the positive pattern. *-mA* pulls the stress before it: *gélmiyor*.

**Do not leave the ending before *mI*.** *\*Geliyorsun mu?* is wrong; the ending goes after *mu*: *Geliyor musun?* Only "they" comes first: *Geliyorlar mı?*

**Do not harmonise *mI* with the stem.** *\*Geliyor mi?* is wrong. *MI* follows *-yor*: *mu*.

**Do not use *kimse* or *hiç* in a positive statement.** *\*Kimse geliyor* does not mean "someone is coming". *Kimse* needs a negative or a question: *Kimse gelmiyor*, *Kimse geliyor mu?*

**Do not make *kimse* plural on the verb.** *\*Kimse gelmiyorlar* is wrong; Göksel and Kerslake give the third person singular (goksel2005 §20.5.3): *Kimse gelmiyor*.

**Do not double the negative to strengthen it.** Spanish *no viene nadie* and French *personne ne vient* have two negatives, and Turkish *Kimse gelmiyor* looks similar. But *-mA* is the only negative there; *kimse* adds "anyone". In a sentence, Göksel and Kerslake note, *kimse* and *hiç* get their negative meaning only from *-mA*, *değil* or *yok* (goksel2005 §20.5), so you never add a second negative to strengthen the first.

## Connections

Japanese and Korean also negate verbs from the inside or right next to them (食べない, 안 먹다 or 먹지 않다), and keep a separate negative "be" for nouns (ではない, 아니다), just as Turkish keeps *-mA* for verbs and *değil* for nouns. Spanish and French use one negative word for both: *no es*, *no viene*. The parallel is typological only.

## Exercises

1. Make negative: *Yapıyorum*, *Bekliyorsun*, *Diyor*, *Gidiyoruz*.
2. Turn into questions: *Ayşe çalışıyor*, *Biliyorsunuz*, *Konuşuyorlar*.
3. Say "Don't you remember?" to one friend.
4. Take apart *aramıyor musunuz*: every piece, its meaning, and where the stress falls.
5. Say "No one is listening" and explain why the verb is singular.
6. A learner writes *\*Ben değil anlıyorum* for "I don't understand". Correct it and give the rule.
7. Ask "Is it tea you're drinking?" (to one friend), then "Are you drinking tea?". What is the difference?
8. Translate *Maalesef Mehmet artık bankada çalışmıyor.*
9. Why is the negative of *anlıyorum* *anlamıyorum* and not *\*anlmıyorum*?

## Worked answers

1. *Yapmıyorum*, *beklemiyorsun*, *demiyor*, *gitmiyoruz*. *-mA* goes after the stem with a high vowel by harmony; *bekle-* and *de-* keep their whole stems, and *git-* keeps its *t* before a consonant.
2. *Ayşe çalışıyor mu?*, *Biliyor musunuz?*, *Konuşuyorlar mı?* *MI* is *mu* after *-yor*; the ending *-sunuz* moves onto it; "they" stays before it.
3. *Hatırlamıyor musun?* The negative inside the verb, *hatırla-mı-yor*, then *mu* with the ending *-sun*. Stress: *hatırlámıyor musun*.
4. *Ara-mı-yor mu-sunuz*: *ara-* "look for, phone", *-mı-* negative, *-yor* progressive, *mu* question, *-sunuz* second person plural. "Aren't you looking for (it)?" or "Aren't you phoning?" The stress falls before *-mA*: *arámıyor musunuz*.
5. *Kimse dinlemiyor.* *Kimse* is grammatically singular, and Göksel and Kerslake give the third person singular verb with it (goksel2005 §20.5.3). The negative meaning comes from *kimse* together with *-mA*.
6. *(Ben) anlamıyorum.* A verb is negated with *-mA* after its stem, not with *değil*, which negates nominal sentences (tur-02-04).
7. *Çay mı içiyorsun?* puts *mI* on *çay*: it asks about the drink, or, if you can see the cup, checks your guess. *Çay içiyor musun?* puts *mI* after the verb: a neutral question with no assumption (goksel2005 §19.1.4).
8. "Unfortunately Mehmet no longer works at the bank." *Artık* with the negative means "no longer"; *-(I)yor* describes his current job.
9. *-mA* attaches to the whole stem *anla-*. The stem vowel changes only when *-(I)yor* touches it directly; in the negative *-yor* touches *-mA*, whose own vowel becomes high instead.
