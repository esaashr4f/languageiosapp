## Before you start

This review covers part 2: the nominal sentence with no verb and *bir* for "a" (tur-02-01), the personal endings (tur-02-02), the plural *-lAr* and when Turkish leaves it out (tur-02-03), *değil* (tur-02-04), yes-no questions with *mI* (tur-02-05), the locative *-DA* with *var* and *yok* (tur-02-06), word order (tur-02-07), the progressive *-(I)yor* (tur-02-08), and the negative *-mA* with questions on verbs (tur-02-09). It also relies on harmony, consonant changes and stress from part 1.

Work each exercise before you read its answer. Every answer names the lesson to reread if you went wrong. Stress marks (the acute) are the guide's, not Turkish spelling.

## Passage

**A. An evening in Istanbul.** A constructed passage, using only words and grammar from parts 1 and 2.

1. Mehmet mühendis değil, öğretmen. Şimdi İstanbul'da, bir otelde.
2. Otel çok eski ama temiz ve rahat.
3. Odada bir yatak, bir masa ve iki sandalye var, ama televizyon yok.
4. Akşam Mehmet bir lokantada yemek yiyor.
5. Garson: "Türk müsünüz?" Mehmet: "Evet, Türküm, ama İstanbul'da yaşamıyorum. Ankara'da yaşıyorum."
6. Garson: "Et yiyor musunuz?" Mehmet: "Hayır, et yemiyorum. Balık var mı?"
7. Garson: "Maalesef bugün balık yok. Peynir ve ekmek var." Mehmet: "Tamam. Kahve var mı?" Garson: "Var."

**B. Two short exchanges from Wiktionary's usage examples.** Each is quoted from the usage example in the entry named (our translation follows in the answers).

1. *Evli değil misiniz?* (wiktionary, entry *değil*).
2. *Pirinç var mı? — Evet, var.* (wiktionary, entry *var*). New word: *pirinç* "rice".

## Exercises

1. Translate sentences 1 and 2 of passage A.
2. Translate sentences 3 and 4.
3. Translate sentence 5.
4. Translate sentences 6 and 7.
5. Translate the two exchanges of passage B, and take apart *değil misiniz*.
6. In sentence 1, why is there no *bir* before *öğretmen*, but a *bir* before *otelde*?
7. In sentence 1, where is the main stress of *Mehmet mühendis değil*, and why?
8. In sentence 3, why is it *iki sandalye* and not *\*iki sandalyeler*? And why does *var* come last?
9. In sentence 4, explain the order *Akşam Mehmet bir lokantada yemek yiyor*, and say which word is next to the verb and why.
10. In sentence 5, take apart *müsünüz*, *Türküm* and *yaşamıyorum*, and say where the stress falls in *yaşamıyorum*.
11. In sentence 6, compare *yiyor* and *yemiyorum*: why *yi-* in one and *ye-* in the other?
12. In sentence 7, why is the waiter's answer simply *Var*?
13. Say "We are not tired, but we are hungry."
14. Say "Are the students at school?" (no subject pronoun).
15. Say "There are no flowers in the garden, but there are three trees."
16. Say "Are you (one friend) waiting for a bus?" and "Aren't you (polite) coming?"
17. Say "Is it Ayşe who is reading the newspaper?" (a newspaper, not a specific one).
18. Correct *\*Ali var doktor* ("Ali is a doctor").
19. Correct *\*Ben yorgun değilum* ("I am not tired").
20. Correct *\*Hasta mısınızlar?* ("Are they ill?").
21. Correct *\*Çok kitaplar masada var* ("There are many books on the table").
22. Correct *\*Ayşe değil geliyor* ("Ayşe isn't coming").
23. Correct *\*Kimse geliyor* ("No one is coming").

## Worked answers

1. Sentence 1: "Mehmet isn't an engineer, he's a teacher. He's in Istanbul now, at a hotel." *Değil* denies *mühendis*, and the second predicate *öğretmen* corrects it with the same subject (tur-02-04). *İstanbul'da* and *otelde* are locative predicates with no ending for "he" (tur-02-06). Sentence 2: "The hotel is very old, but clean and comfortable." A nominal sentence with no verb (tur-02-01); *ama* joins the contrasting predicates (tur-02-04).
2. Sentences 3 and 4: "In the room there's a bed, a table and two chairs, but there's no television. In the evening Mehmet eats at a restaurant." Place first, things next, *var* or *yok* last (tur-02-06). *Yemek yiyor* is "eats a meal", a bare object with *-(I)yor*; it can mean "is eating" or describe this evening's plan (tur-02-08).
3. Sentence 5: "Waiter: 'Are you Turkish?' Mehmet: 'Yes, I'm Turkish, but I don't live in Istanbul. I live in Ankara.'" The waiter uses polite *-sInIz* to a stranger (tur-02-02, tur-02-05).
4. Sentences 6 and 7: "Waiter: 'Do you eat meat?' Mehmet: 'No, I don't eat meat. Is there any fish?' Waiter: 'Unfortunately there's no fish today. There's cheese and bread.' Mehmet: 'OK. Is there coffee?' Waiter: 'There is.'" *Et yiyor musunuz* asks about a habit (tur-02-08, tur-02-09); *var mı* asks whether something exists (tur-02-06).
5. Passage B: "Aren't you married?" and "Is there any rice? — Yes, there is." *Değil mi-siniz*: *değil* "not", then the question particle *mi*, harmonised with the *i* of *değil*, then *-siniz*, polite or plural "you". The ending moves onto *mI*, the last piece of the predicate (tur-02-04, tur-02-05).
6. A job is given with the bare noun, so *öğretmen* has no *bir* (tur-02-01). *Bir otelde* is "at a hotel": *bir* is "a", the hotel is not one the listener knows, and the locative goes on the noun after it (tur-02-06).
7. On *değil*. In a negative "x is y" sentence the denial is the news and carries the main stress (goksel2005 §23.1; tur-02-04). In the positive *Mehmet öğretmen* it would fall on *öğretmen*.
8. A number already says "more than one", so the noun stays bare (tur-02-03). *Var* is the predicate, and the predicate comes last; the things that exist stand just before it (tur-02-06, tur-02-07).
9. Time word, subject, place, bare object, verb. A time word may go before or after the subject (tur-02-07). *Yemek* is a bare object, so it stands directly before *yiyor*; nothing may separate them except a particle.
10. *mü-sünüz*: the question particle, harmonised with the *ü* of *Türk*, plus the polite "you" ending (tur-02-05). *Türk-üm*: "I am Turkish", *-(y)Im* with no buffer after a consonant, harmonised to *-üm* (tur-02-02). *Yaşa-mı-yor-um*: stem *yaşa-*, negative *-mA* with its vowel raised before *-yor*, progressive, "I" (tur-02-09). Stress: *yaşámıyorum*, before the unstressable *-mA*.
11. *ye-* becomes *yi-* only when *-(I)yor* touches it directly: *yiyor* (tur-02-08). In the negative, *-yor* touches *-mA*, so the stem stays *ye-*: *yemiyorum* (tur-02-09).
12. The answer to *var mı?* repeats the predicate: *Var* "there is", or *Yok* "there isn't". Turkish answers by repeating the predicate word, here with *evet* left out (tur-02-06).
13. *Yorgun değiliz ama açız.* The ending goes on *değil* in the first half and directly on *aç* in the second (tur-02-04, tur-02-02).
14. *Öğrenciler okulda mı?* The locative predicate *okulda*, then *mI* harmonised with its *a* (tur-02-06, tur-02-05). With the subject word said, no *-lAr* is needed on the predicate.
15. *Bahçede çiçek yok, ama üç ağaç var.* Place first; no *bir* in the plain negative; a bare noun after a number (tur-02-03, tur-02-06).
16. *Otobüs mü bekliyorsun?* or *Otobüs bekliyor musun?* The first, with *mI* on *otobüs*, asks about the thing waited for or checks a guess; the second is neutral (tur-02-09). *Gelmiyor musunuz?*: the negative inside the verb, then *mu* with the polite ending; stress *gélmiyor musunuz* (tur-02-09).
17. *Ayşe mi gazete okuyor?* *MI* follows the questioned subject; the bare object *gazete* keeps its place next to the verb (tur-02-05, tur-02-07, tur-02-09).
18. *Ali doktor.* *Var* means "there is", not "is"; a nominal sentence has no word for "is" (tur-02-01, tur-02-06).
19. *Ben yorgun değilim.* The ending sits on *değil* and harmonises with its *i*, not with *yorgun* (tur-02-04).
20. *Hastalar mı?* "They" is the one ending that comes before *mI*, not after it (tur-02-05).
21. *Masada çok kitap var.* After *çok* the noun stays bare (tur-02-03), and the place comes first in an existential sentence (tur-02-06).
22. *Ayşe gelmiyor.* Verbs are negated with *-mA* inside the verb, not with *değil* (tur-02-09).
23. *Kimse gelmiyor.* *Kimse* means "no one" only together with a negative; in a statement it needs *-mA*, *değil* or *yok* (tur-02-09).
