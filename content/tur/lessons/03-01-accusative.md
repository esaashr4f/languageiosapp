## The idea

So far every object in this guide has been bare: *kitap okuyorum* "I'm reading (a book, books)". A bare object names only a *kind* of thing, not any particular one.

When you mean a particular book, one you and the listener both have in mind, Turkish adds a case suffix: *kitabı okuyorum* "I'm reading the book". This suffix is the **accusative**, *-(y)I*. It marks the direct object of a verb, and it is obligatory when that object is definite (goksel2005 §14.3.3.2). A **direct object** is the thing or person a verb acts on directly, with no "to", "from" or "at" in between. **Definite** means the speaker assumes the listener can tell exactly which one is meant.

**Why mark only some objects?** Turkish has no word for "the" (tur-02-01). For objects, the accusative does that job: it says "this noun is the object" and "you know which one". A bare object is welded to the verb as part of the activity, and its identity does not matter. *Kitap okuyorum* describes what you are doing (book-reading). *Kitabı okuyorum* is about a book.

The subject never takes the accusative: the subject of a main clause is bare (goksel2005 §14.3.3.1).

**Specific, not only definite.** An object can also be new to the listener while the speaker has a particular one in mind. *Bir öğrenci arıyorum* "I'm looking for a student" means any student will do. *Bir öğrenciyi arıyorum* means "I'm looking for a (particular) student", one the speaker could name (goksel2005 §22.2.1). **Specific** means the speaker has one particular thing in mind, whether or not the listener knows it. So *bir* "a" and the accusative can stand together; they answer different questions.

## How it is built

**The suffix -(y)I.** It has a four-way vowel (tur-01-05) and a buffer *y* after a vowel (tur-01-06).

1. **Does the noun end in a vowel?** Then put *y* first: *araba-yı*, *kapı-yı*, *su-yu*, *kahve-yi*. After a consonant, no buffer: *ev-i*, *okul-u*.
2. **Choose the vowel by the last vowel of the noun**: *i* after e, i; *ı* after a, ı; *u* after o, u; *ü* after ö, ü. *Ev-i*, *kız-ı*, *yol-u*, *göz-ü*.
3. **Check the stem.** The suffix starts with a vowel, so the changes of tur-01-06 apply: *kitab-ı*, *çocuğ-u*, *reng-i*, but *saat-i*.
4. **Proper names** take the apostrophe of tur-01-08: *Ali'yi*, *Ayşe'yi*, *İstanbul'u*, *Ankara'yı*. Stress stays where the name has it: *Ánkara'yı*.

The plural comes before the case, as with the locative: *kitap-lar-ı* "the books".

**Two new stem changes.** They appear before any suffix that starts with a vowel, and the accusative is the first such suffix you will use on almost every noun.

- **The last vowel drops.** In some nouns the high vowel of the last syllable disappears before a vowel: *isim* "name", *ismi*; *şehir* "city", *şehri*; *nehir* "river", *nehri*; *oğul* "son", *oğlu*; *ağız* "mouth", *ağzı*; *burun* "nose", *burnu*. The reason, for the Arabic words: Arabic *ism* has no vowel between *s* and *m*, so Turkish inserts one only when the word stands bare, and drops it when a suffix vowel can carry the consonants instead (goksel2005 §2.3). A few native words (*oğul*, *ağız*, *burun*) behave the same way. Before a consonant the vowel stays: *şehirde*, *isimler*.
- **The last consonant doubles.** In some Arabic loans the last consonant doubles before a vowel: *hak* "right", *hakkı*; *sır* "secret", *sırrı*. Arabic has the double consonant in these roots (*ḥaqq*, *sirr*, tdk); Turkish cannot end a word in a double consonant, so it shows only when a vowel follows (goksel2005 §2.2). Before a consonant it is single: *haklar*.

Neither change can be predicted from the bare form; the words are learnt one by one (goksel2005 §§2.2, 2.3). *Kadın* looks like *burun* but keeps its vowel: *kadını*. The vocabulary notes always give the accusative.

**The chain of thought.** "I want to say: I'm washing the shirt."

1. The verb is *yıkamak*, stem *yıka-*: *yıkıyorum* (tur-02-08).
2. Is the shirt a particular one we both know? Yes: accusative.
3. *Gömlek* ends in a consonant: no buffer. Last vowel *e*: *-i*. The *k* comes between vowels: *ğ*. *Gömleği.*
4. Object before the verb: *Gömleği yıkıyorum.*

**Word order.** A bare object must stand directly before the verb. An object placed further left normally takes the accusative even if it is not definite (goksel2005 §14.3.3.2: "usually necessary"): *Ali kitap okuyor*, but *Kitabı Ali okuyor* "It's Ali who's reading the book".

**Stress.** *-(y)I* takes the stress like most suffixes: *kitabí*, *arabayí* (tur-01-07).

## Where it applies

- **An object the listener can identify**, from the situation or from earlier talk: *Kitabı okuyorum*, *Haberi duyuyor musunuz?*
- **Proper names**, always: *Ayşe'yi tanıyor musun?*, *İstanbul'u seviyorum* (goksel2005 §22.1).
- **After *bu*** and the other pointing words of tur-03-11: *bu filmi*, *bu şehri*. They make the noun definite.
- **Pronouns as objects**: *beni* "me", *seni* "you", shown as previews in part 2; the full set is in tur-03-10.
- ***Herkes*** "everyone" and ***her şey*** "everything", which count as definite (goksel2005 §22.1): *Herkesi çağırıyoruz*, *Her şeyi biliyor* "he knows everything".
- ***Kimse*** "anyone, no one" with a negative, and the question word *kim* "who" (tur-03-12): these are not definite, but they always take it as objects (goksel2005 §22.1): *Kimseyi tanımıyorum* "I don't know anyone".
- **A specific indefinite**, with *bir*: *Bir öğrenciyi arıyorum* (goksel2005 §22.2.1).
- **One of a group already mentioned**: if you have been talking about some books, *iki kitabı* is "two of the books" (goksel2005 §14.3.3.2).
- **Any object not right before the verb**, whatever its meaning, as a rule: *Gazeteyi Mehmet okuyor* (goksel2005 §14.3.3.2).
- **A plural object meaning a whole class**: *Kedileri sevmiyorum*, *Doktorları sevmiyor* (goksel2005 §22.4.3). A singular bare object can say the same: *Kedi sevmiyorum*.
- **Something treated as an institution**: *Okulu sevmiyor* "he doesn't like school" (goksel2005 §22.4.3).
- **An object with a possessive suffix**, always: *kitabımı* "my book" (tur-03-04).

The bare object is used:

- **When only the kind matters**: *Mehmet bilgisayar satıyor*, *Çay içiyoruz*.
- **In fixed verb phrases**: *yemek yemek* "eat, have a meal", *sigara içmek* "smoke", *ders çalışmak* "study" (goksel2005 §22.3).
- **In negatives and questions about "any" at all**, where no particular thing is meant (goksel2005 §22.3): *Gazete okumuyor* "he doesn't read newspapers", *Fotoğraf istiyor musun?* "do you want a photo (any photo)?".

## Watch out

**Do not leave a known object bare.** *\*Kapı açıyorum* for "I'm opening the door" makes it an activity, "I do door-opening". *Kapıyı açıyorum.* English, Spanish and French mark "the" before the noun, so learners forget to mark it after.

**Do not put the accusative on a subject.** *\*Ali'yi geliyor* is wrong: it marks a known *object*, not any known thing. *Ali geliyor.*

**Do not use it with verbs that take another case.** *\*Ali'yi bakıyorum* is wrong: *bakmak* "look at" and *inanmak* "believe" take the dative, *Ali'ye bakıyorum* (tur-03-02).

**Do not think *bir* rules out the accusative.** "A" and "the" are opposites; *bir* and *-(y)I* are not. *Bir öğrenciyi arıyorum* is correct (goksel2005 §22.2.1).

**Do not separate a bare object from its verb.** *\*Kitap Ali okuyor* is wrong. *Ali kitap okuyor*, or *Kitabı Ali okuyor.*

**Do not forget it with *herkes* and *kimse*.** *\*Herkes çağırıyoruz* and *\*Kimse görmüyorum* are wrong: *Herkesi çağırıyoruz*, *Kimseyi görmüyorum.*

**Do not keep a dropping vowel.** *\*Şehiri*, *\*isimi*, *\*oğulu*, *\*ağızı* are wrong: *şehri*, *ismi*, *oğlu*, *ağzı*. And do not drop it before a consonant: *\*şehrde* is wrong; *şehirde*.

**Do not drop a vowel by analogy.** *Kadın*, *insan*, *otobüs* have nothing to lose: *kadını*, *insanı*, *otobüsü*, not *\*kadnı*. Only listed words lose their vowel (goksel2005 §2.3).

**Do not forget the doubling.** *\*Hakı*, *\*sırı* are wrong: *hakkı*, *sırrı*. (*Sırı* is the accusative of another word, *sır* "glaze"; tdk.)

**Do not confuse -(y)I with the "his, her" suffix.** *Arabayı* "the car (object)" is not *arabası* "his car" (tur-03-04). After a consonant both are *evi*; the sentence decides.

## Connections

Japanese を and Korean 을/를 mark nearly every object, known or not; Turkish *-(y)I* marks only the specific ones, so it does part of the work of "the" as well. Spanish comes closer in one corner: it puts *a* before a specific person as object (*busco a un estudiante* "I'm looking for a particular student", *busco un estudiante* "any student"), which matches *bir öğrenciyi arıyorum* against *bir öğrenci arıyorum*. Turkish applies the same idea to things as well as people.

## Exercises

1. Put into the accusative: *ev*, *araba*, *kitap*, *göz*, *su*, *ayakkabı*.
2. Put into the accusative, watching the stem: *isim*, *nehir*, *ağız*, *hak*, *kadın*, *saat*.
3. Translate both and explain the difference: *Ali gazete okuyor* and *Ali gazeteyi okuyor*.
4. Say "We're opening the window" and "We're opening windows (as a job)".
5. Say "Do you know Mehmet?" (to one friend).
6. Say "I'm looking for a (particular) photograph", then "I'm looking for a photograph (any will do)".
7. A learner writes *\*Bu kazak beğeniyorum* for "I like this jumper". Correct it and say why.
8. Take apart *oğlu* as an object. Where has the *u* of *oğul* gone, and which *u* is left?
9. Correct *\*Herkes tanıyorum* ("I know everyone") and *\*Şehiri görüyor musun?* ("Do you see the city?").
10. Why must it be *Bilgisayarı Ayşe kullanıyor* and not *\*Bilgisayar Ayşe kullanıyor* for "It's Ayşe who uses the computer"?

## Worked answers

1. *Evi*, *arabayı*, *kitabı*, *gözü*, *suyu*, *ayakkabıyı*. After a consonant no buffer: *ev-i*, *göz-ü*. After a vowel the buffer *y*: *araba-yı*, *su-yu*, *ayakkabı-yı*. *Kitap* ends in *p*, which becomes *b* between vowels (tur-01-06).
2. *İsmi*, *nehri*, *ağzı*, *hakkı*, *kadını*, *saati*. The first three lose their last high vowel; *hak* doubles its *k*; *kadın* loses nothing; *saat* keeps its *t* and takes a front vowel (tur-01-08).
3. "Ali is reading a newspaper (newspapers)" and "Ali is reading the newspaper". The bare object names the activity; the accusative points at one known paper.
4. *Pencereyi açıyoruz.* — *Pencere açıyoruz.* After a vowel, *-yi*. The bare object names the activity.
5. *Mehmet'i tanıyor musun?* A name as object is always in the accusative, after the apostrophe (tur-02-09 for *musun*).
6. *Bir fotoğrafı arıyorum.* — *Bir fotoğraf arıyorum.* *Bir* with the accusative is a specific photograph the speaker has in mind; without it, any photograph that fits.
7. *Bu kazağı beğeniyorum.* *Bu* makes the object definite; *k* becomes *ğ* between vowels.
8. *Oğl-u*: the stem *oğul* loses the *u* of its last syllable before a suffix vowel, and the *u* you see is the accusative's own vowel, chosen by four-way harmony from the *o* of *oğl-*. "The son" as object. (The same form will also mean "his son" in tur-03-04.)
9. *Herkesi tanıyorum.* *Herkes* counts as definite and takes the accusative as an object. *Şehri görüyor musun?* *Şehir* loses its second *i* before a vowel.
10. *Ayşe* is in the focus slot, so the object is not next to the verb, and an object away from the verb normally takes the accusative (goksel2005 §14.3.3.2).
