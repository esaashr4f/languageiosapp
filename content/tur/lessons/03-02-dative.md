## The idea

The locative *-DA* says where something *is* (tur-02-06). The **dative**, *-(y)A*, says where something is *going*: *okulda* "at school", but *okula* "to school". Göksel and Kerslake list its core meanings as the destination of a movement and the recipient of an action (goksel2005 §14.3.3.3). One suffix covers English "to", "towards", "into" and "onto".

**Why one case for places and people?** In Turkish, giving is a kind of sending. A book that goes to Ayşe is moving towards her, as a bus goes towards Ankara, so the same case marks both: *Ankara'ya gidiyorum* "I'm going to Ankara", *Ayşe'ye bir kitap veriyorum* "I'm giving Ayşe a book". Spanish and French do the same with *a* and *à* (*voy a Madrid*, *le doy un libro a Ana*). The dative is the "goal" case.

**The dative as a verb's partner.** Many verbs that take a direct object in English take the dative in Turkish, because Turkish sees their partner as a goal the action is aimed at, not as a thing acted on. You have already met two: *bakmak* "look" and *inanmak* "believe" were introduced without an object (tur-02-08, tur-02-09) because their partner is in the dative: *Ali'ye bakıyorum* "I'm looking at Ali", *Ayşe'ye inanıyorum* "I believe Ayşe". Göksel and Kerslake give a list of such verbs, many of them verbs of feeling (goksel2005 §14.3.3.3). There is no rule that predicts them, so each one's vocabulary note says "takes the dative".

## How it is built

**The suffix -(y)A.** Two-way harmony (tur-01-04) and a buffer *y* after a vowel (tur-01-06).

1. **Vowel at the end?** Add *y*: *araba-ya*, *oda-ya*, *kahve-ye*, *su-ya*. After a consonant, none: *ev-e*, *okul-a*.
2. **Front or back?** *e* after e, i, ö, ü; *a* after a, ı, o, u: *ev-e*, *göl-e*, *okul-a*, *yol-a*.
3. **Stem changes**, because the suffix starts with a vowel: *kitab-a*, *sokağ-a*, *çocuğ-a*, *ağac-a*; *hakk-a*; *ağz-a*, *şehr-e*. For words that lose a vowel, Göksel and Kerslake note that some speakers keep it before *-(y)A*: *şehre* or *şehire* (goksel2005 §2.3). This guide writes *şehre*, as Wiktionary's table does.
4. **Names** take the apostrophe: *Ankara'ya*, *İstanbul'a*, *Ali'ye*, *Mehmet'e*.

**Verbs with two partners.** *Vermek* "give", *göndermek* "send", *göstermek* "show", *söylemek* "say", *anlatmak* "tell", *sormak* "ask" take a thing and a person. The thing follows tur-03-01: bare if only the kind matters, accusative if it is a particular one. The person is in the dative.

The chain of thought, for "I'm giving the key to Mehmet":

1. The verb: *vermek*, stem *ver-*: *veriyorum*.
2. Who receives? Mehmet: dative, after the apostrophe: *Mehmet'e*.
3. What is given? The key, a known one: *anahtar* + *-ı*: *anahtarı*.
4. Order: in a neutral sentence the definite object may come first or second; the new information goes next to the verb (tur-02-07). *Anahtarı Mehmet'e veriyorum* answers "who are you giving the key to?"; *Mehmet'e anahtarı veriyorum* answers "what are you giving Mehmet?".

If the thing is bare, it must stand next to the verb, as always: *Mehmet'e anahtar veriyorum* "I'm giving Mehmet a key".

**Motion and position.** The verb decides which case a place takes. Verbs of motion take the dative of the goal: *gitmek*, *gelmek*, *dönmek* "return", *girmek* "enter", *binmek* "get on". Verbs of putting take the dative of the place something ends up: *koymak* "put". Verbs of being and doing somewhere take the locative.

**Stress.** *-(y)A* takes the stress: *okulá*, *evé* (tur-01-07). Names keep their own: *Ánkara'ya*.

## Where it applies

- **The goal of a movement**: *okula*, *eve*, *Ankara'ya gitmek*; *odaya girmek* "enter the room", *otobüse binmek* "get on the bus" (goksel2005 §14.3.3.3).
- **Where something is put or sent**: *masaya koymak*, *Paris'e göndermek* "send to Paris". English "on, in, into" with a verb of putting are all dative.
- **Sitting down on something**: *oturmak* with the dative is "sit down on", with the locative "be sitting, live" (wiktionary): *sandalyeye oturuyor* "she's sitting down on the chair", *sandalyede oturuyor* "she's sitting on the chair".
- **The receiver**: *Ayşe'ye vermek*, *müdüre göstermek*, *Ali'ye söylemek*, *Mehmet'e sormak*, *öğrencilere anlatmak*.
- **The person something is done for**, in some verbs: *bakmak* with a person can mean "look after" (*çocuğa bakmak*, goksel2005 §14.3.3.3).
- **Price**: *elli liraya almak*, *satmak*.
- **Verbs whose partner is a goal**: *bakmak* "look at", *inanmak* "believe", *güvenmek* "trust", *benzemek* "resemble", *başlamak* "begin" (*derse başlamak* "begin the lesson"), *kızmak* "be angry with", *sevinmek* "be glad about" (goksel2005 §14.3.3.3; wiktionary).
- **After some adjectives**: *yakın* "near" and *uygun* "suitable" take the dative of what something is near or suitable for: *okula yakın bir ev* "a house near the school" (goksel2005 §14.3.3.3).
- **After some postpositions**: *doğru* "towards", *göre* "according to", *kadar* "until, as far as" (tur-05-06).
- **Purpose, with a verb**: *görmeye geliyorum* "I'm coming to see" (part 6).
- **The person made to do something**, in causatives (tur-07-02).
- **Pronouns**: *bana* "to me" and *sana* "to you" are irregular (tur-03-10).

## Watch out

**Do not use the locative for "to".** *\*Okulda gidiyorum* says where you are going *at*, which is not a place to go. *Okula gidiyorum.* The reverse is also wrong: *\*Ankara'ya yaşıyorum* for "I live in Ankara" needs the locative, *Ankara'da yaşıyorum*.

**Do not put "look at" and "believe" in the accusative.** English, Spanish and French treat them as direct objects (*mirar algo*, *croire quelqu'un*). *\*Ali'yi bakıyorum*, *\*Ayşe'yi inanıyorum* are wrong: *Ali'ye bakıyorum*, *Ayşe'ye inanıyorum*. The same goes for *güvenmek*, *benzemek*, *binmek*, *girmek*, *başlamak*.

**Do not drop the dative on "home".** English "go home" has no preposition; Turkish needs the case: *\*Ev gidiyorum* is wrong; *Eve gidiyorum.*

**Do not think "on" is always the locative.** *Kitap masada* "the book is on the table" is position; *Kitabı masaya koyuyorum* "I'm putting it on the table" is movement onto it. With *koymak* the place is dative: *\*Kitabı masada koyuyorum* is wrong in this meaning.

**Do not put the dative on the thing given.** *\*Kitaba Ali'yi veriyorum* reverses the roles. The receiver is dative, the thing bare or accusative: *Kitabı Ali'ye veriyorum.*

**Do not separate a bare object from the verb with the dative.** *\*Hediye Ayşe'ye veriyorum* is wrong: a bare object touches the verb. *Ayşe'ye hediye veriyorum*, or with a known present *Hediyeyi Ayşe'ye veriyorum.*

**Do not put the question itself in the dative.** You ask *someone* (dative) *something* (object): *Mehmet'e bir soru soruyorum* "I'm asking Mehmet a question". The question itself is never dative.

**Do not forget the buffer or the stem change.** *\*Arabaa*, *\*kitapa*, *\*sokaka* are wrong: *arabaya*, *kitaba*, *sokağa*.

**Do not harmonise *-(y)A* four ways.** It has only *e* and *a*: *\*okulu* is the accusative, not the dative. *Okula* "to school", *okulu* "the school (object)".

## Connections

Spanish *a* and French *à* cover both the goal and the receiver, like *-(y)A* (*voy a casa*, *se lo doy a Ana*). Japanese splits them less neatly: に marks both a destination and a receiver, and へ a direction; Korean uses 에 for places and 에게 for people. Japanese also puts the partner of some verbs in に where English has an object (*彼に会う* "meet him"), which is the same kind of surprise as Turkish *Ali'ye bakmak*. These are parallels in type, not signs of a shared origin.

## Exercises

1. Put into the dative: *göl*, *oda*, *kapı*, *ağaç*, *nehir*, *İstanbul*.
2. Say "We're going to the library" and "We're at the library".
3. Say "I'm giving the photograph to Ayşe" with the photograph known.
4. Say "She's sending her neighbour a letter" (any letter; no "her").
5. Correct *\*Öğrenciler öğretmeni bakıyor* ("The students are looking at the teacher").
6. Translate *Mehmet'e güvenmiyor musun?*
7. Why is it *masaya koyuyorum* but *masada yazıyorum*?
8. Say "The child looks like Ali" and take apart your verb.
9. A learner writes *\*Hediye Ayşe'ye veriyorum* for "I'm giving Ayşe a present". Correct it in two ways.
10. Say "a city near the sea".

## Worked answers

1. *Göle*, *odaya*, *kapıya*, *ağaca*, *nehre*, *İstanbul'a*. *Göl* is front: *-e*. *Oda*, *kapı* end in a vowel: *-ya*. *Ağaç* turns *ç* into *c* before a vowel (tur-01-06). *Nehir* loses its second *i* (tur-03-01). A name takes the apostrophe.
2. *Kütüphaneye gidiyoruz.* — *Kütüphanedeyiz.* Movement towards takes the dative, position the locative with the personal ending on it (tur-02-06).
3. *Fotoğrafı Ayşe'ye veriyorum.* The known photograph is accusative (tur-03-01), the receiver dative.
4. *Komşuya bir mektup gönderiyor.* The receiver is dative; the letter is new, so it is bare after *bir*, next to the verb.
5. *Öğrenciler öğretmene bakıyor.* *Bakmak* takes the dative; the accusative *öğretmeni* would need a verb that takes a direct object, like *görmek*: *öğretmeni görüyorlar*.
6. "Don't you trust Mehmet?" *Güvenmek* takes the dative; *-mA* is inside the verb and *mu-sun* asks the question (tur-02-09).
7. *Koymak* is a verb of putting: the table is where the book ends up, so dative. *Yazmak* happens at a place, so locative.
8. *Çocuk Ali'ye benziyor.* *Benz-iyor*: the stem *benze-* loses its final *e* before *-(I)yor* (tur-02-08), and *benzemek* takes the dative.
9. *Ayşe'ye hediye veriyorum* (a present, no particular one, next to the verb) or *Hediyeyi Ayşe'ye veriyorum* (a known present in the accusative, free to move).
10. *Denize yakın bir şehir.* *Yakın* takes the dative of what something is near, and comes after it, like an adjective before its noun (tur-02-01).
