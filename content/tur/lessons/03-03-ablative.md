## The idea

You now have a case for where something is (*-DA*) and a case for where it goes (*-(y)A*). The third place case says where it *comes from*: the **ablative**, *-DAn*. *Okulda* "at school", *okula* "to school", *okuldan* "from school". Göksel and Kerslake group its meanings around departure, separation, source and cause (goksel2005 §14.3.3.5).

The three place cases form a set. In shape the ablative looks like the locative with *n* added. Göksel and Kerslake list it as one suffix, and so does this guide; the likeness is a memory aid, not a claim about its history.

**One idea, many English words.** "Starting point" is the core, and the other uses grow from it:

- the place something leaves: *evden çıkmak* "leave the house";
- the person something comes from: *Ali'den bir mektup* "a letter from Ali";
- the way through which something moves, with *geçmek* "pass": *köprüden geçmek* "cross by the bridge";
- the cause an action starts from: *korkudan* "out of fear";
- the thing a feeling starts from: *köpekten korkmak* "be afraid of the dog";
- the whole that a part is taken from: *bu elmalardan iki tane* "two of these apples";
- the material a thing is made from: *tahtadan bir masa* "a table of wood".

English uses "from", "of", "out of", "off", "through", "than" and plain direct objects for these. Turkish uses one case, because they share the same picture: something moving away from a point.

## How it is built

**The suffix -DAn.** It behaves exactly like the locative, with *n* added (tur-02-06).

1. **D: voiceless last sound** (p, ç, t, k, f, h, s, ş)? Then *t*, otherwise *d*: *kitap-tan*, *sokak-tan*, *iş-ten*, but *ev-den*, *okul-dan*, *araba-dan*.
2. **A: front or back?** *-den* after e, i, ö, ü; *-dan* after a, ı, o, u.
3. **No buffer and no stem change**, because the suffix starts with a consonant: *kitaptan*, not *\*kitabdan*; *şehirden*, not *\*şehrden*; *haktan*.
4. **Names**: *İstanbul'dan*, *Türkiye'den*, *Ali'den*.

So there are four shapes: *-den*, *-dan*, *-ten*, *-tan*. The plural comes first: *ev-ler-den*.

**The ablative as predicate.** Like the locative, an ablative phrase can be the predicate of a nominal sentence and take personal endings: *Ben İstanbul'danım* "I'm from Istanbul", *Ayşe Ankara'dan* "Ayşe is from Ankara", *Japonya'dan mısınız?* "Are you from Japan?"

**From ... to.** Starting point and goal sit side by side, starting point first: *Ankara'dan İstanbul'a* "from Ankara to Istanbul", *evden okula* "from home to school".

The chain of thought, for "I'm getting off the bus":

1. The verb is *inmek* "get off, go down", stem *in-*: *iniyorum*.
2. What does the action move away from? The bus: ablative.
3. *Otobüs* ends in *s*, voiceless: *t*. Last vowel *ü*, front: *-ten*. *Otobüsten.*
4. *Otobüsten iniyorum.*

Compare its opposite from tur-03-02: *otobüse biniyorum* "I'm getting on the bus". Each verb of motion takes the case of its direction.

**Stress.** *-DAn* takes the stress like the other cases: *evdén*, *okuldán*.

## Where it applies

- **Movement away from a place**: *okuldan gelmek*, *evden çıkmak*, *otobüsten inmek*, *ağaçtan düşmek* "fall from a tree", *ormandan kaçmak* "run away from the forest".
- **Where someone is from**, as a predicate: *İstanbul'danım*, *Fransa'dan mısınız?*
- **From ... to**: *Ankara'dan İstanbul'a*, *evden okula*.
- **Leaving and separating**: *Ali işten ayrılıyor* "Ali is leaving his job" (*ayrılmak*).
- **The source of a thing or of information**: *Ali'den mektup almak*, *Ayşe'den duymak*, *öğretmenden öğrenmek* "learn from the teacher".
- **The way through or over**, with *geçmek* and other verbs of motion: *köprüden geçmek*, *parktan geçmek*, *kapıdan girmek* "come in by the door", *pencereden bakmak* (goksel2005 §14.3.3.5).
- **Cause**: *korkudan*, *sıcaktan* "because of the heat".
- **Verbs of feeling, especially of dislike or fear**: *korkmak* "be afraid of", *hoşlanmak* "like, enjoy", *bıkmak* "get fed up with" (goksel2005 §14.3.3.5): *Ayşe kahveden hoşlanmıyor*, *Bu işten bıkıyorum*.
- **Adjectives**: *memnun* "pleased with" (*işten memnun* "pleased with the work", goksel2005 §15.5) and *uzak* "far from" (*okuldan uzak*; tdk *işlerden uzak*).
- **Part of a whole**: *bu elmalardan iki tane*, *peynirden bir kilo* "a kilo of the cheese" (goksel2005 §14.5).
- **Material**: *tahtadan bir masa*.
- **Comparison**: *Ali Ayşe'den uzun* "Ali is taller than Ayşe", even without the word for "more" (goksel2005 §14.3.3.5; tur-05-02).
- **After some postpositions**: *önce* "before", *sonra* "after": *dersten sonra* "after the lesson" (tur-05-06).
- **Time words that stress earliness**: *şimdiden* "already, as early as now", *sabahtan* "since the morning" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1).
- **Fixed words**: *gerçekten* "really" (tur-02-05) is *gerçek* "real" plus the ablative; *neden* "why" is *ne* "what" plus the ablative (tur-03-12).

## Watch out

**Do not put the place you leave in the accusative.** English "leave the house", "exit the room", "get off the bus" look like direct objects. *\*Evi çıkıyorum* and *\*Otobüsü iniyoruz* are wrong: *Evden çıkıyorum*, *Otobüsten iniyoruz*.

**Do not put what you fear or enjoy in the accusative.** *\*Köpeği korkuyorum* and *\*Kahveyi hoşlanıyorum* are wrong: *Köpekten korkuyorum*, *Kahveden hoşlanıyorum*. *Sevmek* "love, like" does take the accusative (*Kahveyi seviyorum*); *hoşlanmak* says much the same with the ablative. Each verb's case is in its vocabulary note.

**Do not use the locative for "from".** *\*İstanbul'da geliyorum* means nothing sensible. *İstanbul'dan geliyorum.* Listen for the final *n*: *evde* is "at home", *evden* is "from home".

**Do not mix up "buy from" and "buy for".** *Ali'den kitap alıyorum* is "I'm buying a book from Ali". *Ali'ye kitap alıyorum* is "I'm buying a book for Ali": the dative marks the person who receives it (goksel2005 §22.2.1).

**Do not use the ablative for every English "from".** Falling is *from* a place but *to* the ground: *ağaçtan düşüyor* "he's falling from the tree", but *yere düşüyor* "he's falling to the ground", where English says "on the floor".

**Do not change the stem.** *\*Kitabtan*, *\*sokağdan*, *\*şehrden* are wrong. A consonant-initial suffix leaves the stem as it is: *kitaptan*, *sokaktan*, *şehirden*.

**Do not ignore the voiceless rule.** *\*Kitapdan*, *\*işden* are wrong: *kitaptan*, *işten*. The *D* of *-DAn* follows the same rule as the *D* of *-DA*.

**Do not think "through" always needs the ablative.** *Geçmek* can also take a direct object: *köprüyü geçmek* "cross the bridge" is correct too (wiktionary). The ablative stresses the route, the accusative the thing crossed.

**Do not mark the part in a partitive.** *\*Bu elmalardan iki elmayı* repeats the noun and marks it. The whole is ablative, the part stays bare: *bu elmalardan iki tane*.

## Connections

Japanese から and Korean 에서/에게서 mark the starting point in the same way, and Japanese uses から also for cause and for material in some phrases (木から作る "make from wood"). Spanish and French *de* cover the source and the material (*una mesa de madera*, *une table en bois*) but not fear or comparison. Korean 보다 and Japanese より mark the standard of comparison with a separate particle; Turkish reuses its ablative, a pattern found in many languages.

## Exercises

1. Put into the ablative: *orman*, *köprü*, *sokak*, *ağaç*, *Almanya*, *nehir*.
2. Say "We're coming from the market" and "We're going to the market".
3. Say "Are you (polite) from France?"
4. Translate *Mehmet uçaktan iniyor ve otobüse biniyor.*
5. Correct *\*Odayı çıkıyor* ("He's leaving the room").
6. Say "Ayşe isn't afraid of dogs".
7. What is the difference between *Ali'ye bir hediye alıyorum* and *Ali'den bir hediye alıyorum*?
8. Take apart *kütüphanedeniz*.
9. Say "I'd like a kilo of this cheese."
10. Why is it *kitaptan* but *kitabı*?

## Worked answers

1. *Ormandan*, *köprüden*, *sokaktan*, *ağaçtan*, *Almanya'dan*, *nehirden*. Voiceless *k* and *ç* give *t*; nothing else changes the *d*. Because the suffix starts with a consonant, *ağaç* keeps its *ç* and *nehir* keeps its *i*.
2. *Pazardan geliyoruz.* — *Pazara gidiyoruz.* Starting point in the ablative, goal in the dative (tur-03-02).
3. *Fransa'dan mısınız?* The ablative phrase is the predicate; *mI* follows it and takes the polite ending (tur-02-05).
4. "Mehmet is getting off the plane and onto the bus." *İnmek* takes the ablative of what is left, *binmek* the dative of what is boarded.
5. *Odadan çıkıyor.* The place left is in the ablative, not the accusative.
6. *Ayşe köpeklerden korkmuyor.* *Korkmak* takes the ablative; dogs in general, plural. (*Köpekten korkmuyor* also works as a generic singular.)
7. "I'm buying a present for Ali" and "I'm getting (or buying) a present from Ali". The dative marks the receiver, the goal; the ablative the source (tur-03-02).
8. *Kütüphane-den-iz*: "library", the ablative *-den* after the front vowel *e*, the "we" ending *-(y)Iz* with no buffer after *n*. "We're from the library" (for example, staff introducing themselves).
9. *Bu peynirden bir kilo istiyorum.* The whole is ablative, the amount bare (goksel2005 §14.5).
10. *-(y)I* starts with a vowel, which turns *p* into *b* (tur-01-06). *-DAn* starts with a consonant, so the *p* stays, and its own *d* becomes *t* after it.
