## The idea

English puts "my" in front of the noun. Turkish puts it at the end, as a suffix: *ev* "house", *evim* "my house", *evin* "your house", *evi* "his, her or its house". A **possessive suffix** is a suffix on the thing owned that says who owns it. You saw one as a preview in *annem* "my mother" (tur-02-06).

**Why a suffix, and why there?** Turkish adds meaning from left to right, each suffix applying to everything before it (tur-00-02). A noun's suffixes come in a fixed order: plural, then possessive, then case (goksel2005 §8.1). *Ev-ler-im-de* is "house - more than one - mine - at": "in my houses". The possessive sits between number and case because it narrows down which houses are meant before the case says what role they play in the sentence.

**The owner can stay unsaid.** The suffix already tells you the person, so a separate word for "my" is not needed, just as *geliyorum* needs no *ben* (tur-02-08). *Evim büyük* is a full sentence: "my house is big". A separate owner word, *benim* "my", exists, and you will meet it with the genitive (tur-03-05); it adds emphasis or clarity.

**Possessed means definite.** A noun with a possessive suffix refers to something identifiable, so as a direct object it always takes the accusative (goksel2005 §14.3.3.2): *kitabımı okuyorum* "I'm reading my book", never *\*kitabım okuyorum*.

## How it is built

**The six suffixes.** Each has a version after a consonant and one after a vowel (goksel2005 §8.1.2). The bracketed letters appear only where needed: *(I)* after a consonant, *(s)* after a vowel.

- **1SG** "my": *-(I)m*: *ev-im*, *okul-um*; *araba-m*.
- **2SG** "your" (one person, familiar): *-(I)n*: *ev-in*, *okul-un*; *araba-n*.
- **3SG** "his, her, its": *-(s)I*: *ev-i*, *okul-u*; *araba-sı*.
- **1PL** "our": *-(I)mIz*: *ev-imiz*, *okul-umuz*; *araba-mız*.
- **2PL** "your" (several people, or one person politely): *-(I)nIz*: *ev-iniz*, *okul-unuz*; *araba-nız*.
- **3PL** "their": *-lArI*: *ev-leri*, *okul-ları*; *araba-ları*.

The vowels follow four-way harmony (tur-01-05) except in *-lArI*, whose first vowel is two-way like the plural.

**Step by step**, for "our teacher":

1. The noun: *öğretmen*. It ends in a consonant, so the *(I)* is used.
2. Harmony: last vowel *e*, so *I* is *i*: *-imiz*.
3. *Öğretmenimiz.*

And for "his car": *araba* ends in a vowel, so the *(s)* is used: *arabası*.

**Stem changes.** Every possessive suffix except *-lArI* starts with a vowel after a consonant-final noun, so the stem changes of tur-01-06 and tur-03-01 apply: *kitap* → *kitabım*, *çocuk* → *çocuğum*, *renk* → *rengim*; *oğul* → *oğlum*, *isim* → *ismim*, *fikir* → *fikrim*; *hak* → *hakkım*. *Su* "water" has an irregular *y*: *suyum*, *suyun*, *suyu* (wiktionary).

**With the plural.** The plural comes first: *ev-ler-im* "my houses", *araba-lar-ımız* "our cars". There is never more than one *-lAr* for "their": *evleri* can mean "their house", "their houses" or "his houses" (goksel2005 §14.3.2). Context, or an owner word (tur-03-05), decides.

**With a case.** The case goes last. After the 1st and 2nd persons it follows directly: *ev-im-de* "in my house", *oda-m-a* "to my room", *kitab-ım-ı* "my book" (object), *araba-nız-dan* "from your car". After the 3rd person an extra *n* appears: *evinde* "in his house". That *n* has a lesson of its own (tur-03-06); until then, 3rd-person forms with a case are shown only as previews.

**Stress.** The possessives take the stress like other stressable suffixes: *evím*, *arabamíz* (goksel2005 §4.3.1). In words with fixed stress it stays put: *ámcam* "my uncle".

## Where it applies

- **Ownership**: *evim*, *arabanız*, *biletim*.
- **Family and other relationships**: *annem*, *oğlum*, *eşim* "my husband, my wife", *arkadaşım*, *komşumuz*, *öğretmenimiz*.
- **Body parts**: *başım*, *elim*, *ayağım*, *dişim*. Turkish marks the owner every time.
- **Parts and properties of things**: *odamın penceresi* "my room's window" (tur-03-05), *arabanın rengi*.
- **Names, ages, addresses, numbers**: *Adım Ayşe* "my name is Ayşe", *adresiniz*, *numaran*.
- **"A ... of mine"**: *bir arkadaşım*, *bir komşumuz* "a neighbour of ours".
- **Thoughts and feelings**: *fikrim* "my opinion", *hayatım* "my life".
- **Possessed objects**, always with the accusative: *kitabımı*, *arabanızı* (goksel2005 §14.3.3.2).
- **With any case after it**: *evimde*, *odama*, *arabanızdan*; 3rd person in tur-03-06.
- **"Having"**: *arabam var* "I have a car" (tur-03-09).
- **Compounds**: *Türk kahvesi* "Turkish coffee" uses the 3SG suffix without any owner (tur-03-08).
- **Clauses**: possessive suffixes also mark the subject of nominalised clauses: *geldiğim* "(the fact) that I came" (part 6).

## Watch out

**Do not forget the vowel/consonant switch.** *\*Arabaım*, *\*arabaı*, *\*arabaımız* are wrong. After a vowel: *arabam*, *arabası*, *arabamız*. After a consonant: *evim*, *evi*, *evimiz*, never *\*evsi*.

**Do not mix up the 3SG -(s)I and the accusative -(y)I.** After a vowel they differ: *arabası* "his car", *arabayı* "the car (object)". After a consonant they are identical, *evi*, and you must read the sentence: *Evi büyük* "his house is big"; *Evi görüyorum* "I see the house" or "I see his house" (tur-03-01).

**Do not confuse "my" with "I am".** *Öğretmenim* is both "my teacher" (possessive *-(I)m*) and "I'm a teacher" (personal ending *-(y)Im*, tur-02-02). After a vowel they part: *arabam* "my car", but *hastayım* "I'm ill", with the buffer *y*. Stress also differs: possessive *öğretmením* (stressed), personal ending *öğretmenim* with the stress before it (goksel2005 §4.3.2).

**Do not double the plural.** *\*Evlerleri* and *\*kardeşlerleri* are wrong. *-lAr* appears once, and *evleri*, *kardeşleri* carry all the meanings (goksel2005 §14.3.2).

**Do not use a bare noun for body parts.** *\*Baş ağrıyor* sounds like a general statement about heads. Say whose: *Başım ağrıyor*. Spanish *me duele la cabeza* and French *j'ai mal à la tête* use "the"; Turkish uses "my".

**Do not leave a possessed object bare.** *\*Biletim arıyorum* is wrong. A possessed noun is definite, so as an object: *Biletimi arıyorum*.

**Do not forget the stem changes.** *\*Kitapım*, *\*çocukum*, *\*oğulum*, *\*fikirim* are wrong: *kitabım*, *çocuğum*, *oğlum*, *fikrim*. And *su* is *suyum*, not *\*sum*.

**Do not put the possessive after the case.** *\*Evdeim* breaks the fixed order. Number, possession, case: *evimde*.

**Do not use the familiar "your" with strangers.** *Adın ne?* is fine to a child or a friend. To an adult you do not know, *Adınız ne?*, as with *sen* and *siz* (tur-02-02).

## Connections

Hungarian and Finnish also mark the owner with a suffix, and so does Arabic: *baytī* "my house", *baytuka* "your house". Korean and Japanese do not: they put a separate word before the noun (내 집, 私の家), which matches the Turkish genitive word *benim* of tur-03-05 rather than the suffix. Turkish uses both, and the suffix is the one that cannot be left out.

## Exercises

1. Give all six possessive forms of *kulak*.
2. Give "my", "his" and "their" for *oda*, *kitap*, *oğul*.
3. Say "My grandfather is very old" and "Our grandchildren are in Paris".
4. Translate *Amcanız doktor mu?*
5. Say "I'm looking for my key" and "Are you looking for your key?" (to a friend).
6. Take apart *teyzemden* and *ayaklarımız*.
7. Why can *evleri* mean three things? List them.
8. Correct *\*Arabaım çok eski* and *\*Telefonum arıyorum*.
9. Say "My teeth hurt" and explain why the verb can stay singular.
10. *Öğretmenim* has two meanings. What are they, and how would stress tell them apart?

## Worked answers

1. *Kulağım*, *kulağın*, *kulağı*, *kulağımız*, *kulağınız*, *kulakları*. Every suffix but *-lArI* starts with a vowel, so *k* becomes *ğ* (tur-01-06); the vowel is *ı* after *a*.
2. *Odam*, *odası*, *odaları*; *kitabım*, *kitabı*, *kitapları*; *oğlum*, *oğlu*, *oğulları*. *Oda* takes the short forms and *(s)*. *Kitap* softens before a vowel but not before *-lArI*. *Oğul* loses its *u* before a vowel only (tur-03-01).
3. *Dedem çok yaşlı.* *Yaşlı* is used for people (tur-02-02). *Torunlarımız Paris'te.* Plural, then possessive, then nothing more; the locative goes on the place (tur-02-06).
4. "Is your uncle a doctor?" (polite or plural "your"; the uncle is on the father's side). *Amca-nız*, with *-nIz* after a vowel.
5. *Anahtarımı arıyorum.* — *Anahtarını arıyor musun?* The possessed object takes the accusative in both. In *anahtarını* the accusative follows the 2SG *-ın* directly.
6. *Teyze-m-den*: aunt, my, from: "from my aunt". *Ayak-lar-ımız*: foot, plural, our: "our feet". The *k* of *ayak* stays because *-lAr* starts with a consonant.
7. "Their house" (*ev* + *-leri*), "their houses" (the same form standing for both plurals), and "his houses" (*ev* + *-ler* + *-i*). Turkish writes *-lAr* only once (goksel2005 §14.3.2).
8. *Arabam çok eski.* After a vowel the 1SG possessive is only *-m*. *Telefonumu arıyorum.* A possessed object must be in the accusative (tur-03-01).
9. *Dişlerim ağrıyor.* A plural subject that is a thing usually takes no *-lAr* on its predicate (tur-02-03, tur-02-08). *Dişim ağrıyor* is also correct, for one tooth.
10. "My teacher" and "I am a teacher". The possessive is stressed: *öğretmením*. The personal ending is not, so the stress stays before it: *öğretmenim* with the stress on *-men-* (goksel2005 §4.3.2; tur-02-02).
