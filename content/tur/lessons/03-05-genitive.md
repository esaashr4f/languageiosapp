## The idea

The possessive suffix says only the *person* of the owner: *evi* is "his, her or its house" (tur-03-04). To say *whose* house, you name the owner and put it in the **genitive** case, *-(n)In*: *Ali'nin evi* "Ali's house". The **possessor** is the owner, the person or thing something belongs to.

**Two marks, one relationship.** English marks the possessor once (*Ali's*), Spanish and French mark it once with a preposition (*la casa de Ali*, *la maison d'Ali*). Turkish marks *both* ends: the genitive on the owner, the possessive on the thing owned. Göksel and Kerslake call this the **genitive-possessive construction**: a phrase made of a genitive noun followed by a noun with a possessive suffix that agrees with it in person (goksel2005 §14.4).

- *Ali'nin ev-i*: Ali-GEN house-3SG.POSS, "Ali's house".
- *oda-nın kapı-sı*: room-GEN door-3SG.POSS, "the door of the room".
- *benim ev-im*: my house-1SG.POSS, "my house".

The double marking makes each half self-sufficient. *Evi* alone means "his house"; the genitive word only fills in who "he" is. That is why the genitive word can be left out when the person is clear, and why it is used when it is not.

**Order: owner first.** The owner always comes first, as every modifier does in Turkish (tur-02-07): *Ali'nin evi*, never \**evi Ali'nin* in a neutral sentence. Like Japanese 私の家 and Korean 나의 집, the relation reads "of-me house".

## How it is built

**The suffix -(n)In.** Four-way harmony; a buffer *n* after a vowel.

1. **After a consonant**: *-In*: *ev-in*, *okul-un*, *göz-ün*, *Mehmet'in*.
2. **After a vowel**: *-nIn*: *araba-nın*, *kapı-nın*, *kahve-nin*, *Ali'nin*, *Ayşe'nin*.
3. **Stem changes**, because the suffix starts with a vowel after a consonant: *kitab-ın*, *çocuğ-un*, *şehr-in*, *oğl-un*, *hakk-ın*.
4. **Exception**: *su* has *suyun* (wiktionary), with the same irregular *y* as *suyu*, *suyum*.

**The pronouns.** The 1st person pronouns take *-Im* instead (goksel2005 §8.1.3): *benim* "my", *bizim* "our". The others are regular: *senin* "your", *onun* "his, her, its" (with the *n* that *o* always shows before a suffix, tur-03-10), *sizin* "your (plural, polite)", *onların* "their".

**The chain of thought**, for "the price of the book":

1. Who or what is the owner? The book: *kitap*, in the genitive. It ends in *p* + a vowel suffix: *kitabın*.
2. What is owned? The price: *fiyat*. The owner is 3rd person singular, so *-(s)I*: *fiyatı*.
3. Owner first: *kitabın fiyatı*.

And for "our teacher", with emphasis: *bizim* + *öğretmen* with 1PL *-(I)mIz*: *bizim öğretmenimiz*.

**Plural owners.** With a plural owner and one thing owned, the 3SG suffix is usual: *öğrencilerin öğretmeni* "the students' teacher". *Öğrencilerin öğretmenleri* would be read as "the students' teachers" (goksel2005 §14.4).

**When to say the genitive pronoun.** *Benim*, *senin* and the rest are normally left out, because the possessive suffix already says the person: *Adım Mehmet*, not usually *Benim adım Mehmet*. They are used (goksel2005 §18.1.5.1) to **contrast** owners (*Benim adım Mehmet; senin adın ne?*), to **focus** on the owner ("it's *my* bag"), and to open a new topic (*Benim bir sorum var* "I have a question", tur-03-09). They are not used when the owner is also the subject of the sentence: *Anahtarımı arıyorum* "I'm looking for my key", without *benim*.

**The genitive as predicate.** "It is Ali's" is the genitive noun alone, as the predicate of a nominal sentence: *Bu çanta Ali'nin* "this bag is Ali's"; *Bu ev bizim* "this house is ours" (goksel2005 §14.3.3.6). The thing owned is not repeated.

**Stress.** *-(n)In* takes the stress: *evín*, *arabanín*; names keep theirs: *Ánkara'nın*.

## Where it applies

- **Ownership**: *Ali'nin evi*, *Ayşe'nin çantası*.
- **Family and other relations**: *Mehmet'in annesi*, *öğrencilerin öğretmeni*.
- **Parts and properties of a particular thing**: *kapının rengi*, *evin çatısı* "the roof of the house", *kitabın son sayfası* "the last page of the book", *şiirin anlamı* "the meaning of the poem".
- **"Of" with places**: *Türkiye'nin başkenti*, *şehrin merkezi* "the centre of the city".
- **Makers and owners**: *romanın yazarı*, *evin sahibi*, *şirketin patronu*.
- **Price, name, number and the like**: *kazağın fiyatı*, *otelin adı*, *odanın numarası*.
- **As predicate, "X's", "mine", "ours"**: *Bu çanta Ayşe'nin*, *Bu ev bizim*.
- **Genitive pronouns for contrast, focus or a new topic**: *Benim adım Mehmet, senin adın ne?*
- **Chains**: the thing owned can itself be an owner: *Ali'nin annesinin adı* "the name of Ali's mother" (goksel2005 §14.4); the *n* inside *annesinin* is explained in tur-03-06.
- **"Have"**: *Ali'nin arabası var* "Ali has a car" (tur-03-09).
- **The subject of a nominalised clause**: *Ali'nin geldiğini* "that Ali came" (part 6).

## Watch out

**Do not forget the possessive on the thing owned.** *\*Ali'nin ev* is wrong in standard Turkish: both ends are marked. *Ali'nin evi.* In very informal speech *benim ev* and *senin çocuklar* occur, but not in writing, and never in "have" sentences (goksel2005 §14.4).

**Do not reverse the order.** Spanish *la casa de Ali* and French *la maison d'Ali* put the owner last. Turkish puts it first: *Ali'nin evi*, not *\*evi Ali'nin* as a neutral phrase.

**Do not use the dative or ablative for "of".** "The capital of Turkey" is not *\*Türkiye'den başkent*. "Of" meaning belonging is the genitive: *Türkiye'nin başkenti*. The ablative's "of" is "out of, made of" (tur-03-03).

**Do not add a possessor pronoun for the subject's own things.** *\*Ben benim anahtarımı arıyorum* is wrong; English "my" is already in *-(I)m*: *Anahtarımı arıyorum* (goksel2005 §18.1.5). *Benim* would point to someone else's key or to contrast.

**Do not overuse the genitive pronouns.** *Benim adım Mehmet* is correct but emphatic. As a plain answer, *Adım Mehmet.*

**Do not put the wrong person on the thing owned.** The possessive agrees with the owner: *benim evim*, *senin evin*, *Ali'nin evi*. *\*Benim evi* and *\*Ali'nin evim* are wrong.

**Do not repeat the thing owned in a genitive predicate.** *Bu çanta Ayşe'nin* is enough; *Bu çanta Ayşe'nin çantası* is correct but heavy, like English "this bag is Ayşe's bag".

**Do not use -nIn after a consonant.** *\*Mehmet'nin*, *\*evnin* are wrong: the *n* is a buffer and appears only after a vowel. *Mehmet'in*, *evin*.

**Do not confuse the genitive with 2SG possessive.** After a consonant both are *-In*: *evin* is "of the house" or "your house". The next word decides: *evin kapısı* "the door of the house" (a possessed noun follows), *evin büyük* "your house is big". After a vowel they differ: *arabanın* "of the car", *araban* "your car".

**Do not use a genitive for "a kind of".** *Çocuğun kitabı* is "the child's book", a particular child's. "A children's book", a kind of book, is a compound without the genitive (tur-03-08).

## Connections

Japanese の and Korean 의 mark the owner and put it first, like *-(n)In*: 私の家, 나의 집 "my house". Neither marks the thing owned. Arabic does the reverse of Turkish: it marks the owner with the genitive and puts it *after* the thing owned, which takes no article (*bayt ʿAlī* "Ali's house"). Turkish marks both ends. Persian's *ezāfe* (*xāne-ye ʿAli*) is also "thing owned first"; Ottoman Turkish borrowed it for set phrases (tur-07-11).

## Exercises

1. Put into the genitive: *kral*, *şirket*, *köşe*, *kitap*, *şehir*, *su*.
2. Say "Ayşe's brother" and "the meaning of the word".
3. Say "the roof of the house" and "the last page of the novel".
4. Translate *Bu kalem senin mi?* and answer "No, it isn't mine. It's Mehmet's."
5. Say "The king's voice is very beautiful."
6. Correct *\*Mehmet'nin arabası* and *\*Ayşe'nin kardeş*.
7. Why is *benim* missing in *Ödevimi yapıyorum* "I'm doing my homework"?
8. *Evin büyük* and *evin kapısı*: what does *evin* mean in each?
9. Say "The students' teacher is Turkish" and explain the possessive you chose.
10. Translate *Şirketin patronu Paris'te, ama şirketin merkezi İstanbul'da.*

## Worked answers

1. *Kralın*, *şirketin*, *köşenin*, *kitabın*, *şehrin*, *suyun*. After a consonant *-In*; after a vowel *-nIn*. *Kitap* softens; *şehir* loses its *i* (tur-03-01); *su* has its irregular *y*.
2. *Ayşe'nin kardeşi*; *kelimenin anlamı*. Owner in the genitive, first; the thing owned with the 3SG possessive.
3. *Evin çatısı*; *romanın son sayfası*. *Son* is an adjective here, before *sayfa*, and the possessive goes on the noun: *sayfası*.
4. "Is this pen yours?" — *Hayır, benim değil. Mehmet'in.* The genitive is the predicate; *değil* negates it (tur-02-04); the second sentence needs no repeated *kalem*.
5. *Kralın sesi çok güzel.* *Ses* ends in a consonant, so the 3SG possessive is *-i*.
6. *Mehmet'in arabası*: the *n* of *-(n)In* is only for vowels. *Ayşe'nin kardeşi*: the thing owned must carry the possessive.
7. The owner, "I", is also the subject, so the genitive pronoun is left out; *-(I)m* in *ödevim* already says "my" (goksel2005 §18.1.5).
8. In *evin büyük*, *evin* is "your house" (2SG possessive), and *büyük* is the predicate. In *evin kapısı*, *evin* is "of the house" (genitive), because a possessed noun follows it.
9. *Öğrencilerin öğretmeni Türk.* One teacher of several students takes the 3SG *-(s)I*; *öğretmenleri* would suggest several teachers (goksel2005 §14.4).
10. "The company's boss is in Paris, but the company's headquarters is in Istanbul." Each phrase repeats *şirketin*; the locative goes on the place predicate (tur-02-06). *Merkez* here means "head office".
