## The idea

You can say "in my house", *evimde*, and "his house", *evi*. Put the two together, "in his house", and an *n* appears that belongs to neither: *evinde*, not *\*evide*. Likewise *arabasında* "in his car", *Ali'nin evine* "to Ali's house", *annesinden* "from her mother".

This *n* appears whenever a case suffix follows the 3rd-person possessive *-(s)I* or *-lArI*. Göksel and Kerslake treat it as part of those two suffixes: their full forms are *-(s)I(n)* and *-lArI(n)*, and the *n* shows only when a case follows (goksel2005 §8.1.2). This guide calls it the **pronominal n**, because the same *n* turns up after the pronoun *o*: *onu* "him (object)", *ona* "to him" (tur-03-10), and after *bu* "this": *bunu* (tur-03-11).

**Why is it there?** The sources used for this guide describe where the *n* appears but do not give its history, so this lesson does not offer one. What it does is useful: it keeps the possessive audible. Without it, *evi* + *-(y)A* would give *\*eviye* or *\*eve*, and "to his house" would sound like "to the house". With the *n*, *evine* can only contain a possessive.

**Which suffixes take it.** Göksel and Kerslake list the case suffixes (goksel2005 §6.2):

- accusative: *evi* + *-(y)I* → *evini*;
- dative: → *evine*;
- locative: → *evinde*;
- ablative: → *evinden*;
- genitive: → *evinin*.

The plural, the 1st and 2nd person possessives and anything else you have met take no *n*: *evleri* "their house" is *-lArI* with nothing after it, and *evimde* has no 3rd person.

## How it is built

**The rule.** After *-(s)I* or *-lArI*, every case suffix starts with *n* instead of its usual buffer:

1. **Accusative** *-(y)I* becomes *-nI*: *ev-i-ni*, *araba-sı-nı*, *ev-leri-ni*.
2. **Dative** *-(y)A* becomes *-nA*: *ev-i-ne*, *araba-sı-na*.
3. **Locative** *-DA* becomes *-nDA*: *ev-i-nde*, *araba-sı-nda*. The *D* is always *d*, because *n* is voiced.
4. **Ablative** *-DAn* becomes *-nDAn*: *ev-i-nden*, *araba-sı-ndan*.
5. **Genitive** *-(n)In* is *-nIn* anyway: *ev-i-nin*, *araba-sı-nın*.

The vowels follow harmony from the possessive's vowel: *oda-sı-nda* (back), *ev-i-nde* (front), *göz-ü-nde*.

In this guide's glosses the *n* goes with the case, as the buffer *y* does: *ev-i-nde* `house-3SG.POSS-LOC`.

**The chain of thought**, for "I'm staying at Ali's house":

1. The verb: *kalmak* "stay", *kalıyorum*.
2. The place: Ali's house, a genitive-possessive phrase (tur-03-05): *Ali'nin evi*.
3. Staying is at a place: locative. The case goes on the end of the whole phrase, on *evi*.
4. *Evi* ends in the 3SG possessive, so the locative is *-nDA*: *evinde*.
5. *Ali'nin evinde kalıyorum.*

And "I'm looking for his wallet": *cüzdan* + 3SG *-ı* = *cüzdanı*; a possessed object takes the accusative (tur-03-04); after *-(s)I* that is *-nI*: *Cüzdanını arıyorum.*

**The case goes on the last word.** In a genitive-possessive phrase the owner keeps its genitive and the thing owned carries the case of the whole phrase (goksel2005 §14.4): *Ali'nin evinden* "from Ali's house", not *\*Ali'den evi*.

**Chains.** Because the genitive is a case, a possessed noun in the genitive also shows the *n*: *Ali'nin annesinin adı* "Ali's mother's name". Read it from the left: *Ali'nin* → *annesi* "Ali's mother" → *annesinin* "of Ali's mother" → *adı* "her name".

**A new ambiguity.** After a consonant, the 2SG possessive *-(I)n* and the 3SG *-I* + *n* look the same before a case: *evini* is "your house" or "his house" as an object; *evinde* is "in your house" or "in his house" (goksel2005 §14.3.2). After a vowel they differ: *arabanı* "your car", *arabasını* "his car". Where it matters, add the genitive pronoun: *senin evinde*, *onun evinde*.

## Where it applies

- **After the 3SG possessive -(s)I, before any case**: *evini*, *evine*, *evinde*, *evinden*, *evinin*; *arabasını*, *arabasına*, *arabasında*, *arabasından*, *arabasının*.
- **After the 3PL possessive -lArI, before any case**: *evlerini*, *evlerine*, *evlerinde*, *evlerinden*, *evlerinin*.
- **In genitive-possessive phrases**, on the thing owned, whenever the phrase has a case: *Ali'nin evinde*, *şehrin merkezine*.
- **In chains**, before the genitive: *annesinin adı*.
- **On possessed objects**, which always take the accusative: *cüzdanını arıyor*.
- **On place nouns with a possessive**: *odasında*, *cebinde*; in part 5 the location nouns *iç* "inside", *üst* "top" work the same way: *evin içinde* "inside the house" (tur-05-07).
- **After the compound marker**, which is the same suffix: *Türk kahvesini* "Turkish coffee (object)" (tur-03-08).
- **After the pronoun *o* and the pointing words *bu*, *şu*, *o***: *onu*, *ona*, *bunu*, *bunda* (tur-03-10, tur-03-11).
- **After -ki used as a pronoun**: *evdekini* "the one in the house (object)" (tur-03-14).
- **Not** before the plural, the personal endings, or *-(y)lA* "with": *arabasıyla* "with his car" has a *y*, not an *n* (goksel2005 §8.1.4; tur-03-13).

## Watch out

**Do not use the ordinary buffer after a 3rd-person possessive.** *\*Arabasıyı*, *\*arabasıya*, *\*evie* are wrong. After *-(s)I* the accusative and dative start with *n*: *arabasını*, *arabasına*, *evine*.

**Do not drop the possessive vowel.** *\*Evnde*, *\*arabasnda* are wrong. The possessive stays whole and the *n* follows it: *evinde*, *arabasında*.

**Do not use t after the n.** *\*Evinte*, *\*cebinte* are wrong. *N* is voiced, so *-DA* and *-DAn* keep *d*: *evinde*, *cebinden* (tur-01-06).

**Do not put the n after 1st and 2nd persons.** *\*Evimnde*, *\*arabanızna* are wrong. Only the 3rd person possessives take it: *evimde*, *arabanıza*.

**Do not confuse *evine* "to his house" with *eve* "home".** *Eve gidiyor* is "he's going home". *Evine gidiyor* is "he's going to his (own or someone else's) house". *Ali'nin evine gidiyor* names the house.

**Do not split the phrase's case.** *\*Ali'ye evi gidiyorum* for "I'm going to Ali's house" puts the case on the owner. The owner stays genitive and the case goes last: *Ali'nin evine gidiyorum.*

**Do not forget the accusative on a possessed object.** *\*Kitabı okuyor* for "he's reading his book" is a different sentence: "he's reading the book". With the possessive and the accusative: *Kitabını okuyor.*

**Do not over-read *-ını* as "your".** *Evini* after a consonant may be "your house" or "his house"; after a vowel *arabanı* is only "your car" and *arabasını* only "his car". The context, or *senin* and *onun*, decides (goksel2005 §14.3.2).

## Exercises

1. Put *odası* "his room" into all five cases.
2. Say "in her bag", "from her bag" and "her bag" as an object.
3. Say "I'm going to Mehmet's house."
4. Say "The children are in their room."
5. Take apart *kardeşlerinin* and give two possible meanings.
6. Correct *\*Ayşe'nin arabasıda* and *\*Kitabısını okuyor*.
7. Why is it *evimde* but *evinde*?
8. Say "Ali is moving to his grandfather's village."
9. Translate *Dosyaları çekmecesinde saklıyor.*
10. *Eve dönüyor* or *evine dönüyor*: which says more, and what?

## Worked answers

1. *Odasını*, *odasına*, *odasında*, *odasından*, *odasının*. Every case after *-(s)I* starts with *n*; the vowels follow the *ı* of *-sı*.
2. *Çantasında*, *çantasından*, *çantasını*. The locative and ablative keep *d* after *n*.
3. *Mehmet'in evine gidiyorum.* Owner in the genitive, the case on the thing owned, with *n* after its *-i* (tur-03-05).
4. *Çocuklar odalarında.* "Their room" is *odaları*; the locative adds *-nda*.
5. *Kardeş-ler-i-nin* "of his brothers and sisters", or *kardeş-leri-nin* "of their brother(s) and sister(s)". The single *-lAr* serves both readings (tur-03-04).
6. *Ayşe'nin arabasında*: the locative after *-sı* is *-nda*. *Kitabını okuyor*: the possessive is *-ı* after a consonant; *-sı* is only for vowels, and the accusative after it is *-nı*.
7. *Evim* is 1st person, which takes no *n*; *evi* is 3rd person, which does.
8. *Ali dedesinin köyüne taşınıyor.* *Dedesinin* "of his grandfather" (genitive after *-si*, with *n*), *köyüne* "to his village" (dative after *-ü*, with *n*). *Taşınmak* takes the dative.
9. "She keeps the files in her drawer." *Dosyaları* is a known object; *çekmecesinde* is drawer + 3SG possessive + *-nde*.
10. *Evine dönüyor* says more: "he's going back to his house", naming whose house with the possessive. *Eve dönüyor* is "he's going (back) home".
