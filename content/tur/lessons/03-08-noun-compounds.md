## The idea

English can put one noun in front of another and make a new name: "bus ticket", "tea glass", "bedroom". Turkish does the same, with one addition. The second noun carries the 3rd-person possessive suffix: *otobüs bileti* "bus ticket", *çay bardağı* "tea glass", *yatak odası* "bedroom" (literally "bed room-its"). This is a **noun compound**: two nouns that together name one kind of thing. Göksel and Kerslake call this the **-(s)I compound** and say it is by far the commoner type (goksel2005 §10.2).

**The same suffix, another job.** In *Ali'nin evi* the *-(s)I* means "his": the house belongs to Ali. In *otobüs bileti* nothing belongs to anything. The ticket is not the bus's; it is a ticket *for* the bus, a *kind* of ticket. Göksel and Kerslake put it plainly: in compounds the suffix does not signify possession; it only marks the second noun as compounded with the one before (goksel2005 §10.2). This guide glosses it `3SG.POSS` all the same, because it is the same suffix with the same forms and the same *n* before a case.

**Genitive phrase or compound?** The difference is the first noun.

- In a **genitive phrase** the first noun is a particular thing, in the genitive: *çocuğun kitabı* "the child's book", that child's.
- In a **compound** the first noun is bare and names a kind: *çocuk kitabı* "a children's book", a book of the children's kind.

So the genitive answers "whose?" and the compound answers "what sort?". *Türkiye'nin başkenti* is "the capital of Turkey"; *Türk kahvesi* is "Turkish coffee", a sort of coffee, wherever it is made.

## How it is built

**Two nouns, the second marked.** The first noun has no suffix at all. The second takes *-(s)I*: *-I* after a consonant, *-sI* after a vowel (tur-03-04).

1. **Name the kind**: "tooth" → *diş*.
2. **Name the thing**: "brush" → *fırça*.
3. **Mark the second**: *fırça* ends in a vowel, so *-sı*: *diş fırçası* "toothbrush".

Two more: *doğum* "birth" + *gün* "day" → *doğum günü* "birthday"; *portakal* + *su* → *portakal suyu* "orange juice" (*su* takes its irregular *y*).

**Plural.** The plural goes on the second noun, before the marker: *otobüs biletleri* "bus tickets", *çay bardakları* "tea glasses" (goksel2005 §10.2).

**Case.** The case goes after the marker, with the *n* of tur-03-06: *Türk kahvesini* "Turkish coffee (object)", *yatak odasında* "in the bedroom", *otobüs durağına* "to the bus stop".

**Adjectives and *bir*.** They go before the whole compound, never between its parts (goksel2005 §10.4): *yeni bir otobüs bileti* "a new bus ticket", *büyük bir yatak odası* "a big bedroom".

**Possessed compounds.** A noun can carry only one possessive suffix, so "my" replaces the marker (goksel2005 §14.3.2.1): *yatak odası* "bedroom" → *yatak odam* "my bedroom", *yatak odan* "your bedroom". The 3rd person shows no change: *yatak odası* is "bedroom" and "his bedroom". With *Ali'nin* in front, *Ali'nin yatak odası*, it is his.

**Compounds inside compounds.** A compound can be the first part of another: *Türk dili* "the Turkish language" + *bölüm* "department" → *Türk Dili Bölümü*. Each head carries one marker (goksel2005 §10.3).

**Bare compounds.** A smaller group of compounds has no marker at all (goksel2005 §10.1.1). The first noun says the sex, the nationality or the material of the second: *kız kardeş* "sister", *erkek kardeş* "brother", *kız arkadaş* "girlfriend", *Alman mühendis* "a German engineer", *taş duvar* "stone wall".

**Stress.** Most compounds are stressed on the first part (goksel2005 §4.2): *çáy bardağı*, *dóğum günü*, *búgün* "today".

**Written as one word.** Some compounds have fused in spelling: *buzdolabı* "fridge" (*buz* "ice" + *dolap*), *başkent*, *büyükanne*, *ayakkabı*. They still behave as compounds: *buzdolabını*, plural *buzdolapları* (wiktionary). A few, like *ayakkabı*, have become plain nouns: plural *ayakkabılar* (goksel2005 §10.2).

## Where it applies

**-(s)I compounds:**

- **Purpose or type**: *otobüs bileti*, *diş fırçası*, *çay bardağı*, *yatak odası*, *çocuk kitabı*.
- **What something contains or is made from, as a product**: *portakal suyu*, *elma suyu* "apple juice".
- **Events and occasions**: *doğum günü*, *futbol maçı*, *Türkçe dersi* "Turkish lesson".
- **People by field**: *Türkçe öğretmeni* "Turkish teacher", *futbol takımı* "football team".
- **Things typical of a nation**: *Türk kahvesi*, *Alman ekmeği* "German bread", *Fransız müziği* "French music" (goksel2005 §10.2). The nationality word is a noun, so it needs the marker on the thing.
- **Proper names of places and institutions**: *Van Gölü*, *Ağrı Dağı* "Mount Ararat", *İstanbul Üniversitesi*, *Ankara Lokantası*.
- **Days and dates**: *cuma günü* "Friday", *doğum günü* (goksel2005 §10.2; tur-05-04).
- **Inside other compounds**: *Türk Dili Bölümü*.
- **With an owner**: *Ayşe'nin doğum günü*, *Ali'nin yatak odası*.
- **With any case**, with *n*: *otobüs durağında*, *Türk kahvesini*.

**Bare compounds:**

- **Sex**: *kız kardeş*, *erkek kardeş*, *kız arkadaş*, *erkek arkadaş*.
- **Nationality of a person**: *Alman mühendis*, *Türk öğretmen* "a Turkish (national) teacher".
- **Material**: *taş duvar* "stone wall".
- **Adjective + noun fused as one word**: *büyükanne*.

## Watch out

**Do not drop the marker.** *\*Türk kahve*, *\*otobüs bilet*, *\*doğum gün* are wrong. English "bus ticket" has no suffix; Turkish needs one on the second noun: *Türk kahvesi*, *otobüs bileti*, *doğum günü*.

**Do not put the genitive on the first noun when you mean a kind.** *Çocuğun kitabı* is one child's book; *otobüsün bileti* is "the ticket of the bus", a strange idea. For a kind, the first noun is bare: *çocuk kitabı*, *otobüs bileti*.

**Do not put an adjective inside.** *\*Otobüs yeni bileti* is wrong: *yeni bir otobüs bileti* (goksel2005 §10.4).

**Do not put the plural on the first noun.** *\*Otobüsler bileti* for "bus tickets" is wrong: *otobüs biletleri*. Even "children's books" is *çocuk kitapları*, with the first noun bare.

**Do not stack two possessives.** *\*Yatak odasım* and *\*diş fırçasım* keep the marker and add "my". "My" replaces it: *yatak odam*, *diş fırçam*.

**Do not confuse *Türkçe öğretmeni* and *Türk öğretmen*.** The first is "a teacher of Turkish" (any nationality); the second is "a teacher who is a Turk" (any subject). Spanish *profesor de turco* and *profesor turco* make the same split.

**Do not use the bare form for things of a nation.** *\*Türk kahve* treats *Türk* like an English adjective. It is a noun, so the head needs the marker: *Türk kahvesi* (goksel2005 §10.2). The bare pattern is only for people: *Alman mühendis*.

**Do not leave a nationality compound object bare.** *\*Türk kahvesi sevmiyor* for "he doesn't like Turkish coffee" is wrong: such objects take the accusative even in a general statement (goksel2005 §22.4.3): *Türk kahvesini sevmiyor*.

**Do not treat every fused word alike.** *Buzdolabı* keeps the marker inside (*buzdolapları*), but *ayakkabı* has become a plain noun (*ayakkabılar*, not *\*ayakkapları*) (goksel2005 §10.2). The vocabulary notes give the plural where it matters.

## Connections

English and Japanese make compounds by putting two nouns side by side (bus ticket, バス停 *basutei* "bus stop"); Korean does the same (버스 정류장). Turkish adds a marker on the second noun. Arabic and Persian put the head first instead: Arabic *kurat al-qadam* "football", literally "ball of the foot". *Portakal* comes from the name of Portugal (tdk).

## Exercises

1. Build compounds: *elma* + *su*; *kahve* + *fincan*; *futbol* + *takım*; *Türkçe* + *ders*.
2. Make plural: *otobüs bileti*, *çay bardağı*, *diş fırçası*.
3. Say "my birthday", "your (one friend) bedroom" and "our football team".
4. Translate *Ayşe'nin kız kardeşi İstanbul Üniversitesinde çalışıyor.*
5. What is the difference between *İngilizce öğretmeni* and *İngiliz öğretmen*? (*İngilizce* "English, the language" is *İngiliz* + *-CA*, like *Türkçe*.)
6. Say "I'm putting the toothbrush in the cup".
7. Correct *\*Bir Türk kahve istiyorum* and *\*Otobüs durakta bekliyoruz*.
8. Explain why *Türk kahvesini seviyorum* has the accusative though no particular cup is meant.
9. Say "a big stone wall".
10. Why is it *ayakkabılar* but *buzdolapları*?

## Worked answers

1. *Elma suyu*, *kahve fincanı*, *futbol takımı*, *Türkçe dersi*. The first noun stays bare; the second takes *-(s)I* (*su* has the irregular *suyu*).
2. *Otobüs biletleri*, *çay bardakları*, *diş fırçaları*. The plural goes on the second noun, before the marker, which is then *-I* after the consonant of *-lAr* (goksel2005 §10.2).
3. *Doğum günüm*, *yatak odan*, *futbol takımımız*. The possessive replaces the compound marker (goksel2005 §14.3.2.1).
4. "Ayşe's sister works at Istanbul University." *Kız kardeşi*: a bare compound with the 3SG possessive for "her". *İstanbul Üniversitesinde*: a name compound with the locative after *n* (tur-03-06). Names of institutions take no apostrophe before a case ending (tdk, spelling rules on the apostrophe), unlike personal and place names: *Ali'nin*, but *İstanbul Üniversitesinde*.
5. *İngilizce öğretmeni* is a compound: a teacher of English, of any nationality. *İngiliz öğretmen* is a bare compound: a teacher who is English, of any subject (goksel2005 §10.1.1).
6. *Diş fırçasını fincana koyuyorum.* A known toothbrush is a definite object: marker, *n*, accusative. *Koymak* takes the dative (tur-03-02).
7. *Bir Türk kahvesi istiyorum*: a thing typical of a nation needs the marker. *Otobüs durağında bekliyoruz*: the compound needs its marker, then *n*, then the locative.
8. Compounds whose first part is a nationality must take the accusative as generic objects, with verbs such as *sevmek*, even when no particular cup is meant (goksel2005 §22.4.3). An activity object stays bare: *Türk kahvesi içiyoruz*.
9. *Büyük bir taş duvar.* A material compound needs no marker, and the adjective and *bir* go before the whole compound.
10. *Ayakkabı* is now a single noun and its *-ı* is no longer felt as a marker, so the plural goes at the end. *Buzdolabı* is still felt as *buz* + *dolap* + marker, so the plural goes before the marker (goksel2005 §10.2; wiktionary).
