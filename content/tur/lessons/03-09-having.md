## The idea

Turkish has no everyday verb "to have". To say "I have a car", it says "my car exists": *Arabam var*. To say "I have no time", "my time does not exist": *Zamanım yok*. You already know both halves: the possessive suffix (tur-03-04) and the existential words *var* and *yok* (tur-02-06).

Göksel and Kerslake call this a **possessive existential sentence**: a sentence that asserts the existence of something possessed (goksel2005 §12.1.1.2). Its parts, in order:

1. the owner in the genitive, if it needs saying: *benim*, *Ali'nin*;
2. any time or place words;
3. the thing owned, with a possessive suffix agreeing with the owner: *arabam*, *arabası*;
4. *var* or *yok*.

So *(Benim) bir arabam var* "I have a car"; *Ali'nin iki kardeşi var* "Ali has two brothers and sisters"; *Zamanımız yok* "we have no time".

**Why this way?** The pattern is the existential sentence with the place replaced by an owner. *Masada bir kitap var* "on the table a book exists"; *Ali'nin bir kitabı var* "Ali's book exists", that is, Ali has a book. Japanese and Korean do much the same with their existence verbs (私には車がある, 나는 차가 있다).

**Two ways to have.** Turkish separates having something as a lasting relation from having it with you now (goksel2005 §12.1.1.2):

- **Possessive**: *Ali'nin parası var* "Ali has money (he is well off)". For family, parts, works, property: anything treated as a permanent link.
- **Locative**: *Ali'de para var* "Ali has money (on him)". The owner is in the locative, like a place, and the thing is bare: what is with him at the moment.

The locative pattern also lets the thing be definite: *Bu kitap Ali'de* or *Bu kitap Ali'de var* "Ali has this book (it is with him)". The possessive pattern cannot: the thing owned in a "have" sentence is never a particular, known one (goksel2005 §12.1.1.2).

## How it is built

**The chain of thought**, for "Ayşe has a dog":

1. Who owns? Ayşe, 3rd person: genitive *Ayşe'nin*.
2. What is owned? A dog: *köpek*, with the 3SG possessive: *köpeği* (k becomes ğ).
3. One dog, new to the listener: *bir* before it.
4. It exists: *var*.
5. *Ayşe'nin bir köpeği var.*

For "I don't have a passport": owner "I", so the 1SG possessive on *pasaport*: *pasaportum*; it does not exist: *yok*. *Pasaportum yok.* *Benim* is left out unless contrasted: *Benim pasaportum yok, ama Ali'nin var* "I don't have a passport, but Ali does".

**Questions and answers.** *MI* follows *var* or *yok*: *Arabanız var mı?* "Do you have a car?" The answer repeats the predicate: *Var* or *Yok*, often with *evet* or *hayır*: *Evet, var.* *Hayır, yok.*

**Numbers.** After a number the thing stays singular, as always (tur-02-03): *İki çocuğum var* "I have two children".

**Questions and negatives about any at all.** The thing usually stays singular and bare of *bir*: *Kardeşin var mı?* "Do you have any brothers or sisters?"; *Hiç arabam yok* "I have no car at all" (goksel2005 §22.3).

**Needing.** *İhtiyaç* "need" works the same way, with the dative of what is needed: *Yardıma ihtiyacım var* "I need help", literally "my need for help exists".

**Stress.** As in other existential sentences (tur-02-06), the main stress falls on the thing owned in the positive and on *yok* in the negative: *Bir arabam var*, with the stress in *arabam*; *Arabam yok*, with the stress on *yok*.

**Other tenses.** "I had a car" and "I will have a car" need the past and *olmak* "be": *arabam vardı* (tur-04-08), *arabam olacak* (tur-04-16).

## Where it applies

**The possessive pattern, for lasting relations** (goksel2005 §12.1.1.2):

- **Family and friends**: *İki çocuğum var*, *İstanbul'da bir arkadaşım var*.
- **Property**: *Arabam var*, *Bisikletin var mı?*, *Bir dairemiz var* "we have a flat".
- **Parts of a whole**: *Masanın dört ayağı var*, *Evin büyük bir bahçesi var*.
- **Works and products**: *Bu yazarın üç romanı var* "this writer has (written) three novels".
- **Time, plans, appointments, obligations**: *Zamanım yok*, *Vaktin var mı?*, *Bir randevum var*, *Sınavım var*, *İşim var* "I'm busy (I have work)".
- **Problems, questions, ideas**: *Bir sorum var* "I have a question", *Sorun yok* "no problem", *Bir fikrim var*.
- **Illness**: *Ateşim var*, *Öksürüğüm var*, *Gribim var* "I've got the flu".
- **Luck, permission, rights**: *Şansımız yok*, *İzniniz var mı?*, *Hakkımız var* "we have a right".
- **Needing**, with the dative: *Yardıma ihtiyacım var*.
- **Not having at all**: *Hiç param yok*.

**The locative pattern, for what is on someone now:**

- *Ali'de para var*, *Ayşe'de bir kalem var mı?* "Has Ayşe got a pen (on her)?"
- **A particular thing someone has**: *Bu kitap Ayşe'de*, *Anahtar Mehmet'te* "Mehmet has the key".

**Related patterns:**

- *Bende* "on me", *sende* "on you" (tur-03-10): *Bende para yok* "I haven't got any money on me".
- *Sahip olmak* "possess, own", formal (*sahip* + *olmak*, tur-04-16).

## Watch out

**Do not look for a verb "have".** *\*Ben bir araba var* and *\*Bir arabam varım* are wrong. The possessive goes on the thing, and *var* takes no personal ending: *Bir arabam var*.

**Do not leave out the possessive.** *\*Benim araba var* is wrong even in speech: in "have" sentences the possessive suffix is obligatory (goksel2005 §14.4). *Benim arabam var.*

**Do not put the owner in the dative or nominative.** Spanish *tengo*, French *j'ai* and English "I have" make the owner the subject. *\*Ali iki kardeşi var* and *\*Ali'ye iki kardeşi var* are wrong: *Ali'nin iki kardeşi var.*

**Do not use the accusative on the thing owned.** *\*Arabamı var* is wrong: the thing owned is the subject of *var*, and subjects are bare (tur-03-01).

**Do not "have" a particular thing with the possessive.** *\*Bu kitabım var* for "I have this book" is wrong: the possessive pattern is for things not yet identified (goksel2005 §12.1.1.2). Use the locative: *Bu kitap bende* (tur-03-10), *Bu kitap Ayşe'de*.

**Do not confuse the two patterns.** *Ali'nin parası var* means he is a person with money. *Ali'de para var* means he has some on him. A rich man may have none on him: *Ali'nin parası var, ama şimdi Ali'de para yok.*

**Do not use the plural in a general question.** In questions and negatives with *var* and *yok* the thing is normally singular and bare (goksel2005 §22.3): *Kardeşiniz var mı?*, not *Kardeşleriniz var mı?*, for "do you have any brothers or sisters?"

**Do not use değil for "not have".** *\*Arabam değil* means "it's not my car". "I don't have a car" is *Arabam yok.*

**Do not put the possessive on the owner.** *\*Ayşem'in köpek var* mixes up the ends. The genitive goes on the owner, the possessive on the thing: *Ayşe'nin köpeği var*.

**Do not forget the dative with *ihtiyaç*.** *\*Yardım ihtiyacım var* is wrong: *Yardıma ihtiyacım var.*

## Connections

Korean 있다/없다 and Japanese ある/ない serve both "there is" and "have", like *var* and *yok*: 나는 차가 있다, 私には車がある. Arabic has no verb "have" either and uses a preposition with the owner (*ʿindī sayyāra* "with me a car"), which is close to the Turkish locative pattern *bende araba var*. Spanish and French, like English, use a verb (*tener*, *avoir*).

## Exercises

1. Say "I have a bicycle" and "I don't have a bicycle".
2. Say "Ayşe has three cousins".
3. Ask a stranger "Do you have a driving licence?" and give both answers.
4. Say "We have no problem."
5. Translate *Bu evin iki yatak odası ve küçük bir bahçesi var.*
6. Correct *\*Ben bir köpek var* and *\*Mehmet'in köpek var*.
7. Say "Mehmet has the key" (a particular key). Why not *\*Mehmet'in anahtarı var*?
8. What is the difference between *Ali'nin parası yok* and *Ali'de para yok*?
9. Say "I need a passport."
10. Say "Do you (one friend) have time today?" and explain the order.

## Worked answers

1. *Bisikletim var.* — *Bisikletim yok.* The possessive on the thing, then *var* or *yok*. *Bir* is optional in the positive: *Bir bisikletim var.*
2. *Ayşe'nin üç kuzeni var.* Owner in the genitive, singular noun after a number with the 3SG possessive.
3. *Ehliyetiniz var mı?* — *Evet, var.* / *Hayır, yok.* Polite *-(I)nIz* for a stranger; the answer repeats *var* or *yok* (tur-02-06).
4. *Sorunumuz yok.* (Or, as a fixed phrase, *Sorun yok* "no problem".)
5. "This house has two bedrooms and a small garden." *Bu evin* is the owner; each thing owned has the 3SG possessive: *odası* (which is also the compound marker, tur-03-08), *bahçesi*. The adjective and *bir* stand before *bahçesi*.
6. *Benim bir köpeğim var*: there is no verb "have"; the possessive goes on *köpek*. *Mehmet'in köpeği var*: the thing owned needs the possessive.
7. *Anahtar Mehmet'te.* A particular, known key can only be located with someone. *Mehmet'in anahtarı var* would mean "Mehmet has a key (of his own)", not the one we mean (goksel2005 §12.1.1.2).
8. *Ali'nin parası yok*: Ali has no money, he is poor or broke. *Ali'de para yok*: he has no money on him right now (goksel2005 §12.1.1.2).
9. *Pasaporta ihtiyacım var.* What is needed takes the dative.
10. *Bugün vaktin var mı?* (or *zamanın*). The time word comes first, then the possessed noun, then *var* and *mı* (goksel2005 §12.1.1.2). *Vakit* loses its *i* before the vowel of *-In*.
