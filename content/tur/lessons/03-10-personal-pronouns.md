## The idea

You met the six personal pronouns in tur-02-02: *ben* "I", *sen* "you", *o* "he, she, it", *biz* "we", *siz* "you (plural or polite)", *onlar* "they". A **pronoun** is a word that stands in for a noun. Like nouns, Turkish pronouns take the case suffixes, so "me", "to me", "from me" and "my" are all *ben* plus a case: *beni*, *bana*, *benden*, *benim*.

Almost everything here follows from what you already know. There are three irregularities, each fixed (goksel2005 §§6.2, 18.1.1):

1. **Bana and sana.** In the dative, *ben* and *sen* change their vowel: *bana* "to me", *sana* "to you", not *\*bene*, *\*sene*. *Biz* and *siz* are regular: *bize*, *size*.
2. **The n of o.** *O* never takes a case suffix or the plural directly. An *n* comes first: *onu*, *ona*, *onda*, *ondan*, *onun*, and *onlar* itself is *o* + *n* + *-lAr*. This is the same *n* you saw after the 3rd-person possessive (tur-03-06).
3. **Benim and bizim.** The 1st person genitive is *-Im*, not *-In* (tur-03-05).

**Why are pronouns so often missing?** The verb ending or the possessive suffix already names the person: *geliyorum* is "I'm coming", *evim* is "my house". A subject pronoun adds nothing that the ending does not say, so Turkish leaves it out unless it has a job to do (goksel2005 §18.1.5). Objects that have been mentioned are dropped too, as long as it is clear what they are (goksel2005 §28.4.3): *Ali'yi tanıyor musun? — Evet, tanıyorum* "Do you know Ali? — Yes, I know (him)".

## How it is built

**The full set** (wiktionary; goksel2005 §18.1.1):

- *ben*: *beni*, *bana*, *bende*, *benden*, *benim*.
- *sen*: *seni*, *sana*, *sende*, *senden*, *senin*.
- *o*: *onu*, *ona*, *onda*, *ondan*, *onun*.
- *biz*: *bizi*, *bize*, *bizde*, *bizden*, *bizim*.
- *siz*: *sizi*, *size*, *sizde*, *sizden*, *sizin*.
- *onlar*: *onları*, *onlara*, *onlarda*, *onlardan*, *onların*.

**Choosing the case** is the same as for a noun. The verb decides: *görmek* takes the accusative (*seni görüyorum*), *bakmak* the dative (*sana bakıyorum*), *korkmak* the ablative (*senden korkuyorum*) (tur-03-01 to tur-03-03).

**The chain of thought**, for "I'm giving it to her":

1. The verb: *vermek*, which takes the receiver in the dative and the thing as object (tur-03-02).
2. The receiver "her": *o* + *n* + dative: *ona*.
3. "It": a known thing. If it has just been mentioned, leave it out; if not, *onu*.
4. The subject "I" is in the ending: *veriyorum*.
5. *Ona veriyorum*, or with the object said, *Onu ona veriyorum*.

**When to say a subject pronoun.** Göksel and Kerslake list the cases (goksel2005 §18.1.5.1). The commonest:

- **Contrast**: *Ben çay içiyorum, sen kahve içiyorsun* "I'm drinking tea, you're drinking coffee".
- **Focus**, "it's me who": *Bu mektubu ben yazıyorum*, with the stress on *ben*.
- **Choosing from a group**: "Who wants tea? — *Ben!*"
- **Opening a conversation or a new topic**: *Ayşe, ben şimdi çıkıyorum* "Ayşe, I'm going out now".

**O and onlar for people.** Because *o* only says "3rd person", it is used for someone already clearly identified, usually just mentioned; otherwise a name or a noun is clearer (goksel2005 §18.1.5). It covers "he", "she" and "it" alike: Turkish has no gender (tur-02-02).

**Siz and sen.** *Siz* is plural "you", and also the polite "you" to one person: strangers, older people, superiors. *Sen* is for friends, family, children (goksel2005 §18.1.1). The choice runs through the verb endings, possessives and pronouns together: *Siz nasılsınız?* (tur-03-12), *sizin eviniz*.

**Locative "on me".** *Bende*, *sende*, *onda* combine with *var* and *yok* for "have on me" (tur-03-09): *Bende para yok* "I haven't got any money on me".

## Where it applies

- **As objects**, always in the accusative: *beni*, *seni*, *onu*, *bizi*, *sizi*, *onları* (goksel2005 §22.1).
- **As receivers and goals**: *bana vermek*, *sana söylemek*, *ona yardım etmek*, *bize gelmek* "come to us, come to our place".
- **As partners of dative verbs**: *bana bakıyor*, *sana inanıyorum*, *ona güveniyoruz*, *size teşekkür ediyorum*.
- **As sources and causes**: *benden korkuyor*, *ondan bir mektup*, *sizden memnunuz*.
- **In comparisons**: *Ali benden uzun* "Ali is taller than me" (tur-05-02).
- **"On me", "with you"**: *bende*, *sende* with *var* and *yok*; *Anahtar sende mi?*
- **"At our place"**: *bizde* "at our house, in our family", *bize* "to our place".
- **As owners**: *benim*, *senin*, *onun* for contrast or focus (tur-03-05), and as predicates: *Bu benim*.
- **As subjects**, only for contrast, focus, choice or a new topic (goksel2005 §18.1.5.1).
- **After postpositions**: *benim için* "for me", with the genitive (tur-05-05).
- **With "with"**: *benimle* "with me" (tur-03-13).

## Watch out

**Do not regularise the dative.** *\*Bene* and *\*sene* are wrong: *bana*, *sana*. But *\*baza* and *\*saza* are wrong too: *bize*, *size* are regular.

**Do not attach a case suffix straight to o.** *\*Ou*, *\*oa*, *\*oda* (which is "room"!) are wrong: *onu*, *ona*, *onda*.

**Do not use the subject pronoun every time.** *Ben Türkçe öğreniyorum. Ben her gün çalışıyorum* repeats *ben* where Spanish *yo* would also be dropped. Say it once, or not at all: *Türkçe öğreniyorum. Her gün çalışıyorum.*

**Do not use a pronoun to repeat a known object.** English needs "I know him"; Turkish answers *Evet, tanıyorum*. *Evet, onu tanıyorum* is correct but marks *onu*, as if contrasting him with others.

**Do not use the wrong case because English has none.** "Look at me", "believe me", "help me", "thank you" have direct objects in English, but *bakmak*, *inanmak*, *yardım etmek* and *teşekkür etmek* take the dative: *\*Beni bakıyorsun* is wrong: *Bana bakıyorsun.*

**Do not use o for things you can point at.** For an object in view Turkish uses *bu* or *şu* (tur-03-11). *O* is "it" mainly for something already mentioned.

**Do not use sen with strangers.** *Sen nereden geliyorsun?* to an older stranger is rude. *Siz nereden geliyorsunuz?* All the endings change with it.

**Do not double "my" with benim when the owner is the subject.** *\*Ben benim çantamı arıyorum* is wrong (tur-03-05): *Çantamı arıyorum.*

**Do not confuse onun "his" with onu "him".** *Onun arabası* "his car" is genitive; *onu görüyorum* "I see him" is accusative.

## Connections

Japanese and Korean drop pronouns even more freely than Turkish, and Japanese uses 彼 "he" far less than English uses "he", much as Turkish is sparing with *o*. Spanish drops subject pronouns as Turkish does, because the verb ending says who, and uses *yo*, *tú* for contrast (*yo bebo té, tú bebes café*): the same rule. French *tu*/*vous* matches *sen*/*siz* closely.

## Exercises

1. Give the dative and ablative of all six pronouns.
2. Say "He's looking at me" and "I'm looking at him".
3. Say "Are you (polite) helping them?"
4. Translate *Bize inanmıyorlar.*
5. Say "Is this book yours (one friend)? — No, it's hers."
6. Correct *\*Bene bir hediye veriyor* ("He's giving me a present").
7. In *Ayşe nerede? — Bilmiyorum, onu görmüyorum*, could *onu* be left out? What would change?
8. Say "We miss you (one friend)" and "They miss us".
9. Why is it *onda* but *bende*?
10. Say "I'm phoning you (polite) now."

## Worked answers

1. *Bana, sana, ona, bize, size, onlara*; *benden, senden, ondan, bizden, sizden, onlardan*. Only *bana* and *sana* are irregular; *o* adds *n* before the case suffix.
2. *Bana bakıyor.* — *Ona bakıyorum.* *Bakmak* takes the dative (tur-03-02).
3. *Onlara yardım ediyor musunuz?* *Yardım etmek* takes the dative; *mu-sunuz* is polite.
4. "They don't believe us." *İnanmak* takes the dative: *bize*. The negative is inside the verb (tur-02-09).
5. *Bu kitap senin mi? — Hayır, onun.* Genitive pronouns as predicates (tur-03-05).
6. *Bana bir hediye veriyor.* The dative of *ben* is *bana*.
7. Yes: *Bilmiyorum, görmüyorum* is natural, because Ayşe has just been named (goksel2005 §28.4.3). Saying *onu* adds a little weight to "her", but the meaning is the same.
8. *Seni özlüyoruz.* — *Bizi özlüyorlar.* *Özlemek* takes a direct object in the accusative.
9. *O* always takes *n* before a case suffix (goksel2005 §6.2); *ben* ends in a consonant already. Both are the locative *-DA*.
10. *Şimdi size telefon ediyorum.* *Telefon etmek* takes the dative; *etmek* becomes *ed-* before the vowel of *-(I)yor*.
