## The idea

English points with two words, "this" and "that". Turkish has three: *bu*, *şu* and *o*. A **demonstrative** is a word that points at something: "this book", "that one".

Göksel and Kerslake describe the three as a scale of distance with a twist (goksel2005 §18.2):

- ***bu*** "this": near the speaker, or something already being talked about.
- ***o*** "that": further away, often out of sight, and usually something already mentioned. It is the same word as the pronoun *o* "he, she, it" (tur-03-10).
- ***şu*** "this, that (here, look)": something the speaker is drawing attention to *for the first time*, usually with a look or a gesture. It can also point forward to what is about to be said: *Sorun şu:* "the problem is this:".

So the main difference between *bu* and *şu* is not distance but newness. *Bu* is "this one we're dealing with"; *şu* is "this one, look". Spanish has three demonstratives too (*este*, *ese*, *aquel*), Japanese and Korean have three series (こ・そ・あ, 이・그・저), but none of them divides the work in quite the same way.

**Two uses of each word.** Each demonstrative can stand before a noun as a **determiner**, a word that limits which thing a noun means: *bu kitap* "this book". Or it can stand alone as a **pronoun**: *bu* "this one". As a determiner it takes no suffixes; the noun does: *bu kitabı* "this book (object)". As a pronoun it takes them itself, with the *n* that *o* always shows (goksel2005 §6.2): *bunu* "this (object)", *buna* "to this", *şundan* "from that".

**Places.** From the same three roots come *bura-* "here", *şura-* "there (that place I'm showing you)" and *ora-* "there". They never stand bare: they always have a case or a possessive (goksel2005 §18.3.1). *Burada* "here, in this place", *buraya* "(to) here", *buradan* "from here"; *burası* "this place".

## How it is built

**As a determiner**: demonstrative + noun, with the case on the noun.

1. "I'm reading this book": the object is definite because of *bu*, so accusative on the noun: *Bu kitabı okuyorum.*
2. "Look at that house": *bakmak* takes the dative, on the noun: *Şu eve bak*, preview of the imperative (tur-04-02).

**As a pronoun**: the demonstrative takes the case, after *n*.

- *bu*: *bunu*, *buna*, *bunda*, *bundan*, *bunun*; plural *bunlar* "these", *bunları*...
- *şu*: *şunu*, *şuna*, *şunda*, *şundan*, *şunun*; *şunlar*.
- *o*: *onu*, *ona*, *onda*, *ondan*, *onun*; *onlar*.

So *onlar* is both "they" and "those"; context decides.

**Places**, step by step:

1. Where something is: locative: *burada*, *şurada*, *orada* "here, (just) there, there".
2. Where to: dative: *buraya*, *şuraya*, *oraya*.
3. Where from: ablative: *buradan*, *şuradan*, *oradan*.
4. The place as a thing, a subject or object: the 3SG possessive: *burası* "this place, here", *orası* "that place". *Burası çok sessiz* "it's very quiet here".

In speech the *a* before *-DA* and *-DAn* often drops: *burda*, *orda*, *nerden* (goksel2005 §6.2). Write the full forms.

**Manner.** *Böyle* "like this, so", *şöyle* "like this (as I'll show you)", *öyle* "like that" (tur-02-05) are the manner words of the same set.

**Stress.** *Burada*, *orada* and the other place words are stressed on their first syllable, *búrada*, *órada*, because their *-rA* does not take stress (goksel2005 §4.3.2).

**İşte.** *İşte* "here it is, there you are" presents something: *İşte anahtar!* "Here's the key!" It is a separate word from *işte* "at work" (*iş* + locative), which sounds the same.

## Where it applies

- **Pointing at a thing near you**: *bu kitap*, *bu*; something just mentioned: *Bu çok önemli* "this is very important".
- **Drawing attention to something new**, with a gesture: *şu bina*, *Şuna bak* "look at that".
- **Pointing forward to what you are about to say**: *Sorun şu:*, *Şöyle:* "like this:" (goksel2005 §18.2).
- **Something far, out of sight, or mentioned before**: *o dükkân*, *O film çok güzel* "that film (we talked about) is very good".
- **As pronouns with any case**: *bunu*, *buna*, *şundan*, *onun*; plural *bunlar*, *şunlar*, *onlar*.
- **Making the object definite**: *bu kitabı*, *şu kazağı* (tur-03-01).
- **Places**: *burada*, *şurada*, *orada*; *buraya*, *oraya*; *buradan*, *oradan*; *burası*, *orası* as subjects; *burası*, *burayı* as objects (goksel2005 §18.3.1).
- **Time**: *bu sabah* "this morning", *bu akşam* "this evening", *bu yıl* "this year", *o gün* "that day".
- **Manner**: *böyle*, *şöyle*, *öyle*: *Böyle yapıyoruz* "we do it like this".
- **Presenting**: *İşte anahtar*, *İşte burası* "here's the place".

## Watch out

**Do not put the case on a determiner.** *\*Bunu kitap okuyorum* is wrong. Before a noun the demonstrative stays bare and the noun takes the case: *Bu kitabı okuyorum.*

**Do not forget the n on a pronoun.** *\*Buyu*, *\*şuya*, *\*buda* are wrong: *bunu*, *şuna*, *bunda*.

**Do not use bura- bare.** *\*Bura güzel* is wrong: *Burası güzel* "it's nice here" (goksel2005 §18.3.1).

**Do not use the locative for "to here".** English "come here" has no preposition. *\*Buradaya* and *\*Burada geliyor* are wrong: *Buraya geliyor* "he's coming here".

**Do not use burası for "here" as a place of action.** *\*Burası çalışıyorum* is wrong: where something happens is locative: *Burada çalışıyorum*. *Burası* is "this place" as a subject or object.

**Do not treat şu as "that" by distance alone.** A Spanish speaker may map *este/ese/aquel* onto *bu/şu/o*. *Şu* is mainly "this, look", for something new; *o* is for what is out of sight or already mentioned (goksel2005 §18.2).

**Do not use o for something you are pointing at.** For a thing in view that you are showing, *şu* or *bu*; *o* points back to something known.

**Do not leave a demonstrative object bare.** *\*Bu film izliyoruz* is wrong: *bu* makes the object definite, so *Bu filmi izliyoruz.*

**Do not read onlar always as "they".** *Onlar benim değil* is "those aren't mine". Context decides between people and things.

## Connections

Japanese こ・そ・あ (これ, それ, あれ; ここ, そこ, あそこ) and Korean 이・그・저 also have three degrees and build place words from them, as Turkish builds *burada*, *şurada*, *orada*. The Japanese and Korean middle term そ/그 is mainly "near you" or "the one just mentioned"; Turkish *şu* is "the one I'm showing you", so the three systems do not match term for term. Spanish *aquí*, *ahí*, *allí* follow distance.

## Exercises

1. Put *bu* (pronoun) into all five cases and the plural.
2. Say "this city", "this city" as an object, and "from this city".
3. Say "Is the toilet there?" and "He's coming here".
4. Translate *Oradan buraya otobüs var mı?*
5. Say "It's very quiet here."
6. Correct *\*Bunu ev çok büyük* and *\*Şuya bakıyor*.
7. When would you say *Bu ne?* and when *Şu ne?*
8. Say "Those aren't ours."
9. Take apart *buranın* and say what *buranın müzesi* might mean.
10. Why is it *Burada çalışıyorum* but *Burası güzel*?

## Worked answers

1. *Bunu*, *buna*, *bunda*, *bundan*, *bunun*; *bunlar*. The pronoun takes the *n* before every suffix (goksel2005 §6.2).
2. *Bu şehir*; *bu şehri*; *bu şehirden*. The demonstrative stays bare; the noun takes the case, losing its *i* only before a vowel (tur-03-01).
3. *Tuvalet orada mı?* (or *şurada mı?* while pointing). *Buraya geliyor.* Movement to here is dative.
4. "Is there a bus from there to here?" *Oradan* is the starting point, *buraya* the goal; *var mı* asks whether one exists (tur-02-06).
5. *Burası çok sessiz.* *Burası* is "this place" as the subject.
6. *Bu ev çok büyük*: as a determiner, *bu* takes no case. *Şuna bakıyor*: as a pronoun *şu* needs *n* before the dative, and *bakmak* takes the dative.
7. *Bu ne?* for something you are both looking at or dealing with; *Şu ne?* when you spot something new and point at it (goksel2005 §18.2).
8. *Onlar bizim değil.* *Onlar* "those"; the genitive *bizim* as predicate (tur-03-05).
9. *Bura-nın*: here + genitive, "of this place". *Buranın müzesi* is "this place's museum", the museum here.
10. *Burada* is a locative: where the working happens. *Burası* is "this place" as the subject of *güzel*.
