## The idea

A yes-no question needs *mI* (tur-02-05). A question that asks *who*, *what*, *where* and so on needs a **question word** instead, and no *mI*. The question word does the asking on its own: *Ali geliyor mu?* "Is Ali coming?", but *Kim geliyor?* "Who is coming?"

**The question word stays in place.** English moves its question word to the front: "You saw *Ali*" becomes "*Who* did you see?". Turkish does not move anything. It puts the question word where the answer will go, and the answer goes, as new information always does, just before the verb (tur-02-07). So the commonest place for a question word is right before the predicate (goksel2005 §19.2.2):

- *Ali'yi gördüm* "I saw Ali" (preview of the past) → *Kimi gördün?* "Whom did you see?"
- *Ali evde* → *Ali nerede?* "Where is Ali?"
- *Ayşe yarın geliyor* → *Ayşe ne zaman geliyor?* "When is Ayşe coming?"

Japanese and Korean work the same way: 誰を見た？, 누구를 봤어? keep 誰 and 누구 where the object stands.

**Question words are nouns, so they take cases.** *Kim* "who" and *ne* "what" decline like any noun: *kimi* "whom", *kime* "to whom", *kimden* "from whom", *kimin* "whose"; *neyi*, *neye*, *neden* (goksel2005 §19.2.1). The case is chosen exactly as it would be for the answer. If the answer is *Ali'ye*, the question is *kime*. *Nere-* "where" is a stem like *bura-*: it always takes a case (tur-03-11).

**Question words carry the stress** of the sentence (goksel2005 §19.2): *KİM geliyor?* with the stress on *kim*.

## How it is built

**The words** (goksel2005 §19.2):

- *kim* "who"; *ne* "what";
- *nerede* "where (at)", *nereye* "where (to)", *nereden* "where from", *neresi* "what place";
- *ne zaman* "when";
- *nasıl* "how";
- *neden*, *niçin*, *niye* "why";
- *hangi* "which" (before a noun), *hangisi* "which one";
- *kaç* "how many", *ne kadar* "how much, how long".

**The chain of thought**, for "Who are you giving the book to?":

1. Build the answer sentence first: *Kitabı Ayşe'ye veriyorum.*
2. Replace the unknown with the question word in the same case: *Ayşe'ye* → *kime*.
3. Keep it before the verb, and change the ending to "you": *Kitabı kime veriyorsun?*
4. No *mI*.

**Accusative or not.** Objects follow tur-03-01. *Kim* as object is always *kimi* (goksel2005 §22.1). *Ne* as object is usually bare, *Ne istiyorsun?* "What do you want?", but *Neyi istiyorsun?* asks "which of these things do you want?" (goksel2005 §19.2.1.2). *Hangi* + noun as object always takes the accusative: *Hangi kitabı okuyorsun?*

**Where.** Choose the case as for a place: *nerede* (locative) for where something is, *nereye* (dative) for where to, *nereden* (ablative) for where from. *Neresi* asks what a place is: *Burası neresi?* "Where are we? What is this place?" (goksel2005 §19.2.1.3). *Nereli* asks where someone is from: *Nerelisiniz?* "Where are you from?" (tdk).

**How many, how much.** *Kaç* stands where a number would, and the noun stays singular: *Kaç kardeşin var?* "How many brothers and sisters do you have?" *Saat kaç?* "What time is it?" *Kaç yaşındasın?* "How old are you?", literally "you are in how many of age". *Ne kadar* asks about amount, degree and price: *Bu kazak ne kadar?* "How much is this jumper?" (goksel2005 §§19.2.1.5, 19.2.1.6).

**Personal endings.** A question word can be the predicate and take personal endings: *Nasılsın?* "How are you?", *Kimsiniz?* "Who are you?", *Nerelisin?* "Where are you from?".

**Stress.** *Nerede*, *nereye*, *nasıl*, *nereli* are stressed on the first syllable: *nérede*, *násıl* (goksel2005 §4.3.2; tdk).

## Where it applies

- **Who**: *kim*, as subject (*Kim geliyor?*), predicate (*O kim?*), and in every case: *kimi*, *kime*, *kimde*, *kimden*, *kimin*. *Kimler* asks about several people (goksel2005 §19.2.1.1).
- **What**: *ne*, bare as object (*Ne istiyorsun?*) or in the accusative for a choice among known things (*Neyi istiyorsun?*); *neye bakıyorsun?* "what are you looking at?"; *neler* "what things" (goksel2005 §19.2.1.2).
- **Where**: *nerede*, *nereye*, *nereden*; *neresi* for the identity of a place; *nereli* for origin.
- **When**: *ne zaman*: *Ne zaman geliyorsun?*
- **How**: *nasıl*, for manner (*Nasıl gidiyoruz?*) and condition (*Nasılsınız?*); before a noun, "what kind of": *nasıl bir ev* (goksel2005 §19.2.1.9).
- **Why**: *neden*, *niçin*, *niye*: same meaning; *neden* may stand anywhere before the predicate (goksel2005 §19.2.2).
- **Which**: *hangi* + noun (*hangi kitap*), and *hangisi* "which one" (*Hangisi senin?*).
- **How many**: *kaç* + singular noun; *saat kaç?*; *kaç yaşında?*; *kaçta?* "at what time?" (tur-05-04).
- **How much**: *ne kadar*: price (*Bu ne kadar?*), amount, degree.
- **Inside a phrase**: *kimin evi* "whose house", *hangi şehirde* "in which city", *ne kadar şeker* "how much sugar".
- **With personal endings**: *Nasılsın?*, *Kimsin?*, *Nerelisiniz?*, *Neredesin?* "where are you?".

## Watch out

**Do not add mI to a question word.** *\*Kim geliyor mu?* and *\*Nerede mi?* are wrong: *Kim geliyor?*, *Nerede?* The question word already asks.

**Do not move the question word to the front.** *\*Kimi Ali görüyor?*, by analogy with "Whom does Ali see?", is wrong as a neutral question: it puts *Ali*, not the question word, in the slot before the verb. Keep the question word where the answer goes, just before the verb: *Ali kimi görüyor?* (goksel2005 §19.2.2).

**Do not forget the case.** "Who are you talking to?" is not *\*Kim konuşuyorsun?* ("who are you, talking?"). The verb *konuşmak* takes the partner with *-(y)lA* (tur-03-13): *Kiminle konuşuyorsun?* Likewise "Who are you looking at?": *Kime bakıyorsun?*

**Do not leave kim bare as an object.** *\*Kim arıyorsun?* is wrong: *Kimi arıyorsun?* (goksel2005 §22.1).

**Do not use nerede for "where to".** *\*Nerede gidiyorsun?* is wrong: *Nereye gidiyorsun?* Spanish distinguishes *dónde* and *adónde* in the same way, and French *où* does not.

**Do not use nere- bare.** *\*Nere?* is non-standard (goksel2005 §19.2.1.3). Say *Nerede?*, *Nereye?*, *Neresi?*

**Do not use the plural after kaç.** *\*Kaç kardeşlerin var?* is wrong: *Kaç kardeşin var?*

**Do not confuse neden "why" and neden "from what".** *Neden korkuyorsun?* can mean either "why are you afraid?" or "what are you afraid of?" (*korkmak* takes the ablative). The context decides; *niye korkuyorsun?* can only be "why".

**Do not use ne for "what ... like".** *\*Hava ne?* does not ask about the weather. English "what is it like?" is *nasıl*: *Hava nasıl?* "What's the weather like?" But "what is your name?" asks for a thing: *Adın ne?*

## Connections

Japanese 何, 誰, どこ and Korean 무엇, 누구, 어디 stay in place like the Turkish words, and they too take the case of the answer (誰に, 누구에게 "to whom" like *kime*). Japanese still adds か at the end of a question-word question, which Turkish never does with *mI*. Spanish *¿adónde?* and *¿de dónde?* match *nereye* and *nereden*.

## Exercises

1. Ask about the word in brackets: *Ayşe [Ankara'ya] gidiyor*; *Mehmet [kahve] içiyor*; *[Ali] geliyor*.
2. Ask "Where is the museum?" and "Where are you (polite) going?"
3. Ask "Whose car is this?"
4. Ask "Who are you giving the present to?" (to a friend).
5. Ask "Which book are you reading?" and explain the case.
6. Ask "How many children do they have?"
7. Correct *\*Nerede gidiyorsun mu?*
8. Ask "What is this place?" and "Where are you from?" (to a friend).
9. Translate *Ne zaman Türkiye'ye dönüyorsunuz?*
10. Why is it *Ne istiyorsun?* but *Kimi istiyorsun?*

## Worked answers

1. *Ayşe nereye gidiyor?* — *Mehmet ne içiyor?* — *Kim geliyor?* Each question word takes the place and case of the answer: dative *nereye* for *Ankara'ya*, bare *ne* for the bare object *kahve*, *kim* for the subject.
2. *Müze nerede?* — *Nereye gidiyorsunuz?* Location takes *nerede*, movement to takes *nereye*; the polite ending is *-sunuz*.
3. *Bu araba kimin?* The genitive *kimin* is the predicate (tur-03-05).
4. *Hediyeyi kime veriyorsun?* The receiver is dative: *kime*. The present is known: *hediyeyi* (tur-03-02, tur-03-01).
5. *Hangi kitabı okuyorsun?* A noun with *hangi* is definite in kind (one of a known set), and as an object it always takes the accusative (goksel2005 §19.2.1.4).
6. *Kaç çocukları var?* The 3PL possessive *-lArI* on *çocuk*; *kaç* takes the singular (tur-03-09).
7. *Nereye gidiyorsun?* Movement takes *nereye*, and a question word never takes *mI*.
8. *Burası neresi?* — *Nerelisin?* *Neresi* asks about the place itself (goksel2005 §19.2.1.3); *nereli* takes the personal ending.
9. "When are you going back to Turkey?" *Ne zaman* stands before the verb; *Türkiye'ye* is the goal.
10. *Ne* as object is bare when nothing particular is expected (goksel2005 §19.2.1.2). *Kim* as object always takes the accusative (goksel2005 §22.1).
