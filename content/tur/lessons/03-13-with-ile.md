## The idea

English "with" covers a companion ("with Ali"), a tool ("with a knife"), a manner ("with pleasure"), and "by" covers a means of travel ("by bus"). Turkish covers all four with one word, ***ile***, which in practice is almost always attached to the noun as a suffix, ***-(y)lA***: *Ali'yle* "with Ali", *bıçakla* "with a knife", *zevkle* "with pleasure", *otobüsle* "by bus" (goksel2005 §§8.1.4, 17.2.1).

Göksel and Kerslake call it the **comitative-instrumental** marker: "comitative" for the companion, "instrumental" for the tool or means. This guide glosses it `INS`. It behaves partly like a case and partly like a postposition (a word placed after a noun to show its relation, like English "with" placed before). Göksel and Kerslake list five case suffixes and treat it separately (goksel2005 §14.3.3); other grammars count it as a sixth or seventh case. What matters for you is how it works.

**The same marker means "and".** *Ali'yle Ayşe* is "Ali and Ayşe": two people, side by side, with the first "with" the second (goksel2005 §28.3.1.1). *Annemle babam* "my mother and father". This use joins nouns only; *ve* joins anything.

**It does not take the stress.** Unlike the case suffixes, *-(y)lA* is unstressable and puts the stress on the syllable before it: *kalémle*, *arabáyla*, *Alí'yle* (goksel2005 §§4.3.2, 8.1.4).

## How it is built

**The suffix -(y)lA.** Two-way harmony (tur-01-04); a buffer *y* after a vowel.

1. **After a consonant**: *-la*, *-le*: *kalem-le*, *otobüs-le*, *tren-le*, *uçak-la*.
2. **After a vowel**: *-yla*, *-yle*: *araba-yla*, *taksi-yle*, *Ayşe'yle*, *metro-yla*.
3. **No stem change**: it starts with a consonant (or the buffer *y*), so *kitap* stays: *kitapla*; *uçak* stays: *uçakla*.
4. **Proper names** keep the apostrophe: *Ali'yle*, *Mehmet'le*.

**The separate word ile** means the same and is written after the noun: *Ali ile*, *otobüs ile*. Göksel and Kerslake note that it is much rarer than the suffix (goksel2005 §8.1.4); you will see it mainly in formal writing.

**After a possessive.** *-(y)lA* goes last, after the possessive, like a case, but it takes **no** pronominal *n*: *annem-le* "with my mother", *araba-m-la* "with my car", *araba-sı-yla* "with his car", *el-i-yle* "with his hand" (goksel2005 §§4.3.2, 8.1.4).

**After pronouns**, the genitive comes first for the singular pronouns, *biz*, *siz*, the demonstratives and *kim* (goksel2005 §8.1.4):

- *benimle* "with me", *seninle*, *onunla*, *bizimle*, *sizinle*;
- *bununla* "with this", *şununla*, *onunla*;
- *kiminle* "with whom".

Plural *onlar* takes it directly: *onlarla* "with them". In everyday speech *benle*, *kimle* are also heard (goksel2005 §8.1.4).

**The chain of thought**, for "I'm going to Ankara with my brother by train":

1. The verb: *gidiyorum*.
2. The goal: *Ankara'ya* (tur-03-02).
3. The companion: *ağabey* + *-(I)m* + *-(y)lA*. *Ağabeyim* ends in *m*: *-le*. *Ağabeyimle.*
4. The means: *tren* + *-le*: *trenle*.
5. Order: companion and means before the goal or after it; the newest information nearest the verb: *Ağabeyimle trenle Ankara'ya gidiyorum.*

**Birlikte and beraber.** "Together with" adds *birlikte* or *beraber* after the *-(y)lA* phrase: *Ali'yle birlikte* "together with Ali". On their own they mean "together": *Birlikte çalışıyoruz* "we work together".

## Where it applies

- **A companion**: *Ali'yle*, *annemle*, *arkadaşlarımla*; with *birlikte* or *beraber* for "together with" (goksel2005 §17.2.1).
- **A partner in verbs of doing something together**: *konuşmak* "talk with", *tanışmak* "meet", *evlenmek* "marry", and *ilgilenmek* "be interested in, take care of" (goksel2005 §13.1.2.2; wiktionary).
- **A tool**: *bıçakla*, *kalemle*, *elle* "by hand", *kaşıkla*.
- **Means of transport or communication**: *otobüsle*, *trenle*, *uçakla*, *vapurla*, *taksiyle*; *telefonla* "by phone", *mektupla* "by letter" (goksel2005 §17.2.1).
- **Manner**: *zevkle* "with pleasure", *dikkatle* "carefully", *sevgiyle* "with love".
- **"And" between nouns**: *Ali'yle Ayşe*, *annemle babam*, *kahveyle çay* (goksel2005 §28.3.1.1).
- **After some adjectives**: *ilgili* "related to": *müzikle ilgili bir kitap* "a book about music" (goksel2005 §15.5).
- **Question**: *Kiminle?* "With whom?", *Neyle?* "With what? By what means?" (goksel2005 §19.2.1.2).
- **After pronouns**: *benimle*, *seninle*, *onunla*, *bizimle*, *sizinle*, *onlarla*; *bununla*, *şununla*.

## Watch out

**Do not confuse -(y)lA with -lI.** Both translate as "with", but *-lI* (tur-01-05) makes an adjective describing a noun: *sütlü kahve* "coffee with milk", *şekerli çay* "tea with sugar". *-(y)lA* links a noun to a verb or to another noun: *kahveyi sütle içiyorum* "I drink coffee with milk". *\*Sütle kahve istiyorum* for "I'd like a coffee with milk" means "I want milk and coffee". Say *Sütlü kahve istiyorum.*

**Do not put the n after a possessive.** *\*Arabasınla* and *\*elinle* for "with his car", "with his hand" are wrong: *arabasıyla*, *eliyle*. (*Elinle* exists, but means "with your hand".)

**Do not attach it to bare singular pronouns.** *\*Benle* and *\*kimle* are colloquial; in standard Turkish: *benimle*, *kiminle*. But *\*onlarınla* is wrong the other way: *onlarla*.

**Do not stress it.** *Kalemlé* is wrong: *kalémle*. The stress falls before *-(y)lA* (goksel2005 §4.3.2).

**Do not use the dative with konuşmak for "talk to".** *Ali'ye konuşuyorum* is odd for a conversation; *Ali'yle konuşuyorum* (wiktionary). English, Spanish (*hablar a*) and French (*parler à*) all push towards the dative.

**Do not use the accusative with evlenmek and tanışmak.** *\*Ayşe'yi evleniyor* is wrong: *Ayşe'yle evleniyor*. *Tanımak* "know someone" takes the accusative; *tanışmak* "meet (each other)" takes *-(y)lA*: *Onu tanıyorum*, but *Onunla tanışıyorum.*

**Do not use -(y)lA for "and" between verbs or sentences.** *\*Okuyorumla yazıyorum* is impossible. Between clauses use *ve*: *Okuyorum ve yazıyorum.*

**Do not drop the buffer.** *\*Arabala*, *\*taksile* are wrong after a vowel: *arabayla*, *taksiyle*.

## Connections

Japanese uses two particles for this work, と for companions ("友達と", which also means "and") and で for tools and means ("バスで"); Korean has 와/과 or 하고 and (으)로. Turkish *-(y)lA* covers both, and its "and" use matches Japanese と exactly. Spanish *con* and French *avec* cover the companion, the tool and the manner, but not transport (*en autobús*, *en bus*).

## Exercises

1. Add *-(y)lA*: *tren*, *vapur*, *metro*, *kaşık*, *Mehmet*, *kız kardeşim*.
2. Say "with me", "with them" and "with whom?".
3. Say "I'm going home by taxi."
4. Say "Ayşe is talking to her mother on the phone."
5. Translate *Ali'yle Mehmet bir bankada çalışıyor.*
6. What is the difference between *şekerli çay* and *çayı şekerle içiyorum*?
7. Correct *\*Ayşe'yi evleniyor* and *\*Arabasınla geliyor* ("he's coming in his car").
8. Say "Are you interested in football?" (to a friend).
9. Where is the stress in *kalemle* and *arabayla*, and why?
10. Say "I'm eating with a fork and a knife."

## Worked answers

1. *Trenle*, *vapurla*, *metroyla*, *kaşıkla*, *Mehmet'le*, *kız kardeşimle*. Two-way harmony; *y* only after a vowel; no stem change.
2. *Benimle*, *onlarla*, *kiminle?* Singular pronouns and *kim* take the genitive first; *onlar* does not (goksel2005 §8.1.4).
3. *Eve taksiyle gidiyorum.* The goal *eve* is dative; the means *taksiyle*.
4. *Ayşe annesiyle telefonla konuşuyor.* *Annesiyle*: after the 3SG possessive, *y*, not *n*. *Telefonla* "by phone".
5. "Ali and Mehmet work at a bank." *-(y)lA* joins the two subjects; the verb may stay singular or take *-lAr*.
6. *Şekerli çay* is "sweetened tea", a kind of tea (*-lI* makes an adjective). *Çayı şekerle içiyorum* is "I drink the tea with sugar", the sugar linked to the drinking.
7. *Ayşe'yle evleniyor*: *evlenmek* takes *-(y)lA*. *Arabasıyla geliyor*: no *n* before *-(y)lA*.
8. *Futbolla ilgileniyor musun?* *İlgilenmek* takes *-(y)lA*.
9. *Kalémle*, *arabáyla*: *-(y)lA* is unstressable and puts the stress on the syllable before it (goksel2005 §4.3.2).
10. *Çatalla bıçakla yiyorum* or *Çatal ve bıçakla yiyorum.* Each tool can take *-(y)lA*, or *ve* can join the two before one *-(y)lA*.
