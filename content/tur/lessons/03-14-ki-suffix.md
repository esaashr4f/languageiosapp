## The idea

A locative phrase like *masada* "on the table" can be a predicate: *Kitap masada* "the book is on the table" (tur-02-06). But it cannot stand before a noun to describe it. "The book on the table" is not *\*masada kitap*. Turkish first turns the place into a describing word with the suffix ***-ki***: *masadaki kitap* "the book on the table", literally "the on-the-table book".

Göksel and Kerslake give *-ki* two functions (goksel2005 §8.1.4): forming **adjectival phrases** that stand before a noun (*masadaki kitap*), and forming **pronouns** that stand for a noun (*masadaki* "the one on the table"). It attaches to three kinds of word:

1. **a locative**: *ev-de-ki* "(the one) in the house", *sokak-ta-ki araba* "the car in the street";
2. **a time word**: *bugün-kü gazete* "today's newspaper", *yarın-ki toplantı* "tomorrow's meeting", *şimdi-ki* "the present (one)";
3. **a genitive**, as a pronoun only: *benim-ki* "mine", *Ali'nin-ki* "Ali's (one)".

**Why is it needed?** Turkish puts every modifier before its noun (tur-02-07), and the modifier has to be adjective-like. Nouns in the locative or the genitive are not; *-ki* makes them so. English can say "the book on the table" with the phrase after the noun; Turkish must convert the phrase and move it in front.

**An odd suffix.** *-ki* does not follow vowel harmony. It is *-ki* after any vowel: *masadaki*, *odadaki*, *Ali'ninki*. The one exception: after *dün* "yesterday" and *gün* "day" (and words containing *gün*) it is usually *-kü*: *dünkü*, *bugünkü* (goksel2005 §8.1.4). It is stressable: *evdekí* (goksel2005 §4.3.1). It should not be confused with the separate word *ki* "that" (tur-06-12).

## How it is built

**Describing a noun**, step by step, for "the children in the garden":

1. The place: *bahçe* in the locative: *bahçede*.
2. Turn it into a describing word: *-ki*: *bahçedeki*.
3. Put it before the noun: *bahçedeki çocuklar*.

**Standing for a noun.** Leave the noun out when it is clear: *Bahçedeki çocuklar oynuyor. Evdekiler uyuyor* "The children in the garden are playing. The ones in the house are asleep." The *-ki* word then takes plural and case suffixes like a noun (goksel2005 §8.1.4):

- plural: *evdeki-ler* "the ones in the house";
- case, with *n* before it, as after the 3rd-person possessive (tur-03-06): *evdeki-ni* "the one in the house (object)", *masadaki-ne* "to the one on the table", *benimki-nden* "from mine".

**Possessive pronouns.** *-ki* after a genitive gives "mine", "yours", "Ali's" and so on: *benimki*, *seninki*, *onunki*, *bizimki*, *sizinki*, *onlarınki*; *Ali'ninki*, *annemin-ki*. These are pronouns only: they never stand before a noun (goksel2005 §18.5).

**Time words.** *-ki* goes directly on words of time: *bugünkü*, *dünkü*, *yarınki*, *şimdiki*, *akşamki* "this evening's", *sabahki* "this morning's". A phrase with *bu* works too: *bu yılki* "this year's" (goksel2005 §8.1.4). *Önce* and *sonra* give *önceki* "previous" and *sonraki* "next, following" (tdk).

**Stacking.** Because *-ki* makes a noun-like word, it can go through the cycle again: *evdekilerin* "of the ones in the house", *Ali'ninkiler* "Ali's ones". Göksel and Kerslake show long chains (goksel2005 §8.1.4), but two layers are as far as you need.

## Where it applies

- **After a locative, before a noun**: *masadaki kitap*, *sokaktaki araba*, *İstanbul'daki arkadaşım* "my friend in Istanbul", *Türkiye'deki üniversiteler* "the universities in Turkey".
- **After a locative, standing alone**: *masadaki*, *evdekiler*, *dolaptakini*.
- **After place words**: *buradaki* "the one here", *oradaki lokanta* "the restaurant there" (goksel2005 §8.1.4).
- **After a locative possessive phrase**: *bahçesindeki ağaçlar*, *cebindeki para* "the money in his pocket".
- **After time words**: *bugünkü*, *dünkü*, *yarınki*, *şimdiki*, *sabahki*, *akşamki*; with *bu*: *bu yılki*, *bu akşamki*.
- **After önce and sonra**: *önceki*, *sonraki* "previous, next".
- **After a genitive, as a pronoun**: *benimki*, *seninki*, *onunki*, *bizimki*, *sizinki*, *onlarınki*, *Ali'ninki*, *annemin-ki*, *kiminki?* "whose (one)?".
- **With plural and case**: *evdekiler*, *seninkini*, *masadakine*, *benimkinden*.
- **With -(y)lA**: *seninkiyle* "with yours", *sokaktakiyle* "with the one in the street" (goksel2005 §8.1.4).

## Watch out

**Do not put a bare locative before a noun.** *\*Masada kitap benim* is wrong for "the book on the table is mine". The locative must become a describing word: *Masadaki kitap benim.*

**Do not put -ki after the noun.** *\*Kitap masadaki* reverses the order. The describing word comes first: *masadaki kitap*. (*Kitap masada* is fine as a full sentence, "the book is on the table".)

**Do not harmonise -ki.** *\*Sokaktakı*, *\*okuldakı* are wrong: *sokaktaki*, *okuldaki*. Only after *gün* and *dün* is it *-kü*.

**Do not attach -ki to other cases.** *\*Okuldanki* or *\*evekı* are wrong; *-ki* follows only the locative, time words and the genitive (goksel2005 §8.1.4). "The letter from Ali" needs a different construction (part 6).

**Do not use -ki with a genitive before a noun.** *\*Benimki araba* is wrong: *benimki* is a pronoun only (goksel2005 §18.5). "My car" is *benim arabam*; "mine" is *benimki*.

**Do not forget the n before a case.** *\*Evdekiyi*, *\*masadakiye* are wrong: *evdekini*, *masadakine* (goksel2005 §6.2).

**Do not confuse -ki with ki "that".** The suffix is joined to the word; the conjunction *ki* (tur-06-12), from Persian, is written separately: *Biliyorum ki ...* "I know that ...".

**Do not repeat the noun.** *Benim arabam eski, senin araban yeni* is correct but heavy; *seninki* is what speakers say.

## Connections

Japanese の and Korean 의 can turn a place phrase into a modifier (テーブルの上の本 "the book on the table"; 책상 위의 책), close to *masadaki kitap*, and Japanese の also makes "mine" (私の). Turkish *-ki* plays both roles but only after a locative, a time word or a genitive. Spanish *el de la mesa*, *el mío* and French *celui de la table*, *le mien* match the pronoun uses.

## Exercises

1. Make describing words: *oda* + locative + *-ki*; *şehir* + locative + *-ki*; *yarın* + *-ki*; *dün* + *-ki*.
2. Say "the shop in this street" and "the people in the shop".
3. Say "This umbrella isn't mine; mine is in the car."
4. Translate *Sonraki tren kaçta?*
5. Correct *\*Masada çiçekler güzel* ("The flowers on the table are pretty").
6. Correct *\*Sokaktakı adam* and *\*Benimki kitap*.
7. Say "I want the one in the window" (object).
8. Take apart *evdekilerin*.
9. Why is it *bugünkü* but *yarınki*?
10. Say "this year's summer is very hot."

## Worked answers

1. *Odadaki*, *şehirdeki*, *yarınki*, *dünkü*. *-ki* follows the locative and does not harmonise, except after *dün*. *Şehir* keeps its *i* before *-de* (tur-03-01).
2. *Bu sokaktaki dükkân*; *dükkândaki insanlar*. The *-ki* word stands before the noun.
3. *Bu şemsiye benim değil; benimki arabada.* *Benimki* "mine" is a pronoun, here the subject of a locative predicate (tur-02-06).
4. "What time is the next train?" *Sonraki* "next" describes *tren*; *kaçta* is *kaç* + locative, "at what time" (tur-03-12).
5. *Masadaki çiçekler güzel.* A locative cannot describe a noun until *-ki* is added.
6. *Sokaktaki adam*: *-ki* does not harmonise. *Benim kitabım* "my book", or *benimki* "mine" alone: the genitive + *-ki* cannot stand before a noun (goksel2005 §18.5).
7. *Penceredekini istiyorum.* *Pencere-de-ki-ni*: the locative, *-ki*, then the accusative with *n* (goksel2005 §6.2).
8. *Ev-de-ki-ler-in*: house, in, the one, plural, genitive: "of the ones in the house".
9. After *gün* (in *bugün*) and *dün*, *-ki* is usually *-kü*; elsewhere it stays *-ki* (goksel2005 §8.1.4).
10. *Bu yılki yaz çok sıcak.* (Also *bu yaz çok sıcak*, "this summer".) *Bu yıl* is a time phrase, so it takes *-ki* directly.
