## The idea

You already know one verb form well: *gel-mi-yor-um* "I am not coming". Stem, negative, *-(I)yor*, person. Part 4 adds a dozen more suffixes. They do not go anywhere you like. Every Turkish verb that ends a sentence is built on one fixed frame, a row of **slots**, and each new suffix belongs in exactly one of them. This lesson shows the whole frame first, so that every later lesson only fills in one slot.

Göksel and Kerslake give the order of the suffixes in a full verb as (goksel2005 §8.2):

1. **stem**: *gel-*, *yap-*, *oku-*;
2. **voice**: suffixes that change who does what to whom, such as "be done" or "make someone do" (part 7);
3. **negation**: *-mA* (tur-02-09);
4. **ability**: *-(y)Abil* "can" and *-(y)AmA* "cannot" (tur-04-13);
5. **tense, aspect or mood**: one suffix from a closed set: *-(I)yor*, *-DI*, *-(y)AcAK*, the aorist, *-mIş*, *-mAlI* and a few more;
6. **copula**: *-(y)DI* "was", *-(y)mIş* "apparently", *-(y)sA* "if" (tur-04-08);
7. **person**: who does it, *-(y)Im*, *-sIn* and their relatives;
8. **-DIr**: "surely, it is the case that" (tur-04-11).

Slots 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 are optional. Slot 5 is filled in every verb except two kinds of command (tur-04-02). Slot 7 is filled in every such verb, though for "he, she, it" the filler is nothing: *geliyor* (goksel2005 §8.2). A verb built like this is called **finite**: it is complete, carries a person, and can end a sentence. Part 6 teaches the non-finite verbs, which swap slots 5 to 8 for noun endings.

**Why this order?** Each suffix adds its meaning to everything on its left. Read *gel-mi-yor-um* from the stem outwards: "come", then "not come", then "not-coming is going on", then "I am the one". The negative is next to the stem because it changes the action itself. Tense comes later because it places that whole action, negative and all, in time. The person comes last because it says whose the whole package is. Swap any two and the meaning breaks: a person ending inside the stem would say whose the stem is before there is anything to own.

**One tense at a time.** Slot 5 holds one suffix only. Göksel and Kerslake state that suffixes in the same column cannot co-occur on a stem (goksel2005 §8.2.3). So "was coming" cannot be *-(I)yor* plus *-DI*. Turkish solves this with slot 6: the copular markers are separate pieces that can follow any tense, as in *geliyordu* "he was coming" (tur-04-10). The two slots look alike, but they are different jobs.

**The noun predicate uses the same back half.** You met the person endings after nouns in tur-02-02: *öğretmen-im* "I am a teacher". A noun has no slots 2 to 5, but it has slots 6 to 8: *öğretmen-di-m* "I was a teacher" (tur-04-08), *öğretmen-dir* "he is a teacher" (tur-04-11). A verb with its tense is, from slot 6 on, treated like a noun predicate. This is why the same endings serve both.

## How it is built

To build any finite verb, go through the slots from left to right and ask one question at each.

1. **What is the action?** Find the stem by dropping *-mAk*: *yapmak* → *yap-*.
2. **Is the voice changed?** Not yet in this guide.
3. **Is it negative?** Then add *-mA*: *yap-ma-*.
4. **Is it about ability?** Not yet.
5. **What tense or aspect?** Ongoing: *-(I)yor*. After *-mA*, its vowel becomes high and harmonises: *yap-mı-yor*.
6. **Is the whole thing in the past, reported, or conditional?** Preview: for "was", add *-(y)DI*: *yap-mı-yor-du*.
7. **Who?** For "we", after this copula, *-k*: *yap-mı-yor-du-k* "we weren't doing (it)".
8. **Is it a formal or confident statement?** Not here.

Three rules run through every step.

**Harmony looks one step back.** Each suffix takes its vowel from the vowel just before it, not from the stem. In *yap-mı-yor-du-k*, *-mı* follows *a*, *-du* follows the *o* of *-yor*. You saw this in tur-02-08: the ending after *-yor* is always back and rounded.

**Sound changes happen at each joint.** *Git-* becomes *gid-* before a vowel (*gidiyor*) but stays *git-* before *-mA* (*gitmiyor*). A stem-final *a* or *e* turns into a high vowel before *-yor*: *oyna-* → *oynuyor*, *dene-* → *deniyor* (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3). The rule applies to whatever piece stands before the suffix, stem or not.

**Stress falls on the last syllable that can take it.** Most suffixes are stressable and pull the stress to the end. Some are not. Göksel and Kerslake list the copular markers, *-DIr*, the 1st and 2nd person endings of the *-(I)yor* set, and *-mA* among those that cannot take stress (goksel2005 §4.3.2). *-(I)yor* puts the stress before itself, and *-mA* overrides it: *yapíyoruz*, *yápmıyoruz*, *yápmıyorduk*.

## Where it applies

- **Every finite verb**, in every tense this part teaches. Each later lesson names its slot: the past *-DI*, the future *-(y)AcAK*, the aorist and *-mIş* in slot 5 (tur-04-03 to tur-04-07); the copular markers in slot 6 (tur-04-08, tur-04-10); *-DIr* in slot 8 (tur-04-11); *-mAlI* in slot 5 (tur-04-12); *-(y)Abil* and *-(y)AmA* in slot 4 (tur-04-13); the optative in slot 5 with its own person endings (tur-04-14).
- **Compound verbs** with *etmek*, such as *yardım etmek* "help" and *teşekkür etmek* "thank" (tur-03-10). The noun stays bare; the whole template goes on *etmek*: *yardım ediyorum*, *yardım etmiyorum*.
- **Noun predicates**, from slot 6 on: *öğretmenim* (slot 7), *öğretmendim* (6 and 7, tur-04-08), *öğretmendir* (8, tur-04-11). The same holds for *var*, *yok* and *değil*: *yoktu* "there wasn't" (tur-04-08).
- **The question particle *mI***. It is written apart, but it slots into the template: with *-(I)yor* it stands between the tense and the person, *geliyor musun* (tur-02-09). Its place changes with the tense (tur-04-15).
- **Reading long verbs.** When you meet a long verb, find the stem, then read the suffixes off one slot at a time. The order is always the same, so each piece can only be one thing.
- **Non-finite verbs** (part 6) keep slots 1 to 4 and replace the rest with a noun-making suffix and noun endings.

## Watch out

**Do not negate a verb with a separate word.** Spanish *no vengo*, French *je ne viens pas* and English "I do not come" put the negative outside the verb. Turkish puts it inside, in slot 3. *\*Değil geliyorum* and *\*Geliyorum değil* are wrong: *değil* negates noun predicates (tur-02-04). Say *Gelmiyorum*.

**Do not put the negative after the tense.** *\*Geliyormaum* or *\*gel-iyor-ma* treat *-mA* like an ending added at the end. It belongs in slot 3: *gelmiyor*.

**Do not stack two tense suffixes.** "I was coming" is not *\*geldiyorum* (past plus progressive) and "I was going to come" is not *\*gelecekiyorum*. Slot 5 takes one suffix. The second meaning goes into slot 6: *geliyordum*, *gelecektim* (tur-04-10).

**Do not put the person ending before the tense.** *\*Gelimiyor* or *\*gelum-iyor* model Turkish on languages where person comes early. Person is slot 7, after tense: *geliyorum*.

**Do not harmonise with the stem.** *\*Geliyordi*, *\*geliyorduk* with a front vowel, or *\*gelmiyordük* look back to *gel-*. The copula follows the *o* of *-yor*: *geliyordu*, *geliyorduk*.

**Do not use the noun copula as if it were a tense suffix.** *-(y)DI* after a noun means "was" (*evdeydim* "I was at home"); *-DI* on a stem means "happened" (*geldim* "I came"). They share a gloss, PST, and a sound, but they sit in different slots (tur-04-03, tur-04-08).

**Do not write the template across two words.** Spanish *estaba viniendo* splits auxiliary and verb. Turkish writes the whole chain as one word: *geliyordum*, not *\*geliyor dum* or *\*geliyor idim* in ordinary use. The separate form *idi* exists but is old-fashioned and emphatic (goksel2005 §8.3.1).

**Do not leave a finite verb without slot 5.** *\*Gelum* for "I come" has a person ending but no tense. Only the commands of tur-04-02 can do without one.

## Connections

Japanese and Korean verbs are built the same way: a stem, then suffixes in a fixed order, with negation and tense inside the word and politeness or mood further out. Japanese 食べさせられなかった (tabe-sase-rare-nakat-ta) "was not made to eat" runs stem, causative, passive, negative, past, much like Turkish slots 1 to 6. The likeness is typological: the languages are not related (Glottolog has no family linking them).

## Exercises

1. Name the slots filled in *yürümüyorsunuz*, and give its meaning.
2. Build "they are trying (it)", with no subject word.
3. Build "I'm not laughing", step by step through the slots, and mark the stress.
4. Take apart *temizliyoruz*. Why is there an *i* where the infinitive *temizlemek* has *e*?
5. A learner writes *\*Ben değil oynuyorum* for "I'm not playing". Correct it and say which slot the negation belongs in.
6. Why is *\*geldiyor* impossible, and how does Turkish say "he was coming"?
7. In the preview *Geliyordum*, why is the person ending *-m* and not *-um*, and why is the copula *-du* and not *-di*?
8. Translate *Ayşe ağlamıyor, gülüyor* and *Bu akşam ne pişiriyorsun?*

## Worked answers

1. Stem *yürü-* (1), negative *-mü* (3), tense *-yor* (5), person *-sunuz* (7): "you (several, or polite) are not walking". The negative's vowel is high and rounded after *ü*.
2. *Deniyorlar.* Stem *dene-*; its *e* becomes *i* before *-yor*; with no subject word and people meant, *-lAr* is needed (tur-02-08).
3. Stem *gül-*. Negative: *gül-me-*. Tense: before *-yor* the negative's vowel becomes high and harmonises with *ü*: *gül-mü-yor*. Person "I": *-um*. *Gülmüyorum*, stressed *gǘlmüyorum* on the first syllable, because *-mA* pulls the stress onto the syllable before it.
4. *Temizl-iyor-uz*: stem *temizle-* "clean", *-(I)yor*, 1st plural *-uz*. A stem-final *a* or *e* becomes a high vowel before *-yor*, and harmonises: after the *i* of *temiz*, it is *i* (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3).
5. *Oynamıyorum.* Verbs are negated inside the word, in slot 3, right after the stem; *değil* is for noun predicates. *Ben* is not needed unless you stress "I".
6. Slot 5 holds one suffix, and *-DI* and *-(I)yor* both belong there. "He was coming" uses *-(I)yor* in slot 5 and the copula *-(y)DI* in slot 6: *geliyordu*.
7. After the copula *-(y)DI* the person endings come from a different set, in which "I" is *-m* (tur-04-03 teaches it). The copula's vowel follows the nearest vowel, the *o* of *-yor*, so it is *-du*.
8. "Ayşe isn't crying, she's laughing." *Ağla-mı-yor*: the stem keeps its *a* before *-mA*. "What are you cooking this evening?" *Pişir-iyor-sun*; *bu akşam* "this evening" is a time phrase at the front.
