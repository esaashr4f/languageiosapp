## The idea

Until now every verb has been in *-(I)yor*: things going on. This lesson gives you the past, which is the form you will hear most in any story about yesterday:

*Dün geldim.* "I came yesterday."

The suffix is ***-DI***. It goes in slot 5, the tense slot, so it replaces *-(I)yor*; it does not add to it. Göksel and Kerslake describe it as marking both **past tense** and **perfective aspect** (goksel2005 §21.2.1). **Aspect** is how an event is viewed: perfective means seen from outside, as a finished whole with a beginning and an end. *Geldim* presents the coming as one completed event. "I was coming", seen from inside as it unfolded, is a different form (tur-04-10).

Because it presents a finished event, *-DI* covers both English "I came" and "I have come". *Ödevini bitirdin mi?* is "Did you finish your homework?" and also "Have you finished your homework?" Turkish does not make the distinction English makes between those two.

*-DI* is also the past the speaker vouches for: something seen, done or known for certain. Turkish has a second past, *-mIş*, for things learnt second-hand or inferred (tur-04-07). For now, *-DI* is the ordinary past.

**A second set of person endings.** After *-DI*, the personal endings are not the ones you know from *-(I)yor*. They are shorter:

- "I" *-m*, "you" *-n*, "he, she, it" nothing;
- "we" *-k*, "you" (plural or polite) *-nIz*, "they" *-lAr*.

*Geldim*, *geldin*, *geldi*, *geldik*, *geldiniz*, *geldiler*. This guide calls them the **k-set**, after the *-k* of "we"; the endings after nouns and *-(I)yor* are the **z-set**, after *-(y)Iz*. Göksel and Kerslake list them as two of four groups of person markers, and give the rule: the k-set follows *-DI* and the conditional *-sA*, and the copular markers *-(y)DI* and *-(y)sA*; the z-set follows the other tenses and nouns (goksel2005 §8.4). The suffix in front chooses the set. You never choose freely.

The k-set's *-m*, *-n*, *-nIz* look like the possessive suffixes *-(I)m*, *-(I)n*, *-(I)nIz* of tur-03-04 after a vowel. The sources used for this guide do not explain the history of the two sets, so treat the likeness as a memory aid only.

## How it is built

To say what happened, take five steps.

1. **Find the stem**: *yap-* "do".
2. **Negative?** Add *-mA*: *yap-ma-*. Unlike before *-yor*, its vowel stays *a* or *e*: *yapma-*, *gelme-*.
3. **Add -DI.** Two choices, as with the locative *-DA* (tur-01-06, tur-02-06):
   - **D**: *t* after a voiceless consonant (*p, t, k, ç, f, s, ş, h*), *d* everywhere else: *yap-tı*, *git-ti*, *konuş-tu*, but *gel-di*, *oku-du*, *gelme-di*.
   - **I**: four-way harmony with the vowel before: *-dı/-tı*, *-di/-ti*, *-du/-tu*, *-dü/-tü*.
   No buffer is needed after a vowel: *oku-du*, *bekle-di*. Stems do not change before *-DI*, since it begins with a consonant: *git-ti*, *et-ti*, *de-di*, *ye-di*.
4. **Add the k-set ending**: *-m*, *-n*, nothing, *-k*, *-nIz*, *-lAr*. *-nIz* and *-lAr* harmonise with *-DI*: *yaptınız*, *yaptılar*; *okudunuz*; *gördünüz*.
5. **Put the stress.** *-DI* and the k-set endings take stress, so it moves to the end: *geldím*, *geldilér*. *-mA* pulls it back before itself: *gélmedim*, *yápmadık* (goksel2005 §4.3.2).

A worked chain: "We didn't understand." Stem *anla-*. Negative: *anla-ma-*. Past: after the vowel *a*, *d* and *ı*: *anlamadı*. "We", k-set: *-k*. *Anlamadık*, stressed *anlámadık*.

**Questions.** With *-DI*, the person ending stays on the verb and *mI* follows the whole word: *Geldin mi?* "Did you come?", *Anladınız mı?* "Did you understand?" This is unlike *-(I)yor*, where the ending moved onto *mI* (*geliyor musun*). Tur-04-15 explains why; for now, notice the difference.

## Where it applies

- **Finished events in the past**, the plain past of narration: *Dün geldim*, *Geçen hafta İzmir'e gittik*.
- **English present perfect**: anything done and complete, however recent: *Film bitti mi?* "Has the film finished?", *Ne yaptınız?* "What have you done?" (goksel2005 §21.2.1).
- **Just now**: with *demin* "just now": *Ali demin çıktı* "Ali has just gone out." Also *çoktan* "long ago, already": *Çoktan bitirdim* "I finished ages ago, I've already finished."
- **Bounded stretches of time**, even with repetition: *Geçen hafta her akşam kitap okudum* "Last week I read every evening." The whole week is viewed as finished (goksel2005 §21.3.1).
- **Present states reached by a change.** Verbs that mean "get into a state" use *-DI* for the state you are now in (goksel2005 §21.3.3): *acıktım* "I'm hungry", *susadım* "I'm thirsty", *yoruldum* "I'm tired", *bıktım* "I'm fed up", *kızdım* "I'm angry", *geç kaldım* "I'm late". *Anladım* "I see, I understand" and *sevindim* "I'm glad" work the same way.
- **Sequences of events**: *Önce ..., sonra ...*: each verb in *-DI*.
- **Questions about completed events**, with *mI* after the person ending: *Geldin mi?*, *Gelmediniz mi?* "Didn't you come?"
- **Time words** that go with it: *dün*, *dün akşam* "yesterday evening", *geçen hafta*, *geçen yıl*, *iki gün önce*, *demin*, *çoktan*.

## Watch out

**Do not use the -(I)yor endings after -DI.** *\*Geldium*, *\*geldiyim*, *\*geldiz* carry the z-set over. After *-DI* the ending is the k-set: *geldim*, *geldik*.

**Do not put the person ending on mI.** By analogy with *geliyor musun*, learners write *\*Geldi misin?* The person stays on the verb: *Geldin mi?* (tur-04-15).

**Do not write d after a voiceless consonant.** *\*Yapdım*, *\*gitdi*, *\*konuşdu* ignore assimilation: *yaptım*, *gitti*, *konuştu*. And *\*geltim* puts *t* after a voiced *l*: *geldim*.

**Do not change gid- or ed- before -DI.** *\*Giddim*, *\*eddik* treat *-DI* as if it began with a vowel. It begins with a consonant: *gittim*, *ettik*.

**Do not raise the negative's vowel.** *\*Gelmidim*, *\*yapmıdık* copy *gelmiyor*. The high vowel appears only before *-yor* (and a few *y*-suffixes): *gelmedim*, *yapmadık*.

**Do not use -(I)yor for these present states.** "I'm hungry" is not *\*Acıkıyorum*, which means "I'm getting hungry". It is *Acıktım*, or the adjective sentence *Açım* (tur-02-02). "I'm tired": *Yoruldum* or *Yorgunum*, not *\*Yoruluyorum*.

**Do not use -DI for the Spanish imperfect.** Spanish *Ali leía cuando llegué* "Ali was reading when I arrived" sets a background. *-DI* views events as finished, so *\*Ali kitap okudu* for "Ali was reading" says that the reading happened and was over. The background is *-(I)yordu*: *Ali kitap okuyordu* (tur-04-10); "used to" is *-(A/I)rdI*. *-DI* is the Spanish preterite: *leyó*, *llegué*.

**Do not use -DI on a noun for "was".** "I was ill" is not built from *-DI* in slot 5, because a noun has no slot 5. It is the copula of tur-04-08: *hastaydım*.

**Do not translate "did" with a separate word.** English "Did you come?" and "I didn't come" use *do*; Turkish has nothing to match it: *Geldin mi?*, *Gelmedim.* Do not use *yapmak* here: *\*Gelmek yapmadım* is nonsense.

## Connections

Japanese 〜た and Korean -았/었- cover "did" and many "has done" uses, as *-DI* does. Japanese also uses 〜た for present states reached by a change: お腹がすいた (onaka ga suita) "I'm hungry", literally "the stomach emptied", which is the same idea as *acıktım*. Spanish, by contrast, splits the Turkish *-DI* between the preterite *comí* and the perfect *he comido*.

## Exercises

1. Put *Ali kitap okuyor* into the past.
2. Build "You (one friend) didn't wait", step by step.
3. Take apart *konuştunuz* and *ettik*. Why *t* in both?
4. Translate *Susadınız mı?* and explain why it is in the past.
5. A learner writes *\*Dün İzmir'e gitdim*. Correct it.
6. Build "Did they wake up early?" with no subject word.
7. Say "We came home late yesterday evening."
8. Why is *\*Yoruluyorum* the wrong way to say "I'm tired", and what are the two right ways?
9. Correct *\*Geldi misin?* and say why.

## Worked answers

1. *Ali kitap okudu.* *Oku-* ends in a vowel: *d*, harmonising *u*. Third person: no ending.
2. Stem *bekle-*. Negative: *bekle-me-* (vowel stays *e* before a consonant). Past: *bekle-me-di*. "You", k-set: *-n*. *Beklemedin*, stressed *beklémedin*.
3. *Konuş-tu-nuz*: stem, past (*t* after the voiceless *ş*, *u* after *u*), 2nd plural *-nuz* harmonising with *-tu*. *Et-ti-k*: *t* after the voiceless *t*; *et-* stays *et-* because *-DI* begins with a consonant; "we" *-k*.
4. "Are you thirsty?" *Susamak* means "get thirsty": the past says you have become thirsty and so are thirsty now (goksel2005 §21.3.3). *Mı* follows the full verb.
5. *Dün İzmir'e gittim.* After the voiceless *t*, *-DI* is *-ti*.
6. *Erken uyandılar mı?* *Uyan-dı-lar*, then *mı* after the whole word. With no subject word and people meant, *-lAr* is used.
7. *Dün akşam eve geç geldik.* Time phrase first, the goal in the dative (tur-03-02), *geç* before the verb, k-set *-k*.
8. *Yorulmak* means "get tired", so *yoruluyorum* is "I'm getting tired". The state is the past *yoruldum* (tur-04-03), or the adjective *yorgunum* (tur-02-02).
9. *Geldin mi?* After *-DI* the person ending stays on the verb, and *mI* follows it unchanged (tur-04-15 gives the full rule).
