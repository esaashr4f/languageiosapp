## The idea

*Yarın geleceğim.* "I'll come tomorrow."

The future suffix is ***-(y)AcAK***. It goes in slot 5, the tense slot, where *-(I)yor* and *-DI* go, so it replaces them. Göksel and Kerslake call it the only verbal suffix that is an explicit marker of future tense (goksel2005 §21.2.3). It states what the speaker expects to happen: a prediction, an intention, a plan.

You already have one way to talk about the future: *Yarın geliyorlar* "they're coming tomorrow" (tur-02-08). The two are not the same. Göksel and Kerslake explain that *-(I)yor* for the future shows confidence that a fixed schedule will run as arranged, while *-(y)AcAK* is an independent prediction, and is what you use when a schedule has broken down (goksel2005 §21.2.3). *Tren saat beşte kalkıyor* is the timetable; *Tren ne zaman kalkacak?* is what you ask when the train is late.

**Person endings.** After *-(y)AcAK* come the z-set endings, the ones you know from *-(I)yor* and from nouns (goksel2005 §8.4): *-(y)Im*, *-sIn*, nothing, *-(y)Iz*, *-sInIz*, *-lAr*.

**The K.** The suffix ends in *k*, written **K** in the citation because it changes. When a vowel-initial ending follows, *k* becomes *ğ*: *gelecek* but *geleceğim*, *geleceğiz*. This is the rule you met with nouns (*köpek*, *köpeği*, tur-01-06): a final *k* in a word of more than one syllable softens to *ğ* between vowels. Before a consonant it stays: *geleceksin*, *gelecekler*.

## How it is built

To say what will happen, take five steps.

1. **Find the stem**: *gel-*, *oku-*, *git-*.
2. **Negative?** Add *-mA*: *gel-me-*. It is written *-me*, *-ma* here, even though it is pronounced with a high vowel (see below).
3. **Add -(y)AcAK.**
   - Two-way harmony: *-ecek* after front vowels, *-acak* after back vowels.
   - Buffer *y* after a vowel: *oku-yacak*, *bekle-yecek*, *gelme-yecek*.
   - Consonant changes before the vowel: *git-* becomes *gid-*, *et-* becomes *ed-*: *gidecek*, *yardım edecek*.
   - *de-* and *ye-* write *i*: *diyecek*, *yiyecek* (goksel2005 §2.6).
4. **Add the z-set ending**, softening *k* to *ğ* before a vowel: *gel-eceğ-im*, *gel-ecek-sin*, *gel-ecek*, *gel-eceğ-iz*, *gel-ecek-siniz*, *gel-ecek-ler*.
5. **Put the stress** on the *cAK* syllable. The 1st and 2nd person endings cannot take stress, so it stays there: *gelecék*, *gelecéğim*, *gelecéksiniz*; *-lAr* can: *geleceklér*. With *-mA*, the stress moves before the negative: *gélmeyecek* (goksel2005 §§4.3.2, 8.2.3.3).

A worked chain: "We won't stay." Stem *kal-*. Negative: *kal-ma-*. Future after a vowel: *kal-ma-yacak*. "We": *-(y)Iz*, vowel-initial, so *k* becomes *ğ*: *kalmayacağız*, stressed *kálmayacağız*.

**Questions** work as with *-(I)yor*: *mI* follows the tense, and the person ending moves onto it. *Gelecek misin?* "Will you come?", *Kalacak mısınız?* "Will you stay?" With "they", *-lAr* stays on the verb: *Gelecekler mi?* (tur-04-15).

**How it is said.** Göksel and Kerslake describe two pronunciations (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3). Even in careful speech the first vowel is often said like a high vowel: *kalacak* close to "kalıcak", *dönecek* close to "dönücek". And in everyday speech, when a vowel-initial ending follows, the *ğ* and the vowel next to it merge, so that *geleceğim* sounds like "gelicem", *yapacağım* like "yapıcam", *geleceğiz* like "gelicez", *geleceksin* like "gelicen". Wiktionary records *yapıcam* as an informal spoken variant of *yapacağım* that is out of place in writing. The same raising explains why *-mA* is pronounced with a high vowel before the *y*: *gelmeyecek* sounds like "gelmiyecek" (goksel2005 §§2.6, 8.2.2). Write the full forms.

## Where it applies

- **Predictions**: *Yarın yağmur yağacak*, *Belki yakında değişecek.*
- **Intentions and decisions**: *Yarın geleceğim*, *Karar vereceğim* "I'll decide."
- **Plans you state as facts**: *Seneye evleneceğiz.* Göksel and Kerslake note that a 1st-person *-(y)AcAK* can carry more conviction than a promise in the aorist, because it presents the action as a plan already made (goksel2005 §21.4.4.6; the aorist comes in tur-04-05).
- **When a schedule is in doubt**: *Konser ne zaman başlayacak?* "When is the concert going to start?" after a delay, against the timetable's *başlıyor* (goksel2005 §21.2.3).
- **Orders from authority**: *Gitmeyeceksin!*, *Ödevini bitireceksin* "You'll finish your homework." (goksel2005 §21.4.4.2).
- **Questions about the future**, with *mI* taking the person ending: *Gelecek misin?*
- **Time words** that go with it: *yarın*, *gelecek hafta*, *haftaya* "next week", *seneye* "next year", *yakında* "soon", *ileride* "later on", *hafta sonu*.
- **In the past**, as "was going to": *gelecektim* (tur-04-10). And as a noun or adjective: *gelecek* "the future; next" (this lesson's vocabulary), and in clauses (part 6).

## Watch out

**Do not use the present for a prediction.** Spanish *mañana llueve* and English "the train leaves at five" use the present for the future. Turkish *-(I)yor* does too, but only for fixed arrangements. For a forecast, *\*Yarın yağmur yağıyor* sounds as if the rain had been scheduled. Use *Yarın yağmur yağacak.*

**Do not keep k before a vowel.** *\*Gelecekim*, *\*yapacakız* ignore the softening: *geleceğim*, *yapacağız*.

**Do not soften k before a consonant.** *\*Geleceğsin*, *\*geleceğler*: *ğ* never stands before a consonant here. *Geleceksin*, *gelecekler*.

**Do not use the k-set after the future.** *\*Gelecekk*, *\*gelecekm* copy *geldik*, *geldim*. The future takes the z-set: *geleceğiz*, *geleceğim*.

**Do not write the spoken forms.** *Gelicem*, *yapıcam*, *gelmiyecek* are how the words sound in casual speech, and you will see them in informal messages. In writing use *geleceğim*, *yapacağım*, *gelmeyecek*.

**Do not drop the buffer or add one.** *\*Okuacak*, *\*bekleecek*: after a vowel the *y* is needed: *okuyacak*, *bekleyecek*. *\*Gelyecek* adds one after a consonant: *gelecek*.

**Do not forget gid- and ed-, or de- and ye-.** *\*Gitecek*, *\*etecek*, *\*deyecek*, *\*yeyecek*: *gidecek*, *edecek*, *diyecek*, *yiyecek*.

**Do not use "will" for requests.** English "Will you open the window?" is a request, not a question about the future. *Pencereyi açacak mısın?* asks about your plans, and can sound like a challenge. A request uses the aorist: *Pencereyi açar mısınız?* (tur-04-06).

**Do not put the person ending after mI with "they".** *\*Gelecek mi-ler* or *\*gelecek miler*: *-lAr* stays on the verb, *Gelecekler mi?*

## Connections

Korean has a parallel to the Turkish split between a fixed plan and a prediction: the plain present 내일 가요 "I'm going tomorrow" for arrangements, and -ㄹ 거예요 for what you expect or intend. Japanese has no future suffix at all and uses the non-past (明日行きます), so a Japanese speaker must learn that Turkish marks the future when it is a prediction. Spanish *voy a* + infinitive, like *-(y)AcAK*, often expresses a plan.

## Exercises

1. Put *Ayşe mektup yazıyor* into the future.
2. Build "I won't wait", step by step.
3. Take apart *gideceksiniz* and explain the *d* and the *k*.
4. Ask a stranger "Will you stay at the hotel?"
5. A learner writes *\*Yarın sana telefon edecekim.* Correct it.
6. Say "It's going to snow at the weekend" (*kar* "snow").
7. What is the difference between *Yarın İzmir'e gidiyoruz* and *Yarın İzmir'e gideceğiz*?
8. Write *yapıcam* and *gelmiyecek* as they should be written, and say what they mean.

## Worked answers

1. *Ayşe mektup yazacak.* Back vowel: *-acak*; third person, no ending.
2. Stem *bekle-*. Negative *bekle-me-*. Future after a vowel: *bekle-me-yecek*. "I": *-(y)Im*, which begins with a vowel, so *k* becomes *ğ*: *beklemeyeceğim*, stressed *beklémeyeceğim*.
3. *Gid-ecek-siniz*: stem *git-* becomes *gid-* before the vowel of the suffix; *-ecek* by front harmony; *-siniz* begins with a consonant, so *k* stays. "You will go."
4. *Otelde kalacak mısınız?* Locative for "at" (tur-02-06); polite *-sInIz* goes on *mI*.
5. *Yarın sana telefon edeceğim.* Before the vowel of *-im*, *k* becomes *ğ*.
6. *Hafta sonu kar yağacak.* *Yağmak* is used for snow as for rain.
7. Both mean "We're going to İzmir tomorrow". *Gidiyoruz* presents a fixed arrangement, with confidence that it will happen as planned; *gideceğiz* states it as a decision or prediction (goksel2005 §21.2.3).
8. *Yapacağım* "I will do", *gelmeyecek* "he won't come". The written forms keep the full suffix and the *e* of *-mA*.
