## The idea

The most common Turkish phrase you will say is a verb in a tense you have not met yet:

*Teşekkür ederim.* "Thank you." Literally "I do thanks."

*Ederim* is *et-* "do" in the **aorist**. The aorist is the fourth tense of slot 5. Its main jobs are habits, general truths, willingness and promises: what someone does as a rule, or is prepared to do, rather than what is going on now. Its meaning is the subject of the next lesson. This lesson is about its form, which is the least regular thing in the Turkish verb.

The name is a borrowing from Greek grammar, where it means something else, and it fits Turkish poorly. Wiktionary's headword lines call the same form the "simple present". This guide keeps "aorist", the usual name in grammars of Turkish (dossier; goksel2005 §8.2.3.3).

**Three shapes.** The suffix is *-r* after a vowel and either ***-Ar*** (*-ar*, *-er*) or ***-Ir*** (*-ır*, *-ir*, *-ur*, *-ür*) after a consonant. Göksel and Kerslake give the distribution (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3):

1. **Stems ending in a vowel** take *-r*: *oku-r* "reads", *anla-r* "understands", *bekle-r* "waits", *de-r* "says", *ye-r* "eats".
2. **Consonant-final stems of more than one syllable** take *-Ir*: *konuş-ur* "speaks", *anlat-ır* "tells", *düşün-ür* "thinks", *otur-ur* "sits".
3. **Consonant-final stems of one syllable** take *-Ar*: *yap-ar*, *bak-ar*, *iç-er*, *sev-er*, *git-* → *gid-er*, *et-* → *ed-er*, *gül-er*, *sor-ar*.
4. **Except thirteen** one-syllable stems, which take *-Ir*: *al-*, *bil-*, *bul-*, *dur-*, *gel-*, *gör-*, *kal-*, *ol-*, *öl-*, *san-*, *var-*, *ver-*, *vur-*: *alır*, *bilir*, *bulur*, *durur*, *gelir*, *görür*, *kalır*, *olur*, *ölür*, *sanır*, *varır*, *verir*, *vurur*.

Twelve of the thirteen end in *l* or *r*; *san-* "suppose" is the odd one out. Göksel and Kerslake note that among the forty-odd one-syllable stems ending in *l* or *r*, which suffix a stem takes is unpredictable (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3): *gel-ir* but *gül-er*, *vur-ur* but *sor-ar*, *dur-ur* but *kır-ar*. The sources used here give no reason for the list. Learn the thirteen as a set. This is why the dictionaries and this guide's vocabulary notes give every verb's aorist (dossier §4).

*Ol-* "be, become" is on the list; you will meet it properly in tur-04-16.

**Person endings.** The aorist takes the z-set (goksel2005 §8.4): *gelirim*, *gelirsin*, *gelir*, *geliriz*, *gelirsiniz*, *gelirler*. *Teşekkür ederim* is *ed-er-im*: "do-AOR-I".

## How it is built

To build an aorist, take four steps.

1. **Find the stem** and count its syllables: *gel-* (one), *konuş-* (two), *oku-* (ends in a vowel).
2. **Choose the suffix.**
   - Vowel at the end: *-r*.
   - Two syllables or more, consonant at the end: *-Ir*.
   - One syllable, consonant at the end: is it one of the thirteen? Then *-Ir*. Otherwise *-Ar*.
3. **Apply the consonant changes.** *-Ar* and *-Ir* begin with a vowel, so *git-* becomes *gid-* and *et-* becomes *ed-*: *gider*, *eder*. Final *k* in a one-syllable stem does not change: *kalkar*, *bakar*.
4. **Add the z-set ending.** The aorist ends in a consonant, so no buffer: *-Im*, *-sIn*, nothing, *-Iz*, *-sInIz*, *-lAr*, harmonising with the aorist's vowel: *yapar-ım*, *bilir-iz*, *görür-sünüz*, *okur-lar*.

**Stress** falls on the aorist syllable, and the 1st and 2nd person endings leave it there: *gelír*, *gelírim*, *yapársınız*; *-lAr* takes it: *gelirlér* (goksel2005 §4.3.2).

A worked chain: "We know." Stem *bil-*: one syllable, ends in *l*. Is it one of the thirteen? Yes: *-Ir*, front unrounded, *bil-ir*. "We": *-Iz*, *biliriz*.

Another: "You (polite) wait." *Bekle-* ends in a vowel: *bekle-r*. *-sInIz* after *e*: *beklersiniz*.

**Questions** work as with *-(I)yor* and the future: *mI* follows the aorist and takes the person ending: *Çay içer misiniz?* "Would you like some tea?", literally "Do you drink tea?" (goksel2005 §21.4.4.5). The next lesson explains why such a question is an offer.

**The negative** has its own, even less regular, form: *-mAz*. It is the subject of tur-04-06.

## Where it applies

This lesson covers the form; tur-04-06 maps the meanings in full. In brief, you will use the aorist for:

- **Set phrases of politeness**, nearly all in the aorist: *Teşekkür ederim*, *Rica ederim*, *Affedersiniz*, *Özür dilerim* "I apologise", *Görüşürüz*, *Umarım* "I hope".
- **Habits and repeated actions**: *Babam her sabah gazete okur*, with *her zaman* "always", *sık sık* "often", *bazen* "sometimes".
- **General truths and lasting facts**: *Kediler balık sever*, *Ayşe üç dil bilir.*
- **Opinions and guesses**: *Sanırım*, *Belki yarın gelir.*
- **Offers and requests**: *Çay içer misiniz?*
- **Promises** made as you speak: *Yarın ararım* "I'll call tomorrow" (tur-04-06).
- **In the past**, for "used to" and "would": *gelirdim* (tur-04-10).

## Watch out

**Do not give every one-syllable stem -Ir.** *\*Yapır*, *\*içir*, *\*sevir* generalise from *gelir*. Only the thirteen take *-Ir*: *yapar*, *içer*, *sever*.

**Do not give the thirteen -Ar.** *\*Geler*, *\*alar*, *\*biler*, *\*görer* generalise from *yapar*: *gelir*, *alır*, *bilir*, *görür*.

**Do not judge by the last consonant alone.** *Gül-* ends in *l* like *gel-*, but *güler*. *Sor-* ends in *r* like *vur-*, but *sorar*. Only the list decides.

**Do not give two-syllable stems -Ar.** *\*Konuşar*, *\*düşüner* treat them like *yap-*: *konuşur*, *düşünür*. The exceptions are stems built on a one-syllable verb, such as *affet-* (*affeder*) and other *etmek* compounds (*yardım eder*).

**Do not add a vowel after a vowel stem.** *\*Okuur*, *\*anlıyar*, *\*bekleyer*: a vowel stem takes plain *-r*, and its own vowel does not change: *okur*, *anlar*, *bekler*. Raising the stem vowel is something only *-(I)yor* does.

**Do not forget gid- and ed-.** *\*Giter*, *\*teşekkür eterim*: *gider*, *teşekkür ederim*.

**Do not stress the ending.** *\*Gelirím*, *\*ederím*: the stress stays on the aorist: *gelírim*, *edérim*.

**Do not translate English "I think" with düşünmek.** *Düşünüyorum* is "I am thinking (about something)". For an opinion, use *sanırım* "I suppose" (or other ways in tur-06-12): *\*Düşünürüm yarın gelir* is wrong; *Sanırım yarın gelir.*

**Do not use the aorist for what is happening right now.** *\*Şimdi kitap okurum* for "I am reading a book now" is wrong. Göksel and Kerslake state that the aorist never expresses progressive aspect (goksel2005 §21.3.2). Use *Şimdi kitap okuyorum.*

## Connections

The Japanese plain non-past 〜る (食べる "eat, will eat") and the Korean plain present cover much of the same ground as the aorist: habits, general truths and the near future. Neither language has the Turkish split between the aorist and *-(I)yor* in the present, which the next lesson explains. French and Spanish keep politeness formulas in the present, much as Turkish keeps them in the aorist: *je vous remercie*, *le agradezco*, *teşekkür ederim*.

## Exercises

1. Give the aorist 3rd singular of *almak*, *atmak*, *bulmak*, *gülmek*, *söylemek*, *çalışmak*.
2. Build "We drink tea every morning."
3. Build "I think (suppose) Ali knows", step by step.
4. Take apart *görürsünüz* and *bakarlar*.
5. A learner writes *\*Ben her gün yapır*. Correct it and explain.
6. Why does *konuşmak* take *-Ir* but *koşmak* take *-Ar*?
7. Translate *Ali bazen geç kalır* and *Sık sık İstanbul'a gideriz.*
8. Say "Excuse me, thank you" and the reply "You're welcome."

## Worked answers

1. *Alır* (one of the thirteen), *atar* (one syllable, not on the list), *bulur* (one of the thirteen), *güler* (one syllable, not on the list, though it ends in *l*), *söyler* (vowel stem: *-r*), *çalışır* (three syllables: *-Ir*).
2. *Her sabah çay içeriz.* *İç-* has one syllable and is not on the list: *-er*; "we" *-iz*.
3. *Sanırım Ali bilir.* *San-* is the odd one of the thirteen: *san-ır*, with "I" *-ım*. *Bil-* is also on the list: *bil-ir*, third person, no ending.
4. *Gör-ür-sünüz*: stem *gör-* (one of the thirteen), *-ür* by rounded front harmony, 2nd plural *-sünüz*: "you see". *Bak-ar-lar*: *bak-* takes *-Ar*, 3rd plural *-lar*: "they look".
5. *Ben her gün yaparım.* *Yap-* takes *-Ar*; only the thirteen take *-Ir* among one-syllable stems. The person ending *-ım* is also needed.
6. *Konuş-* has two syllables, so it takes *-Ir* (*konuşur*). *Koş-* has one, and is not among the thirteen, so it takes *-Ar* (*koşar*). The rule counts syllables.
7. "Ali is sometimes late." *Geç kalır*: *kal-* is one of the thirteen. "We often go to Istanbul." *Gid-er-iz*: *git-* becomes *gid-* before the vowel.
8. *Affedersiniz, teşekkür ederim.* — *Rica ederim.* All three are aorists of *etmek* compounds.
