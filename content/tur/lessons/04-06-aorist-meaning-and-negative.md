## The idea

Compare two sentences that both translate as "Ayşe doesn't eat meat":

*Ayşe et yemiyor.* *Ayşe et yemez.*

The first, with *-(I)yor*, reports what you have observed: these days, as far as you can see, she isn't eating meat. The second, with the aorist, states a rule about Ayşe: she is someone who does not eat meat. Göksel and Kerslake draw exactly this line (goksel2005 §21.4.1.1). *-(I)yor* describes a state of affairs the speaker has seen or experienced; the aorist makes a statement of a more general, theoretical kind, a rule or a principle. You could add *bu hafta* "this week" to the first sentence, but not to the second: a rule has no time limit.

That is the core of the aorist. It is not really about time. It presents a situation as **not a directly known fact**, but as something general, assumed, offered or promised. Göksel and Kerslake describe its uses under generalisations, assumptions and volitional utterances (goksel2005 §§21.4.1, 21.4.4). Four kinds stand out:

1. **Generalisations**: general truths, rules, and the typical behaviour of a class or a person: *Güneş doğudan doğar* "the sun rises in the east".
2. **Assumptions about the future**: what you expect will happen, without a plan: *Ali yarın gelmez* "Ali won't come tomorrow, I expect". Without an adverb, such a statement is about as confident as English "probably" (goksel2005 §21.4.1.4).
3. **Offers and requests**, as 2nd-person questions: *Kapıyı açar mısınız?* "Would you open the door?" (goksel2005 §21.4.4.5).
4. **Promises and commitments**, in the 1st person: *Yarın ararım* "I'll call you tomorrow". Saying it is making the promise (goksel2005 §21.4.4.6).

What links them: in every case, the speaker speaks of what someone does, will do or would do by nature or by will, not of an event he has watched.

**The negative is irregular.** After *-mA*, the aorist is *-z*, and the two together make ***-mAz***: *yapmaz* "doesn't do", *gelmez* "doesn't come". In the 1st singular and plural the *z* disappears: *yapmam* "I don't do", *yapmayız* "we don't do". Göksel and Kerslake call the way the aorist combines with the negative "highly irregular" (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3). The sources used here give no history for it. This guide glosses *-mAz* as one piece, NEG.AOR, since the two meanings cannot be separated in it, and *-mA* alone as NEG.AOR in *yap-ma-m*.

One thing is regular: the thirteen *-Ir* stems of the last lesson make their negatives like every other verb: *gelmez*, *bilmez*, *almaz*.

## How it is built

To make a negative aorist, take four steps.

1. **Find the stem**: *iç-* "drink".
2. **Add -mAz**, two-way harmony: *iç-mez*, *yap-maz*, *oku-maz*. No buffer, no change to the stem: *gitmez*, *etmez*, *demez*.
3. **Add the person ending.**
   - 1st singular: drop the *z*, add *-m*: *iç-me-m*.
   - 1st plural: drop the *z*, add *-yIz*: *iç-me-yiz*, *yap-ma-yız*.
   - Others: keep *-mAz* and add the z-set: *içmezsin*, *içmez*, *içmezsiniz*, *içmezler*.
4. **Put the stress.** Unlike *-mA* elsewhere, *-mAz* takes the stress: *içméz*, *içmém*, *içméyiz*, *içmézsiniz*. With *-lAr* it moves to the end: *içmezlér* (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3).

A worked chain: "We don't work on Saturdays." Stem *çalış-*. Negative aorist: *çalış-maz*. "We": drop *z*, add *-yız*: *çalışmayız*. *Cumartesi çalışmayız.*

**Questions** follow the pattern of the positive aorist: *mI* follows the verb and takes the person ending. Before *mI*, the *z* of the 1st person comes back: *gelmez miyim?*, *yetişmez miyiz?* "won't we be in time?" (goksel2005 §27.2.2). The positive: *gelir misin?*, *içer misiniz?*

**Choosing between -(I)yor and the aorist**, step by step. Ask: am I reporting what I see happening, now or these days? Then *-(I)yor*. Am I stating what is generally so, what I expect, offer or promise? Then the aorist.

## Where it applies

- **General truths and laws of nature**: *Güneş doğudan doğar.*
- **Proverbs and sayings**: *It ürür, kervan yürür.* A proverb states a timeless truth, so the aorist is its natural form.
- **Typical behaviour of a class**, with a bare or plural subject: *Türkler çok çay içer*, *Kediler balık sever.* With a bare subject the aorist makes it generic: *Kedi balık sever* "cats like fish", while *Kedi balık yiyor* is "the cat is eating fish" (goksel2005 §21.4.1.1).
- **Character and lasting habits of a person**: *Ayşe et yemez*, *Mehmet çok konuşur*, *Babam her sabah gazete okur.* Frequency words: *her zaman*, *sık sık*, *bazen*, *genellikle*; with the negative, *hiç* or *hiçbir zaman* "never" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.3): *Hiçbir zaman geç kalmaz* "He's never late."
- **Regular arrangements**: *Cumartesi çalışmayız.*
- **Rules and instructions**, in notices, recipes and laws. These usually use the passive (part 7).
- **Assumptions about the future**: *Ali yarın gelmez*, *Belki gelir*, *Sanırım yağmur yağar.*
- **Hopes**: *Umarım*, *inşallah* with the aorist: *İnşallah geç kalmazlar.*
- **Requests**: *-(y)Ar mIsInIz?*: *Kapıyı açar mısınız?*, *Bana yardım eder misiniz?*
- **Offers**: *Çay içer misiniz?*; persuasively negative: *Bizimle gelmez misin?*
- **Promises and commitments**, 1st person: *Yarın ararım*, *Geç kalmam*, *Söz veririm* "I promise."
- **Set phrases** in the negative: *Bilmem* "no idea", and *Olmaz* "no way, impossible" from *olmak* (tur-04-16).
- **In compound forms**: past habit *-(A/I)rdI* (tur-04-10).

## Watch out

**Do not keep the z in the 1st person.** *\*Gelmezim*, *\*yapmazım*, *\*gelmeziz* look regular but are wrong. *Gelmem*, *yapmam*, *gelmeyiz*.

**Do not drop the z elsewhere.** *\*Gelmesin* for "you don't come" is the command "let him not come". "You don't come" is *gelmezsin*. *\*Gelme* is "don't come!", not "he doesn't come": *gelmez*.

**Do not drop the z before mI.** *\*Gelme miyim?*, *\*yapma mıyız?*: in questions the 1st person keeps *-mAz*: *gelmez miyim?*, *yapmaz mıyız?*

**Do not use -mAz on the thirteen as -mIr.** *\*Gelmir*, *\*bilmir* carry *-Ir* into the negative: *gelmez*, *bilmez*.

**Do not use the aorist for what is going on.** *\*Şu anda yağmur yağar* for "it's raining right now" is wrong: *Şu anda yağmur yağıyor.* The aorist never presents an action as in progress (goksel2005 §21.3.2).

**Do not use -(I)yor for a generic truth about a bare noun.** *\*Kedi balık seviyor* for "cats like fish" talks about one particular cat. For the class: *Kedi balık sever*, or *Kediler balık sever.*

**Do not make requests with -(I)yor or the future.** *\*Kapıyı açıyor musunuz?* asks "Are you opening the door?" *\*Kapıyı açacak mısınız?* asks about the hearer's plans, and can sound like a challenge. A request is *Kapıyı açar mısınız?*

**Do not confuse "I'm not coming" with "I won't come".** *Gelmiyorum* reports a plan or an observed fact: "I'm not coming (I've arranged otherwise)". *Gelmem* is a refusal or a rule: "I won't come; I don't come to such things."

**Do not stress -mAz like -mA.** *\*Gélmez*, *\*yápmam* follow the rule for *-mA*. The negative aorist is the exception: *gelméz*, *yapmám*.

## Connections

Korean draws a related line between the plain present and -고 있다 for what is happening now, but Turkish goes further: its aorist is also the form of offers (*içer misiniz?*), much as English uses "would you" and Japanese uses 〜ませんか (飲みませんか "won't you have a drink?"), a negative question like *gelmez misin?*. The negative-question offer is a typological parallel, not a sign of shared origin.

## Exercises

1. Give the negative aorist of *bilmek* in all six persons.
2. Translate, and say why the aorist is used: *Mehmet hiçbir zaman yalan söylemez.*
3. Build "We'll wait" as a promise, "We don't wait (for anyone)" as a rule, and "Won't you wait?" as a persuasive question.
4. What is the difference between *Ayşe kahve içmiyor* and *Ayşe kahve içmez*?
5. Ask a waiter politely: "Would you bring the bill?"
6. A learner writes *\*Ben et yemezim* and *\*Şimdi kitap okurum.* Correct both.
7. Translate *Umarım geç kalmazsınız* and take apart the last word.
8. Choose between *uyuyor* and *uyur* in *Bebek şimdi ...* ("The baby is asleep now") and *Bebekler çok ...* ("Babies sleep a lot").

## Worked answers

1. *Bilmem, bilmezsin, bilmez, bilmeyiz, bilmezsiniz, bilmezler.* *Bil-* is one of the thirteen, but its negative is regular *-mAz*; the 1st persons drop the *z*.
2. "Mehmet never lies." A statement about his character, a rule with no time limit, so the aorist; *hiçbir zaman* needs the negative (goksel2005 §16.4.1.3). *Yalan söyle-* "tell a lie", *-mez*.
3. Promise: *Bekleriz*, a vowel stem with *-r* and "we" *-iz*. Rule: *Beklemeyiz*, with no *z* in the 1st plural. Question: *Beklemez misiniz?*, where *mI* takes *-siniz* and the *z* stays.
4. *İçmiyor* reports what you observe: she isn't drinking coffee (these days). *İçmez* states a rule: she is someone who doesn't drink coffee (goksel2005 §21.4.1.1).
5. *Hesabı getirir misiniz?* *Getir-* has two syllables: *-ir*; a request is a 2nd-person aorist question.
6. *Ben et yemem*: the 1st singular drops the *z*. *Şimdi kitap okuyorum*: an action in progress needs *-(I)yor*.
7. "I hope you won't be late." *Kal-maz-sınız*: stem, negative aorist (with *z*, since this is 2nd person), 2nd plural.
8. *Bebek şimdi uyuyor*: a state going on now. *Bebekler çok uyur*: typical behaviour of a class.
