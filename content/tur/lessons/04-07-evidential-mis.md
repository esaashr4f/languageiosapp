## The idea

Turkish has two pasts. You know *-DI*: *Ali geldi* "Ali came". Here is the other:

*Ali gelmiş.* "Ali has come, apparently." / "I hear Ali came."

Both say the coming is over. The difference is how the speaker knows. With *-DI*, the speaker vouches for it: he saw it, did it, or knows it as a plain fact. With ***-mIş***, the knowledge is **indirect**: he was told, he read it, he worked it out from what he can see, or he has only just found out. This kind of marking is called **evidentiality**: grammar that shows the source of the speaker's information.

Göksel and Kerslake list two main sources for *-mIş* (goksel2005 §21.4.3):

1. **Information from others**, spoken or written: *Ali gelmiş* "(they tell me) Ali has come". English uses "apparently", "it seems", "I gather".
2. **Results**: you see the effect but did not see the event. The street is wet: *Yağmur yağmış* "it's been raining". Your keys are not in your pocket: *Anahtarımı unutmuşum* "I seem to have forgotten my key".

They stress that this is **not optional**. If you report something you know only second-hand with *-DI*, you imply that you witnessed it, which breaks the conventions of conversation (goksel2005 §21.4.3.1). An English speaker can say "Ali came" whatever his source; a Turkish speaker has to choose.

*-mIş* also does the work of the past in **stories that the teller did not live through**: folk tales, anecdotes, jokes. The Nasreddin Hoca stories, the best-known Turkish anecdotes, are told in *-mIş* from start to finish. Then the Hoca himself speaks, about what he knows at first hand, and switches to *-DI* (example 11).

This lesson covers *-mIş* as the last tense marker on a verb, which this guide glosses PST.EVID: past plus evidential, in one piece. Göksel and Kerslake note that *-mIş* in this position marks both evidentiality and perfectivity (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3). Two related uses come later: *-(y)mIş* after a noun or another tense, which is evidential only (tur-04-08), and *-mIş* before another marker, *gelmişti* "he had come", where it has no evidential meaning at all (tur-04-10).

## How it is built

*-mIş* is regular. Take four steps.

1. **Find the stem**: *gel-*, *oku-*, *yap-*.
2. **Negative?** Add *-mA*, with its vowel unchanged: *gel-me-*, *yap-ma-*.
3. **Add -mIş**, four-way harmony: *-miş*, *-mış*, *-muş*, *-müş*. It begins with a consonant, so there is no buffer and no stem change: *oku-muş*, *git-miş*, *et-miş*, *de-miş*, *ye-miş*, *gelme-miş*.
4. **Add the z-set ending**, as after *-(I)yor*, the aorist and the future (goksel2005 §8.4): *gelmiş-im*, *gelmiş-sin*, *gelmiş*, *gelmiş-iz*, *gelmiş-siniz*, *gelmiş-ler*.

**Stress** falls on *-mIş*: *gelmíş*, *gelmíşim*, *gelmíşsiniz*; *-lAr* takes it: *gelmişlér*. With *-mA*, before the negative: *gélmemiş*.

**In speech**, the *s* of *-sIn* and *-sInIz* is often dropped after *-mIş*: *gelmişsin* is heard as *gelmişin* (goksel2005 §8.4). Write *-sin*.

**Questions** follow the z-set pattern: *mI* after *-mIş*, with the person ending: *Gelmiş mi?* "Has he come (do you know)?", *Duymuş musun?* "Have you heard?" An evidential question expects the hearer to answer from indirect knowledge: you ask someone reading a message *Ne demiş?* "What does he say?" (goksel2005 §21.4.3.4).

A worked chain. Your friend tells you that the neighbours have got divorced. You pass it on. The event is over: past. You did not witness it: *-mIş*, not *-DI*. Stem *boşan-*, *-mış* after *a*, "they" *-lar*: *Komşular boşanmışlar.*

## Where it applies

- **Hearsay**: anything you pass on from someone else, a newspaper, the radio, a message: *Komşumuz piyangoyu kazanmış*, *Ali gelmiş.* English "apparently", "I hear", "they say" (goksel2005 §21.4.3.1).
- **Inference from results**: *Yağmur yağmış*, *Lastik patlamış*, *Hırsız arabamızı çalmış.* Also comments on how something was made or done, judged from the result: tasting a dish, *Bu yemeğe tuz koymamışlar* "they haven't put any salt in this".
- **Things you did without knowing**: asleep, as a baby, absent-mindedly: *Çok uyumuşum*, *Anahtarımı unutmuşum.*
- **Sudden discovery** of a past event: *Meğer Ali gelmiş!* "Ali had come, as it turns out!" *Meğer* "it turns out" goes with *-mIş* and marks a revised belief (goksel2005 §16.3 (iv)). *Galiba* "probably, I think" also often accompanies it: *Galiba yanlış otobüse binmişiz* "I think we've got on the wrong bus."
- **Traditional narrative**: folk tales, anecdotes, jokes, history told from hearsay: the Nasreddin Hoca stories, and tales that begin *Bir varmış, bir yokmuş* "once upon a time" (literally "there was one, there wasn't one"; the copular form is taught in tur-04-08).
- **What others say about you**, in the 2nd person: *Türkçe öğreniyormuşsun* "I hear you're learning Turkish" (copular, tur-04-08); with a finished event, *Ehliyet almışsın* "I hear you got your driving licence."
- **Questions** where you expect the answer to be second-hand: *Ne demiş?* "What did he say (in his letter)?", *Film bitmiş mi?* "Has the film finished (can you see)?"
- **Only past, completed events.** For hearsay about the present or future (*he is ill*, *he will come*), the copular *-(y)mIş* is added to another tense or a noun (tur-04-08): *Hastaymış*, *Gelecekmiş.*

## Watch out

**Do not use -DI for hearsay.** You read in the paper that the team won. *\*Takım kazandı* presents it as something you know at first hand. Turkish expects *Takım kazanmış* (goksel2005 §21.4.3.1).

**Do not use -mIş for your own witnessed actions.** *\*Dün sinemaya gitmişim* says you have no memory of going, or have just been told you went. For what you did and remember: *Dün sinemaya gittim.*

**Do not use -mIş for doubt.** *-mIş* is not "maybe". *Ali gelmiş* states that Ali came; only the source is indirect. For "maybe he came", use *belki*, *galiba* or the forms of tur-04-11.

**Do not use the k-set after -mIş.** *\*Gelmişm*, *\*gelmişk* copy *geldim*, *geldik*. *-mIş* takes the z-set: *gelmişim*, *gelmişiz*.

**Do not raise the negative's vowel.** *\*Gelmimiş*, *\*yapmımış*: *-mA* keeps its vowel before *-mIş*: *gelmemiş*, *yapmamış*.

**Do not harmonise -mIş with o or ö.** *\*Gelmöş*, *\*okumoş*: *-mIş* has only the four high vowels: *okumuş*, *görmüş*.

**Do not mix the two pasts at random in a story.** In a tale told in *-mIş*, a sudden *-DI* makes the narrator a witness. Use *-DI* in a *-mIş* story only for quoted speech, as in the Hoca's punch line.

**Do not use -mIş for "has done" in the English sense.** English "I have finished" is *Bitirdim*, with *-DI* (tur-04-03). *Bitirmişim* would mean "I seem to have finished (I've just noticed)."

**Do not look for -mIş in every English "apparently".** If you saw it happen and English says "apparently" out of politeness, Turkish still uses *-DI*.

## Connections

Korean -더라 and -네 share part of *-mIş*'s ground: -더라 reports what the speaker observed earlier, and -네 marks a fresh discovery (비가 왔네 "oh, it's rained"), like *yağmur yağmış*. Japanese 〜らしい and 〜そうだ cover hearsay ("I hear"), but they are optional, while Turkish requires *-mIş* whenever the source is indirect.

## Exercises

1. Build "They haven't come, apparently."
2. Your friend tells you *Ayşe hastaneye gitti.* Pass it on to someone else.
3. Translate *Meğer biz yanlış otobüse binmişiz.*
4. Take apart *unutmuşsunuz* and give its meaning in two possible situations.
5. You open the door and see broken glass on the floor. Say "Someone has broken the window."
6. A learner writes *\*Dün akşam çok güzel bir film izlemişim*, meaning to describe an evening he remembers. Correct it.
7. Why does the Hoca's punch line use *-DI* when the rest of the anecdote uses *-mIş*?
8. Ask a friend who is reading a message from Mehmet: "What did Mehmet say?"

## Worked answers

1. *Gelmemişler.* Stem *gel-*, negative *-me-* (its vowel unchanged before a consonant), *-miş*, "they" *-ler*. Stressed *gélmemişler*.
2. *Ayşe hastaneye gitmiş.* You did not see it; you were told. Using *gitti* would claim first-hand knowledge (goksel2005 §21.4.3.1).
3. "It turns out we'd got on the wrong bus." *Meğer* signals a late discovery; *bin-miş-iz* is *binmek* "get on" + *-mIş* + "we". *Binmek* takes the dative (tur-03-02).
4. *Unut-muş-sunuz*: stem, *-muş* after *u*, 2nd plural *-sunuz*. "You've forgotten (it), I see" (from a result: the umbrella left on the table), or "you forgot, I hear" (someone told me).
5. *Biri pencereyi kırmış.* You see the result, not the event.
6. *Dün akşam çok güzel bir film izledim.* What you did and remember takes *-DI*. *İzlemişim* would mean you had no memory of it.
7. The narrator was not there: the story is passed down, so *-mIş*. The Hoca speaks of his own experience, so *-DI*: *Yorgan gitti, kavga bitti.*
8. *Mehmet ne demiş?* An evidential question: the answer will come from the message, not from the hearer's own experience (goksel2005 §21.4.3.4).
