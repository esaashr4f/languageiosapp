## The idea

You can say *Hastayım* "I am ill" (tur-02-02). How do you say "I was ill"? A noun or an adjective has no tense slot: *-DI* needs a verb stem. Turkish uses slot 6 instead, the **copula** slot of tur-04-01, which nouns share with verbs:

*Hastaydım.* "I was ill."

There are three **copular markers**, each glossed like the verb suffix it resembles (goksel2005 §8.3.2):

- ***-(y)DI*** "was": *hastaydı* "he was ill"; glossed PST.
- ***-(y)mIş*** "is or was, apparently": *hastaymış* "he is ill, I hear" or "he was ill, I hear"; glossed EVID.
- ***-(y)sA*** "if (it) is": *hastaysa* "if he is ill"; glossed COND. Conditional sentences are taught in full in tur-06-10, so the examples here carry a preview label.

They attach to anything that can be a predicate: an adjective (*hasta-ydı*), a noun (*öğretmen-di*), a locative (*evde-ydi*), and the words *var*, *yok* and *değil* (*var-dı*, *yok-tu*, *değil-di*). They follow the case or possessive endings and come before the person ending.

**Why the y?** The three markers are the old verb *i-* "be" with the suffixes *-DI*, *-mIş*, *-sA*. Göksel and Kerslake describe them as contracted forms of *idi*, *imiş* and *ise*, which still exist as separate words but are now seldom used (goksel2005 §§8.3.1, 8.3.2); Wiktionary traces *idi* to Proto-Turkic \*er-ti "was". After a consonant, the *i* disappears: *öğretmen idi* → *öğretmendi*. After a vowel, two vowels would meet, and the *i* survives as *y*: *hasta idi* → *hastaydı*. The *y* is not a buffer consonant of the usual kind but what is left of the verb (goksel2005 §8.3.1).

**Person endings.** The markers choose the set, as the tense suffixes do (goksel2005 §8.4): *-(y)DI* and *-(y)sA* take the k-set (*hastaydım*, *hastaysak*); *-(y)mIş* takes the z-set (*hastaymışım*, *hastaymışız*). This is the same pairing as verbal *-DI* (k-set) and *-mIş* (z-set).

**What -(y)mIş means.** It adds evidentiality and nothing else: no tense (goksel2005 §21.4.3.1). *Ali hastaymış* can be "Ali is ill (they say)" or "Ali was ill (they say)"; only context or a time word decides. It also marks a **new discovery**: something you have just found out, often with surprise (goksel2005 §21.4.3.3). Tasting a dish, *Bu yemek çok lezzetliymiş!* "This is delicious!"

## How it is built

To put a noun predicate in the past, take five steps.

1. **Build the predicate** as before: *hasta*, *öğretmen*, *İstanbul'da*, *evde*, *yok*.
2. **Choose the marker**: *-(y)DI*, *-(y)mIş* or *-(y)sA*.
3. **y or not?** After a vowel, *y*: *hasta-ydı*, *evde-ymiş*, *öğrenci-yse*. After a consonant, none: *öğretmen-di*, *yok-muş*, *var-sa*.
4. **Harmony and D.** *-(y)DI* and *-(y)mIş* harmonise four ways, *-(y)sA* two ways. The D of *-(y)DI* becomes *t* after a voiceless consonant: *yok-tu*, *Türk'-tü*, *aç-tı* "was hungry", but *var-dı*, *değil-di*.
5. **Add the person ending** from the right set:
   - after *-(y)DI* and *-(y)sA*, the k-set: *-m*, *-n*, nothing, *-k*, *-nIz*, *-lAr*;
   - after *-(y)mIş*, the z-set: *-(y)Im*, *-sIn*, nothing, *-(y)Iz*, *-sInIz*, *-lAr*.

A worked chain: "We were at home." Predicate: *ev-de*. Past: *-(y)DI*, after the vowel *e*: *evdeydi*. "We", k-set: *-k*. *Evdeydik.*

Another: "I hear they were in Ankara." *Ankara'da*, evidential *-(y)mIş* after *a*: *Ankara'daymış*. "They": *-lAr*, usually after the marker (goksel2005 §8.4): *Ankara'daymışlar.*

**Stress.** The copular markers cannot take stress, and they leave it where it was: *hastá*, *hastáydım*; *evdé*, *evdéydik*; *İstánbul'daydım* (goksel2005 §4.3.2.1).

**Questions.** *mI* comes before *-(y)DI* and *-(y)mIş*, and the marker and person ending go onto it: *Hasta mıydın?* "Were you ill?", *Evde miymiş?* "Is he at home, do they say?" (goksel2005 §11.1.1.5). Wiktionary's tables for *mi* give *miydim*, *miymiş* and so on.

**var, yok, değil.** *Var-dı* "there was", *yok-tu* "there wasn't", *değil-di* "was not". With *yok* and *değil*, "they" normally comes before the marker: *değillerdi*, *yoklarmış* (goksel2005 §8.4).

## Where it applies

- **Past states, on any predicate**: adjectives (*hastaydım*, *yorgundum*, *açtım* "I was hungry"), nouns (*öğretmendi*, *öğrenciydiler*), places (*evdeydik*, *İstanbul'daydık*), possessives (*Bu ev babamındı* "this house was my father's").
- **Past existence and "had"**: *vardı*, *yoktu*: *Evde kimse yoktu*, *Bir köpeğimiz vardı.* Göksel and Kerslake use *vardı* and *yoktu* as the past of the existential sentence (goksel2005 §21.2.1).
- **Negative past**: *değildi*, *değildik*; *yoktu*.
- **Hearsay about a present or past state**: *Ali hastaymış*, *Annesi evdeymiş*, *Çok zenginlermiş* "they're very rich, they say".
- **Discovery and surprise**: *Ne güzelmiş!* "How lovely!", *Bu yemek çok lezzetliymiş!*, *Hiç para yokmuş* "there's no money at all (I find)".
- **Fairy tales and anecdotes**: *Bir varmış, bir yokmuş*; *Bir zamanlar bir kral varmış* "once there was a king".
- **Conditions**: *-(y)sA* for "if (it) is": *Hastaysan*, *Zamanın varsa*, *Evdeyse* "if he's at home" (tur-06-10 for the full system).
- **Questions**: *mI* before the marker: *Evde miydi?*, *Hasta mıymış?*
- **After a tense on a verb**: the same three markers follow *-(I)yor*, the aorist, the future and *-mIş*: *geliyordu*, *gelirdi*, *gelecekti*, *geliyormuş*, *geliyorsa* (tur-04-10).
- **As separate words**: *idi*, *imiş*, *ise* are written apart in older and very formal texts, or for emphasis (goksel2005 §8.3.1). You will meet them in the readings of part 8.

## Watch out

**Do not leave out the y after a vowel.** *\*Hastadım*, *\*evdedik*, *\*hastamış*: after a vowel the copula needs *y*: *hastaydım*, *evdeydik*, *hastaymış*.

**Do not add y after a consonant.** *\*Öğretmenydi*, *\*yokydu*: *öğretmendi*, *yoktu*.

**Do not forget that D can be t.** *\*Yokdu*, *\*Türk'düm*, *\*açdım*: after a voiceless consonant, *t*: *yoktu*, *Türk'tüm*, *açtım*.

**Do not confuse açtım "I was hungry" with açtım "I opened".** They are spelt alike: *aç-tı-m* (adjective + copula) and *aç-tı-m* (verb *açmak* + past). Context decides. *Kapıyı açtım* is "I opened the door"; *Çok açtım* is "I was very hungry".

**Do not use the z-set after -(y)DI, or the k-set after -(y)mIş.** *\*Hastaydıyım*, *\*hastaymışm*: *hastaydım*, *hastaymışım*.

**Do not stack two copular markers of this kind.** *\*Hastaydımış* for "I hear he was ill" is impossible. *-(y)mIş* alone covers the past: *Hastaymış*, with a time word if needed: *Dün hastaymış.*

**Do not read -(y)mIş as a past tense.** *Ali evdeymiş* is not only "Ali was at home". It is just as often "Ali is at home (I'm told)". Only *-(y)DI* adds the past.

**Do not use the copula for "became".** *\*Ali öğretmendi* does not mean "Ali became a teacher". "Became" needs a verb, *olmak*: *Ali öğretmen oldu* (tur-04-16).

**Do not put mI after the copula.** *\*Evdeydi mi?* is heard, but the standard order puts *mI* first: *Evde miydi?*

**Do not drop the apostrophe.** *\*Ankaradaydık*: endings on a proper name follow an apostrophe: *Ankara'daydık.*

**Do not build "had" with a pronoun and a bare noun.** *\*Benim köpek vardı*: the thing owned needs its possessive, as in the present: *Benim köpeğim vardı*, or *Köpeğim vardı.*

## Connections

Japanese adds the past to nouns and adjectives in the same way, with a copula: 病気だった (byōki datta) "was ill", like *hastaydı*; and Korean has 아팠어요 / 학생이었어요. Spanish has two verbs, *ser* and *estar*, and a choice of tenses (*era*, *fue*, *estaba*, *estuvo*); Turkish has one marker, *-(y)DI*, for "was" in all of them. The Korean discovery ending -네 (맛있네! "oh, it's tasty!") is close in use to *lezzetliymiş!*

## Exercises

1. Put into the past: *Yorgunum*, *Evde değiliz*, *Bahçede çocuklar var*.
2. Build "Were you (polite) at the concert?"
3. Translate *Partide çok ünlü biri varmış* and say what kind of knowledge it reports.
4. Take apart *Ankara'daydılar* and *değildik*.
5. Your friend has just met your sister and says she is very nice. Use *tatlı* "sweet, nice": "Your sister's very sweet!" (you have just found out).
6. A learner writes *\*Dün çok meşguldüydüm* and *\*Film eğlencelidi*. Correct both.
7. Translate *Hava çok sıcaktı ama otobüs boştu.*
8. Make "If the shop is closed, we'll come tomorrow" with *dükkân*, *kapalı*, and the future (preview).
9. What two meanings can *Mehmet hastanedeymiş* have?

## Worked answers

1. *Yorgundum* (*n* is voiced: *d*; *u* by harmony; k-set *-m*). *Evde değildik* (*değil-di-k*). *Bahçede çocuklar vardı* (*var-dı*).
2. *Konserde miydiniz?* *Konser-de* "at the concert", then *mI*, which takes the past copula and the person ending: *mi-ydi-niz*.
3. "Apparently there was (or is) someone very famous at the party." *Var-mış*: hearsay, with no tense of its own; context suggests the past (goksel2005 §21.4.3.1).
4. *Ankara-da-ydı-lar*: Ankara, locative, past copula with *y* after the vowel, 3rd plural after the marker: "they were in Ankara". *Değil-di-k*: not, past copula, k-set "we": "we were not".
5. *Kız kardeşin çok tatlıymış!* *Tatlı-ymış*: a new discovery, so *-(y)mIş* (goksel2005 §21.4.3.3).
6. *Dün çok meşguldüm*: one copular marker, after a consonant without *y*; *meşgul* takes front vowels, *meşguldüm* (Wiktionary). *Film eğlenceliydi*: after a vowel, *y*.
7. "The weather was very hot, but the bus was empty." *Sıcak-tı* (*t* after *k*), *boş-tu* (*t* after *ş*).
8. *Dükkân kapalıysa yarın geleceğiz.* *Kapalı-ysa*, with *y* after the vowel; the main clause in the future (tur-04-04).
9. "Mehmet is in hospital, I hear" or "Mehmet was in hospital, I hear". *Hastane-de-ymiş*: locative, then the evidential copula, which has no tense of its own (goksel2005 §21.4.3.1). A time word such as *dün* would fix it in the past.
