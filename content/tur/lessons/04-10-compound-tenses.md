## The idea

Slot 5 holds one tense. Slot 6 holds a copular marker. Put one of each on the same verb and you get a **compound tense**:

*Geliyordu.* "He was coming." *Gelirdi.* "He used to come." *Gelmişti.* "He had come."

You already know both halves. *Geliyor* is "he is coming"; the past copula *-(y)DI* of tur-04-08 moves the whole predicate into the past, just as it moves *hasta* "ill" to *hastaydı* "was ill". Göksel and Kerslake describe the past copula as presenting a situation as it was at some time in the past, from the inside (goksel2005 §21.2.1). So *geliyordu* is "is coming", seen from a point in the past: "was coming".

This is why Turkish needs no separate past progressive, past habitual or pluperfect suffix. One tense plus one copula covers them all:

- ***-(I)yordu***: "was doing", the past of *-(I)yor*: an action in progress, or a state, at a past time.
- ***-(A/I)rdI***: "used to do, would do", the past of the aorist: a characteristic habit; also "would (have)" for things that did not happen.
- ***-mIştI***: "had done": an event completed before a point in the past.
- ***-(y)AcAktI***: "was going to do": a past expectation, often one that did not come true.

The evidential copula *-(y)mIş* stacks the same way and adds "I hear, it seems": *geliyormuş* "he's coming, apparently", *gelirmiş* "he comes (they say)", *gelecekmiş* "he's going to come, I hear". The conditional copula *-(y)sA* does too: *geliyorsa* "if he's coming", in tur-06-10.

**-mIş changes meaning here.** On its own at the end of a verb, *-mIş* is evidential: *gelmiş* "he came, apparently" (tur-04-07). Before a copular marker it loses the evidential meaning and keeps only completion. Göksel and Kerslake state that when *-mIş* is followed by a copular marker or *-DIr*, it indicates only perfectivity (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3). *Gelmişti* is a plain "he had come", which you can say of what you saw yourself. This guide glosses this *-mIş* as PRF, **perfect**: the action is complete and its result counts at the reference time.

**-DIydI.** In informal speech the past *-DI* can also take the past copula: *geldiydi*, *geldiydim*. Göksel and Kerslake say it means the same as *-mIştI* but is restricted to informal registers (goksel2005 §8.3.2). Recognise it; write *-mIştI*.

## How it is built

To build a compound tense, take five steps.

1. **Stem and negative**: *gel-*, *gel-me-*.
2. **Tense** in slot 5: *-(I)yor*, the aorist, *-mIş* or *-(y)AcAK*: *geliyor*, *gelir*, *gelmiş*, *gelecek*. For the negative: *gelmiyor*, *gelmez*, *gelmemiş*, *gelmeyecek*. The negative aorist keeps its *z* in all persons here, since the person ending no longer touches it: *gelmezdim*, *gelmezdik* (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3).
3. **Copula** in slot 6. These four tense suffixes end in a consonant, so there is no *y*: *-DI* or *-mIş*, harmonising with the tense: *geliyor-du*, *gelir-di*, *gelmiş-ti*, *gelecek-ti*. D becomes *t* after *ş* and *k*.
4. **Person**, chosen by the copula: after *-(y)DI* the k-set, after *-(y)mIş* the z-set: *geliyordum*, *geliyormuşum*. *K* of the future stays *k*, since a consonant follows: *gelecektim*.
5. **"They"**: *-lAr* usually goes between the tense and the copula: *geliyorlardı*, *gelirlerdi*, *gelmişlerdi*, *geleceklerdi*, *geliyorlarmış*; it can also come last (goksel2005 §8.4).

**Stress** stays where the tense put it, because the copula and the person endings after it cannot take stress: *gelíyordum*, *gelírdim*, *gelmíştim*, *gelecéktim*; negative *gélmiyordum*, *gelmézdim* (goksel2005 §4.3.2).

**Questions.** *mI* goes before the copula, and the copula and person go onto it (goksel2005 §11.1.1.5): *Geliyor muydun?* "Were you coming?", *Gelir miydi?* "Did he use to come?", *Başlamış mıydınız?* "Had you started?"

A worked chain: "When we got home, the children had already gone to bed." Turkish has not yet given you "when" clauses, so say it in two sentences. "We arrived home late": *Eve geç vardık.* Then the earlier event: stem *yat-*, completed before that point: *-mIş*, then past copula: *yatmıştı*. *Çocuklar çoktan yatmıştı.*

## Where it applies

**-(I)yordu**

- **An action in progress at a past moment**, often as the background to an event in *-DI*: *Duş alıyordum, telefon çaldı.*
- **Past states** with verbs that take *-(I)yor* for states: *biliyordum* "I knew", *istiyordum* "I wanted", *oturuyorduk* "we were living".
- **Observed past habits**: *O zamanlar çok yüzüyordu* "at that time she swam a lot". Göksel and Kerslake note that *-(I)yordu* and *-(A/I)rdI* are often interchangeable for past habits; *-(A/I)rdI* presents the habit as a lasting characteristic (goksel2005 §21.3.2).
- **A past schedule**: *Ertesi gün annesi geliyordu* "his mother was coming the next day" (goksel2005 §21.2.3).

**-(A/I)rdI**

- **"Used to", "would"** for characteristic habits: *Her yaz köye giderdik*, *Dedem masal anlatırdı.*
- **What would happen or would have happened**, contrary to fact: *Ben bu ceketi almazdım.* Conditional sentences of this kind come in tur-06-11.
- **Polite wishes that cannot be met**: *Çok isterdim* "I would have loved to".

**-mIştI**

- **"Had done"**, before a past reference point: *Çocuklar çoktan yatmıştı.*
- **A past quite separate from now**: *Bu filmi görmüştüm*, *Ben sana söylemiştim!* "I told you (that time)!" (goksel2005 §21.2.1).
- Informally, *-DIydI* in the same senses: *Söylediydim.*

**-(y)AcAktI**

- **"Was going to"**, often with "but": *Sana telefon edecektim ama unuttum.*
- **A plan for the future that is now off**, even with a future time word: *Gelecek hafta gelecekti* "he was going to come next week" (goksel2005 §21.2.3).

**With -(y)mIş**: hearsay and discovery about ongoing, habitual or future situations: *Türkçe öğreniyormuşsun*, *Ali yarın gelecekmiş*, *Ali çok çay içermiş* "Ali drinks a lot of tea, they say".

**With -(y)sA**: *geliyorsa*, *gelirse*, *gelmişse*: conditions (tur-06-10).

## Watch out

**Do not stack two tense suffixes.** *\*Geldiyordu*, *\*gelecekiyordu*: slot 5 holds one; the second piece must be a copula: *geliyordu*, *gelecekti*.

**Do not harmonise the copula with the stem.** *\*Geliyordi*, *\*gelmiştı*: the copula follows the vowel just before it: *geliyordu*, *gelmişti*.

**Do not use the z-set after -(y)DI.** *\*Geliyorduyum*, *\*gelirdiyiz*: *geliyordum*, *gelirdik*.

**Do not drop the z of -mAz in the compound.** *\*Gelmedim* for "I didn't use to come" is the plain negative past "I didn't come". The negative habit is *gelmezdim*.

**Do not read -mIştI as hearsay.** *Ali gelmişti* is "Ali had come", which you may have seen with your own eyes. Wiktionary's tables also list a double form, *gelmişmiş*, which this guide does not teach.

**Do not use -DI for the Spanish or French imperfect.** *\*Eskiden her yaz köye gittik* lists summers as finished events; for "we used to go", use *giderdik* or *gidiyorduk*. Spanish *íbamos*, French *nous allions*.

**Do not use -(A/I)rdI for an action in progress.** *\*Telefon çaldı, duş alırdım* would mean "I would take a shower". The aorist never presents an action as in progress (goksel2005 §21.3.2): *duş alıyordum*.

**Do not use -(y)AcAktI for "would" in general.** *\*Ben bu ceketi almayacaktım* is "I wasn't going to buy this jacket" (a plan). For "wouldn't have bought": *almazdım*. Göksel and Kerslake allow *-(y)AcAktI* in a "would" sense only for planned or scheduled events (goksel2005 §21.4.1.3).

**Do not put mI after the copula.** *\*Geliyordun mu?*, *\*gelirdi mi?*: *mI* comes before it: *Geliyor muydun?*, *Gelir miydi?*

**Do not stress the copula.** *\*Gelirdím*, *\*geliyordúm*: *gelírdim*, *gelíyordum*.

## Connections

French and Spanish have one imperfect (*je venais*, *venía*) for both "was coming" and "used to come", beside a perfective past (*je suis venu*, *vine*) that matches *-DI*. Turkish divides the ground differently, splitting the imperfect into *-(I)yordu* (in progress) and *-(A/I)rdI* (characteristic habit). Japanese 〜ていた and Korean -고 있었다 build "was doing" the same way, from a progressive plus a past: *okuyordu*, 読んでいた, 읽고 있었다.

## Exercises

1. Put *Ayşe mektup yazıyor* into the past progressive, and *Ayşe her gün mektup yazar* into the past habitual.
2. Build "We had already eaten", step by step.
3. Translate *O sırada yağmur yağıyordu ve sokakta kimse yoktu.*
4. Take apart *gelmeyecektiniz* and *tırmanırlardı*.
5. Say "I was going to buy a ticket, but I didn't have money."
6. Ask a friend: "Were you sleeping?" and "Had you seen this film?"
7. A learner writes *\*Eskiden her sabah kahve içtim* for "I used to drink coffee every morning", and *\*Ben duş alırdım, postacı geldi* for "I was having a shower when the postman came." Correct both.
8. Translate *Komşumuz eskiden doktormuş* and *Komşumuz İzmir'e taşınıyormuş.*
9. What is the difference between *Ali gelmiş* and *Ali gelmişti*?

## Worked answers

1. *Ayşe mektup yazıyordu.* *Ayşe her gün mektup yazardı.* In both the past copula goes after the tense, *-du* after *-yor* and *-dı* after *-ar* (tur-04-10).
2. Stem *ye-*. Completed before a past point: *-mIş*: *yemiş*. Past copula, after *ş*: *-ti*: *yemişti*. "We", k-set: *-k*. *Zaten yemiştik.* (*Zaten* "already, anyway".)
3. "At that moment it was raining and there was no one in the street." *Yağıyordu*: the background, in progress; *yoktu*: past copula on *yok* (tur-04-08).
4. *Gel-me-yecek-ti-niz*: stem, negative, future, past copula, 2nd plural: "you weren't going to come". *Tırman-ır-lar-dı*: stem (two syllables, *-Ir*), aorist, 3rd plural before the copula, past copula: "they used to climb".
5. *Bilet alacaktım ama param yoktu.* *Al-acak-tı-m*: a past intention; *para-m yok-tu*: "my money was non-existent", the past of a "have" sentence (tur-03-09, tur-04-08).
6. *Uyuyor muydun?* *Bu filmi görmüş müydün?* *MI* comes before the copula, which takes the person ending (goksel2005 §11.1.1.5).
7. *Eskiden her sabah kahve içerdim* (or *içiyordum*): a past habit, not a finished event. *Ben duş alıyordum, postacı geldi*: the action in progress is *-(I)yordu*; the aorist cannot express progress.
8. "Our neighbour used to be a doctor, I hear": *doktor-muş*, the evidential copula on a noun; *eskiden* places it in the past (tur-04-08). "Our neighbour is moving to İzmir, I hear": *-(I)yor* + *-(y)mIş*, hearsay about something in progress.
9. *Gelmiş* is evidential: "he has come, apparently" (tur-04-07). *Gelmişti* has no evidential meaning: "he had come" (before some past moment), whether you saw it or not (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3).
