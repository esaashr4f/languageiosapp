## The idea

In speech, "Ankara is the capital of Turkey" needs no "is": *Ankara Türkiye'nin başkenti.* In an encyclopedia, a textbook or a news bulletin you will read:

*Ankara Türkiye'nin başkentidir.*

The ending ***-DIr*** is the last slot of the template, slot 8, after the person ending (goksel2005 §8.3.3). It has two jobs that look opposite at first:

1. **Formal "is"**: a statement of fact, a definition, a rule, presented as having authority. Göksel and Kerslake note that this use is nowadays largely confined to formal language (goksel2005 §21.4.1.1).
2. **"Must be, probably is"**: an assumption. *Ali evdedir* in conversation does not state that Ali is at home; it says "Ali will be at home, I expect". Göksel and Kerslake list *-DIr* with the aorist as the main markers of probability judgements, usually with an adverb such as *herhalde* "probably" or *mutlaka* "definitely" (goksel2005 §21.4.1.4).

What joins them is generalisation. Göksel and Kerslake call *-DIr* the "generalizing modality marker": it presents a statement not as something just observed, but as true on general grounds, whether those grounds are authority (the encyclopedia) or reasoning (Ali is always home at this hour). A third, small job follows from this: with a length of time, *üç yıldır* means "for three years (and still)" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.2).

**Where it comes from.** Wiktionary traces *-DIr* to the Proto-Turkic verb \*tur- "stand", the same root as *durmak* "stand, stop, stay" (tur-04-02). "Ali stands at home" became "Ali is at home"; the old verb survives only as an ending.

**Where it goes.** *-DIr* attaches to:

- **noun predicates**: *başkentidir*, *kapalıdır*, *evdedir*, *vardır*, *değildir*;
- **verbs** with *-(I)yor*, *-mIş*, *-(y)AcAK*, *-mAlI* (tur-04-12) and the formal *-mAktA* (tur-07-08): *biliyordur*, *unutmuştur*, *başlayacaktır* (goksel2005 §8.3.3).

It never follows *-DI* or the aorist: there is no *\*geldidir* or *\*gelirdir* (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3).

**-mIş before -DIr.** As before a copular marker, *-mIş* here loses its evidential meaning: *gelmiştir* is "he has come; he must have come", not hearsay (goksel2005 §21.4.1.2). This guide glosses it PRF, as in *gelmişti*.

## How it is built

To add *-DIr*, take four steps.

1. **Build the predicate complete with its person ending**: *yorgun-sunuz* "you are tired", *bil-iyor* "he knows", *unut-muş-um* "I have forgotten".
2. **Add -DIr after the person ending.** Four-way harmony, and *t* after a voiceless consonant: *yorgunsunuz-dur*, *biliyor-dur*, *unutmuşum-dur*, *kapalı-dır*, *ücretsiz-dir*, *Türk'-tür*, *yok-tur*.
3. **"They"**: *-lAr* can come before or after: *gelmişlerdir* or *gelmiştirler* (goksel2005 §8.3.3).
4. **Stress**: *-DIr* cannot take stress, so it stays where it was: *kapalídır*, *evdédir*, *bilíyordur* (goksel2005 §4.3.2).

**Questions** in the 3rd person put *mI* before *-DIr*: *Evde midir?* "Is he at home, do you think?" (goksel2005 §11.1.1.5).

A worked chain: "You must be tired." State: *yorgun*. "You" (polite): *yorgunsunuz*. An assumption: add *-DIr*, after *u*: *-dur*. Add the adverb that gives your confidence: *Herhalde yorgunsunuzdur.*

Another: "I've been learning Turkish for three years." Length of time, *üç yıl*, plus *-DIr*: *üç yıldır*. The learning is still going on: *-(I)yor*, not *-DI*: *Üç yıldır Türkçe öğreniyorum.*

## Where it applies

- **Definitions and classifications** in formal language: *Ankara Türkiye'nin başkentidir*, *Su hayattır* "water is life".
- **Notices, rules and instructions**: *Müze pazartesi kapalıdır*, *Giriş ücretsizdir*, *Burada sigara yasaktır* "smoking is forbidden here".
- **Proverbs and maxims**: *Söz gümüşse, sükût altındır.*
- **Formal writing and broadcasts**, on verbs: *-mIştIr* as a formal past (*yazmıştır*), *-(y)AcAktIr* as a formal future (*başlayacaktır*), *-mAlIdIr* (tur-04-12), *-mAktAdIr* (tur-07-08). Göksel and Kerslake give the equivalences *-mIştIr* = *-DI*, *-(y)AcAktIr* = *-(y)AcAK* in formal style (goksel2005 §21.4.1.2).
- **Assumptions about the present or past**, in any person, usually with *herhalde* "probably", *mutlaka* "definitely", *kesinlikle* "certainly" or *belki* "perhaps": *Ali evdedir*, *Yorgunsunuzdur*, *Unutmuştur* "he must have forgotten".
- **Emphasis** that something is so: Wiktionary notes that in conversation the "is" sense is usually omitted and used only to stress that something is a fact: *Bu benim evimdir* "this IS my house".
- **Existence**: *vardır* "there is, there must be", *yoktur* "there isn't (surely)".
- **Duration up to now**: *üç yıldır*, *iki haftadır* "for two weeks", with *-(I)yor*.
- **Questions** asking for the hearer's guess: *Evde midir?*

## Watch out

**Do not add -DIr to every sentence.** In conversation *-DIr* makes a plain statement sound bookish or like a guess. "I'm a teacher" is *Öğretmenim*; *\*Öğretmenimdir* would mean "I must be a teacher". Leave it for formal style and for assumptions.

**Do not put -DIr before the person ending.** *\*Yorgundursun*, *\*evdedirim*: *-DIr* is slot 8, after the person: *yorgunsundur*, *evdeyimdir*.

**Do not add -DIr to -DI or the aorist.** *\*Geldidir*, *\*gelirdir* do not exist (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3). For "he must have come", use *-mIş*: *gelmiştir*. For a formal habit, the aorist alone is already general: *gelir*.

**Do not read -mIştIr as hearsay.** *Yazmıştır* in a history book is a confident statement, not "apparently wrote". The evidential meaning belongs only to final *-mIş* (tur-04-07).

**Do not use the past for "have been doing".** English "I have been learning Turkish for three years" tempts *\*Üç yıl Türkçe öğrendim*, which means "I studied Turkish for three years (and that's over)". The ongoing situation needs *-DIr* on the time and *-(I)yor* on the verb: *Üç yıldır Türkçe öğreniyorum.*

**Do not forget the t after voiceless consonants.** *\*Yokdur*, *\*Türk'dür*: *yoktur*, *Türk'tür*.

**Do not confuse it with the causative -DIr.** Part 7 teaches a voice suffix of the same shape: *yap-tır-* "have something done". That one is in slot 2, next to the stem; *-DIr* "is" is in slot 8, at the very end.

**Do not mistake -DIr for certainty.** *Ali evdedir* in conversation is weaker than *Ali evde*: it is an inference, not an observation. To state what you know, leave it out.

## Connections

Japanese has a similar split between plain だ and written である (首都である "is the capital"), and uses でしょう / だろう for confident guesses, much as *-DIr* serves both formal statements and assumptions. Korean 이다 written in plain style (-이다) is the formal "is" of textbooks.

## Exercises

1. Make these into formal written statements: *İstanbul çok büyük bir şehir*, *Bu kural çok önemli*.
2. Build "They must be at home" in two orders.
3. Take apart *başlamıştır* and give its meaning in a history book and in conversation.
4. Translate *Herhalde bir sebebi vardır* and *Mutlaka unutmuşsundur.*
5. Say "We've been living in İzmir for two years."
6. A learner introduces himself with *\*Benim adım Mehmet'tir, öğretmenimdir.* What impression does this give, and what should he say?
7. Correct *\*Dün geldidir* ("he must have come yesterday").
8. Translate *Giriş ücretsizdir, fakat fotoğraf çekmek yasaktır*, and say which word is a preview (*fotoğraf çekmek* "take photographs").

## Worked answers

1. *İstanbul çok büyük bir şehirdir.* *Bu kural çok önemlidir.* *-DIr* after the predicate; *şehir* keeps its *i* before a consonant (tur-03-01 notes the vowel loss only before vowels).
2. *Evdedirler* or *Evdelerdir.* *-lAr* may come before or after *-DIr* (goksel2005 §8.3.3).
3. *Başla-mış-tır*: stem, *-mIş* as PRF, *-DIr*. In a history book: "began", a formal past (goksel2005 §21.4.1.2). In conversation: "it must have begun", an assumption.
4. "There must be a reason." "You must have forgotten, surely." *Unut-muş-sun-dur*: *-DIr* after the person ending (tur-04-11).
5. *İki yıldır İzmir'de oturuyoruz.* *İki yıl-dır* "for two years", and *-(I)yor* because it still goes on. *Yaşıyoruz* is also right.
6. It sounds stiff, and *öğretmenimdir* sounds like a guess about himself: "I must be a teacher". He should say *Benim adım Mehmet, öğretmenim.*
7. *Dün gelmiştir.* *-DIr* cannot follow *-DI*; "must have" uses *-mIş* + *-DIr* (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3).
8. "Admission is free, but taking photographs is forbidden." *Fotoğraf çekmek* uses the infinitive *-mAk* as the subject of a sentence, which part 6 teaches (tur-06-01): a preview.
