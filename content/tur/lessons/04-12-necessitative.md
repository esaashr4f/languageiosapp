## The idea

*Gitmeliyim.* "I must go." / "I should be going."

The suffix ***-mAlI*** goes in slot 5, the tense slot, and says that an action is **necessary** in the speaker's eyes. This guide calls it the **necessitative**, after Wiktionary's tables; Göksel and Kerslake call it the obligative and describe it as the grammatical marker of necessity in Turkish (goksel2005 §21.4.2.2).

The key word is "in the speaker's eyes". Göksel and Kerslake contrast *-mAlI* with the other ways of expressing necessity, built with the words *lazım* and *gerek* "necessary" (goksel2005 §21.4.2.2):

- *-mAlI* expresses an obligation that the speaker **perceives or imposes**: *Gitmeliyim* "I must go (I feel I should)".
- *lazım*, *gerek* present an **objective** obligation, arising from outside: *Gitmem lazım* "I have to go (circumstances require it)".

They compare the difference to English "must" and "have to". So *-mAlI* covers "must", "should" and "ought to": a duty, a piece of advice, an order, a resolution. The *lazım* construction needs a verbal noun, taught in tur-06-02; with an ordinary noun it already works: *Bana bir sözlük lazım* "I need a dictionary", literally "a dictionary is necessary to me".

**Person endings.** *-mAlI* takes the z-set, with the buffer *y* where the ending starts with a vowel, because *-mAlI* ends in a vowel (goksel2005 §8.4): *gelmeliyim*, *gelmelisin*, *gelmeli*, *gelmeliyiz*, *gelmelisiniz*, *gelmeliler*.

**The past** adds the copula *-(y)DI*: *gelmeliydin*. Göksel and Kerslake note that in most cases this is counterfactual: the obligation was not fulfilled (goksel2005 §21.4.2.2). *Gelmeliydin* is "you should have come (but you didn't)".

## How it is built

To say what someone must do, take five steps.

1. **Find the stem**: *iç-* "drink", *yat-* "go to bed".
2. **Negative?** "Must not" is *-mA* before *-mAlI*: *iç-me-meli*. The negative goes on the action: "must (not drink)".
3. **Add -mAlI.** The first vowel is two-way (*a*, *e*), the second matches it (*ı* after *a*, *i* after *e*): *-malı*, *-meli*. No buffer is needed before it, since it begins with a consonant, and no stem changes: *gitmeli*, *etmeli*, *demeli*, *okumalı*.
4. **Add the z-set ending**, with *y* before a vowel: *-yIm*, *-sIn*, nothing, *-yIz*, *-sInIz*, *-lAr*: *yatmalıyım*, *içmemelisin*.
5. **Stress** falls on the last syllable of *-mAlI*, and the 1st and 2nd person endings leave it there: *gelmelí*, *gelmelíyim*. With *-mA*, before the negative: *gélmemeli* (goksel2005 §4.3.2).

A worked chain: "You shouldn't drink coffee in the evening." Stem *iç-*. Negative: *iç-me-*. Necessity: *-meli*: *içmemeli*. "You", familiar: *-sin*: *içmemelisin*. *Akşam kahve içmemelisin.*

**Questions**: *mI* follows *-mAlI* and takes the person ending: *Gitmeli miyim?* "Should I go?", *Ne yapmalıyız?* "What should we do?" uses no *mI*, since *ne* is a question word.

## Where it applies

- **Your own sense of duty or resolution**, 1st person: *Gitmeliyim*, *Erken yatmalıyım*, *Komşumuza yardım etmeliyiz.*
- **Advice and instructions** to others, 2nd person: *Dinlenmelisin*, *Bu ilacı almalısınız.* With *bence* or *herhalde* it is softer.
- **What someone else ought to do**, 3rd person: *Ali bunu bilmeli* "Ali ought to know this." Göksel and Kerslake note that the 3rd singular with no subject can also be impersonal, "one ought to" (goksel2005 §21.4.2.2): *Bence bunu bilmeli* "I think one ought to know this". With a subject: *Bunu herkes bilmeli* "everyone ought to know this".
- **Prohibitions and warnings**: *-mA-mAlI*: *Sigara içmemelisin*, *Kimseye söylememelisin* "you mustn't tell anyone".
- **Should have**: *-mAlIydI*, for an obligation not met: *Ali'ye söylemeliydin*, *Daha erken gelmeliydik* "we should have come earlier".
- **In a story**, *-mAlIydI* can also mean "had to, now must", with the outcome still open (goksel2005 §21.4.2.2).
- **Formal rules**: *-mAlIdIr* in the 3rd person: *Herkes kurallara dikkat etmelidir.*
- **Questions asking for advice**: *Gitmeli miyim?*, *Ne yapmalıyım?*, *Kime sormalıyız?* "Whom should we ask?"
- **Hearsay about an obligation**: *-mAlIymIş*: *Gelmeliymiş* "he has to come, apparently" (Wiktionary's tables).
- **"Must be"** as a deduction, with *olmak*: *Evde olmalı* "he must be at home" (tur-04-16).
- **Needing a thing**: *lazım* or *gerek* with the dative of the person: *Bana bir sözlük lazım*, *Size ne lazım?* "What do you need?"

## Watch out

**Do not leave out the y.** *\*Gelmelim*, *\*gelmeliiz*: *-mAlI* ends in a vowel, so the vowel-initial endings take *y*: *gelmeliyim*, *gelmeliyiz*.

**Do not harmonise the I four ways.** *\*Okumalu*, *\*görmelü*: the second vowel follows the first vowel of the suffix, not the stem: *okumalı*, *görmeli*.

**Do not form the past with -DI.** *\*Gelmelidim* drops the *y* of the copula; *\*geldimeli* reverses the slots. "Should have come" is *gelmeliydim*.

**Do not use -mAlIydI for "had to (and did)".** *Gelmeliydim* says you did not come. "I had to come" in the sense "I was obliged to and so I did" is built with *gerek-* or *zorunda kal-* and a verbal noun, as in *gelmem gerekti* (goksel2005 §21.4.2.2; tur-06-02).

**Do not use -mAlI for every "have to".** "I have to work on Saturdays (it's in my contract)" is an outside necessity. *Cumartesi çalışmalıyım* presents it as your own feeling of duty. The *lazım* or *gerek* construction fits better: *Cumartesi çalışmam lazım* (preview of tur-06-02).

**Do not put -mAlI on a noun.** *\*Evdemeli*, *\*doktormalı*: *-mAlI* goes on verb stems only. "He must be at home" uses *olmak*: *Evde olmalı* (tur-04-16).

**Do not say "I need" with a person ending on lazım.** *\*Ben sözlük lazımım* copies English "I need". The needer is dative and *lazım* is the predicate: *Bana sözlük lazım.*

**Do not negate with değil.** *\*Gitmeli değilim* for "I mustn't go": put *-mA* in slot 3: *Gitmemeliyim.*

**Do not put mI after the person ending.** *\*Gitmeliyim mi?*: *Gitmeli miyim?*

## Connections

English "must" and "have to" are the closest match to the split between *-mAlI* and *lazım*, as Göksel and Kerslake point out. Japanese 〜なければならない and Korean -아야 하다 / -어야 하다 are neutral between the two, and Spanish *debo* and *tengo que* split roughly as English does: *debo ir* is close to *gitmeliyim*, *tengo que ir* to *gitmem lazım*.

## Exercises

1. Build "We must wait" and "We mustn't wait".
2. Build "You (polite) should rest", step by step.
3. Take apart *okumamalısınız* and translate it.
4. Ask "What should I do?" and "Should we go?"
5. Say "You should have phoned me" (familiar).
6. A learner writes *\*Ben yeni bir telefon lazımım* and *\*Gelmelim*. Correct both.
7. Translate *Bence daha az şeker yemelisin* (*daha az* "less").
8. Your boss has told you to come in on Sunday. Which is the better choice, *Pazar günü çalışmalıyım* or the preview *Pazar günü çalışmam lazım*? Why?

## Worked answers

1. *Beklemeliyiz.* *Beklememeliyiz.* The negative *-mA* comes before *-mAlI*; "we" is *-yiz* with the buffer *y*.
2. Stem *dinlen-*. Necessity: *-meli*, front vowels: *dinlenmeli*. Polite "you": *-siniz*: *dinlenmelisiniz*.
3. *Oku-ma-malı-sınız*: read, negative, necessitative, 2nd plural: "You mustn't read (it); you shouldn't read."
4. *Ne yapmalıyım?* (a question word, so no *mI*). *Gitmeli miyiz?* (*mI* takes the person ending).
5. *Bana telefon etmeliydin.* *Telefon etmek* takes the dative; *-mAlI* + past copula *-ydi* + k-set *-n*: an obligation not met.
6. *Bana yeni bir telefon lazım*: the needer is dative and *lazım* takes no person ending. *Gelmeliyim*: *y* before the vowel-initial ending.
7. "I think you should eat less sugar." *Bence* "in my opinion"; *ye-meli-sin* "you should eat".
8. *Çalışmam lazım* fits better: the obligation comes from outside, from the boss. *Çalışmalıyım* would present it as your own sense that you ought to (goksel2005 §21.4.2.2).
