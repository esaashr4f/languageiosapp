## The idea

*Türkçe konuşabilirim.* "I can speak Turkish."
*Yüzemem.* "I can't swim."

"Can" and "cannot" are not separate verbs in Turkish. They are suffixes in slot 4, between the negative and the tense (tur-04-01):

- ***-(y)Abil*** "can, be able to; may": *konuş-abil-ir* "can speak".
- ***-(y)AmA*** "cannot": *yüz-eme-m* "I can't swim".

Göksel and Kerslake call them the markers of possibility and impossibility (goksel2005 §21.4.2.1). Neither can stand on its own: a tense must follow in slot 5. *Gelebil-* is a new stem, "be able to come", which then takes *-ir*, *-di*, *-ecek*, *-iyor* like any stem: *gelebilir*, *gelebildi*, *gelebilecek*, *gelebiliyor*.

**Why "know".** *-(y)Abil* contains the verb *bil-* "know". Göksel and Kerslake list it among a small set of suffixes made from verb roots (goksel2005 §8.2.3.2). The sources used here do not trace the history, but the link is easy to remember: Spanish *sé nadar* and French *je sais nager* "I can swim" also use "know" for a skill. Because *bil* is a root, its vowel never harmonises: *-abil*, *-ebil*, never \**-abıl*. Only the linking *A* harmonises.

**Why a different "cannot".** "Cannot" is not *-(y)Abil* plus the negative. It is a separate possibility suffix *-(y)A* followed by the negative *-mA*: *gel-e-me-* "not be able to come". Göksel and Kerslake note that this *-(y)A* appears only in negative forms, and is the only tense-aspect-mood suffix that comes before *-mA* (goksel2005 §8.2.3.1). This guide writes *-(y)AmA* as one piece, IMPOT "impotential", since the two parts never separate.

**What the tense adds.** Göksel and Kerslake make a point that English hides (goksel2005 §21.4.2.1). With the aorist, *-(y)Abilir* only says the possibility exists; whether it happens is left open: *Ali yüzebilir* "Ali can swim". With *-DI*, *-mIş* or *-(I)yor*, the possibility is taken as realised: *Biletleri bulabildik* "we managed to find the tickets" (and found them); *Uyuyamadım* "I couldn't sleep" (and didn't).

**The many meanings of -(y)Abilir.** In the aorist, *-(y)Abil* covers ability ("can"), permission ("may, can"), polite requests ("could you"), and speculation ("may, might"): *Ali geç kalabilir* "Ali may be late". Göksel and Kerslake show one sentence with all three readings: objective, permissive, speculative (goksel2005 §21.4.2.1). Context decides.

## How it is built

**"Can"**, in four steps.

1. **Stem**: *yüz-* "swim", *oku-* "read".
2. **Add -(y)Abil**: *y* after a vowel; two-way harmony on the first vowel; *bil* unchanged: *yüz-ebil*, *oku-yabil*. Stem changes before the vowel: *git-* → *gid-ebil*, *et-* → *ed-ebil*; *de-*, *ye-* → *diyebil*, *yiyebil*.
3. **Add a tense.** The new stem ends in *bil*: the aorist is always *-ir* (*bil-* is one of the thirteen): *yüzebilir*. Or *-di*, *-ecek*, *-iyor*, *-meli*: *yüzebildi*, *yüzebilecek*, *yüzebiliyor*.
4. **Person and stress**: *yüzebilirim*. Stress as for the tense that follows: *yüzebilírim* (goksel2005 §8.2.3.2).

**"Cannot"**, in four steps.

1. **Stem**: *yüz-*.
2. **Add -(y)AmA**: *y* after a vowel, two-way harmony: *yüz-eme*, *taşı-yama*, *gid-eme*.
3. **Add a tense**, as after the negative *-mA*:
   - before *-(I)yor* the vowel rises: *yüzemiyor*, *taşıyamıyor*;
   - *-DI*, *-(y)AcAK*: *yüzemedi*, *yüzemeyecek*;
   - the aorist is *-z*, absent in the 1st singular and plural: *yüzemem*, *yüzemezsin*, *yüzemez*, *yüzemeyiz*, *yüzemezsiniz*, *yüzemezler*.
4. **Stress** falls on the *A* of *-(y)AmA*, the syllable before the negative: *yüzémem*, *anlayámıyorum* (goksel2005 §§4.3.2.1, 8.2.3.3).

This guide glosses the aorist after *-(y)AmA* as AOR when the *z* is there (*yüz-eme-z*, `swim-IMPOT-AOR`), and folds it into IMPOT.AOR in the 1st persons, where it is absent (*yüz-eme-m*, `swim-IMPOT.AOR-1SG`), as with *-mAz* in tur-04-06.

**"May not"** is the negative of the action plus *-(y)Abil*: *gel-me-yebil-ir* "he may not come" (goksel2005 §21.4.2.1). "May not be able to": *gel-eme-yebil-ir-im* "I may not be able to come".

A worked chain: "Could you take a photo of us?" A polite request: *-(y)Abil* + aorist question. Stem *çek-*: *çek-ebil-ir*. *mI* takes the person: *çekebilir misiniz*. The object, "our photo": *fotoğraf-ımız-ı*. *Fotoğrafımızı çekebilir misiniz?*

## Where it applies

- **Ability and skills**: *Türkçe konuşabilirim*, *Ayşe piyano çalabilir*, *Yüzemem.* For knowing a language *bilmek* also works: *Japonca biliyorum.*
- **Physical possibility**: *Bu kutuyu taşıyamıyorum*, *Kapıyı açamıyorum* "I can't open the door."
- **Managing or failing on one occasion**, with *-DI*, *-mIş*: *Bulabildik*, *Uyuyamadım*, *Gelememiş* "he couldn't come, apparently".
- **Ability in the future**: *Gelemeyeceğim*, *Yakında Türkçe okuyabileceksin* "soon you'll be able to read Turkish".
- **Permission**: granting, *Arabamı kullanabilirsin* "you can drive my car"; asking, *Pencereyi açabilir miyim?*, *Bir soru sorabilir miyim?* "May I ask a question?"
- **Prohibition**: *Burada park edemezsiniz*, *Burada fotoğraf çekemezsiniz.*
- **Polite requests**: *Yardım edebilir misiniz?*, more tentative than *yardım eder misiniz?* (goksel2005 §21.4.4.5).
- **Speculation**: *Ali geç kalabilir*, *Kar yağabilir* "it may snow"; negative speculation with the negative before *-(y)Abil*: *Gelmeyebilir* "he may not come".
- **Could have**: *-(y)Abilirdi* and *-(y)AmAzdI*: *Bana söyleyebilirdin*, *Ben bunu yapamazdım* "I couldn't have done this."
- **General truths about what is possible**: *Kediler yüzebilir* "cats can swim"; *Bebekler yürüyemez* "babies can't walk."
- **"Can't be"** about a state, with *olmak*: *Ali evde olamaz* (tur-04-16).

## Watch out

**Do not harmonise bil.** *\*Konuşabılır*, *\*okuyabılır*, *\*kalabılır*: the root *bil* is always *bil*: *konuşabilir*, *okuyabilir*, *kalabilir*. (Wiktionary's potential table for *kalmak* has this error.)

**Do not make "cannot" from -(y)Abil plus the negative.** *\*Yüzebilmem* for "I can't swim" puts *-mA* after *-(y)Abil*. Turkish forms "cannot" with *-(y)AmA*, before the tense: *yüzemem*. (A word spelt *yüzebilmem* does exist, but it is the verbal noun "my being able to swim", tur-06-02.)

**Do not use a separate verb for "can".** Spanish *puedo* and French *je peux* tempt learners to look for a verb. There is none: *\*Yapmak bilirim* means nothing. Use the suffix: *yapabilirim.*

**Do not forget the tense after -(y)Abil.** *\*Gelebilim*, *\*gelebil*: slot 5 must be filled: *gelebilirim*, *gelebildim*.

**Do not keep the z in the 1st person.** *\*Gelemezim*, *\*gelemeziz*: as with *-mAz*, the 1st persons drop it: *gelemem*, *gelemeyiz*.

**Do not drop the y after a vowel.** *\*Okuabilir*, *\*taşıamıyorum*: *okuyabilir*, *taşıyamıyorum*.

**Do not put "may not" in the wrong place.** *Gelemeyebilir* is "he may not be able to come"; *gelmeyebilir* is "he may not come". The first negates the ability, the second the action.

**Do not use -(y)Abil with -DI for general ability.** *\*Çocukken yüzebildim* says you managed to swim on one occasion. "As a child I could swim" is the habit in the past: *Çocukken yüzebiliyordum* or *yüzebilirdim* (the *-ken* clause is taught in part 6).

**Do not use -(y)AmAz for speculation.** *\*Ali gelemez* does not mean "Ali probably won't come"; it says he is unable to. For speculation, use *gelmeyebilir* "he may not come", or *herhalde gelmez* (goksel2005 §21.4.2.1).

**Do not stress -bil when -(y)AmA is meant.** *Anlayámıyorum* has stress on the *ya*; the negative *-mA* inside *-(y)AmA* pulls it there.

## Connections

Spanish *saber* and French *savoir* "know (how)" also mean "can" for skills, *sé nadar*, *je sais nager*, a parallel to the *bil-* "know" inside *-(y)Abil*. Japanese and Korean, like Turkish, put "can" inside the verb: 泳げる (oyogeru) "can swim", 수영할 수 있다; Japanese even has a potential suffix, as Turkish does. Japanese 〜てもいいですか "may I?" and Korean -아도 돼요? correspond to *açabilir miyim?*.

## Exercises

1. Build "We can wait" and "We can't wait".
2. Build "Can you (polite) read Arabic?" step by step.
3. Take apart *yapamayacaksınız* and translate it.
4. Ask a stranger "Could you help me?", and a friend "Can I use your phone?"
5. What is the difference between *Ayşe gelmeyebilir* and *Ayşe gelemeyebilir*?
6. A learner writes *\*Ben yüzebilmem* and *\*Okuyabılırım*. Correct both.
7. Translate *Dün akşam sana telefon edemedim, çünkü telefonum yoktu.* (*çünkü* "because")
8. Say "You can't smoke here" as a notice, and "He couldn't find his key, apparently."

## Worked answers

1. *Bekleyebiliriz*: *y* after the vowel stem, *-ebil*, aorist *-ir*, "we" *-iz*. *Bekleyemeyiz*: *-(y)AmA*, aorist with no *z* in the 1st plural.
2. Stem *oku-*. Ability: *-(y)Abil*, with *y* after the vowel: *okuyabil*. Aorist after *bil*: *-ir*: *okuyabilir*. Question: *mI* with the polite ending: *okuyabilir misiniz*. *Arapça okuyabilir misiniz?*
3. *Yap-ama-yacak-sınız*: do, cannot, future (with *y* after the vowel of *-ama*), 2nd plural: "you won't be able to do (it)".
4. *Bana yardım edebilir misiniz?* *Telefonunu kullanabilir miyim?* Both are aorist questions with *-(y)Abil*: a request and a request for permission (goksel2005 §21.4.2.1).
5. *Gelmeyebilir*: the action is negated: "she may not come" (perhaps she won't choose to). *Gelemeyebilir*: the ability is negated: "she may not be able to come".
6. *Ben yüzemem*: "cannot" is *-(y)AmA*, not *-(y)Abil* + *-mA*. *Okuyabilirim*: *bil* does not harmonise.
7. "Yesterday evening I couldn't phone you, because I didn't have my phone." *Ed-eme-di-m*: realised inability with *-DI*; *telefon-um yok-tu*: a past "have" sentence (tur-04-08).
8. *Burada sigara içemezsiniz* (or formally, *içilmez*, a passive of part 7). *Anahtarını bulamamış*: *-(y)AmA* + *-mIş* for hearsay.
