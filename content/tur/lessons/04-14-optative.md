## The idea

*Gidelim!* "Let's go!"
*Bakayım.* "Let me have a look."

The commands of tur-04-02 tell "you" and "him" what to do. For "me" and "us" Turkish uses another form, the **optative**: a mood that expresses a wish or proposal, "let me", "let's". Its suffix is ***-(y)A***, in slot 5, followed by its own person endings (goksel2005 §§8.2.3.3, 8.4):

- ***-(y)AyIm*** "let me": *gel-eyim*, *bak-ayım*;
- ***-(y)AlIm*** "let's": *gel-elim*, *bak-alım*.

Göksel and Kerslake describe *-(y)AyIm* as an action the speaker proposes to perform, often accompanying the action itself, and *-(y)AlIm* as an action proposed for the speaker and others together (goksel2005 §21.4.4.3). As questions, both become consultative: *Gideyim mi?* "Shall I go?", *Ne yapalım?* "What shall we do?"

**The other persons.** Wiktionary's tables give a full optative: *geleyim*, *gelesin*, *gele*, *gelelim*, *gelesiniz*, *geleler*. Göksel and Kerslake state that the optative occurs mostly in the 1st person; the 2nd person forms appear mainly in clauses of purpose, "so that you ...", which come in part 6; for the 3rd person, ordinary speech uses the command *-sIn* instead (goksel2005 §§8.2.3.3, 8.4). Wiktionary calls the whole set "optative"; WALS (feature 73A) does not count Turkish as having an inflectional optative. The label is disputed; the forms are not. This lesson teaches the two you will use every day.

**One piece or two?** *-(y)AyIm* is the optative *-(y)A* plus the 1st-person ending *-yIm*; *-(y)AlIm* is *-(y)A* plus *-lIm* (goksel2005 §8.4, group 3). Because the endings belong only to the optative, this guide glosses each fused form as one piece: `gel-eyim` `come-OPT.1SG`, `gel-elim` `come-OPT.1PL`.

**How it sounds.** The *y* tends to raise the vowel before it in speech (goksel2005 §2.6): *geleyim* is often heard close to "geliyim", *bakayım* close to "bakıyım". Write *-AyIm*.

## How it is built

To make a "let me" or "let's" form, take four steps.

1. **Find the stem**: *bak-*, *oku-*, *git-*.
2. **Negative?** "Let's not" is *-mA* first: *gitme-*, *söyleme-*.
3. **Add the ending**, two-way harmony on its first vowel, four-way on the second:
   - "let me": *-ayım* / *-eyim*; after a vowel, *-yayım* / *-yeyim*;
   - "let's": *-alım* / *-elim*; after a vowel, *-yalım* / *-yelim*.
   The buffer *y* comes after any vowel, including the negative's: *oku-yayım*, *bekle-yelim*, *gitme-yelim*. The usual stem changes apply before the vowel: *gid-elim*, *ed-eyim*, *di-yelim*, *yi-yelim* (Wiktionary's tables for *gitmek*, *etmek*, *demek*, *yemek*).
4. **Stress** falls on the last syllable: *gidelím*, *bakayím*. Göksel and Kerslake list only the 2nd-person optative endings *-sIn*, *-sInIz* as unstressable, not *-yIm* and *-lIm* (goksel2005 §4.3.2 (iv b)). With *-mA* it falls before the negative: *gítmeyelim* (goksel2005 §4.3.2.1).

**Questions.** *mI* follows the whole form, after the person ending (goksel2005 §11.1.1.5): *Bakayım mı?* "Shall I look?", *Gidelim mi?* "Shall we go?"

A worked chain: "Let's not tell Mehmet." Stem *söyle-*. Negative: *söyle-me-*. "Let's", after a vowel: *-yelim*: *söylemeyelim*. Mehmet is the person told: dative. *Mehmet'e söylemeyelim.*

## Where it applies

- **Suggestions for "us"**: *Gidelim*, *Oturalım* "let's sit down", *Akşam yemeği yiyelim* "let's have dinner".
- **"Let's not"**: *Söylemeyelim*, *Acele etmeyelim* "let's not hurry".
- **Offers and announcements by the speaker**: *Çantanı taşıyayım*, *Bakayım*, *Bir çay içeyim* "I'll have a tea", *Sana bir şey söyleyeyim* "let me tell you something".
- **Asking what the hearer wants**: *Pencereyi açayım mı?*, *Garsonu çağırayım mı?*; with question words, *Nereye gidelim?* "Where shall we go?", *Ne sipariş edelim?* "What shall we order?"
- **Asking another person's opinion of a plan**: *Taksi çağıralım mı?* "Shall we call a taxi?"
- **Set phrases**: *Bakalım* "we'll see"; *Hadi bakalım* "come on, then"; *Diyelim ki* "let's say that" (with the conjunction *ki*, tur-06-12).
- **Purpose clauses** with the 2nd person forms: *gelesin diye* "so that you come" (part 6).
- **Wishes and conditions** in informal speech, with the copula: *Geleydim* "if only I had come" (goksel2005 §8.2.3.3; the conditional is taught in tur-06-10).

## Watch out

**Do not use the command endings for "let's".** *\*Gidin!* is "go!" to several people, which leaves you behind. "Let's go" is *Gidelim.*

**Do not use -sIn for "let me".** *\*Gelsinim*, *\*bakayın* mix the two sets. "Let me" is *-(y)AyIm*: *geleyim*, *bakayım*.

**Do not use the 3rd-person optative for "let him".** *Gele* is in Wiktionary's table, but in ordinary speech "let him come" is the command *Gelsin* (tur-04-02).

**Do not use the future for an offer.** *Çantanı taşıyacağım* announces a plan: "I'm going to carry your bag". The offer is *Çantanı taşıyayım*. Similarly, *\*Gidecek miyiz?* is not "shall we go?", but a question about the plan: "are we going (to go)?". The proposal is *Gidelim mi?*

**Do not put mI before the person ending.** *mI* follows the whole optative form: *Gidelim mi?*, not *\*Gide mi-lim?* This is the opposite of *gidiyor muyuz* (tur-04-15).

**Do not forget the buffer after a vowel.** *\*Okuayım*, *\*bekleelim*, *\*gitmeelim*: *okuyayım*, *bekleyelim*, *gitmeyelim*.

**Do not write the spoken forms.** *Geliyim*, *bakıyım* are how you will hear them; write *geleyim*, *bakayım*.

**Do not translate English "let me" as permission.** "Let me go!" in the sense "release me!" is a request to someone else: *Bırak beni!* "Let go of me!" The optative proposes your own action; it does not ask for permission. For "may I?", use *-(y)Abilir miyim?* (tur-04-13).

**Do not use -(y)AlIm with a stem changed for -yor.** *\*Bekliyelim*, *\*anlıyalım* copy the raising of *bekliyor*. In writing the stem keeps its vowel: *bekleyelim*, *anlayalım*.

## Connections

Japanese 〜ましょう (行きましょう "let's go") and 〜ましょうか "shall we?" match *gidelim* and *gidelim mi?* closely, and Korean -아요/어요 or -자 (가자 "let's go") does the same work. Spanish uses the subjunctive, *vamos* or *vayamos*, and French the imperative, *allons-y*; Turkish, like Japanese, has a form only for the speaker's own proposals.

## Exercises

1. Build "Let's wait" and "Let's not wait".
2. Build "Shall I help you?" (polite) step by step.
3. Take apart *dinlenelim* and *çağırmayayım*.
4. Make three suggestions for the evening: go to the cinema, have dinner at a restaurant, watch a film at home.
5. A learner says *\*Gidin, geç kaldık!* to his friend, meaning "Let's go, we're late!" Correct it.
6. Translate *Menüye bakalım. Ne sipariş edelim? Ben bir bira içeyim.*
7. What is the difference between *Kapıyı açayım* and *Kapıyı açacağım*?
8. A friend asks *Yarın plaja gidelim mi?* Reply "We'll see."

## Worked answers

1. *Bekleyelim.* *Beklemeyelim.* After the vowel of *bekle-* and of *-mA*, the buffer *y*.
2. "Help" is *yardım etmek*, with the dative. Stem *et-*, which becomes *ed-* before the vowel. "Let me": *-eyim*: *edeyim*. Question: *mI* after the ending. *Size yardım edeyim mi?*
3. *Dinlen-elim*: rest, "let's": "let's rest". *Çağır-ma-yayım*: call, negative, "let me" with *y* after the vowel: "let me not call (him); I won't call".
4. *Sinemaya gidelim. Bir lokantada akşam yemeği yiyelim. Evde film izleyelim.* Each verb takes *-(y)AlIm*; *ye-* becomes *yi-*, and *izle-* takes *y*.
5. *Hadi gidelim, geç kaldık!* *Gidin* is a command to others; "let's" is *-(y)AlIm*.
6. "Let's look at the menu. What shall we order? I'll have a beer." *Bak-* takes the dative; *ed-elim* is consultative with a question word; *iç-eyim* announces the speaker's choice.
7. *Açayım* is an offer or an announcement made as you do it: "let me open the door". *Açacağım* states a plan or a decision: "I'm going to open the door" (tur-04-04).
8. *Bakalım.* "Let's see": the outcome is open.
