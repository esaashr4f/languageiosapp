## The idea

You have met the question particle *mI* in two positions on a verb:

*Geliyor musun?* "Are you coming?" — *mI* inside, before the person ending.
*Geldin mi?* "Did you come?" — *mI* outside, after the person ending.

This lesson gathers the rule for every form in part 4. It depends on one thing only: **which set of person endings the verb uses.**

- **z-set endings** (after nouns, *-(I)yor*, the aorist, *-(y)AcAK*, *-mIş*, *-mAlI*, and the copula *-(y)mIş*) **move onto mI**: *geliyor mu-sun*, *gelir mi-sin*, *gelecek mi-sin*, *gelmiş mi-sin*, *gelmeli mi-sin*; and with a noun, *hazır mı-sın* "are you ready?" (tur-02-05).
- **k-set endings** (after *-DI*, and after the copulas *-(y)DI* and *-(y)sA*) **stay on the verb**, and *mI* comes after: *geldin mi*, *geldiniz mi*.
- **Optative endings stay on the verb** too: *geleyim mi*, *gelelim mi* (tur-04-14).
- **3rd plural -lAr always stays on the verb**, whatever the set: *geliyorlar mı*, *gelirler mi*, *geldiler mi*.
- **The copular markers -(y)DI and -(y)mIş move onto mI**, with their person ending: *geliyor mu-ydu-n* "were you coming?", *hasta mı-ymış* "is he ill, (did they say)?".

Göksel and Kerslake give exactly this distribution (goksel2005 §11.1.1.5): *mI* comes before the copular markers and the z-set endings (except 3rd plural), and after the k-set endings, the optative endings and *-lAr*.

**Why the split?** Look at where each set comes from. The z-set endings are the same ones a noun takes as a predicate: *öğretmen-im*, *öğretmen mi-yim?* They are "be" endings that attach to a whole predicate, not to a verb stem, and *mI*, which questions the predicate, goes in front of them. The copular markers behave the same way, since they too can follow a noun (tur-04-08). The k-set endings, by contrast, are welded to *-DI* and *-sA*, and *mI* cannot get between them. Göksel and Kerslake treat *mI*, the copular markers and the z-set endings after them as clitics, pieces that lean on a whole phrase (goksel2005 ch. 11). The sources give the distribution, not its history; take this as a way of remembering it.

**Spelling and stress.** *mI* is always written as a separate word and harmonises with the vowel before it: *geliyor mu*, *geldin mi*, *gelmez mi*, *kalır mı*. It cannot take stress and puts it on the syllable before it (goksel2005 §19.1): *gelíyor musun*, *geldín mi*.

## How it is built

To ask a yes-no question about the verb, take three steps.

1. **Build the statement**: *Ali'yi gördünüz.* "You saw Ali."
2. **Find the last suffix before the person ending.** Is it *-DI*? Then the ending stays: *gördünüz*, and *mI* follows: *gördünüz mü*. Is it *-(I)yor*, the aorist, *-(y)AcAK*, *-mIş* or *-mAlI*? Then the ending moves: *görüyorsunuz* → *görüyor musunuz*.
3. **Harmonise mI** with the vowel just before it, and add *y* before a vowel-initial ending: *mu-yum*, *mi-yiz*.

**Compound tenses.** With a copula after the tense, *mI* goes between them, and the copula goes onto *mI*: *geliyordun* → *geliyor muydun?*; *gelirdi* → *gelir miydi?*; *gelmiştiniz* → *gelmiş miydiniz?* "had you come?" (goksel2005 §11.1.1.5).

**-DIr**, in the 3rd person, follows *mI* too: *evdedir* → *evde midir?* (tur-04-11).

**Asking about one word.** To question one element rather than the whole sentence, put *mI* after that element, which then stands before the verb; the verb keeps all its endings (tur-02-09; goksel2005 §19.1.1.1): *Ali mi geldi?* "Was it Ali who came?", *Dün mü geldin?* "Was it yesterday you came?"

**Alternatives** put *mI* after each choice, optionally linked by *yoksa* "or" (goksel2005 §19.1.2): *Geldin mi, gelmedin mi?* "Did you come or not?", *Çay mı istersin, kahve mi?* "Would you like tea or coffee?"

**Tags.** To ask for agreement, add *değil mi?* after a statement, with a comma (goksel2005 §19.1.1.2): *Yarın geliyorsun, değil mi?* "You're coming tomorrow, aren't you?"

## Where it applies

- **-(I)yor**: *Geliyor musun?*, *Geliyor muyuz?*, *Geliyorlar mı?*
- **Aorist**: *Gelir misin?*, *Gelmez misiniz?*, *Gelirler mi?*; the 1st person negative regains its *z*: *gelmez miyim?* (tur-04-06).
- **Future**: *Gelecek misin?*, *Gelecekler mi?*
- **-mIş**: *Gelmiş mi?*, *Duymuş musun?* (tur-04-07).
- **-mAlI**: *Gitmeli miyim?* (tur-04-12).
- **Ability**: the tense after *-(y)Abil* decides: *gelebilir misin?* (aorist), *gelebildin mi?* (*-DI*) (tur-04-13).
- **-DI**: *Geldin mi?*, *Gelmediniz mi?*, *Geldiler mi?*
- **Optative**: *Geleyim mi?*, *Gelelim mi?*
- **Command -sIn**: *Gelsin mi?* "Should he come?" (tur-04-02).
- **Compound tenses and copulas**: *Geliyor muydun?*, *Gelir miydi?*, *Gelmiş miydi?*, *Gelecek miydi?*; *Hasta mıydın?*, *Evde miymiş?*
- **-DIr**: *Evde midir?*
- **Focus on one element**: *Ali mi geldi?*, *Dün mü geldin?*, *Simit mi aldın?*
- **Alternatives**: *Geldin mi, gelmedin mi?*, *Çay mı, kahve mi?*
- **Tags**: *..., değil mi?*; *öyle mi?* "is that so? really?" in reply to news (goksel2005 §19.1.1.2).
- **Never with a question word**: *Ne zaman geldin?*, *Nereye gidiyorsun?* take no *mI* (tur-03-12).

## Watch out

**Do not move a k-set ending onto mI.** *\*Geldi misin?*, *\*gördü müsünüz?* copy *geliyor musun*. After *-DI* the ending stays: *Geldin mi?*, *Gördünüz mü?*

**Do not leave a z-set ending on the verb.** *\*Geliyorsun mu?*, *\*gelirsiniz mi?*, *\*geleceksin mi?* copy *geldin mi*. The ending moves: *Geliyor musun?*, *Gelir misiniz?*, *Gelecek misin?*

**Do not move -lAr onto mI.** *\*Geliyor mular?*, *\*gelecek miler?*: "they" stays on the verb: *Geliyorlar mı?*, *Gelecekler mi?*

**Do not put mI after the copula.** *\*Geliyordun mu?*, *\*hastaydın mı?*: the copula goes onto *mI*: *Geliyor muydun?*, *Hasta mıydın?*

**Do not join mI to the word.** *\*Geldinmi*, *\*geliyormusun* are common spelling mistakes. Write *mI* apart, and its endings joined to it: *Geldin mi?*, *Geliyor musun?*

**Do not harmonise mI with the stem.** *\*Geliyor misin*: *mI* follows the vowel just before it, the *o* of *-yor*: *musun*.

**Do not add mI to a question word.** *\*Ne zaman geldin mi?* has two question markers. Use one: *Ne zaman geldin?*

**Do not invert the word order.** English and French questions move the verb ("Are you...?", "Viens-tu?"). Turkish keeps the statement order and adds *mI*: *Ali geliyor mu?*, not *\*Geliyor mu Ali?* in neutral speech.

**Do not use değil mi to question a verb directly.** *\*Geliyorsun değil mi* without a pause is a tag, not a neutral question. For a plain question, use *Geliyor musun?*

## Connections

Japanese か and Korean -까 also come at the end of the predicate, and Japanese has a similar contrast between particles after a whole predicate and endings inside it: 行きますか puts か after the polite ending, much as *geldin mi* puts *mi* after the person. Unlike か, *mI* can also sit inside a sentence, right after the word in question, which Japanese does with のは…ですか constructions rather than with the particle itself.

## Exercises

1. Turn into questions: *Ali Türkçe biliyor*, *Ali Türkçe öğrendi*, *Ali Türkçe öğrenecek*.
2. Turn into questions: *Kebap yediniz*, *Kebap yersiniz*, *Kebap yemelisiniz*.
3. Ask a friend "Were you sleeping?" and "Had you eaten?"
4. Ask "Is it soup you want, or salad?" (polite).
5. Ask "They've come, haven't they?" with a tag.
6. A learner writes *\*Dondurma yedi misin?*, *\*Gelecek miler?* and *\*Nereye gittin mi?* Correct them.
7. Take apart *gelir miydiniz* and translate it.
8. Ask whether it was Ayşe who made the börek.

## Worked answers

1. *Ali Türkçe biliyor mu?* *Ali Türkçe öğrendi mi?* *Ali Türkçe öğrenecek mi?* In the 3rd singular there is no ending to move; *mI* simply follows the verb.
2. *Kebap yediniz mi?* (*-DI*: the ending stays). *Kebap yer misiniz?* (aorist: the ending moves). *Kebap yemeli misiniz?* (*-mAlI*: the ending moves).
3. *Uyuyor muydun?* *Yemiş miydin?* In both, *mI* goes before the past copula, which takes the k-set ending (goksel2005 §11.1.1.5).
4. *Çorba mı istersiniz, salata mı?* *mI* after each choice; the aorist ending *-siniz* stays on the verb because *mI* is not after the verb here (goksel2005 §19.1.2).
5. *Geldiler, değil mi?* The statement is complete; the tag follows after a comma.
6. *Dondurma yedin mi?*: after *-DI* the ending stays on the verb. *Gelecekler mi?*: *-lAr* never moves onto *mI*. *Nereye gittin?*: a question word takes no *mI*.
7. *Gel-ir mi-ydi-niz*: come, aorist, question, past copula, 2nd plural: "Did you use to come?" or "Would you have come?" (tur-04-10).
8. *Böreği Ayşe mi yaptı?* *mI* after *Ayşe*, which stands just before the verb; the verb keeps its form (tur-02-09).
