## The idea

A noun or adjective can be a predicate in the present (*Evdeyim* "I'm at home"), the past (*Evdeydim*), the evidential (*Evdeymiş*) and the conditional (*Evdeysem*). That is all the copula slot offers (tur-04-08). How do you say "I will be at home"? There is no \**evdeyeceğim*: the future suffix needs a verb stem.

Turkish supplies one: ***olmak*** "be, become". *Evde olacağım* "I'll be at home."

Göksel and Kerslake describe *ol-* as another form of the copula, used in all the cases the copular markers cannot cover: the future, habitual aspect, necessity, possibility and commitment (goksel2005 §12.1.1.3). In other words, *olmak* is the verb you put a noun predicate on when it needs a slot-5 suffix. Once the noun has *olmak* after it, every tense of part 4 is open to it: *olacak*, *olur*, *oluyor*, *olmalı*, *olabilir*, *olsun*, *olalım*.

The same applies to *var* and *yok*. They have *vardı*, *varmış*, *varsa*, but no future: "there will be" is *olacak* (goksel2005 §12.1.1.3). *Yarın toplantı var* "there's a meeting tomorrow" becomes, as a prediction, *Yarın toplantı olacak.* And so does "have": *Arabam var* "I have a car", *Arabam olacak* "I'll have a car", as tur-03-09 promised.

**Be or become?** *Olmak* is also a full verb meaning "become, come to exist, happen" (Wiktionary). Göksel and Kerslake point out that the past copula *-(y)DI* can only express a state, while *oldu* expresses a change: *Ali doktordu* "Ali was a doctor", but *Ali doktor oldu* "Ali became a doctor" (goksel2005 §21.3.4.1). With events, *olmak* is "happen": *Ne oldu?* "What happened?" With existence, it is "come into being": *Ablamın bir bebeği oldu* "my sister has had a baby."

*Ol-* is one of the thirteen *-Ir* stems: aorist *olur* (tur-04-05). It is regular otherwise: *oldu*, *olmuş*, *olacak*, *oluyor*, *olmalı*, *olabilir*, *ol*, *olun*, *olsun*.

## How it is built

To give a noun predicate a tense that the copula lacks, take four steps.

1. **Build the predicate without any ending**: *evde*, *doktor*, *hazır*, *zengin*.
2. **Add olmak after it** as a separate word.
3. **Put the tense, mood and person on ol-**, exactly as on any verb: *ol-acağ-ım*, *ol-ur-um*, *ol-malı-sın*, *ol-un*.
4. **Leave the noun bare.** All endings go on *olmak*: *evde olacağım*, not \**evdeyim olacak*.

A worked chain: "Be careful!" to several people. Predicate: *dikkatli* "careful". Needs a command: *olmak*. Polite plural: *ol-un*. *Dikkatli olun!*

Another: "The children will be ready at eight." Predicate *hazır*. Future: *olacak*. "They", with a subject word: *-lAr* optional. *Çocuklar saat sekizde hazır olacak(lar).* (Clock times come in tur-05-04.)

**sahip olmak.** "Own, possess" is formal *sahip olmak*, from *sahip* "owner" (tur-03-05). What is owned takes the dative: Wiktionary gives *duygularına sahip* "in control of one's emotions", literally "owner to one's feelings". In the present, *sahip* is a noun predicate: *Bu şirket üç otele sahip* "this company owns three hotels"; in other tenses *olmak* carries the tense: *sahip olacak*, *sahip oldu*.

## Where it applies

- **Future of noun predicates**: *Evde olacağım*, *Hazır olacağız*, *Yarın hava güzel olacak* "the weather will be fine tomorrow".
- **Future and other tenses of var, yok and "have"**: *Toplantı olacak*, *Arabam olacak*; habit, *Her yaz burada konser olur* "there's a concert here every summer"; past habit, *Eskiden burada bir sinema olurdu* "there used to be a cinema here".
- **Habitual states**, with *oluyor* or *olur*: *Bu saatte evde oluyorum* "I'm usually at home at this time" (goksel2005 §21.3.4.2).
- **Commands and wishes**: *Sakin ol!* "Calm down!", *Dikkatli olun!*, *Mutlu olun* "be happy".
- **Necessity, deduction and possibility**: *Burada olmalısın* "you must be here", *Evde olmalı* "he must be at home", *Olabilir* "possibly, it may be so", *Olamaz!* "impossible!"
- **Promises**: *Saat beşte orada olurum* "I'll be there at five".
- **Become**: *Doktor oldu*, *Zengin oldu* "he got rich", *Emekli oldum* "I've retired" (Wiktionary, *emekli olmak* "retire").
- **Happen, take place**: *Ne oldu?*, *Kaza oldu*, *Deprem oldu* "there was an earthquake", *Düğün ne zaman olacak?* "when is the wedding?"
- **Time passing**: *İki yıl oldu* "it's been two years" (Wiktionary: "of time: to pass").
- **Evaluations of a past event**: *Toplantı çok sıkıcı oldu* "the meeting turned out very boring" (goksel2005 §21.3.4.1).
- **Owning, formally**: *sahip olmak* with the dative: *Bu şirket üç otele sahip*, *Büyük bir eve sahip oldular* "they came to own a big house".
- **Set phrases**: *Olur* "OK, fine"; *Olmaz* "no, that won't do"; *Olsun* "never mind, it doesn't matter"; *Afiyet olsun* "enjoy your meal"; *Geçmiş olsun* "get well soon; sorry to hear that" (Wiktionary); *Kolay gelsin* (tur-04-02) follows the same pattern with *gelmek*.
- **Infinitive and clauses**: *doktor olmak* "to be a doctor", and every non-finite form in part 6.

## Watch out

**Do not put a tense suffix on a noun.** *\*Evdeyeceğim*, *\*hazırmalıyım*, *\*dikkatlin!*: slot 5 needs a verb. Use *olmak*: *evde olacağım*, *hazır olmalıyım*, *dikkatli ol!*

**Do not put endings on both words.** *\*Evdeyim olacak*, *\*evdeydim olacağım*: the noun stays bare and *olmak* takes everything: *evde olacağım.*

**Do not use oldu for "was".** *\*Ali dün evde oldu* for "Ali was at home yesterday" describes no change. For a state, *-(y)DI*: *Ali dün evdeydi.*

**Do not use -(y)DI for "became".** *\*Ali doktordu* is not "Ali became a doctor". For a change: *Ali doktor oldu.*

**Do not use olmak for the plain present.** *\*Ali doktor oluyor* is not "Ali is a doctor"; it means "Ali is becoming a doctor" or "Ali is (usually) the doctor". *\*Ben öğretmen olurum* is "I'll be a teacher, I'd make a teacher". The plain present needs no verb: *Ali doktor*, *Öğretmenim.*

**Do not keep var with olmak.** *\*Toplantı var olacak*, *\*arabam var olacak*: *olacak* replaces *var*: *Toplantı olacak*, *Arabam olacak.*

**Do not ask "What happened to you?" with a person ending.** *\*Ne oldun?* means "What did you become?" The thing that happened is the subject: *Sana ne oldu?*

**Do not give sahip the accusative.** *\*Üç oteli sahip*: *sahip* takes the dative: *üç otele sahip.*

**Do not harmonise -bil in olabilir.** Wiktionary's potential table for *olmak* prints *olabilur*. The correct form is *olabilir*, as for every verb (tur-04-13).

**Do not translate "there was an accident" with vardı.** *Kaza vardı* describes a state, as if an accident were lying there. For the event: *Kaza oldu.*

## Connections

Spanish splits "be" into *ser* and *estar*, and "become" into *hacerse*, *ponerse*, *llegar a ser*; Turkish splits it differently, into the copula for present and past states and *olmak* for everything else, including "become". Japanese なる "become" and Korean 되다 match *olmak* "become": 医者になった, 의사가 됐다, *doktor oldu*.

## Exercises

1. Make future sentences: *Ben hazırım*, *Hava soğuk*, *Bahçede çok çiçek var*.
2. Build "You (polite) must be tired" two ways: with *-DIr* (tur-04-11) and with *olmak*.
3. Translate *Komşumuz geçen yıl emekli oldu* and *Komşumuz geçen yıl emekliydi*. What is the difference?
4. Say "Don't be sad!" to a friend.
5. A learner writes *\*Yarın evdeyeceğiz* and *\*Toplantı var olacak.* Correct both.
6. Translate *Ne oldu? — Bir deprem oldu, ama kimseye bir şey olmadı.*
7. Reply to a friend who says *Çok hastayım* "I'm very ill".
8. Say "We'll have a big garden" and "Our neighbour owns two houses" (formal).

## Worked answers

1. *Ben hazır olacağım.* *Hava soğuk olacak.* *Bahçede çok çiçek olacak.* The noun stays bare; *olmak* carries the future; *var* is replaced by *olacak* (goksel2005 §12.1.1.3).
2. *Yorgunsunuzdur*: an assumption with *-DIr* after the person ending (tur-04-11). *Yorgun olmalısınız*: a deduction with *olmalı* (goksel2005 §21.4.1.4).
3. "Our neighbour retired last year": *emekli oldu* is a change. "Our neighbour was retired last year": *emekliydi* is a state at that time (goksel2005 §21.3.4.1).
4. *Üzgün olma!* A negative command needs a verb stem: *ol-ma*.
5. *Yarın evde olacağız*: the future needs *olmak*. *Toplantı olacak*: *olacak* replaces *var*.
6. "What happened? — There was an earthquake, but nobody was hurt (literally: nothing happened to anyone)." *Kimseye* is the dative of *kimse*, with the negative *olmadı* (tur-02-09).
7. *Geçmiş olsun!* "Get well soon." Literally "let it be past": the *-sIn* command of *olmak* with the participle *geçmiş* (part 6).
8. *Büyük bir bahçemiz olacak.* *Komşumuz iki eve sahip* (or, in the past, *sahip oldu* "came to own").
