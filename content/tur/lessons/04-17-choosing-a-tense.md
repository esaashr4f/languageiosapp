## The idea

You now have every finite form of the verb. This lesson teaches no new suffix. It shows how the forms fit together as one system, so that you can choose the right one by asking what you mean, not by translating an English tense.

Göksel and Kerslake describe the Turkish system with three separate ideas (goksel2005 §§21.1–21.4):

- **Tense**: where the situation is in time. The basic split is **past and non-past** (goksel2005 §21.2). There is no present-tense suffix: *-(I)yor* with no past copula is, in effect, the present (tur-02-08).
- **Aspect**: how the situation is viewed. **Perfective** sees it from outside, as a finished whole: *-DI*, *-mIş*. **Imperfective** sees it from inside, as going on or as a habit: *-(I)yor*, the aorist, the past copula *-(y)DI* (goksel2005 §21.3.1).
- **Modality**: how the speaker presents what he says. A sentence with *-DI*, *-(I)yor*, or a bare noun predicate is **modally neutral**: a fact the speaker knows directly. The aorist and *-DIr* make generalisations and assumptions; *-mIş* and *-(y)mIş* mark indirect knowledge; *-mAlI* and *-(y)Abil* necessity and possibility; the imperative, optative and aorist questions express will (goksel2005 §21.4).

Every form you know sits somewhere on these three lines. *Geldi* is past, perfective, neutral. *Gelmiş* is past, perfective, evidential. *Gelir* is non-past, imperfective, generalising. *Geliyor* is non-past, imperfective, neutral. *Gelecek* is future, and neutral or assumed depending on context (goksel2005 §21.4).

**Why English misleads.** English chooses mostly by time: "comes", "came", "will come". Turkish chooses as much by viewpoint and source. "He smokes" can be *içiyor* (what I see him doing these days) or *içer* (what kind of person he is). "He came" can be *geldi* (I know) or *gelmiş* (so I'm told). "I'll come" can be *gelirim* (a promise), *geleceğim* (a plan) or *geliyorum* (it's arranged). In each pair, both Turkish forms are correct; they say different things.

## How it is built

To choose a verb form, ask four questions in order.

1. **Is it past, present or future?**
2. **If past**:
   - Did I witness it, or do I know it as plain fact? *-DI*. Do I know it only from others, from its result, or have I just found out? *-mIş* (tur-04-07).
   - Was it in progress, or a background state, at that time? *-(I)yordu*. A characteristic habit? *-(A/I)rdI*, or *-(I)yordu* (tur-04-10).
   - Was it finished before another past event? *-mIştI*. Was it planned and then not done? *-(y)AcAktI*.
3. **If present**:
   - Going on now, or around now; an observed habit; a lasting state such as knowing or liking? *-(I)yor*.
   - A general truth, a rule, someone's character, a proverb? The aorist (tur-04-06).
   - A state reached by a change (hungry, tired, angry)? *-DI*: *acıktım* (tur-04-03).
   - A noun predicate? The bare noun with its person ending; *-DIr* in formal style (tur-04-11).
4. **If future**:
   - A prediction, a decision, a plan stated as fact? *-(y)AcAK* (tur-04-04).
   - A fixed arrangement or timetable? *-(I)yor*.
   - A promise, an offer, a guess about what will probably happen? The aorist.

Then add what the situation needs from the other slots: *-mA* for "not"; *-(y)Abil*, *-(y)AmA* for "can", "cannot"; *-mAlI* for "must"; *-(y)mIş* for hearsay about a present or future situation; *-DIr* for an assumption; *olmak* when a noun predicate needs any of these.

A worked chain. You want to tell a friend that your brother, whom you have not seen for a year, is working in Germany now; your mother told you. Time: present. Viewpoint: ongoing, so *-(I)yor*: *çalışıyor*. Source: your mother, so add the evidential copula: *çalışıyormuş*. *Ağabeyim Almanya'da çalışıyormuş.*

## Where it applies

The map of the whole part, by meaning:

- **Now, in progress**: *-(I)yor*: *Ne yapıyorsun?*
- **These days, as observed**: *-(I)yor*: *Bu günlerde çok çalışıyor.*
- **Lasting mental states**: *-(I)yor*: *biliyorum*, *seviyorum*, *anlıyorum*.
- **Present states reached by change**: *-DI*: *acıktım*, *yoruldum*, *anladım*, *geç kaldım*.
- **Character, rule, general truth, proverb**: aorist: *Ayşe çok kitap okur*, *Güneş doğudan doğar.*
- **Formal "is", definitions, notices**: *-DIr*: *Müze pazartesi kapalıdır.*
- **Past events, known**: *-DI*: *Takım kazandı.*
- **Past events, reported or inferred**: *-mIş*: *Takım kazanmış.*
- **Past in progress, past background**: *-(I)yordu*.
- **Past habits**: *-(A/I)rdI*, *-(I)yordu*.
- **Earlier than another past event**: *-mIştI*.
- **Was going to**: *-(y)AcAktI*.
- **Future, predicted or decided**: *-(y)AcAK*: *Yağmur yağacak*, *Seneye evleneceğiz.*
- **Future, scheduled**: *-(I)yor*: *Tren yarın kalkıyor.*
- **Future, promised or offered**: aorist: *Zamanında gelirim*, *Çay içer misiniz?*
- **Future, guessed**: aorist with *belki*, *herhalde*, *sanırım*: *Belki gelir.*
- **Hearsay about the present or future**: tense + *-(y)mIş*: *çalışıyormuş*, *gelecekmiş.*
- **Assumptions about present or past**: *-DIr*: *evdedir*, *yetişmişlerdir.*
- **Duration up to now**: *-DIr* on the time + *-(I)yor*: *Üç yıldır burada oturuyoruz.*
- **Must, can, let's, commands**: *-mAlI*, *-(y)Abil*, the optative, the imperative; with nouns, *olmak*.

## Watch out

**Do not choose by English tense alone.** "I have lived here for three years" is not *\*Üç yıl burada yaşadım* (which says the three years are over) but *Üç yıldır burada yaşıyorum* (tur-04-11).

**Do not use the aorist for what is happening.** *\*Şu anda yemek yeriz*: in progress needs *-(I)yor*: *Şu anda yemek yiyoruz.*

**Do not use -(I)yor for proverbs and definitions.** *\*It ürüyor, kervan yürüyor* turns a timeless saying into a report of what is happening outside. Proverbs keep the aorist.

**Do not use -DI for news you only heard.** *\*Komşumuz piyangoyu kazandı*, said of something you read, claims first-hand knowledge. Use *kazanmış* (goksel2005 §21.4.3.1).

**Do not use -mIş for what you saw.** *\*Dün seni parkta görmüşüm* means you've just realised it, or someone told you. *Dün seni parkta gördüm.*

**Do not use -(y)AcAK for a promise you make on the spot.** It is not wrong, but *Yarın arayacağım* states a plan; the spontaneous "I'll call you tomorrow" is *Yarın ararım.* The reverse is wrong: *\*Seneye evleniriz* for a wedding already fixed sounds like a guess, "we'll probably get married next year". Use *evleneceğiz.*

**Do not use -DI for "was doing".** *\*Ben geldiğimde Ali kitap okudu* means Ali read once I had arrived. For the background: *okuyordu.*

**Do not use -(y)DI for "became".** *\*Ali geçen yıl doktordu* is "Ali was a doctor last year"; "became" is *oldu* (tur-04-16).

**Do not mix viewpoints in a list of habits.** *\*Her yaz köye giderdik, denizde yüzdük* puts one habit in *-(A/I)rdI* and the next as a single event. Keep the series together: *giderdik, denizde yüzerdik.*

**Do not reach for the future for every "will".** English "will" is often a request (*Kapıyı açar mısınız?*), a promise (*ararım*) or a habit (*Babam akşamları televizyon izler* "my father will watch television in the evenings"). Only predictions and plans take *-(y)AcAK*.

## Connections

Spanish speakers already divide the past by viewpoint (*comí*, *comía*), and Turkish adds a second division by source of knowledge (*yedi*, *yemiş*). Korean has a comparable layer of source and stance in its sentence endings: plain -았어요 for what you know, -았대요 for what you were told, -네요 for a discovery, roughly *-DI*, *-mIş*, *-(y)mIş*. Japanese has no grammatical evidential of this kind; そうだ and らしい are optional additions.

## Exercises

1. Choose the form and translate: (a) your friend is reading right now; (b) your friend is the kind of person who reads a lot; (c) your friend read yesterday, and you saw her.
2. Choose and translate: you read in the newspaper that a famous writer died yesterday.
3. Say "The plane leaves at five" (timetable) and "When is the plane going to leave?" (it is late). Use *saat beşte* "at five".
4. Translate *Eskiden her yaz İzmir'e giderdik, ama bu yıl gitmeyeceğiz.*
5. Say "I've been waiting for an hour" (*bir saat*).
6. Your friend asks you to keep a secret. Promise "I won't tell anyone."
7. Say "When I phoned, Ayşe had already gone out" as two sentences.
8. Correct *\*Şimdi yağmur yağar* and *\*Dün Mehmet'i gördüm, hasta olmuş* (you saw that he was ill).
9. Identify time, viewpoint and source in *gelmezdim*, *geliyormuş* and *gelmiştir*.

## Worked answers

1. (a) *Kitap okuyor*: in progress, *-(I)yor*. (b) *Çok kitap okur*: a characteristic, the aorist. (c) *Dün kitap okudu*: a finished past event you witnessed, *-DI*.
2. *Ünlü bir yazar dün ölmüş.* You know it only from the newspaper, so *-mIş*, not *-DI* (tur-04-07).
3. *Uçak saat beşte kalkıyor.* — *Uçak ne zaman kalkacak?* A timetable takes *-(I)yor*; doubt about it takes *-(y)AcAK* (goksel2005 §21.2.3).
4. "We used to go to İzmir every summer, but this year we're not going to." A past habit in *-(A/I)rdI*, a decision about the future in *-(y)AcAK* (tur-04-10, tur-04-04).
5. *Bir saattir bekliyorum.* *-DIr* on the length of time, *-(I)yor* because the waiting goes on (tur-04-11).
6. *Merak etme, kimseye söylemem.* A promise: the negative aorist, 1st singular with no *z* (tur-04-06).
7. *Ayşe'yi aradım. Ayşe çoktan çıkmıştı.* The phone call is the past reference point, *-DI*; the going out was over before it, *-mIştI* (tur-04-10).
8. *Şimdi yağmur yağıyor*: in progress. *Dün Mehmet'i gördüm, hastaydı*: a state you witnessed is the copula *-(y)DI*; *hasta olmuş* would mean "he had fallen ill, it seems", an inference (tur-04-08, tur-04-16).
9. *Gelmezdim*: past, habitual or contrary to fact, general: "I didn't use to come; I wouldn't have come". *Geliyormuş*: non-past (or past), in progress, reported: "he's coming, I hear". *Gelmiştir*: past, completed, formal or assumed: "he has come; he must have come" (tur-04-11).
