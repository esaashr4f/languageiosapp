## The idea

An **adjective** is a word that describes a noun: *beyaz* "white", *hızlı* "fast". An **adverb** is a word that describes anything else: how, when or how much something is done (*yavaşça* "slowly"), or how strongly an adjective applies (*çok* "very").

You have used adjectives since tur-02-01. Three facts set them apart from Spanish or French ones.

**First: an adjective never changes.** Spanish says *casa blanca* but *casas blancas*: the adjective copies the noun's number and gender. Turkish has no gender, and the adjective copies nothing. *Beyaz ev* "white house", *beyaz evler* "white houses", *beyaz evlerimizde* "in our white houses": *beyaz* stays as it is. The suffixes belong to the whole phrase, and a Turkish phrase puts them once, at its end, on the noun. This is the head-final order of tur-02-07 at work: describing words first, the noun last, the suffixes after the noun.

**Second: the line between adjective and noun is loose.** Göksel and Kerslake note that most words used mainly as adjectives can also be used as nouns, taking plural, possessive and case suffixes as needed (goksel2005 §7.1.1): *küçük* "small", *küçükler* "the little ones". There is no separate word for "one" as in English "the red one". The adjective itself becomes the noun.

**Third: the line between adjective and adverb is loose too.** Many adjectives become adverbs of manner (how something is done) with no change at all, just by standing immediately before the verb (goksel2005 §16.1.2). *Güzel* is "beautiful"; *güzel söyledi* is "he sang it beautifully". *Kötü* is "bad"; *kötü yüzer* is "he swims badly". English needs *-ly*; Spanish needs *-mente*. Turkish needs only the position.

So one word can describe a noun, stand for one, or describe an action, depending on where it stands and what is added to it.

## How it is built

**To describe a noun**, put the adjective in front of it.

1. **Choose the adjective**: *yüksek* "high, tall".
2. **Add a degree word, if you want one, in front of it**: *çok yüksek* "very high", *oldukça yüksek* "quite high", *biraz yüksek* "a bit high" (goksel2005 §15.4.1.1).
3. **If the noun is indefinite, put *bir* after the adjective**: *yüksek bir dağ* "a high mountain" (tur-02-01). A modified adjective must stand before *bir*: *çok yüksek bir dağ* (goksel2005 §15.9.2).
4. **Put demonstratives and numbers first**: *bu iki yüksek dağ* "these two high mountains". Göksel and Kerslake give the order: determiner, number, adjective, noun (goksel2005 §§15.9.1–15.9.2).
5. **Put the suffixes on the noun**: *bu iki yüksek dağda* "on these two high mountains".

**To use an adjective as a noun**, treat it as a noun.

1. **For a kind of people or things in general, add -lAr**: *zengin* "rich", *zenginler* "the rich, rich people"; *gençler* "young people".
2. **For "the ... one" out of a known set, add the third-person possessive -(s)I**: *büyük* "big", *büyüğü* "the big one (of them)". The possessive points back to the set you are talking about: "its big one" (goksel2005 §18.4 (i)). The stem changes as any noun's does: *büyük* → *büyüğü* (tur-01-06).
3. **Add a case if the sentence needs it**: as an object it is specific, so it takes the accusative: *yenisini aldım* "I bought the new one" (goksel2005 §18.4).

**To describe an action**, there are three ways.

1. **Put a plain adjective right before the verb**: *yavaş konuşun* "speak slowly", *iyi yüzüyor* "she swims well".
2. **Add -CA to the adjective**: *yavaşça* "slowly, gently", *sessizce* "quietly", *kolayca* "easily" (goksel2005 §16.1.6 (i)). -CA has four forms: *c* or *ç* by the rule of tur-01-06 (*ç* after a voiceless consonant), and *e* or *a* by two-way harmony: *sessiz-ce*, *yavaş-ça*. It is the same suffix you know from *Türkçe* and *bence* (tur-04-12, tur-04-13). It takes no stress: the stress falls on the syllable before it, *yaváşça*.
3. **With a derived or borrowed adjective, add *olarak* "as"**: *resmî olarak* "officially". Göksel and Kerslake note that most derived adjectives, and adjectives of foreign origin, cannot stand bare before a verb; *-lI* and *-sIz* words are the exception (goksel2005 §16.1.2).

**Where the adverb goes.** Immediately before the verb. If the verb has a bare, non-specific object, the adverb goes before that object, because the object and verb form one unit (goksel2005 §16.1.2): *iyi Türkçe konuşuyor* "he speaks good Turkish". With a specific object, the adverb follows the object: *Türkçeyi iyi konuşuyor* "he speaks Turkish well".

## Where it applies

- **Before a noun, describing it**: *beyaz ev*, *yüksek bir dağ*, *bu iki küçük oda*.
- **After the subject, as the predicate**: *Araba hızlı*; with person endings, *Hastayım* (tur-02-02); with copular markers, *Hızlıydı* (tur-04-08).
- **After a degree word**: *çok*, *biraz*, *oldukça*, *pek* (with a negative, tur-02-09), *fazla* "too": *fazla pahalı* "too expensive" (goksel2005 §16.5). Comparison with *daha* and *en* is tur-05-02.
- **As a noun for a kind**: *zenginler*, *gençler*, *yaşlılar* "old people", *hastalar* "patients, sick people".
- **As a noun for "the ... one"**: *büyüğü*, *yenisi*, *eskisi*, *kırmızısı* "the red one". The noun is understood from the context.
- **As the name of a colour or quality**: *Beyazı severim* "I like (the colour) white", with the accusative of a specific object.
- **As an adverb of manner, bare, before the verb**: *yavaş konuşun*, *iyi yüzüyor*, *kötü yazıyor*, *hızlı koşuyor*, *güzel söyledi*. This works for simple adjectives and also for words in *-lI* and *-sIz* (goksel2005 §16.1.2): *dikkatli konuşun* "speak carefully", *sessiz oturun* "sit quietly".
- **With -CA**: *yavaşça*, *sessizce*, *kolayca*, *açıkça* "openly, clearly", *dikkatlice* "carefully", *iyice* "thoroughly". -CA on a noun gives "like a ...": *çocukça* "childishly; childish" (goksel2005 §16.1.6 (i)).
- **With *olarak***: borrowed and derived adjectives: *resmî olarak*. *Olarak* after a noun means "as, in the role of": *arkadaş olarak* "as a friend" (goksel2005 §16.1.9).
- **With a complement in a fixed case** (goksel2005 §15.5): *su dolu bir şişe* "a bottle full of water" (bare), *size uygun* "suitable for you" (dative), *bundan memnun* "pleased with this" (ablative), *bununla ilgili* "related to this" (-(y)lA).
- **Elsewhere**: *-ki* phrases (*bahçedeki ağaç*, tur-03-14), doubled forms (tur-05-11), *-lI* and *-sIz* words (tur-05-09), relative clauses (part 6).

## Watch out

**Do not make the adjective agree.** By analogy with Spanish *casas blancas*, learners write *\*beyazlar evler*. The plural goes once, on the noun: *beyaz evler*. The same holds for case: *\*beyazda evde* is wrong; *beyaz evde*.

**Do not put the adjective after the noun to describe it.** *\*Ev beyaz* is not "the white house"; it is the sentence "The house is white" (tur-02-01). French *la maison blanche* has the order of a Turkish sentence, not of a Turkish phrase.

**Do not put bir between a degree word and its adjective.** *\*Çok bir güzel ev* and *\*bir çok güzel ev* split a unit. *Çok güzel* is one modifier, and the whole modifier comes before *bir*: *çok güzel bir ev* (goksel2005 §15.9.2). *\*Bir çok güzel ev* would also be misheard, since *birçok* (one word) means "many".

**Do not add a word for "one".** English "the red one" tempts you to write *\*kırmızı bir* or *\*kırmızı tane* for "the red one". Turkish uses the adjective with -(s)I: *kırmızısı*. For "a new one" not yet in view, *yeni bir tane* "a new piece" also exists (goksel2005 §18.4 (iii)).

**Do not leave a bare object between the adverb and the verb in the wrong order.** *\*Türkçe iyi konuşuyor* is heard as odd, because *Türkçe konuşmak* "speak Turkish" is one unit and the adverb is cutting into it. Say *iyi Türkçe konuşuyor*, or make the object specific and say *Türkçeyi iyi konuşuyor* (goksel2005 §16.1.2).

**Do not put the adverb after the verb.** Spanish *habla bien* and French *il parle bien* put the adverb last. *\*Konuşuyor iyi* is wrong in neutral speech; the verb ends the sentence (tur-02-07): *iyi konuşuyor*.

**Do not use a bare borrowed adjective as an adverb.** *\*Resmî evlendiler* fails for the reason above. Use *resmî olarak evlendiler*.

**Do not read iyice as "well".** *İyi* is "good" and, before a verb, "well": *iyi yüzüyor* "she swims well". *İyice* means "thoroughly, properly": *Ellerini iyice yıka* "wash your hands thoroughly".

**Do not stress -CA.** It takes no stress, so the stress falls before it: *yaváşça*, *sessízce*.

## Connections

Korean and Japanese adjectives come before the noun and never agree, as in Turkish, and both languages also turn adjectives into adverbs: Korean 빠르게 from 빠르다 "fast", Japanese 速く from 速い. Those use a suffix; Turkish more often uses position alone. The resemblance is typological, not evidence of a common ancestor. Spanish and French adjectives agree and usually follow the noun, which is the habit to unlearn.

## Exercises

1. Build "in these three big white houses", step by step.
2. Translate *Bu iki kazak çok pahalı. Ucuzu var mı?*
3. A learner writes *\*Ali bir çok akıllı öğrenci.* Correct it and say why.
4. Make adverbs of manner from *sessiz*, *açık* and *kolay* with -CA, and give the stress of each.
5. Say "She speaks good French" with *Fransızca* as a bare object, then "She speaks French well" with a specific object.
6. Translate *Yaşlılar parkta sessizce oturuyor.*
7. Why is *\*Resmî konuştu* wrong for "He spoke officially", and what is the right form?
8. Take apart *kırmızısını* in *Kırmızısını beğendim* and translate the sentence.
9. Correct *\*Konuşun yavaş, lütfen.*

## Worked answers

1. Noun *ev*, plural *evler*, locative *evlerde*. Before it: demonstrative *bu*, number *üç*, then the adjectives *büyük beyaz*: *bu üç büyük beyaz evde*. After a number the noun is singular (tur-02-03), so *evde*, not *evlerde*. Neither adjective changes.
2. "These two jumpers are very expensive. Is there a cheap one?" *Ucuz-u*: the adjective with -(s)I, "the cheap one" out of the jumpers. *Var mı* asks whether one exists (tur-02-06).
3. *Ali çok akıllı bir öğrenci.* *Çok akıllı* is one modifier and must stand before *bir* (goksel2005 §15.9.2). *Bir çok* would also be heard as *birçok* "many".
4. *Sessiz-ce* (after voiced *z*: *c*; after *i*: *e*), *açık-ça* (after voiceless *k*: *ç*; after *ı*: *a*), *kolay-ca*. -CA takes no stress: *sessízce*, *açíkça*, *koláyca*.
5. *Çok iyi Fransızca konuşuyor*: the bare object stays next to the verb and the adverb goes before both. *Fransızcayı iyi konuşuyor*: with the accusative the object is specific, and the adverb comes after it, right before the verb (goksel2005 §16.1.2).
6. "The old people are sitting quietly in the park." *Yaşlı-lar*: adjective as a noun for a kind of people. *Sessiz-ce*: adverb of manner. *Park-ta*: *t* after the voiceless *k* (tur-02-06).
7. *Resmî* is borrowed from Arabic; such adjectives do not stand bare before a verb (goksel2005 §16.1.2). Use *olarak*: *Resmî olarak konuştu.*
8. *Kırmızı-sı-nı*: adjective, third-person possessive "the red one", pronominal *n* and the accusative (tur-03-06). "I liked the red one." *Beğendim* takes an accusative object (tur-02-09, tur-03-01).
9. *Lütfen yavaş konuşun.* The adverb stands right before the verb, and the verb comes last (tur-02-07). *Lütfen* usually opens the request.
