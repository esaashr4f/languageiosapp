## The idea

English changes the adjective to compare: *tall*, *taller*, *tallest*, and sometimes replaces it (*good*, *better*, *best*). Spanish and French put a word in front: *más alto*, *plus grand*, and *el más alto*, *le plus grand*. Turkish does what Spanish does, with two small words, and it never changes the adjective.

- ***daha*** "more" before an adjective makes the **comparative**, the form that says one thing has more of a quality than another: *daha uzun* "taller".
- ***en*** "most" before an adjective makes the **superlative**, the form that picks out the one with the most: *en uzun* "the tallest" (goksel2005 §§15.4.2, 15.4.3).

There are no irregular forms. *İyi* "good" gives *daha iyi* "better" and *en iyi* "best"; *kötü* gives *daha kötü*, *en kötü*.

**"Than" is the ablative.** Turkish has no word for "than". The thing you compare against takes the ablative *-DAn*, the case of the starting point (tur-03-03): *Ali Mehmet'ten daha uzun* "Ali is taller than Mehmet", literally "from Mehmet, Ali is more tall". You measure Ali starting from Mehmet. Since the ablative already says "compared with", *daha* can be left out when the ablative is there: *Ali Mehmet'ten uzun* means the same (goksel2005 §15.4.2). You met this in tur-03-03 and tur-03-14.

**"Most" needs a group.** *En* picks one member of a set, so the set is often named. Turkish names it in one of two ways (goksel2005 §15.4.3):

- as a possessor in the genitive, with the possessive on what is picked out: *sınıfın en iyi öğrencisi* "the class's best pupil";
- with a locative and *-ki* (tur-03-14): *sınıftaki en iyi öğrenci* "the best pupil in the class".

Both are the possession and *-ki* patterns of part 3. Nothing new is needed.

**Daha has other jobs.** Before a verb, a number or a noun, *daha* also means "still", "yet" and "more, another" (wiktionary; goksel2005 §16.4.1.1). This lesson sorts these out, because a learner who knows only "more" misreads them.

## How it is built

**A comparative sentence: "This road is wider than that one."**

1. **What is compared?** *Bu yol* "this road", the subject.
2. **Against what?** "That one": *o*, in the ablative. A demonstrative takes *n* before a case (tur-03-11): *ondan*.
3. **The quality**: *geniş* "wide". Put *daha* before it: *daha geniş*.
4. **Order**: subject, ablative, *daha*, adjective. *Bu yol ondan daha geniş.* The ablative stands before *daha*, never after the adjective.
5. **Degree**: put *çok* "much" or *biraz* "a bit" before *daha*: *çok daha geniş* "much wider", *biraz daha geniş* "a bit wider" (goksel2005 §15.4.2).

**Less.** Use *daha az* "more little": *daha az tehlikeli* "less dangerous" (goksel2005 §15.4.2).

**A superlative: "the biggest city in Turkey".**

1. **The quality with en**: *en büyük*.
2. **The noun after it**: *en büyük şehir* "the biggest city". A superlative is definite: no *bir*.
3. **The group, as a genitive**: *Türkiye'nin*. Since *Türkiye* owns the city, the city takes the possessive: *Türkiye'nin en büyük şehri* (tur-03-05). Or, as a place with *-ki*: *Türkiye'deki en büyük şehir*.
4. **Numbers come after the superlative**: *en büyük üç şehir* "the three biggest cities" (goksel2005 §15.9.2). Without *en*, the number comes first: *üç büyük şehir*.

**"The ... -est one" with no noun.** Add the possessive to the adjective, as in tur-05-01: *bu elmaların en tatlısı* "the sweetest of these apples" (goksel2005 §15.4.3, ex. 57).

**Adverbs compare in the same way.** *Daha* and *en* go before the adverb: *daha hızlı koşar* "he runs faster", *en iyi yüzer* "she swims best". For "more" and "most" with a verb, use *daha çok* or *daha fazla* "more" and *en çok* "most" (goksel2005 §16.9): *daha çok çalışıyor* "he works more".

**Pronunciation.** *Daha* is often said [daː], with the *h* silent (goksel2005 §1.1.1.1).

## Where it applies

- **Adjective against a standard**: *X Y-DAn (daha) adjective*: *Ali Mehmet'ten daha uzun*.
- **With the standard left out**: *Bu daha iyi* "This is better" (goksel2005 §15.4.2, ex. 44).
- **Before a noun**: *daha ucuz bir otel* "a cheaper hotel"; with a standard, *bundan daha ucuz bir otel* "a hotel cheaper than this one" (goksel2005 §15.4.2, ex. 40).
- **Against a pronoun**: *benden*, *senden*, *ondan*, *bizden*, *sizden*, *onlardan*; against "all": *hepsinden daha iyi* "better than all of them", *hepimizden* "than all of us".
- **Against a place or time**: *Bugün dünden daha soğuk* "Today is colder than yesterday". *Dün* takes the ablative like any noun.
- **Degree of difference**: *çok daha*, *biraz daha*.
- **Less**: *daha az*: *daha az şeker* "less sugar".
- **Superlative with a group**: genitive and possessive (*Türkiye'nin en büyük şehri*), or locative and *-ki* (*sınıftaki en çalışkan öğrenci*).
- **Superlative without a noun**: *en iyisi* "the best one", *bu elmaların en tatlısı*.
- **Superlative of an adverb**: *en hızlı koşar* "she runs fastest"; *en çok* and *en az* with verbs: *en çok kedileri severim*.
- **At least, at most**: *en az* before a number means "at least": *en az beş kişi* "at least five people" (goksel2005 §14.2, ex. 14).
- **Daha as "more" with a noun**: *daha çok para* "more money", *daha fazla zaman* "more time" (goksel2005 §16.9).
- **Daha as "another"**: after *bir* and a noun: *bir çay daha*, *bir soru daha* "one more question".
- **Daha as "still" and "yet"**: *daha uyuyor*, *Daha hazır değiliz* "We're not ready yet" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1 (v)).
- **Bir daha "again"** with a negative: *bir daha görmedim*; with a positive verb, use *tekrar* or *yine* (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1 (iv)).
- **Equality** ("as tall as") uses *kadar*: tur-05-05.

## Watch out

**Do not look for a word for "than".** Spanish *que*, French *que* and English *than* tempt you to write *\*Ali daha uzun ki Mehmet* or *\*Ali daha uzun Mehmet*. Turkish has no such word. The standard takes the ablative and comes before the adjective: *Ali Mehmet'ten daha uzun*.

**Do not put the standard after the adjective.** *\*Ali daha uzun Mehmet'ten* is English order. Modifiers come before what they modify: *Mehmet'ten daha uzun*.

**Do not change the adjective.** There is no *-er*, and no irregular *better*: *\*iyiyer* does not exist; *daha iyi*. Do not stack the two words: *\*daha en iyi*.

**Do not put bir after a superlative.** *\*En büyük bir şehir* treats a unique thing as one of many. *En büyük şehir*.

**Do not put a number before en.** *\*Üç en büyük şehir* follows English "three biggest". With *en*, the number comes after the adjective: *en büyük üç şehir* (goksel2005 §15.9.2).

**Do not forget the possessive after a genitive.** *\*Türkiye'nin en büyük şehir* has the genitive but not the possessive that answers it (tur-03-05): *Türkiye'nin en büyük şehri*.

**Do not use a bare locative before the noun.** *\*Türkiye'de en büyük şehir* does not work: a plain locative describes a noun with *-ki* (tur-03-14), or with *arasında* "among" (goksel2005 §15.4.3). Say *Türkiye'deki en büyük şehir*, or keep the locative in the sentence: *Türkiye'de en büyük şehir İstanbul'dur* "In Turkey, the biggest city is Istanbul".

**Do not use az alone for "less".** *\*Bu az pahalı* does not mean "this is less expensive". *Az* without *daha* in a comparison is a colloquial short form of *biraz* and means "a little more" (goksel2005 §15.4.2): *bundan az büyük* "a little bigger than this". "Less" is *daha az*.

**Do not read daha alone before a verb as "more".** *Ali daha çalışıyor* means "Ali is still working". For "Ali works more", say *Ali daha çok çalışıyor*. Likewise *Daha gelmedi* is "He hasn't come yet", not "He didn't come any more" (that is *artık gelmedi*, tur-02-09).

**Do not use tekrar for "not again".** With a negative verb, Göksel and Kerslake give *bir daha* as the only way to say "again" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1 (iv)): *Onu bir daha görmedim*, not *\*Onu tekrar görmedim*.

## Connections

Japanese より (yori) and Korean 보다 (boda) mark the standard of comparison after the noun, as Turkish *-DAn* does, and both can leave out the word for "more" (Japanese もっと, Korean 더) just as Turkish can leave out *daha*. Japanese より also means "from" in formal writing, the same link between "from" and "than" that the Turkish ablative shows. This is a typological parallel, not evidence of common descent. Spanish *más ... que* puts the standard after the adjective, the order to avoid.

## Exercises

1. Build "My sister is younger than me", with *kardeş* and *genç*, step by step.
2. Translate *İzmir İstanbul'dan küçük ama çok güzel.*
3. Build "the most expensive hotel in Ankara" in two ways.
4. A learner writes *\*Ayşe en akıllı bir öğrenci.* Correct it.
5. Translate *Bu üç kitabın en ilginci hangisi?* and take apart *ilginci*.
6. What does *Çocuklar daha uyumadı* mean? Why is it not "The children slept no more"?
7. Say "This coffee is much stronger than the other one", using *sert*.
8. Correct *\*Bu çanta öbüründen az ağır* meant as "This bag is less heavy than the other one", and say what the learner's sentence suggests instead.
9. Say "One more question, please" and "I won't ask again", using *sormak* in the aorist.
10. Translate *Mehmet bizden daha çok çalışıyor ama en az parayı o kazanıyor.*

## Worked answers

1. Subject: *kardeşim* "my sibling". Standard: *ben* in the ablative, *benden*. Quality: *genç*, with *daha*: *daha genç*. *Kardeşim benden daha genç.* *Daha* may be dropped: *Kardeşim benden genç.*
2. "İzmir is smaller than Istanbul, but it is very beautiful." *İstanbul'dan*: ablative as "than", with no *daha* (tur-05-02); *ama* "but" (tur-02-04).
3. Genitive: *Ankara'nın en pahalı oteli*, with the possessive on *otel*. Locative with *-ki*: *Ankara'daki en pahalı otel* (tur-03-14).
4. *Ayşe en akıllı öğrenci.* A superlative is unique, so it takes no *bir*. If you mean "one of the cleverest", that needs *biri*, not *bir*: leave it for now.
5. "Which is the most interesting of these three books?" *İlginc-i*: the adjective with the third-person possessive, "its most interesting (one)"; ç becomes c before the vowel (tur-01-06). The genitive *kitabın* names the group; after a number the noun is singular (tur-02-03).
6. "The children haven't gone to sleep yet." With a negative verb, *daha* means "yet" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1 (v)). "No more" would be *artık* (tur-02-09).
7. *Bu kahve öbüründen çok daha sert.* *Öbürü* "the other one" with the ablative: *öbür-ü-nden*, with the pronominal *n* (tur-03-06). *Çok* before *daha* for "much".
8. *Bu çanta öbüründen daha az ağır*, or more naturally *Bu çanta öbüründen daha hafif* "This bag is lighter than the other one". In a comparison, *az* alone suggests "a little more" (goksel2005 §15.4.2), the opposite of what was meant.
9. *Bir soru daha, lütfen.* *Bir daha sormam.* The negative aorist *sor-ma-m* (tur-04-06) is a firm promise; with a negative verb "again" is *bir daha*.
10. "Mehmet works more than us, but he is the one who earns the least money." *Bizden daha çok*: "more than us" with a verb. *En az parayı*: "the least money", definite, so accusative. *O* before the verb is the focus: "he is the one who" (tur-02-07).
