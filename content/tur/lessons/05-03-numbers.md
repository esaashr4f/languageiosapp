## The idea

Turkish numbers are a clean decimal system. There is a word for each unit (1 to 9), each ten (10 to 90), and each power of ten (hundred, thousand, million). Every other number is built from these by two rules (goksel2005 §15.7.1):

- **Addition: bigger first, then smaller.** *On iki* "ten two" is 12. *Yetmiş üç* is 73. *Yüz kırk beş* is 145. There are no special words like English *eleven*, *twelve*, *thirteen*, and no French *soixante-dix*.
- **Multiplication: smaller first, then bigger.** *Üç yüz* "three hundred" is 300. *Kırk iki bin* is 42,000.

So *iki bin yirmi altı* is "two thousand, twenty, six": 2026. You read a Turkish number exactly as the digits are written, left to right.

**The noun stays singular.** You learnt in tur-02-03 that a Turkish noun without *-lAr* names the kind, and that a number already gives the count: *iki kitap* "two books". This holds for every number in this lesson.

**Two suffixes make new kinds of number word.** A numeral is a word that names a number. From each numeral Turkish builds two more words:

- an **ordinal**, which says the place in a sequence ("third"), with *-(I)ncI*: *üç* → *üçüncü* "third";
- a **distributive**, which says how many each person or thing gets ("three each"), with *-(ş)Ar*: *üç* → *üçer* "three each" (goksel2005 §§15.7.2, 15.7.3).

English needs a phrase for the distributive ("each", "apiece", "in threes"). Turkish has one word. It is the clearest example so far of a meaning that Turkish puts in a suffix and English spreads over words.

## How it is built

**Cardinal numbers** (the plain counting numbers).

1. **Split the number by powers of ten**: 2,345 = 2 thousand, 3 hundred, 40, 5.
2. **Say each part, biggest first**: *iki bin*, *üç yüz*, *kırk*, *beş*: *iki bin üç yüz kırk beş*.
3. **No *bir* before *yüz* or *bin***: 100 is *yüz*, 1,000 is *bin*, 1,100 is *bin yüz*. But *milyon* and *milyar* take it: *bir milyon* (goksel2005 §15.7.1).
4. **Write the words separately**: *on iki*, *üç yüz*.
5. **The noun follows, singular**: *iki bin üç yüz kırk beş kişi*.

**Ordinals: "the third".**

1. **Take the numeral**: *üç*.
2. **Add -(I)ncI**: four-way harmony on both vowels; the first vowel drops after a vowel (wiktionary *-inci*): *üç-üncü*, *iki-nci*, *altı-ncı*, *dokuz-uncu*, *on-uncu*.
3. **Watch the stem**: *dört* ends in *t*, which becomes *d* before a vowel (tur-01-06): *dördüncü*. Other numerals keep their form.
4. **For a long number, only the last word takes the suffix**: *yirmi beşinci* "twenty-fifth".
5. **In writing, a figure with a full stop is an ordinal**: *3. sokak* = *üçüncü sokak* "Third Street" (goksel2005 §15.7.2).

*İlk* "first" is a second word for *birinci* (goksel2005 §15.7.2). *Kaçıncı* "the how-many-th" asks for an ordinal: *Kaçıncı sınıftasın?* "Which year are you in (at school)?"

**Distributives: "two each".**

1. **Take the numeral**: *iki*.
2. **Add -(ş)Ar**: two-way harmony; *ş* only after a vowel: *iki-şer*, *altı-şar*, *bir-er*, *üç-er*, *on-ar*.
3. **Watch the stem**: *dört* → *dörder* (wiktionary).
4. **Make sure several people or things share**: a distributive needs at least one plural noun phrase in the clause, the people who each give or each receive (goksel2005 §15.7.3): *Çocuklara ikişer elma verdim* "I gave the children two apples each".

**Halves and quarters.** Turkish has three words for "half" (goksel2005 §15.7.1):

- *yarım* "half", like a numeral before a noun: *yarım kilo* "half a kilo";
- *buçuk* "and a half", after a number: *iki buçuk* "two and a half";
- *yarı* "half (of)", a noun used with a possessive: *öğrencilerin yarısı* "half of the students".

*Çeyrek* is "a quarter". *Yüzde* "per cent" stands before the number: *yüzde elli* "fifty per cent".

**Part of a group.** "Two of the pens" is the group in the ablative, then the number with the third-person possessive: *kalemlerden ikisi* (goksel2005 §14.5.2). "Half of", "all of" use the genitive: *öğrencilerin yarısı*, *öğrencilerin hepsi* (goksel2005 §14.5.3).

## Where it applies

- **Counting anything**: *beş kişi*, *on dakika*, *üç yüz lira*. With things, *tane* may come between: *beş tane elma* (tur-02-03).
- **Measures and containers**, in the same slot as *tane*: *iki bardak su* "two glasses of water", *bir şişe şarap* "a bottle of wine", *bir kilo domates* (goksel2005 §15.8).
- **Price per unit**: the measure with the possessive: *Domatesin kilosu kaç lira?* "How much is a kilo of tomatoes?" (goksel2005 §15.8).
- **Age**: *yirmi yaşında* (tur-03-12).
- **Approximate numbers**: two numerals side by side, with no "or": *bir iki gün* "a day or two", *beş altı kişi* "five or six people" (goksel2005 §15.7.1).
- **Large vague numbers**: a plural round number with -CA: *yüzlerce kişi* "hundreds of people", *binlerce* "thousands of" (goksel2005 §7.2.2.2).
- **Ordinals as adjectives**: *ikinci gün*, *üçüncü kahvem*; as nouns with the possessive: *ikincisi* "the second one".
- **Numbered streets, floors, years at school**: *dördüncü sınıf*, *3. sokak*.
- **Distributives for sharing**: *birer elma*; for "each costs": *Elmalar birer lira* "The apples are a lira each".
- **Doubled distributives for manner**: *ikişer ikişer* "two by two", *birer birer* "one by one" (goksel2005 §15.7.3).
- **Halves and quarters**: *yarım ekmek* "half a loaf", *üç buçuk*, *bir çeyrek*; with *ve* for "and a quarter": *üç ve bir çeyrek* (goksel2005 §15.7.1). Clock times are tur-05-04.
- **Part of a group**: *kalemlerden ikisi*, *öğrencilerin yarısı*, *yüzde elli*. "Both of them" is *ikisi*; "the two of us" is *ikimiz*, the numeral with the first-person plural possessive (goksel2005 §18.4 (ii)).
- **Zero, single, pair**: *sıfır*, *tek* "single, only; odd (number)", *çift* "pair; even (number)".

## Watch out

**Do not pluralise after a number.** *\*Yirmi iki öğrenciler* repeats the count. *Yirmi iki öğrenci*. The same after *kaç*, *birkaç* and *çok* (tur-02-03).

**Do not say "one hundred" or "one thousand" with bir.** *\*Bir yüz*, *\*bir bin* follow English. Say *yüz*, *bin*. But "a million" needs it: *bir milyon*, not *\*milyon* alone (goksel2005 §15.7.1).

**Do not keep both vowels in an ordinal after a vowel.** *\*İkiinci*, *\*altııncı* ignore the dropped vowel: *ikinci*, *altıncı*, *yedinci*. After a consonant it stays: *birinci*, *beşinci*.

**Do not forget dört → dörd-.** *\*Dörtüncü* and *\*dörter* keep the *t* before a vowel. *Dördüncü*, *dörder*.

**Do not use a distributive with only one giver and one receiver.** *\*Ali'ye birer elma verdim* has nothing to share out among: one giver, one receiver. Say *Ali'ye bir elma verdim*. *Birer* needs a plural: *Çocuklara birer elma verdim* (goksel2005 §15.7.3).

**Do not translate "each" with her for sharing.** *Her* "every" is a determiner: *her çocuk* "every child". "Two apples each" is not *\*her iki elma*, which means "both apples". It is *ikişer elma*.

**Do not mix up the three halves.** "Two and a half" is *iki buçuk*, not *\*iki yarım*. "Half a kilo" is *yarım kilo*, not *\*buçuk kilo*. "Half of the bread" is *ekmeğin yarısı*, not *\*yarım ekmeğin*.

**Do not put yüzde after the number.** English "fifty per cent" tempts *\*elli yüzde*. *Yüzde* comes first: *yüzde elli*. The sign is written first too: *%50* (goksel2005 §15.7.1).

**Do not use the plural possessive on a number.** "Two of them" is *ikisi*, never *\*ikileri*: numerals do not take the third-person plural possessive (goksel2005 §18.4 (ii)).

## Connections

Japanese and Korean (in its Sino-Korean numbers) build numbers the same way: 二千三百四十五 (nisen sanbyaku yonjū go), literally "two thousand three hundred four-ten five". Korean also has native numbers up to 99, which Turkish does not need. Spanish and French have irregular teens and tens (*quince*, *soixante-dix*) that Turkish lacks. These are parallels in design, not signs of a shared ancestor.

## Exercises

1. Write in words: 17, 64, 308, 1,999, 2,026,500.
2. Write the ordinal and the distributive of *altı*, *dört*, *on* and *yirmi*, and explain each spelling.
3. Translate *Kırk yaşında bir adam, üç çocuğu var.*
4. Build "I gave the guests two glasses of tea each."
5. A learner writes *\*Ali'ye üçer kitap verdim*. What is wrong, and how would you fix the meaning?
6. Say "half a glass of water", "three and a half kilos" and "half of the cake".
7. Translate *Bu soruların kaçını anladın?* and take apart *kaçını*.
8. Correct *\*Bu onuncu kitaplar çok pahalı* meant as "These ten books are very expensive".
9. Say "Five or six people came" and "Hundreds of people came."

## Worked answers

1. *On yedi*; *altmış dört*; *üç yüz sekiz*; *bin dokuz yüz doksan dokuz* (no *bir* before *bin*); *iki milyon yirmi altı bin beş yüz* (*milyon* with a numeral before it, then the thousands, then the hundreds).
2. *Altı-ncı*, *altı-şar*: after a vowel, the ordinal loses its first vowel and the distributive takes *ş*. *Dörd-üncü*, *dörd-er*: *t* becomes *d* before a vowel. *On-uncu*, *on-ar*: back rounded *o* gives *u* in the ordinal and *a* in the distributive. *Yirmi-nci*, *yirmi-şer*: front, after a vowel.
3. "A forty-year-old man; he has three children." *Kırk yaşında*, age in the locative of *yaş* (tur-03-12), stands before *bir adam* as a describing phrase (goksel2005 §15.3.2). *Üç çocuğu var*: number, singular noun with the possessive, *var* (tur-03-09).
4. *Misafirlere ikişer bardak çay verdim.* The receivers are plural, in the dative (tur-03-02); the distributive *ikişer* stands before the measure *bardak*, which stands before *çay* (goksel2005 §15.8).
5. One receiver, Ali, and one giver: nothing is shared out, so a distributive is wrong (goksel2005 §15.7.3). Say *Ali'ye üç kitap verdim*. If you meant "to Ali and Ayşe, three each", make the receivers plural: *Ali'yle Ayşe'ye üçer kitap verdim* (tur-03-13).
6. *Yarım bardak su*; *üç buçuk kilo*; *pastanın yarısı* (genitive group, *yarı* with the possessive).
7. "How many of these questions did you understand?" *Kaç-ı-nı*: *kaç* with the third-person possessive "how many of them", pronominal *n*, accusative because the partitive is the object (goksel2005 §14.5.2; tur-03-06). The group *soruların* is in the genitive (§14.5.3).
8. *Bu on kitap çok pahalı.* *Onuncu* is "tenth", and after a number the noun stays singular.
9. *Beş altı kişi geldi.* Two numerals side by side give an approximation (goksel2005 §15.7.1). *Yüzlerce kişi geldi.*
