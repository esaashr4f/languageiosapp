## The idea

Turkish places events in time with the same tools it uses for places. You already have them all: the cases of part 3, the numbers of tur-05-03, and the possessive.

- **The locative -DA "at, in"** marks a point in time, as it marks a point in space: *saat üçte* "at three o'clock", *haziranda* "in June", *1995'te* "in 1995" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1).
- **The dative -(y)A "to"** and the **accusative -(y)I** appear in clock times before and after the hour, with two frozen verb forms: *geçe* "passing", from *geçmek* "pass", and *kala* "remaining", from *kalmak* "remain" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1). *Dörde yirmi kala* "at twenty to four" is literally "to four, twenty remaining". *İkiyi beş geçe* "at five past two" is literally "passing two by five". The hour you pass is the object of passing, so it takes the accusative; the hour you are heading for is a goal, so it takes the dative.
- **No case at all** for many "when" words: *bu sabah* "this morning", *geçen hafta* "last week", *pazartesi* "on Monday". Göksel and Kerslake list the words that stay bare (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1): the parts of the day and the time units *gün*, *hafta*, *ay*, *yıl* after *bu*, *o*, *geçen*, *gelecek*, *ilk*, *son* and ordinals. Days of the week never take the locative.
- **No case either for how long**: *iki saat bekledik* "we waited two hours" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.2).

Two patterns are new:

- **Repeated times** take *-lArI*: *akşamları* "in the evenings", *pazartesileri* "on Mondays" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1).
- **"For" a period that is still going on** takes *-DIr*, the same suffix as tur-04-11, on the length of time: *üç yıldır* "for three years (and still)" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.2 (iii)). The verb is then in the present: *Üç yıldır burada çalışıyorum* "I have been working here for three years".

## How it is built

**Asking and stating the time.** *Saat kaç?* "What time is it?" (goksel2005 §19.2.1.5).

1. **On the hour**: *Saat üç.* "It's three."
2. **Half past**: *buçuk* after the hour: *Saat üç buçuk.* 12:30 is *yarım* (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1).
3. **Minutes past**: the hour in the accusative, the minutes, *geçiyor* "is passing": *Saat ikiyi beş geçiyor* "It's five past two".
4. **Minutes to**: the hour in the dative, the minutes, *var* "there are": *Saat dörde yirmi var* "It's twenty to four", "to four there are twenty".
5. **Quarters**: *çeyrek* in place of the minutes: *Saat yediyi çeyrek geçiyor*, *Saat on ikiye çeyrek var*.

Steps 3 to 5 are the pattern of teaching courses; Göksel and Kerslake give only the "at" forms below.

**At a time.** *Kaçta?* "At what time?" is *kaç* with the locative.

1. **On the hour or half hour**: the locative on the last word: *saat üçte*, *üç buçukta*, *yarımda* "at half past twelve".
2. **Minutes past**: replace *geçiyor* with *geçe*: *ikiyi beş geçe* "at five past two".
3. **Minutes to**: replace *var* with *kala*: *dörde yirmi kala* "at twenty to four".
4. **Twenty-four-hour clock**: the locative on the minutes: *saat yirmi otuzda* "at 20.30" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1).

*Saat* is optional and always comes first. Watch *dört*: *dördü*, *dörde*, *dörtte* (tur-01-06).

**Dates.**

1. **A month**: the locative: *şubatta* "in February", or *şubat ayında* with *ay* "month" as a compound (tur-03-08).
2. **A day of the month**: the number, then the month, with the locative on the month: *on yedi haziranda* "on 17 June". Written *17 Haziran'da*: with an exact date the month has a capital, and so takes the apostrophe (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1).
3. **A year**: said as an ordinary number, with the locative: *bin dokuz yüz doksan beşte*, written *1995'te*. The suffix harmonises with the spoken last word, *beş*.
4. **All three**: the locative only on the last: *8 Kasım 1984'te*.

**Days of the week.** No locative: *Salı geliyorum* "I'm coming on Tuesday". Colloquially the dative is added for the future: *salıya* (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1). Every Tuesday: *salıları*.

**Seasons.** *Yaz* and *kış* have special forms, *yazın* "in summer" and *kışın* "in winter". *İlkbahar* and *sonbahar* take the locative: *ilkbaharda* (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1).

**Ago, later, within, how often.**

- *iki gün önce* "two days ago", *beş gün sonra* "in five days, five days later" (tur-04-03; goksel2005 §16.4.1.1).
- **Within** a period: the locative on the length: *üç günde bitirdim* "I finished it in three days" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1 (iii)).
- **How often**: the unit in the locative, then the count with *kez*: *günde üç kez* "three times a day", *haftada bir* "once a week" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.3).

## Where it applies

- **Clock time**, stating: *Saat üç*, *ikiyi beş geçiyor*, *dörde yirmi var*. At a time: *üçte*, *üç buçukta*, *ikiyi beş geçe*, *dörde yirmi kala*, *yarımda*, *yirmi otuzda*.
- **Asking**: *Saat kaç?*; *Kaçta?*; *Saat kaçta?* (goksel2005 §19.2.1.5).
- **Days**: bare (*salı*), or as a compound with *gün*: *salı günü* (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1); "What day is it today?" is *Bugün günlerden ne?*, literally "today, of the days, what?" (goksel2005 §14.5.1).
- **Months, years, dates**: locative on the last element: *haziranda*, *1995'te*, *17 Haziran 1995'te*. Informally, as a possessive phrase: *haziranın on yedisinde* (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1).
- **Parts of a month or year**: with the possessive and *son* "end": *ayın sonunda* "at the end of the month"; *ayın birinde* "on the first of the month" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1).
- **Decades**: a plural year with the locative: *1980'lerde* "in the 1980s".
- **Seasons**: *yazın*, *kışın*, *ilkbaharda*, *sonbaharda*.
- **Bare time words**: *sabah*, *akşam*, *gece*, *öğleden sonra*; *bu sabah*, *dün akşam*, *yarın sabah*, *geçen hafta*, *gelecek ay*, *ertesi gün*, *ilk gün* (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1 (i)).
- **Plural time words with the locative**: *son yıllarda* "in recent years", *o günlerde* "in those days"; a period noun with the locative: *çocukluğumda* "in my childhood" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1 (iii)).
- **Repeated times**: *sabahları*, *akşamları*, *geceleri*, *pazartesileri*.
- **Duration**: bare: *iki saat*; still going on: *üç yıldır*, or *üç yıldan beri* (tur-05-06); "how long for?": *Kaç yıldır?*
- **Within**: *üç günde*, *bir saatte*.
- **Frequency**: *günde bir kez*, *haftada iki kez*, *yılda bir*; general words *her gün*, *sık sık*, *bazen*, *sürekli* "constantly".
- **Ago and later**: *iki gün önce*, *iki gün sonra*. Before and after a date or event: tur-05-06.
- **Being in time**: *trene yetişmek* "catch the train" (dative).

## Watch out

**Do not put the locative on a day of the week.** *\*Pazartesinde geliyorum* copies *haziranda*. Days take no locative (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1): *Pazartesi geliyorum*.

**Do not use the locative with geçe or kala.** *\*İkide beş geçe* puts the "at" case on the hour. The hour is accusative before *geçe* and dative before *kala*; the phrase as a whole already means "at": *ikiyi beş geçe*, *dörde yirmi kala*.

**Do not mix up the two pairs.** *Geçiyor* and *var* state the time; *geçe* and *kala* say when something happens. *\*Toplantı dörde çeyrek var başlıyor* is two sentences glued together. *Toplantı dörde çeyrek kala başlıyor.*

**Do not swap accusative and dative.** *\*Dördü yirmi kala* and *\*ikiye beş geçe* reverse the logic: you pass the hour already reached (accusative) and are short of the hour ahead (dative).

**Do not translate "for three years" with için.** *\*Üç yıl için burada çalışıyorum* sounds like a planned stay. For a period still going on, use *-DIr*: *Üç yıldır burada çalışıyorum*. *İçin* with a period is for an intended stay: *birkaç gün için Fransa'ya gidiyorum* "I'm going to France for a few days" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.2 (iv)).

**Do not use the past with -DIr when the state continues.** English "I have been living" tempts *\*Üç yıldır İstanbul'da oturdum*. Turkish uses the present: *oturuyoruz*.

**Do not put a case on bu sabah or geçen hafta.** *\*Geçen haftada* and *\*bu sabahta* add a locative that these phrases do not take (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1 (i)).

**Do not say "in winter" as kışta.** Use the fixed form *kışın*; likewise *yazın*. But *ilkbaharda*, *sonbaharda*.

**Harmonise a figure by the spoken word**: *1995'te* (*beş*), *2019'da* (*dokuz*), *2000'de* (*bin*).

**Do not use the plural for "every Monday" without the possessive.** *\*Pazartesiler çalışırım* is not idiomatic; the regular form is *pazartesileri* (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1).

## Connections

Japanese and Korean also use the locative-type particle for clock times (三時に, 세 시에) and leave words like "today" or "last week" bare, as Turkish does. Spanish *a las tres* and French *à trois heures* use a preposition of direction instead. The Turkish "remaining to four" pattern resembles French *quatre heures moins vingt* only in its arithmetic. These are parallels, not inheritance.

## Exercises

1. Say what time it is: 6:00, 9:30, 10:10, 4:45, 12:30.
2. Say "at" each of the same times.
3. Translate *Doktor salıları dokuzu çeyrek geçe gelir.*
4. Build "I was born on 3 May 2001", in writing and in words.
5. A learner writes *\*Cumada sinemaya gidiyoruz.* Correct it.
6. Translate *Beş yıldır Türkçe öğreniyorum* and explain the tense.
7. Say "I drink coffee twice a day" and "We go to the village once a year".
8. Why is it *ikiyi* in *ikiyi on geçe* but *ikiye* in *ikiye on kala*?
9. Translate *Yazın akşamları sahilde yürürüz, kışın evde kalırız.* (*sahil* "seaside, shore")
10. Correct *\*İki saatte bekledik* meant as "We waited for two hours", and say what the learner's sentence would mean.

## Worked answers

1. *Saat altı. Saat dokuz buçuk. Saat onu on geçiyor. Saat beşe çeyrek var. Saat yarım.* For 4:45 count to the next hour, five, in the dative (tur-05-04).
2. *Altıda, dokuz buçukta, onu on geçe, beşe çeyrek kala, yarımda.* The locative goes on the hour or on *buçuk*; *geçe* and *kala* replace *geçiyor* and *var*.
3. "The doctor comes on Tuesdays at a quarter past nine." *Salı-ları*: regular day; *dokuz-u*, accusative before *geçe*; the aorist for a routine (tur-04-06).
4. *3 Mayıs 2001'de doğdum.* In words: *üç mayıs iki bin birde doğdum*. The locative only on the last element, the year; *bir* gives *-de*. *Doğmak* in the past (tur-04-03).
5. *Cuma sinemaya gidiyoruz*, or colloquially *Cumaya*. Days of the week never take the locative (goksel2005 §16.4.1.1).
6. "I have been learning Turkish for five years." *-DIr* on the period says it is still going on, so the verb is in the present *-(I)yor*, where English uses "have been" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.2).
7. *Günde iki kez kahve içerim.* *Yılda bir köye gideriz.* The unit in the locative, then the count; the aorist for habits.
8. Past the hour, the hour is what is passed: the object of *geç-*, accusative. To the hour, the hour is the goal still to be reached: dative, as in *eve gitmek* (tur-03-02).
9. "In summer we walk by the sea in the evenings; in winter we stay at home." *Yazın*, *kışın*: fixed forms; *akşamları*: regular; aorist for habits.
10. *İki saat bekledik.* Duration takes no case. *İki saatte* means "within two hours", which suits finishing something, not waiting.
