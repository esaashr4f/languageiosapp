## The idea

A **postposition** is a word that does the work of an English preposition but comes after its noun (tur-03-13): *ile* "with" was the first. English says "for Ali"; Turkish says *Ali için*, "Ali for". This is the head-final order again: the word that says how the phrase relates to the rest of the sentence comes last, just as a case suffix does.

Postpositions do what cases do, for meanings the six cases do not cover. Turkish has a handful of cases and many postpositions. Göksel and Kerslake sort them by what they require of the noun before them (goksel2005 §17.2):

- a **bare** noun (this lesson);
- a noun in the **dative** or **ablative** (tur-05-06);
- a noun with a **possessive** and a case, the location nouns (tur-05-07).

This lesson covers the four bare postpositions: ***için*** "for", ***gibi*** "like", ***kadar*** "as ... as, as much as" and ***ile*** "with", which you know (goksel2005 §17.2.1).

**One exception: some pronouns go into the genitive.** The noun before these four is left bare: *Ali için*, *çocuk gibi*. But the singular personal pronouns, *biz* and *siz*, the demonstratives and *kim* take the genitive: *benim için* "for me", *senin gibi* "like you", *onun kadar* "as much as him/her" (goksel2005 §17.2.1). Göksel and Kerslake call these eight **genitive-attracting pronouns**: *ben, sen, biz, siz, bu, şu, o, kim*. Their plurals stay bare: *onlar için* "for them", *bunlar gibi* "like these". You met the same behaviour with *ile* in tur-03-13: *benimle*, *onunla*, but *onlarla*.

Why the genitive? The sources used here do not say; treat it as a fact of these eight words. The same eight attract the genitive before the location nouns of tur-05-07, which at least makes them one pattern to learn, not two.

## How it is built

1. **Find the noun phrase**: "my friend": *arkadaşım*.
2. **Is it one of the eight pronouns?** If so, put it in the genitive (*benim*, *senin*, *onun*, *bizim*, *sizin*, *bunun*, *şunun*, *kimin*). If not, leave it bare, whatever it is: a common noun, a name, a plural, a possessed noun. *Arkadaşım* stays as it is.
3. **Add the postposition after it, as a separate word**: *arkadaşım için* "for my friend". It never changes: no harmony, no suffixes (goksel2005 §17.2).
4. **Put the whole phrase where an adverb or adjective goes.** A *gibi* or *kadar* phrase can stand before a noun like an adjective: *senin gibi bir öğrenci* "a student like you". All four can stand before the verb like an adverb: *senin gibi konuşuyor* "he talks like you".

**What each one means.**

- ***için*** "for": for whom or what something is: *Bu hediye senin için* "This present is for you". With nouns like *iş* or *para* it gives a purpose: *iş için* "on business" (goksel2005 §17.2.1 (ii)). *Bunun için*, *onun için* also mean "so, because of this" (goksel2005 §17.2.1 (ii)).
- ***gibi*** "like": similarity: *aslan gibi* "like a lion". *Bunun gibi*, *onun gibi* are close to *böyle*, *öyle* "such" (tur-03-11; goksel2005 §17.2.1 (i)). *Ne gibi* "what kind of" (goksel2005 §19.2.1.9).
- ***kadar*** "as ... as": the same degree, before an adjective or adverb: *Ali kadar uzun* "as tall as Ali". Or the same amount, before a verb: *Ali kadar çalışıyor* "she works as much as Ali" (goksel2005 §17.2.1 (iv)). After a number, *kadar* means "about": *on kadar kişi* "about ten people" (goksel2005 §16.1.10, ex. 26). With a demonstrative it makes *bu kadar* "this much, so", *o kadar* "that much, so" (goksel2005 §17.2.1 (iv)).

## Where it applies

- **İçin, for a person or thing**: *senin için*, *annem için*, *çocuklar için*.
- **İçin, for a purpose** with some nouns: *iş için*, *para için* "for money".
- **İçin with a time**, for an intended stay: *birkaç gün için* "for a few days" (tur-05-04).
- **Bunun için, onun için** "so, that is why", at the start of the second clause.
- **İçin after a clause**, "in order to, because": *-mAk için*, *-DIğI için* (tur-06-09).
- **Gibi, manner**: *kuş gibi şarkı söylüyor*; **gibi, kind**: *senin gibi bir arkadaş*, *aslan gibi bir adam*.
- **Gibi as "such as"** in lists: *elma, armut* ("pear") *gibi meyveler* "fruits such as apples and pears" (wiktionary "such as").
- **Gibi as a noun**: *bunun gibiler* "ones like this", with the plural and possessive (goksel2005 §17.2.1 (i)).
- **Ne gibi** "what kind of": *Ne gibi bir ev istiyorsunuz?*
- **Kadar, the same degree**: *Ali kadar uzun*, *senin kadar akıllı*; with an adverb: *Ali kadar hızlı koşuyor*.
- **Kadar, the same amount**: *Ali kadar çalışıyor*.
- **Kadar, "about"** after a number: *on kadar kişi*, *bir saat kadar* "about an hour". *Yaklaşık* and *aşağı yukarı* "approximately" go before the number instead (goksel2005 §15.8).
- **Bu kadar, o kadar** "so, so much": *o kadar güzel ki ...* (with *ki*, tur-06-12); *ne kadar* "how much" (tur-03-12).
- **Kadar with the dative**, "until, as far as": a different construction, tur-05-06.
- **İle** "with, by": tur-03-13.

## Watch out

**Do not put the postposition before the noun.** *\*İçin Ali*, *\*gibi bir aslan* follow English and Spanish (*para Ali*, *como un león*). *Ali için*, *bir aslan gibi*.

**Do not leave the eight pronouns bare.** *\*Ben için*, *\*sen gibi*, *\*o kadar uzun* (for "as tall as him") are wrong in the standard language: *benim için*, *senin gibi*, *onun kadar uzun* (goksel2005 §17.2.1).

**Do not put ordinary nouns in the genitive.** By analogy with *benim için*, learners write *\*Ali'nin için*, *\*annemin gibi*. Only the eight pronouns take the genitive: *Ali için*, *annem gibi*.

**Do not put the plural pronouns in the genitive.** *\*Onların için* over-applies the rule. Plurals stay bare: *onlar için*, *bunlar gibi* (goksel2005 §17.2.1).

**Do not mix up o kadar and onun kadar.** *O kadar* "that much, so" is a degree word: *o kadar uzun* "so tall". *Onun kadar* is "as ... as him/her": *onun kadar uzun* "as tall as him". The genitive changes the meaning (goksel2005 §17.2.1 (iv)).

**Do not use gibi with daha for "than".** *\*Ali gibi daha uzun* mixes two constructions. "As tall as Ali" is *Ali kadar uzun*; "taller than Ali" is *Ali'den daha uzun* (tur-05-02). *Gibi* says "like", not "as much as".

**Do not add case suffixes to the postposition.** *\*Senin içine* is not "for you" (it means "into your inside", from *iç* "inside", tur-05-07). *İçin* never changes.

**Do not use için for "for" a period that is still going on.** *\*Üç yıl için burada oturuyorum* is wrong for "I've lived here for three years"; that is *Üç yıldır* (tur-05-04).

**Do not use kadar for "about" before the number.** *\*Kadar on kişi* puts it first, as English "about ten". *On kadar kişi*, or *yaklaşık on kişi*.

## Connections

Japanese and Korean put their "for", "like" and "as much as" after the noun too: Japanese のために (no tame ni) "for", のように (no yō ni) "like", ほど (hodo) "as much as"; Korean 처럼 (cheoreom) "like", 만큼 (mankeum) "as much as". Japanese の before ために and ように even links the noun with a genitive-like particle, which looks like Turkish *benim için*, though in Japanese it applies to every noun. These are parallels of type, not of descent.

## Exercises

1. Say "for you (one friend)", "for you (several)", "for them" and "for Ayşe".
2. Translate *Benim gibi bir yabancı için Türkçe zor.*
3. A learner writes *\*Bu kitap Ali'nin için.* Correct it.
4. Build "My sister is as clever as you (polite)."
5. Translate *Yarın çok işim var, onun için gelemem.*
6. Explain the difference between *o kadar uzun* and *onun kadar uzun*.
7. Say "About fifty people live in this village."
8. Translate *Bulutlar pamuk gibi.* (*pamuk* "cotton")
9. Correct *\*İçin annem bir pasta yaptım.*

## Worked answers

1. *Senin için*, *sizin için* (both genitive: two of the eight pronouns), *onlar için* (plural: bare), *Ayşe için* (a name: bare) (tur-05-05).
2. "For a foreigner like me, Turkish is difficult." *Benim gibi*: genitive pronoun before *gibi*; the *gibi* phrase describes *bir yabancı*, and the whole is the complement of *için*.
3. *Bu kitap Ali için.* Only the eight pronouns take the genitive; a name stays bare (goksel2005 §17.2.1).
4. *Kız kardeşim sizin kadar akıllı.* *Siz* takes the genitive before *kadar*; *kadar* before the adjective gives the same degree. (*Kız kardeş* "sister" is glossed here; you know *kardeş*.)
5. "I have a lot of work tomorrow, so I can't come." *Çok işim var* (tur-03-09); *onun için* "so"; *gelemem*, impotential aorist (tur-04-13).
6. *O kadar uzun* "so tall, that tall": *o kadar* is a degree word. *Onun kadar uzun* "as tall as him/her": *onun* is the genitive pronoun before the postposition (goksel2005 §17.2.1 (iv)).
7. *Bu köyde elli kadar kişi yaşıyor*, or *yaklaşık elli kişi*. *Kadar* follows the number; *yaklaşık* precedes it.
8. "The clouds are like cotton." The *gibi* phrase is the predicate.
9. *Annem için bir pasta yaptım.* The postposition follows its noun.
