## The idea

English has prepositions for places: *on*, *under*, *in front of*, *behind*. Turkish has no such words. It uses nouns that name a part of a thing, and possesses them.

*Üst* is "top". *Masanın üstü* is "the table's top", the genitive-possessive phrase of tur-03-05. Add a case, and you have a place: *masanın üstünde* "at the table's top", that is "on the table". Göksel and Kerslake call these **possessive-marked postpositions** (goksel2005 §17.3); this guide calls them **location nouns**: nouns of place that, with a possessive and a case, do the work of English prepositions.

Nothing here is new grammar. It is the genitive (tur-03-05), the third-person possessive with its pronominal *n* (tur-03-06), and the three place cases you know:

- **locative** *-DA* for where something is: *masanın üstünde* "on the table";
- **dative** *-(y)A* for where something goes: *masanın üstüne* "onto the table";
- **ablative** *-DAn* for where it comes from, or the way through: *masanın üstünden* "off the table; over the table" (goksel2005 §17.3.1.1).

The case on the location noun changes with the meaning; that is the point of building them from nouns. Göksel and Kerslake note that these nouns name not only a part of the object but the space beside it: *ön* is "front", and also "the space in front" (goksel2005 §17.3.1).

**The main location nouns** (goksel2005 §17.3.1): *üst* "top: on, above", *alt* "bottom: under, below", *ön* "front: in front of", *arka* "back: behind", *iç* "inside: in, inside", *dış* "outside: outside", *yan* "side: beside, next to; with (a person)", *karşı* "opposite side: opposite", *ara* "space between: between, among", *etraf* or *çevre* "surroundings: around", *öte* "far side: beyond". Add *orta* "middle".

## How it is built

"The cat is under the bed."

1. **The reference object, in the genitive**: *yatak* → *yatağın* (k → ğ before a vowel, tur-01-06).
2. **The location noun, with the possessive that answers the genitive**: third person: *alt-ı*.
3. **The case, chosen by the meaning**: the cat is there, so the locative. After the third-person possessive, the case takes *n* (tur-03-06): *alt-ı-nda*.
4. **The sentence**: *Kedi yatağın altında.*

**With a pronoun**, the possessive changes person, and the pronoun itself is often left out, since the possessive says who (goksel2005 §17.3): *(benim) yanımda* "beside me, with me", *(senin) önünde* "in front of you", *(bizim) arkamızda* "behind us".

**When the genitive is left out.** The reference noun stays bare, and the location noun keeps the third-person singular possessive, when the reference is not a specific thing (goksel2005 §17.3.1.2):

- a non-specific noun: *bir kâğıt üstünde* "on a piece of paper";
- a metaphor or a time: *iki saat içinde* "within two hours", *korku içinde* "in fear".

**Direction and source.** Change only the last suffix:

- *Kutuyu masanın altına koy.* "Put the box under the table." (dative: goal)
- *Kutu masanın altında.* "The box is under the table." (locative)
- *Kutuyu masanın altından al.* "Take the box from under the table." (ablative)
- *Parkın içinden geçtik.* "We went through the park." (ablative: the way through, goksel2005 §17.3.1.1 (iii))

**Two references with ara.** "Between X and Y" joins the two with *ile*, and *ara* takes the possessive: *evle okul arasında* "between the house and the school" (goksel2005 §17.3.1.3 (v)).

## Where it applies

- **Where something is**: locative: *masanın üstünde*, *yatağın altında*, *evin önünde*, *okulun arkasında*, *çantanın içinde*, *evin dışında* "outside the house", *bankanın karşısında*, *evin etrafında* "around the house", *şehrin ortasında* "in the middle of the city".
- **Where something goes**: dative: *masanın üstüne koy*, *yanıma otur*, *evin önüne park et*.
- **Where it comes from, or the way through**: ablative: *dolabın altından*, *parkın içinden geçtik*, *köprünün altından* "under the bridge (passing)".
- **With pronouns**: *yanımda*, *önünde*, *arkamızda*, *aranızda* "among you, between you"; the genitive pronoun may be added for emphasis.
- **Yan with people**: "at someone's place" (*annesinin yanında*), "in someone's presence" (*babasının yanında hiç konuşmaz* "he never speaks in front of his father"), "working for": *Yanında on kişi çalışıyor* "Ten people work for her" (goksel2005 §17.3.1.3 (iv)).
- **İç and ara for "among"**: *öğrenciler arasında* "among the students" (goksel2005 §17.3.1.3 (i)).
- **Non-specific or metaphorical references, without the genitive**: *bir kâğıt üstünde*, *iki saat içinde*, *korku içinde*, *kardeşler arasında* "between siblings (in general)" (goksel2005 §17.3.1.2).
- **As ordinary nouns**: *Evin içi çok güzel* "The inside of the house is very nice"; *arabanın önü* "the front of the car".
- **Bare adverbs**: *içeri*, *dışarı* (tur-04-02) for "in, out" with no reference object; *iç* and *dış* bare only as adjectives: *iç kapı* "inner door", *dış kapı* "outer door" (goksel2005 §7.2.2.2).
- **The abstract group**, built the same way but with a fixed case (goksel2005 §17.3.2): *hakkında* "about" (*film hakkında* "about the film", *benim hakkımda* "about me"); *yerine* "instead of" (*Ali yerine ben geldim* "I came instead of Ali"); *yüzünden* "because of" (*yağmur yüzünden*); *sayesinde* "thanks to" (*senin sayende* "thanks to you"). With these the reference noun is bare unless it is one of the eight pronouns of tur-05-05.

## Watch out

**Do not look for a word for "on" or "under".** *\*Masa üst* or *\*üstünde masa* imitate English. The pattern is genitive, location noun, possessive, case: *masanın üstünde*.

**Do not forget the possessive.** *\*Masanın üstde* and *\*masanın üstte* drop the suffix that answers the genitive (tur-03-05). *Masanın üstünde*.

**Do not forget the pronominal n.** *\*Masanın üstüde* skips the *n* that a case needs after a third-person possessive (tur-03-06): *üstünde*, *üstüne*, *üstünden*.

**Do not use the locative for motion.** *\*Kutuyu masanın altında koy* says where you are when you put it. Putting it somewhere is a goal: *masanın altına koy*.

**Do not keep the genitive with a time or metaphor.** *\*İki saatin içinde* sounds like "inside the two hours (that we know)". For "within", the reference is bare: *iki saat içinde* (goksel2005 §17.3.1.2).

**Do not put the eight pronouns in the dative or bare.** *\*Ben yanımda* and *\*bana yanında* are wrong. The pronoun is genitive or left out, and the possessive agrees: *(benim) yanımda*.

**Do not use içinde for every "in".** "In the house" is usually *evde*, with the plain locative (tur-02-06). *Evin içinde* stresses "inside, indoors", as against outside.

**Do not confuse altı "six" and altı "its bottom".** *Altı* the number and *alt-ı* "its underside" are spelt alike. *Masanın altı* is "the underside of the table", not "six tables".

**Do not use karşı with the wrong case for the wrong meaning.** *Bankaya karşı* is "against the bank" (tur-05-06); "opposite the bank" is *bankanın karşısında*.

## Connections

Japanese and Korean do the same thing with nouns of place: Japanese 机の上に (tsukue no ue ni) "desk-GEN top-at", Korean 책상 위에 (chaeksang wie) "desk top-at". Turkish *masanın üstünde* has the same three parts, with the possessive suffix as an extra link. The parallel is close, but it is typological: no common origin is implied.

## Exercises

1. Build "The bag is behind the door" step by step.
2. Translate *Kitaplar yatağın altından çıktı.*
3. Say "Put the flowers on the table", "The flowers are on the table" and "Take the flowers off the table".
4. A learner writes *\*Ayşe benim yanda oturuyor.* Correct it.
5. Translate *Çocuklar ağacın etrafında koşuyor.*
6. Say "There is a cinema between the bank and the hotel."
7. Translate *Bu film hakkında ne düşünüyorsun?*
8. Why is *iki gün içinde* bare, but *evin içinde* genitive?
9. Correct *\*Arabamızın önde bir otobüs var.*
10. Say "I'll go instead of you" and "Thanks to you, I learnt Turkish."

## Worked answers

1. Reference *kapı*, genitive *kapının*. Location noun *arka*, possessive *arka-sı*, locative with *n*: *arkasında*. *Çanta kapının arkasında.*
2. "The books came out from under the bed." The ablative of the location noun for motion from.
3. *Çiçekleri masanın üstüne koy* (dative: goal). *Çiçekler masanın üstünde* (locative). *Çiçekleri masanın üstünden al* (ablative: source).
4. *Ayşe benim yanımda oturuyor*, or *Ayşe yanımda oturuyor*. The location noun takes the possessive that agrees with the possessor: *yan-ım-da*.
5. "The children are running around the tree." *Etraf-ı-nda*: around, in the locative because the running stays in that space.
6. *Bankayla otel arasında bir sinema var.* Two references joined with *-(y)lA*, *ara-sı-nda* (goksel2005 §17.3.1.3 (v)).
7. "What do you think about this film?" *Hakkında* is fixed in the locative; the reference noun *film* is bare (goksel2005 §17.3.2).
8. *İki gün* is a period of time, not a specific object, so it stays bare (goksel2005 §17.3.1.2 (i)). *Evin içi* is the inside of a particular house: genitive-possessive.
9. *Arabamızın önünde bir otobüs var.* The location noun needs its possessive and the *n*: *ön-ü-nde*.
10. *Senin yerine ben giderim.* *Senin sayende Türkçe öğrendim.* *Sen* is one of the eight pronouns, so it is genitive, and the possessive agrees with it: *yer-in-e*, *saye-n-de* (goksel2005 §17.3.2).
