## The idea

A **conjunction** is a word that joins two words, phrases or sentences of the same kind: "and", "but", "or". You have used *ve* "and" and *ama* "but" since part 2, and *ile* "and" since tur-03-13. This lesson collects the whole set and adds the small word that Turkish uses more than any of them: ***de***.

**Two kinds of joining word.** Göksel and Kerslake distinguish conjunctions, which join two items of the same kind, from **discourse connectives**, which link a sentence to what came before, possibly several sentences back (goksel2005 §28.3): *ve* "and" is a conjunction; *üstelik* "what's more" is a connective. Many words do both jobs.

**The paired conjunctions.** Turkish likes to mark both halves of a pair: *hem ... hem* "both ... and", *ne ... ne* "neither ... nor", *ya ... ya da* "either ... or", *de ... de* "both ... and". Each word stands before (or, for *de*, after) its own half, so the listener knows from the first word what kind of list is coming.

**The particle de.** *De* (also *da*) is a separate word that follows the word it belongs to. Its vowel harmonises (*annem de*, *Ali de*, *sonra da*), but its first letter is always *d*, never *t* (goksel2005 §11.1.1.2). It has three main jobs:

- **"too, also"**, after the stressed word: *Ben de geliyorum* "I'm coming too";
- **"and"**, joining a new sentence to the last: *Akşam yemek yedik, sonra da sinemaya gittik* "We had dinner, and then we went to the cinema";
- **"both ... and"** when it follows each of two items: *Ali de Ayşe de* "both Ali and Ayşe" (goksel2005 §§28.3.1.1 (iii), 28.3.2).

With a negative verb, "too" becomes "either": *Ben de gelmiyorum* "I'm not coming either". English needs two words; Turkish uses one.

*De* is not the locative *-DA*. The locative is a suffix, written joined, and has *t* after voiceless consonants: *kitapta* "in the book". The particle is written apart and never has *t*: *kitap da* "the book too". Spelling keeps them apart, and so does stress: *de* is pre-stressing, like *mI* (tur-01-07), so the stress falls on the word before it.

## How it is built

**Joining two items.**

1. **Decide the relation**: and, but, or, both, neither, either.
2. **Pick the word**:
   - and: *ve* (any items), *-(y)lA* (nouns only, tur-03-13);
   - but: *ama*, *fakat*, *ancak*, *yalnız*: *ama* and *fakat* are interchangeable in meaning (goksel2005 §28.3.4.1), though in everyday speech *ama* is far more common and *fakat* is often felt to be a little more formal or serious (a usage impression, not stated in the grammars used here); *ancak* and *yalnız* mainly introduce a drawback or limitation (goksel2005 §28.3.4.1);
   - or: *ya da*, *veya*; in questions, *yoksa* (tur-02-05; goksel2005 §28.3.3.1);
   - because: *çünkü*, at the start or at the end of the second sentence (goksel2005 §28.3.6).
3. **Put it between the two items**: *çay ve kahve*, *Pahalı ama güzel*.

**Pairs.** Put the first word before the first item and the second before the second:

- *hem* A *hem* (*de*) B "both A and B";
- *ne* A *ne* (*de*) B "neither A nor B";
- *ya* A *ya da* B "either A or B";
- A *de* B *de* "both A and B", with *de* after each.

**Ne ... ne and the verb.** The verb may be positive or negative: *Ne çay ne kahve içer* or *Ne çay ne kahve içmez*, both "He drinks neither tea nor coffee" (goksel2005 §20.4). Each has its own intonation. This guide uses the positive verb, which is never ambiguous.

**De "too".** Put *de* right after the word that "too" belongs to, and stress that word:

- *Ali de geldi.* "Ali came too." (Ali as well as others)
- *Ali geldi de.* "Ali did come, too." (as well as other things happening)
- *Ali bugün de geldi.* "Ali came today too." (today as well as other days)

## Where it applies

- ***Ve***: nouns (*çay ve kahve*), adjectives (*genç ve güzel*), verbs and whole sentences. Between clauses, Turkish often prefers *-(y)Ip* (tur-06-06; goksel2005 §28.3.1.1 (i)).
- ***-(y)lA / ile*** as "and": only between bare nouns: *Ali'yle Ayşe*, *evle okul arasında* (tur-05-07). Never between verbs or adjectives.
- ***Ama, fakat, ancak, yalnız*** "but": *ama* and *fakat* interchangeably (*fakat* often felt slightly more formal or serious; *ama* is the everyday word); *ancak* mainly before a drawback (goksel2005 §28.3.4.1). *Yalnız* "only" (tur-02-05) also means "but, however" at the start of a clause.
- ***Ya da, veya*** "or" in statements and in questions that ask "any of these?" (*Çay ya da kahve var mı?*); ***yoksa*** in questions that ask "which one?" (*Çay mı içersin, yoksa kahve mi?*, tur-02-05).
- ***Ya ... ya da*** "either ... or": *Ya Ali'yle ya da Ayşe'yle konuş* "Speak either to Ali or to Ayşe" (goksel2005 §28.3.3.1).
- ***Hem ... hem (de)*** "both ... and", for any items; also with clauses: *Hem ucuz hem de güzel* "both cheap and nice".
- ***De ... de*** "both ... and", after nouns: *Ali de Mehmet de*.
- ***Ne ... ne (de)*** "neither ... nor": *ne çay ne kahve*. With a list of more than two, each item takes *ne*.
- ***De*** "too": after any stressed word, including the verb: *okudum da*; with a negative, "either".
- ***De*** "and", linking a new sentence or topic: *Sonra da ...*; *Ayşe'ye de bir mektup geldi* "And a letter came for Ayşe" (goksel2005 §28.3.1.1 (iiia)).
- ***Bile*** "even": *selam bile*; after a verb, "already": *okudum bile*.
- ***Çünkü*** "because", for a reason added after the result, mostly in speech; the reason can also come first with *-DIğI için* (tur-06-09; goksel2005 §28.3.6).
- ***Onun için, bunun için*** "so, that is why" (tur-05-05).
- **Additions**: *üstelik* "what's more", *ayrıca* "also, in addition", *hatta* "indeed, even", *bir de* "and also (an afterthought)": *Ekmek al, bir de süt* "Buy bread, and milk too" (goksel2005 §§28.3.1.1–28.3.1.3).
- ***İster ... ister*** "whether ... or" with imperatives: *İster çay iç, ister kahve* "Have tea or coffee, as you like" (goksel2005 §28.3.3.2).

## Watch out

**Do not write de joined, or with t.** *\*Bende geliyorum* means "I'm coming *at my place*" to a reader, because *bende* is the locative of *ben* "on me, at my place" (tur-03-10). "I'm coming too" is *Ben de geliyorum*. And *\*kitap ta* for "the book too" uses the locative's *t*: the particle is always *de* or *da*: *kitap da*.

**Do not use ve for "too".** English "me too" tempts *\*ve ben*. Turkish says *ben de*.

**Do not put de before the word it belongs to.** *\*De ben geliyorum* follows English "also I". *De* follows its word: *Ben de geliyorum*.

**Do not use ile to join verbs or adjectives.** *\*Güzel ile ucuz bir ev* and *\*Okudum ile yazdım* misuse *ile*, which joins only bare nouns (goksel2005 §28.3.1.1 (ii)). Use *ve*: *güzel ve ucuz bir ev*, *okudum ve yazdım*.

**Do not use ya da in a which-one question.** *\*Çay mı ya da kahve mi?* mixes two patterns. A choice question with *mI* uses *yoksa*: *Çay mı, yoksa kahve mi?* (tur-02-05).

**Do not use English word order with çünkü.** *\*Çünkü çok işim var, gelemem* puts "because" first, as English can. *Çünkü* introduces the second sentence, after the result: *Gelemem, çünkü çok işim var.* To put the reason first, use *-DIğI için* (tur-06-09).

**Do not add a negative to ne ... ne to make it "neither".** *Ne ... ne* is already negative. *Ne çay ne kahve içer* means "drinks neither". The negative verb is also correct, but do not think of it as required, as Spanish *no bebe ni té ni café* would suggest (goksel2005 §20.4).

**Do not translate "not ... either" with hiç.** *\*Ben hiç bilmiyorum* is "I don't know at all". "I don't know either" is *Ben de bilmiyorum*.

**Do not confuse bile and de.** *Ali de geldi* "Ali came too (as expected)". *Ali bile geldi* "Even Ali came (unexpectedly)". *Bile* adds surprise.

## Connections

Japanese も (mo) and Korean -도 (-do) do exactly what Turkish *de* does: they follow a noun to mean "too", become "either" with a negative, and doubled mean "both ... and" (Japanese AもBも, Korean A도 B도). This is one of the closest parallels between these languages, and still a typological one. French *ni ... ni* and Spanish *ni ... ni* match *ne ... ne*, but require a negative verb.

## Exercises

1. Translate *Ben de çay istiyorum* and *Ben çay da istiyorum*. What is the difference?
2. Say "Mehmet isn't coming either."
3. Build "Both my mother and my father are teachers" in two ways.
4. Translate *Ne parası var ne de zamanı.*
5. A learner writes *\*Bende yorgunum* for "I'm tired too". Correct it and explain.
6. Join with *çünkü*: *Dışarı çıkmıyoruz.* / *Hava çok soğuk.*
7. Say "Would you like apples or pears?" as an offer, and "Do you want apples or pears?" asking which.
8. Translate *Müşteri garsonla kavga etti, üstelik hesabı da ödemedi.*
9. Correct *\*Ali ile güzel ve akıllı* meant as "Ali is handsome and clever".
10. What does *bile* add in *Çocuk bile bunu biliyor*?

## Worked answers

1. "I want tea too" (I, as well as you) and "I want tea too" (tea, as well as something else). *De* follows the word it adds to, and that word carries the stress (tur-05-08).
2. *Mehmet de gelmiyor.* With a negative, *de* is "either".
3. *Hem annem hem de babam öğretmen.* *Annem de babam da öğretmen.* Note *babam da*: the particle harmonises with *a*.
4. "He has neither money nor time." *Ne ... ne* with the positive *var*; the possessives say whose (tur-03-09).
5. *Ben de yorgunum.* *Bende* is the locative "on me, at my place" (tur-03-10). The particle is a separate word.
6. *Dışarı çıkmıyoruz, çünkü hava çok soğuk.* The reason follows (goksel2005 §28.3.6).
7. *Elma ya da armut ister misiniz?* (any of these). *Elma mı istiyorsunuz, yoksa armut mu?* (which one, tur-02-05).
8. "The customer had a row with the waiter, and what's more he didn't pay the bill either." *Garsonla kavga etmek*: "fight with"; *üstelik* adds; *de* after *hesabı* "the bill too".
9. *Ali yakışıklı* ("handsome") *ve akıllı*. *İle* joins only bare nouns, not predicates (goksel2005 §28.3.1.1 (ii)).
10. Surprise: "Even a child knows this." *Bile* marks the least expected member of a group; *de* would be a neutral "too".
