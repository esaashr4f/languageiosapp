## The idea

Every suffix you have learnt so far is **inflectional**: it fits a word into a sentence (case, person, tense) without making it a different word. A **derivational** suffix makes a new word, with its own meaning, which goes into the dictionary: *göz* "eye" → *gözlük* "glasses" (goksel2005 §7.2). In glosses this guide writes derivational suffixes in lower case, by meaning (*salt-with*), and inflectional ones in capitals (tur-00-03).

Derivation comes first. A derivational suffix stands right after the root, before any inflection: *göz-lük-ler-im* "my glasses", never *\*göz-ler-lük*. You can stack derivational suffixes, each making a new word from the last: *iş* "work" → *işsiz* "unemployed" → *işsizlik* "unemployment" (wiktionary).

Four suffixes do most of the work, and you have seen all four in words already: *tuzlu* (tur-01-05), *ücretsiz* (tur-04-11), *çocukluk* (tur-04-08), *balıkçı* (tur-01-06). Göksel and Kerslake call them productive: speakers use them freely to make new words, not only in old ones (goksel2005 §7.2.2.2). Here they are:

- ***-lI*** "having, with": *şeker* "sugar" → *şekerli* "with sugar"; a place → a person from there: *köy* → *köylü* "villager".
- ***-sIz*** "without, lacking": *şeker* → *şekersiz* "sugar-free"; *para* → *parasız* "penniless; free of charge".
- ***-lIK*** makes nouns: the quality (*iyi* → *iyilik* "goodness; a favour"), a thing for a body part (*göz* → *gözlük*), a place or container (*kitap* → *kitaplık* "bookcase"); and adjectives of time and amount: *gün* → *günlük* "daily".
- ***-CI*** "person who deals in, does or likes": *gazete* → *gazeteci* "journalist", *şarkı* → *şarkıcı* "singer".

*-lI* and *-sIz* are exact opposites. Many pairs exist side by side: *tuzlu/tuzsuz*, *şekerli/şekersiz*, *akıllı/akılsız* "clever/foolish".

## How it is built

**Harmony and assimilation.** Each suffix follows the rules of part 1:

1. ***-lI***: four-way harmony: *tuz-lu*, *süt-lü*, *akıl-lı*, *deniz-li*. No buffer, no change after a vowel: *para-lı*.
2. ***-sIz***: four-way harmony: *tuz-suz*, *süt-süz*, *para-sız*, *ses-siz*.
3. ***-lIK***: four-way harmony; the final *k* becomes *ğ* before a vowel (tur-01-06): *göz-lük*, acc. *gözlüğü*; *kitap-lık*, *kitaplığı*.
4. ***-CI***: *c* after a voiced sound, *ç* after a voiceless consonant; four-way harmony: *gazete-ci*, *şarkı-cı*, *yol-cu*, *süt-çü*, *iş-çi*, *balık-çı*.
5. **Stems change as before a vowel only if the suffix begins with a vowel.** None of these does, so the stem keeps its shape: *kitap-çı*, *kitap-lık*, not *\*kitabçı*.

**A chain of thought: "unemployment".**

1. Start from *iş* "work, job".
2. "Without a job": *-sIz*, harmony after *i*: *işsiz* "unemployed".
3. "The state of being unemployed": *-lIK*, harmony after *i*: *işsizlik*.
4. Inflect it like any noun: *işsizliği* (acc.), *işsizlikte*.

**-lI on a phrase.** *-lI* can attach to a whole adjective-noun phrase and make an adjective of it (goksel2005 §15.3.4): *uzun saçlı bir kız* "a long-haired girl", *dört çocuklu bir aile* "a family with four children". If the phrase is a compound with *-(s)I*, the compound marker is dropped: *yatak odası* "bedroom" → *üç yatak odalı bir ev* "a three-bedroomed house" (goksel2005 §7.2.2.2).

**-lIK on a measure.** On a number and a noun it makes an adjective of size, length or price (goksel2005 §15.3.5): *iki kişilik bir oda* "a room for two", *üç saatlik bir film* "a three-hour film", *beş liralık* "worth five lira".

## Where it applies

**-lI**

- having a thing or quality: *şekerli*, *sütlü*, *tuzlu*, *akıllı*, *hızlı*, *güçlü*, *başarılı* (tur-05-01, tur-05-02);
- wearing or carrying: *gözlüklü* "wearing glasses";
- coming from a place: *köylü*, *İstanbullu*, *Ankaralı*; *nereli* "from where" (tur-03-12);
- on a phrase: *uzun saçlı*, *dört çocuklu*, *deniz manzaralı* "with a sea view";
- on numerals, a group: *üçlü* "trio" (goksel2005 §7.2.2.2).

**-sIz**

- lacking: *şekersiz*, *tuzsuz*, *parasız*, *işsiz*, *evsiz* "homeless", *sessiz* (tur-03-11);
- with people or things, "without" as an adverb: *arabasız* "without a car", *sensiz* "without you" (goksel2005 §7.2.2.2);
- a character lacking a virtue: *saygısız* "disrespectful".

**-lIK**

- a quality or state: *iyilik*, *güzellik* "beauty", *hastalık* "illness", *tembellik* "laziness", *arkadaşlık* and *dostluk* "friendship", *sessizlik* "silence", *çocukluk* "childhood";
- a thing for a body part: *gözlük*;
- a container or place: *kitaplık*, *tuzluk* "salt cellar";
- an adjective of time: *günlük*, *yıllık* "annual"; *aylık* is also a noun, "monthly salary" (wiktionary);
- measure, duration, price: *iki kişilik*, *üç saatlik*, *beş liralık*;
- an intended stay: *birkaç günlüğüne* "for a few days" (goksel2005 §16.4.1.2 (iv)).

**-CI**

- profession: *gazeteci*, *şarkıcı*, *futbolcu*, *işçi* "worker", *kitapçı*, *balıkçı*, *kahveci* "coffee-house keeper";
- a habit or character: *yalancı* "liar" (from *yalan* "lie", tur-03-10);
- an activity: *yolcu* "traveller" (tur-04-08).

## Watch out

**Do not put derivation after inflection.** *\*Kitaplarlık* and *\*gözlerlük* put the plural inside. Derivation first: *kitaplıklar* "bookcases", *gözlükler*.

**Do not use an apostrophe before a derivational suffix.** *\*İstanbul'lu* copies the case pattern *İstanbul'da*. The apostrophe marks only inflection on names: *İstanbullu*, *Ankaralı*, *Koreli* (dossier, from TDK).

**Do not translate "with" by -lI when you mean "together with".** *\*Annemli geldim* is not "I came with my mother". That is *annemle* (tur-03-13). *-lI* describes a quality or possession: *çocuklu bir kadın* "a woman with a child".

**Do not translate "without" by -sIz on a pronoun in the genitive.** *\*Benimsiz* is wrong; *-sIz* goes on the bare pronoun: *bensiz* "without me", *sensiz*.

**Do not use -CI for every profession.** Many are separate words: *doktor*, *öğretmen*, *avukat*, *mühendis*. And *dişçi* "dentist" exists but is marked "proscribed" in Wiktionary. Use a *-CI* word only when you have met it.

**Do not assume -lIK is always a noun.** *Günlük gazete* "daily newspaper" and *iki kişilik oda* use it as an adjective. Its position tells you which.

**Do not forget that -lIK ends in k.** Before a vowel it becomes *ğ*: *gözlüğüm*, *kitaplığa*, *işsizliği*. Do not write *\*gözlükum* or *\*kitaplıka*.

**Do not confuse -lI with the -CA of language names.** *İngilizce* is "English (language)", *İngiliz* "English person"; there is no *\*İngilizli*. But *Koreli* "Korean person" uses *-lI* because *Kore* is a place (tur-02-04).

## Connections

Japanese and Korean have nothing as productive as *-lI* and *-sIz*; they use phrases (砂糖入り, 설탕 없는) where Turkish uses one suffix. Spanish *-ero* and French *-ier* make trades like *-CI* (*zapatero*, *pâtissier*), and *-dad*, *-té* make qualities like *-lIK* (*bondad*, *bonté* beside *iyilik*). The parallel is in function only.

## Exercises

1. Make adjectives meaning "salty", "without salt", "with milk" and "without milk". Explain each vowel.
2. Translate *Kahveniz şekerli mi?*
3. Build "a five-hour journey" from *beş*, *saat* and *yolculuk* "journey".
4. A learner writes *\*Ben İzmir'liyim.* Correct it.
5. Take apart *gözlükçü* "optician" and say what each suffix adds.
6. What do *arkadaş* and *arkadaşlık* mean? Use each in a sentence.
7. Translate *Bu ülkede işsizlik bir sorun, ama hükümet bir şey yapmıyor.* (*hükümet* "government")
8. Correct *\*kitaplarlık*.
9. Translate *Sensiz hayat çok sıkıcı.*

## Worked answers

1. *Tuzlu*, *tuzsuz*: back rounded *u* gives *u*. *Sütlü*, *sütsüz*: front rounded *ü* gives *ü*. Four-way harmony in both suffixes (tur-01-05).
2. "Is your coffee with sugar?" or "Do you take sugar in your coffee?" *Kahve-niz*: 2PL possessive; *şeker-li*: "with sugar"; *mi* after the predicate.
3. *Beş saatlik bir yolculuk.* *-lIK* on the measure phrase *beş saat* makes an adjective (goksel2005 §15.3.5); *bir* follows it.
4. *Ben İzmirliyim.* No apostrophe before a derivational suffix (dossier; TDK). Then the z-set ending *-(y)Im*.
5. *Göz-lük-çü*: "eye", "thing for (the eyes)" = glasses, "person who deals in" = glasses-seller, optician. Each suffix builds on the word before.
6. *Arkadaş* "friend", *arkadaşlık* "friendship". *Ali benim arkadaşım.* *Arkadaşlık çok önemli.*
7. "In this country unemployment is a problem, but the government does nothing." *İş-siz-lik*: two derivations; *ama* (tur-05-08).
8. *Kitaplıklar* "bookcases". The derivational *-lIK* comes before the plural.
9. "Life without you is very boring." *Sen-siz*: *-sIz* on the bare pronoun; *sensiz hayat* "you-less life".
