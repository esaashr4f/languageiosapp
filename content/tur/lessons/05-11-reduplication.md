## The idea

**Reduplication** is repeating a word, or part of it, to change its meaning. Turkish has three kinds, each with its own job (goksel2005 ch. 9):

- **Doubling**: say the word twice: *yavaş yavaş* "slowly, little by little". It makes adverbs of manner, and with nouns and adjectives it suggests many or many kinds (goksel2005 §9.3).
- **Emphatic reduplication**: copy the first syllable, close it with *p*, *s*, *m* or *r*, and put it in front: *mavi* "blue" → *masmavi* "deep blue", *yeni* → *yepyeni* "brand new" (goksel2005 §9.1). This is the one place where Turkish adds something at the front of a word. It intensifies an adjective.
- **m-reduplication**: repeat the word with its first consonant replaced by *m*, or with *m* added before a first vowel: *kitap mitap* "books and things like that", *çay may* "tea and so on" (goksel2005 §9.2). It means "X and that sort of thing", often a little dismissive. It is colloquial.

All three are common in speech. They let one word carry what English says with "very", "and so on", "little by little" or "all sorts of".

## How it is built

**Doubling.**

1. **Take an adverb, adjective, noun or distributive**: *yavaş*.
2. **Say it twice, as two words**: *yavaş yavaş*.
3. **Use it before the verb** for manner: *Yavaş yavaş öğreniyorum* "I'm learning little by little". Or before a noun for many or many kinds: *çeşit çeşit yemek* "all kinds of dishes" (goksel2005 §9.3).

**Emphatic reduplication.**

1. **Take the adjective**: *beyaz*.
2. **Copy up to the first vowel**: *be-*.
3. **Add a closing consonant**, one of *p*, *s*, *m*, *r*: *bem-*. The consonant cannot be the same as the word's own first or second consonant (goksel2005 §9.1), but which one a word takes is not otherwise predictable. Learn each form.
4. **Join it to the word**: *bembeyaz* "snow-white".
5. **If the word begins with a vowel**, the prefix is that vowel plus *p*: *ince* → *ipince* "very thin", *açık* → *apaçık* "perfectly clear".
6. **Stress the first syllable**: *bémbeyaz*, *másmavi* (goksel2005 §9.1).

Only a limited set of adjectives have these forms; Göksel and Kerslake list them in an appendix (goksel2005 Appendix 1), and Wiktionary gives each adjective's "intensive" in its headword line. Do not invent new ones.

**m-reduplication.**

1. **Take the word**: *kitap*.
2. **Replace the first consonant with m**: *mitap*. If the word begins with a vowel, put *m* in front: *ev* → *mev*.
3. **Say the two together**: *kitap mitap*. Suffixes go on both: *kitapları mitapları*.

## Where it applies

**Doubling**

- manner, with adverbs and adjectives: *yavaş yavaş*, *çabuk çabuk* "quickly", *rahat rahat* "comfortably, easily", *usul usul* "slowly and softly" (goksel2005 §9.3 (i));
- *kolay kolay* "easily", only in negative sentences: *Bunu kolay kolay bulamazsın* "You won't find this easily" (goksel2005 §16.1.3);
- manner with nouns: *kapı kapı* "from door to door", *damla damla* "drop by drop";
- time words with disapproval: *sabah sabah* "at this hour of the morning!", *akşam akşam* "so late in the evening" (goksel2005 §16.1.3);
- distributives: *birer birer*, *ikişer ikişer* (tur-05-03);
- before nouns, many or many kinds: *çeşit çeşit*, *boy boy* "of all sizes", *koca koca ağaçlar*;
- set expressions: *sık sık* "often" (tur-04-05);
- in proverbs, doubled verb forms: *damlaya damlaya göl olur* "drop by drop a lake forms" (wiktionary, proverb; the form *damlaya* is a converb, part 6).

**Emphatic reduplication**

- colours: *bembeyaz*, *simsiyah*, *kapkara*, *kıpkırmızı*, *sapsarı*, *yemyeşil*, *masmavi*;
- size and shape: *upuzun*, *ipince*, *dopdolu* "chock-full", *bomboş*;
- state: *tertemiz*, *yepyeni*, *sımsıcak* "piping hot", *kapkaranlık* "pitch dark", *bambaşka* "completely different", *apaçık*.

**m-reduplication**

- any noun or phrase, in speech, for "and so on", often a little dismissive: *kitap mitap*, *çay may*, *kapı mapı* (goksel2005 §9.2).

**mI between copies**: *akıllı mı akıllı*; occasionally as an adverb, *yavaş mı yavaş* (goksel2005 §9.3 (iv)).

## Watch out

**Do not use çok or en with an emphatic form.** *\*Çok masmavi* and *\*en tertemiz* double the intensity. Göksel and Kerslake say these forms cannot take such modifiers (goksel2005 §9.1): *masmavi*, or *çok mavi*.

**Do not invent emphatic forms.** *\*Gepgüzel* or *\*pappahalı* follow the pattern but do not exist (G&K's form for *güzel* is *güpgüzel*). Only the listed adjectives have them, each with its own consonant.

**Do not choose the closing consonant by analogy.** *Masmavi* has *s*, *bembeyaz* *m*, *sapsarı* *p*, *tertemiz* *r*. *\*Mapmavi* or *\*besbeyaz* are wrong. Learn each one.

**Do not write the emphatic form as two words.** *\*Mas mavi* is wrong; it is one word, *masmavi*. Doubling, by contrast, is two words: *yavaş yavaş*, not *\*yavaşyavaş*.

**Do not use m-reduplication in writing or formal speech.** *Kitap mitap* is colloquial (goksel2005 §9.2). In careful style, name the things: *kitaplar ve defterler*.

**Prefer the singular after a doubled noun.** *Çeşit çeşit yemekler* is possible, but Göksel and Kerslake note the modified noun is usually left without the plural: *çeşit çeşit yemek* (goksel2005 §9.3 (iii)).

**Do not use kolay kolay in a positive sentence.** *\*Kolay kolay buldum* is wrong for "I found it easily". Positive: *kolayca buldum* (tur-05-01). *Kolay kolay* needs a negative: *kolay kolay bulamazsın* "you won't find it easily".

**Do not think yavaş yavaş means only "slowly".** It also means "gradually, little by little": *Hava yavaş yavaş ısınıyor* "The weather is gradually warming up".

## Connections

Japanese and Korean double words in much the same way: Japanese 時々 (tokidoki, "time time": sometimes) and 色々 (iroiro, "colour colour": all kinds of) work like *sık sık* and *çeşit çeşit*, and Korean 천천히 (cheoncheonhi) "slowly" contains a doubled root. English "books-shmooks" resembles *kitap mitap* in form and tone. These are parallels of type, not of descent.

## Exercises

1. Give the emphatic forms of *beyaz*, *temiz* and *boş*, and translate *bembeyaz bir gömlek*.
2. Translate *Kar yavaş yavaş eriyor.* (*erimek* "melt")
3. A learner writes *\*Gömleğin çok tertemiz.* Correct it.
4. Say "From door to door" and "Drop by drop".
5. Translate *Sabah sabah ne yapıyorsun?* What does the doubling add?
6. Make m-reduplications of *kahve*, *ekmek* and *ödev*, and say when you would use them.
7. Translate *Bu soruyu kolay kolay çözemezsin.* (*çözmek* "solve")
8. Why is *\*gepgüzel* wrong, and what does G&K give instead?
9. Translate *Çarşıda çeşit çeşit meyve var: elma, armut, portakal.* (*çarşı* "market, shopping street")

## Worked answers

1. *Bembeyaz*, *tertemiz*, *bomboş* (wiktionary). *Bembeyaz bir gömlek* "a snow-white shirt": the emphatic adjective stands before *bir* like any adjective (tur-05-01).
2. "The snow is melting little by little." *Yavaş yavaş*: doubled adjective, manner and gradualness (goksel2005 §9.3).
3. *Gömleğin tertemiz*, or *Gömleğin çok temiz*. An emphatic form takes no degree word (goksel2005 §9.1).
4. *Kapı kapı*. *Damla damla*. Doubled nouns as adverbs of manner.
5. "What are you doing at this hour of the morning?" *Sabah sabah* adds disapproval: it is too early (goksel2005 §16.1.3).
6. *Kahve mahve*, *ekmek mekmek*, *ödev mödev*: the first consonant replaced by *m*, or *m* added before a vowel. Use them in casual speech for "coffee and such", "bread and stuff", "homework and all that" (goksel2005 §9.2).
7. "You won't solve this question easily." *Kolay kolay* with a negative verb, *çöz-eme-z-sin*, the impotential aorist (tur-04-13).
8. Emphatic forms are not free: each listed adjective has its own. G&K give *güpgüzel* (goksel2005 §9.1).
9. "In the market there are all kinds of fruit: apples, pears, oranges." *Çeşit çeşit meyve*: a doubled noun, with the described noun singular.
