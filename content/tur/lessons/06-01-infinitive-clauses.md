## The idea

English links one verb to another with small words: "I want *to* go", "I am afraid *of* swimming", "I decided *that* we would leave". Spanish and French do the same with *que*, *de*, *a*. Turkish has almost none of these. It has a different trick: it turns the second verb into a noun, and then treats that noun like any other noun.

A noun can be the subject of a sentence. It can be an object. It can take a case ending: accusative, dative, locative, ablative. Everything you learned in part 3 about nouns therefore applies to these verb-nouns. A whole clause becomes one big noun, and the verb at its end carries the case. Turkish grammarians call this **nominalisation**: making a clause into a noun. A **nominalised clause** is a clause whose verb has been turned into a noun in this way. Part 6 is about the five suffixes that do it and the meanings each one carries (goksel2005 ch. 24).

The first is the one you have used since part 1 as the dictionary form: ***-mAk***. You know *gelmek* "to come" and *okumak* "to read". The **infinitive** is the form of the verb that names the action without saying who does it or when. That is exactly what *-mAk* does. *Gelmek* has no person and no tense. It is "coming" or "to come" in general.

This lack of a subject is the key to *-mAk*. A *-mAk* clause never has its own subject. Whoever does the action is understood from the main clause: in *Yüzmek istiyorum* "I want to swim", the person swimming is the same person who wants, "I", shown by *-um* on *istiyorum* (goksel2005 §24.4.1). When the two people differ ("I want *you* to swim"), *-mAk* cannot be used; you need *-mA* with a possessive ending, which is the next lesson (tur-06-02).

## How it is built

**Step 1: the verb inside.** Build the inner clause as if it were a sentence, but stop before tense and person. Objects, adverbs and the negative stay inside: *her gün kitap oku-* "read books every day", *geç kal-ma-* "not be late".

**Step 2: add -mAk.** Two-way harmony: *oku-mak*, *gel-mek*, *kal-ma-mak* "not to stay". The negative *-mA* comes first, in its usual slot right after the stem.

**Step 3: ask what job the clause does in the main sentence.** This is the whole point. Treat the clause as a noun and ask what case that noun would need.

- Subject of an adjective or of *lazım*, *gerek*: no case. *Erken kalkmak zor.* "Getting up early is hard."
- Object of *istemek* "want": no case. *istemek* is the one verb that regularly takes a bare *-mAk* clause as its object (goksel2005 §24.4.1.1).
- Object of any other transitive verb (*sevmek*, *bilmek*, *öğrenmek*, *denemek*, *unutmak*): accusative.
- Object of a verb that takes the dative (*başlamak*, *karar vermek*, *alışmak*, and *çalışmak* in the sense "try"): dative.
- With *ısrar etmek* "insist" and with *fayda var* "it is worth it": locative.
- With a verb that takes the ablative (*korkmak*, *vazgeçmek*, *hoşlanmak*, *bıkmak*, *kaçınmak*, *nefret etmek*): ablative.

**Step 4: attach the case.** Before a vowel-initial case ending, the *k* of *-mAk* is spelt and said as *y*: *yüz-mek* + accusative is *yüzmeyi*, *başla-mak* + dative is *başlamaya*. The older spelling was with *ğ* (*yüzmeği*, *başlamağa*), the expected result of *k* between vowels (tur-01-06); today *y* is the usual spelling and pronunciation (goksel2005 §8.5.1.2). Before a consonant-initial case nothing changes: *yüzmekte*, *yüzmekten*.

So a full build of "I am trying not to be late": *geç kal-* → negative *geç kal-ma-* → *-mAk*: *geç kalmamak* → *çalışmak* "try" wants the dative: *geç kalmamaya* → main verb: *Geç kalmamaya çalışıyorum.*

## Where it applies

- **As the object of istemek, with no case**: *Gitmek istiyorum* "I want to go". The formal accusative *gitmeyi istiyorum* exists but is much less common (goksel2005 §24.4.1.2).
- **As the subject of an adjective**: *zor*, *kolay*, *güzel*, *imkânsız*, *gereksiz*, *faydalı*, *zararlı*, *keyifli*, *önemli*: *Bu yolda araba sürmek tehlikeli* "Driving on this road is dangerous".
- **Impersonal necessity**: *-mAk lazım*, *-mAk gerek*, and the verb *gerekiyor*: *Burada bilet almak lazım* "You have to buy a ticket here", meaning anyone (goksel2005 §21.4.2.2). *-mAlI* (tur-04-12) is the speaker's own sense of duty; the *-mAk lazım* type is an outside rule.
- **Personal obligation, outside**: *-mAk zorunda* plus a person ending, or *-mAyA mecbur*: *Gitmeye mecburum* "I am obliged to go". For "I had to (and did)", use *zorunda kaldım* (goksel2005 §21.4.2.2).
- **As an accusative object**: *sevmek*, *bilmek* "know how to", *öğrenmek*, *denemek*, *unutmak*, *düşünmek* "think of (doing)", *beklemek* "expect".
- **As a dative object**: *başlamak*, *karar vermek*, *alışmak* "get used to", *çalışmak* "try", *devam etmek* "keep on", and *mecbur*.
- **As a locative object**: *ısrar etmek* "insist on"; *fayda var / yok*; also "be late in doing": *cevap vermekte geç kaldı* "he was slow to answer" (goksel2005 §8.5.1.2).
- **As an ablative object**: *korkmak*, *vazgeçmek*, *hoşlanmak*, *bıkmak*, *kaçınmak* "avoid", *çekinmek* "hesitate, shy away from", *utanmak* "be embarrassed to", *nefret etmek* "hate".
- **As the complement of a possessed noun**: *Amacım her gün bir sayfa yazmak* "My aim is to write one page a day": the possessive on *amaç* tells you who writes (goksel2005 §24.4.1.1).
- **Before postpositions**: *-mAk yerine* "instead of", *-mAk için* "in order to" (tur-06-09).
- **Talking about a verb as a word**: *"Vazgeçmek" ne demek?* "What does *vazgeçmek* mean?" This is the citation use you know.

## Watch out

**A different subject needs -mA, not -mAk.** "I want you to go" is not *\*Seni gitmek istiyorum*. *-mAk* clauses cannot have a subject of their own, and *seni* would be read as the object of *istiyorum*. The right form, *Gitmeni istiyorum*, is tur-06-02.

**No possessive on -mAk.** *\*Gitmekim lazım* is impossible: *-mAk* never takes a possessive ending (goksel2005 §8.5.1.2). "I have to go" is *Gitmem lazım* (tur-06-02) or *Gitmek zorundayım*.

**Only istemek takes the bare form as object.** By analogy with *istemek*, learners write *\*Yüzmek seviyorum*. *sevmek* needs the accusative: *Yüzmeyi seviyorum*.

**Take the case from the main verb, not from English.** English says "afraid *of* swimming", "start *-ing*", "used *to* getting up". Turkish asks what the Turkish verb takes. *\*Yüzmeyi korkuyorum* is wrong: *korkmak* takes the ablative, *Yüzmekten korkuyorum*. *\*Kalkmayı alıştım* is wrong: *alışmak* takes the dative, *Kalkmaya alıştım*.

**Do not keep the k before a vowel.** *\*Okumakı*, *\*okumaka* do not exist. The forms are *okumayı*, *okumaya*. The spelling with *ğ*, *okumağa*, is old-fashioned (goksel2005 §8.5.1.2).

**No tense inside the clause.** *-mAk* has no tense, so "I decided to go" is *Gitmeye karar verdim*, with the past on the main verb only. Spanish *decidí ir* works the same way, so this one comes naturally.

**The objects inside keep their own cases.** The inner clause is still a verb phrase. A specific object takes the accusative inside it: *Bu kitabı okumak istiyorum* "I want to read this book". Do not put it in the genitive by analogy with "the reading *of* this book": *\*Bu kitabın okumak* is wrong.

**çalışmak "try" is not çalışmak "work".** With a dative *-mAk* clause it means "try": *Uyumaya çalıştım* "I tried to sleep". *\*Uyumayı çalıştım* is wrong.

**Do not use -mAk after için to say "because".** *-mAk için* is "in order to" only; "because" uses another suffix (tur-06-09).

## Connections

Japanese and Korean also turn a clause into a noun and then add a particle: Japanese 泳ぐのが好きだ, Korean 수영하는 것을 좋아해요, where the clause takes が or 을 as *yüzmeyi* takes *-yI*. Turkish puts the case on the verb itself. The similarity is in type only (dossier, section 6). Spanish and French infinitives (*nadar*, *nager*) are the closest European match: they too have no subject of their own, and like *-mAk* they need a different construction when the subject changes (*quiero que nades*, *je veux que tu nages*).

## Exercises

1. Say "I want to sleep" and "I love sleeping". Why do the two infinitives differ?
2. Build "We have started to learn Turkish", step by step.
3. Take apart *vazgeçmekten* and *alışmaya*, and say what each ending does.
4. Translate *Bu şehirde araba sürmek imkânsız.*
5. A learner writes *\*Denize girmeyi korkuyorum.* Correct it and explain.
6. Translate *Amacım her gün yeni bir kelime öğrenmek.* Who learns the word, and how do you know?
7. Say "Instead of taking a taxi, we walked." (*taksiye binmek*, *yürümek*)
8. Translate *Kimseye yalan söylememeye çalışıyorum.*
9. Why is *\*Seni gelmek istiyorum* wrong for "I want you to come"?

## Worked answers

1. *Uyumak istiyorum*; *Uyumayı seviyorum*. *istemek* is the one verb that takes a bare *-mAk* object; *sevmek* takes the accusative, so the clause ends in *-mAyI* (goksel2005 §24.4.1.1–2).
2. Inner clause: *Türkçe öğren-*. Add *-mAk*: *Türkçe öğrenmek*. *başlamak* takes the dative; *k* becomes *y* before the vowel: *Türkçe öğrenmeye*. Main verb in the past, 1PL: *Türkçe öğrenmeye başladık.*
3. *Vazgeç-mek-ten*: verb, infinitive, ablative, because *vazgeçmek* takes the ablative. *Alış-ma-ya*: verb, infinitive (its *k* written *y*), dative, because *alışmak* takes the dative.
4. "Driving in this city is impossible." The clause *bu şehirde araba sürmek* is the subject of *imkânsız*; no person is meant.
5. *Denize girmekten korkuyorum.* "I'm afraid of going into the sea." *korkmak* takes the ablative, so the *-mAk* clause does too; the English "of" does not decide it.
6. "My aim is to learn a new word every day." The speaker learns it: *amacım* has the 1SG possessive, and the subject of the *-mAk* clause is understood from it (goksel2005 §24.4.1.1).
7. *Taksiye binmek yerine yürüdük.* *yerine* follows a bare *-mAk* clause; past and person go on the main verb only.
8. "I try not to lie to anyone." *Söyle-me-me-ye*: negative, infinitive, dative after *çalışmak* "try". *Kimse* with a negative means "anyone" (tur-02-09).
9. *-mAk* clauses have no subject of their own. *Seni* would be the object of *istiyorum* ("I want you"), and *gelmek* would mean that I come. A different subject needs *-mA* with a possessive: *Gelmeni istiyorum* (tur-06-02).
