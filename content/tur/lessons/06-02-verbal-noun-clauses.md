## The idea

Tur-06-01 left a gap. *-mAk* clauses have no subject of their own, so they cannot say "I want *you* to go". Turkish fills the gap with a second verb-noun, ***-mA***, which can carry a possessive ending. The possessive names the subject.

You already know how Turkish says "Ali's house": *Ali'nin evi*, a genitive on the owner and a possessive on the thing owned (tur-03-04, tur-03-05). Turkish says "Ali's going" in exactly the same way: *Ali'nin gitmesi*. The doer of the action is treated as its owner. "You want Ali to go" is then "you want Ali's going": *Ali'nin gitmesini istiyorsun*. This is why the possessive is not decoration. It is the only place the subject of the clause appears.

A **verbal noun** is a noun made from a verb that keeps the verb's objects and adverbs. *-mA* is this guide's verbal noun, glossed VN. You met it frozen in words like *dondurma* "ice cream" (tur-04-15) and *okuma* "reading" (tur-05-10), and as a preview in *kalkmam lazım* (tur-04-12). Here it is productive: any verb can take it.

There is a second difference from *-mAk*, of meaning. *-mA* clauses are about actions as things wanted, asked for, allowed, caused, liked or judged: "I want you to come", "he told us to wait", "your coming matters". They are not used to report facts that someone knows or says ("I know that you came"); those take *-DIK*, the next lesson (goksel2005 §24.4.2.2).

## How it is built

**Step 1: the inner clause, with its own subject.** "Ali comes early": *Ali erken gel-*.

**Step 2: the subject goes into the genitive.** *Ali'nin*. A pronoun subject is usually dropped, as possessors are (tur-03-04), because the possessive ending already says who: *(senin) gelmen* "your coming".

**Step 3: add -mA to the verb.** Two-way harmony: *gel-me*, *oku-ma*. A negative comes first: *gel-me-me* "not coming".

**Step 4: add the possessive for the subject.** *-mA* ends in a vowel, so the possessives are the vowel-final set from tur-03-04: *gelmem*, *gelmen*, *gelmesi*, *gelmemiz*, *gelmeniz*, *gelmeleri*.

**Step 5: add the case the main verb needs**, exactly as in tur-06-01. After a third-person possessive the pronominal *n* comes in (tur-03-06): *gelmesini*, *gelmesine*, *gelmesinden*. And one rule from part 3 matters here: a possessed noun that is a direct object always takes the accusative (tur-03-01). So the object of *istemek* is now *gelmeni*, never bare *gelmen* (goksel2005 §24.4.2.2).

Full build of "I want you to come early": *erken gel-* → *-mA*: *erken gelme* → "you": *erken gelmen* → object of *istemek*, possessed, so accusative: *erken gelmeni* → *Erken gelmeni istiyorum.*

**Who is asked or told.** With *istemek* "ask" and *rica etmek* "request", the person asked is in the ablative; with *söylemek* "tell", in the dative (goksel2005 §24.4.2.2): *Senden gelmeni istiyorum* "I'm asking you to come"; *Ali'ye gelmesini söyledim* "I told Ali to come".

**Reading it aloud.** *Gitmem* "my going" and *gitmem* "I don't go" (negative aorist, tur-04-06) are spelt alike. Stress tells them apart: the negative *-mA* is unstressable, so it is *gítmem* "I don't go"; the verbal noun takes the stress forward to the possessive, *gitmém* "my going" (goksel2005 §4.3.2).

## Where it applies

- **Wanting someone else to act**: *istemek*, *beklemek* "expect", *dilemek* "wish": *Erken gelmeni bekliyorum* "I expect you to come early" (goksel2005 §24.4.2.2).
- **Telling, asking, advising**: *söylemek* (person told in the dative), *istemek* and *rica etmek* (person asked in the ablative), *emretmek* "order", *tavsiye etmek*, *önermek* "suggest". This is how Turkish reports commands: there is no separate "tell someone to" construction.
- **Personal necessity**: *-mA* + possessive + *lazım*, *gerek* or *gerekiyor*: *Gitmemiz gerekiyor* "We have to go". This states an outside need, unlike *-mAlI* (tur-04-12; goksel2005 §21.4.2.2).
- **Allowing, helping, causing**: dative objects of *izin vermek*, *yardım etmek*, *neden olmak* "cause": *Bu karar herkesin üzülmesine neden oldu* "This decision made everyone sad".
- **Making happen or stopping**: accusative objects of *sağlamak* "ensure", *engellemek* "prevent".
- **Feelings about someone's action**: dative with *şaşırmak*, *sevinmek*, *kızmak*, *üzülmek*; ablative with *sıkılmak* "be bored, fed up", *korkmak*, *hoşlanmak*; accusative with *beğenmek*, *affetmek*: *Gelmenize çok sevindim* "I'm so glad you came".
- **Judging an action**, as subject of an adjective: *Onun bunu bilmemesi imkânsız* "It's impossible that he doesn't know this".
- **"The point, the harm of doing"**: *-mAnIn* + a possessed noun such as *faydası*, *zararı*, *anlamı*, with no subject (goksel2005 §24.4.2.1).
- **Before postpositions** (later lessons): *-mAsI için* "so that someone" (tur-06-09); *-mAsIndAn önce / sonra* (tur-06-07); *-mAsInA rağmen* "although" (tur-06-14).
- **A non-specific subject has no genitive**: *Kar yağması güzel* "It's lovely when it snows", where *kar* means snow in general. Add the genitive and the subject becomes definite, "the snow" (goksel2005 §24.4.6).

## Watch out

**Same subject: use -mAk, not -mA.** *\*Gitmemi istiyorum* for "I want to go" is wrong: the subject of a *-mA* clause cannot be the same as the subject of the main clause (goksel2005 §24.4.1). Say *Gitmek istiyorum*. With *lazım* and *gerek* there is no main subject, so *gitmem lazım* is fine.

**Do not forget the accusative.** By analogy with *Gitmek istiyorum*, learners write *\*Gitmen istiyorum*. A possessed noun as direct object must take the accusative: *Gitmeni istiyorum*.

**The subject goes in the genitive, not the nominative or accusative.** English "I want *him* to go" and French *je veux qu'il parte* tempt you to treat "him" as an object: *\*Onu gitmesini istiyorum*. The subject is the owner of the action: *Onun gitmesini istiyorum*, or just *Gitmesini istiyorum*. A named, specific subject cannot drop the genitive: *\*Ali gelmesini istiyorum* is wrong; *Ali'nin gelmesini istiyorum*.

**Do not use -mA for facts.** "I know that you came" is not *\*Gelmeni biliyorum*. Knowing, saying, seeing and remembering a fact take *-DIK* (tur-06-03): *Geldiğini biliyorum*.

**There is no tense on -mA.** *\*Geldimesini istiyorum* is impossible. The time comes from the main verb and from sense.

**Do not look for a subjunctive.** Spanish *quiero que vengas* and French *je veux que tu viennes* have a full verb with its own person. Turkish puts no tense or mood on the inner verb: the person is the possessive, *Gelmeni istiyorum*. Do not try *\*Gelirsini istiyorum* or similar.

**Check the case of the person told or asked.** *söylemek*: dative (*Ali'ye söyledim*). *istemek*, *rica etmek*: ablative (*Ali'den istedim*). *\*Ali'yi gelmesini istedim* is wrong.

**Do not confuse the verbal noun with the negative imperative.** *Gitme!* "Don't go!" (tur-04-02) and *gitme* "going" are spelt alike. The verbal noun almost never stands bare; it has a possessive or a case: *gitmen*, *gitmeye*. Stress also differs: *gítme!* against *gitmé*.

## Connections

Korean and Japanese also nominalise a clause with its own subject: Japanese 君が来るのを待っている, Korean 네가 오기를 바란다 "I hope you come". Korean -기 is close to *-mA* in use, the form for wishes and requests, while Korean -는 것 and Japanese の also cover facts, which Turkish gives to *-DIK*. The parallel is typological only (dossier, section 6). Spanish and French use a subjunctive clause (*quiero que vengas*); Turkish has no subjunctive verb at all here.

## Exercises

1. Say "I want to wait" and "I want you to wait". Explain the difference in form.
2. Build "We want Ayşe to sing a song" step by step.
3. Take apart *toplamalarını* and say what each piece does.
4. A learner writes *\*Onu gelmesini istedim.* Correct it.
5. Translate *Öğretmen bizden her gün kitap okumamızı istiyor.*
6. Say "I have to call my mother" with *lazım*, and "One has to call ahead" (*önceden telefon etmek*) with *lazım*. Why do they differ?
7. Translate *Annem çocukların geç yatmasına izin vermez.*
8. Translate *Gelmenize çok sevindim.* Which word gives the reason for the joy, and in what case?
9. Why is *\*Geldiğimi istiyorum* wrong for "I want to come"? Give the right form.

## Worked answers

1. *Beklemek istiyorum*; *Beklemeni istiyorum*. Same subject: *-mAk*, no possessive, no case after *istemek* (tur-06-01). Different subject: *-mA* + 2SG possessive + accusative, because a possessed object takes the accusative.
2. Inner clause: *Ayşe şarkı söyle-*. Subject in the genitive: *Ayşe'nin*. Verbal noun: *şarkı söyleme*. 3SG possessive: *söylemesi*. Object of *istemek*: accusative with pronominal n: *söylemesini*. *Ayşe'nin şarkı söylemesini istiyoruz.*
3. *Topla-ma-ları-nı*: verb "tidy", verbal noun, 3PL possessive (their tidying), pronominal n, accusative. It is the object of a verb such as *istemek* or *söylemek*.
4. *Onun gelmesini istedim* or *Gelmesini istedim.* The subject of a *-mA* clause is in the genitive, like a possessor; the accusative *onu* would make "him" the object of *istedim*.
5. "The teacher wants us to read a book every day." The person asked is in the ablative (*bizden*); the 1PL possessive on *okumamız* repeats who reads.
6. *Annemi aramam lazım*; *Önceden telefon etmek lazım*. The first is personal: the possessive on *-mA* names me. The second is general: *-mAk* has no subject, so it means "one" (tur-06-01).
7. "My mother doesn't let the children go to bed late." *izin vermek* takes the dative; the subject *çocukların* is genitive; the aorist states a general rule (tur-04-05).
8. "I'm very glad you came." *Gelmenize*: verbal noun, 2PL possessive (polite *siz*), dative, which *sevinmek* takes for its cause.
9. *-DIK* is for facts, and the subject is the same as the main subject. "I want to come" is *Gelmek istiyorum* (tur-06-01).
