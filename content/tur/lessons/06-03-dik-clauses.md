## The idea

Tur-06-02 used *-mA* for actions that are wanted, asked for, allowed or judged. None of those is a claim about what is true. "I want you to come" does not say that you come. But "I know that you came", "he said that he was ill", "I don't know when the film starts" are about facts: whether something is so, and what, who, when or where.

Turkish has a separate pair of verb-nouns for facts: ***-DIK*** and ***-(y)AcAK***. Like *-mA*, they take a possessive ending for the subject and a case ending for their job in the sentence. Unlike *-mA*, they carry tense, because a fact has a time (goksel2005 §24.4.3).

- ***-DIK*** is for something that happens at the same time as the main verb, or before it. *Geldiğini biliyorum* "I know that you came" or "I know that you are coming (now)".
- ***-(y)AcAK*** is for something later than the main verb. *Geleceğini biliyorum* "I know that you will come". You know it as the future tense (tur-04-04); here it is the same suffix used as a noun.

Notice that the tense is **relative**: it is measured from the main verb, not from now. *Hasta olduğunu söyledi* is "He said that he was ill", and *-DIK* means "at the time he said it". English shifts "is" to "was"; Turkish does not need to.

This guide calls *-DIK* the **factive nominaliser** and glosses it NMLZ: "factive" because it presents its clause as a fact. Whether *-DIK* is best called a participle or a nominaliser is debated (dossier, section 6); the forms are not in doubt. *-(y)AcAK* keeps its gloss FUT; the possessive after it shows the noun use.

## How it is built

**Step 1: the inner sentence.** "Ali moved to Ankara": *Ali Ankara'ya taşındı*.

**Step 2: subject into the genitive**, as with *-mA*: *Ali'nin*.

**Step 3: choose -DIK or -(y)AcAK by time.** The moving happened before the knowing: *-DIK*. Remove the tense and person and add *-DIK* to the stem: *taşın-dık*. Four-way harmony and *d/t* after voiceless consonants, eight forms: *-dık, -dik, -duk, -dük, -tık, -tik, -tuk, -tük*.

**Step 4: add the possessive.** Before the vowel of the possessive, final *k* becomes *ğ* (tur-01-06): *taşındığ-ı*. The full set, from *gelmek*: *geldiğim, geldiğin, geldiği, geldiğimiz, geldiğiniz, geldikleri* (wiktionary). For the future: *geleceğim, geleceğin, geleceği, geleceğimiz, geleceğiniz, gelecekleri*. In the third person plural *-lArI* begins with a consonant, so the *k* stays: *geldikleri*, *gelecekleri*.

**Step 5: add the case from the main verb.** *bilmek* takes an accusative object, and after *-(s)I* comes pronominal *n* (tur-03-06): *taşındığını*. So: *Ali'nin Ankara'ya taşındığını biliyorum.*

**Nouns and adjectives as predicates.** A clause like "Ayşe is pregnant" has no verb to take *-DIK*. Supply one: *olmak* "be" (tur-04-16): *Ayşe'nin hamile olduğunu duydum* "I heard that Ayşe is pregnant". The same goes for *var* and *yok*: "there is" becomes *olduğu*, "there isn't" *olmadığı*.

**Questions inside.** A question word stays in the clause where it would stand in the question: *Film ne zaman başlıyor?* → *Filmin ne zaman başladığını bilmiyorum* "I don't know when the film starts". A yes-no question uses a doubled verb, *gelip gelmeyeceğini* "whether he will come or not" (goksel2005 §24.4.3.2); the *-(y)Ip* in it is tur-06-06.

## Where it applies

- **Object of verbs of knowing, thinking and saying**, in the accusative: *bilmek*, *anlamak*, *sanmak*, *düşünmek*, *hatırlamak*, *unutmak*, *öğrenmek*, *duymak*, *görmek*, *fark etmek*, *söylemek*, *yazmak*, *anlatmak*, *sormak*, *açıklamak*, *kabul etmek*, *itiraf etmek* "confess", *iddia etmek* "claim", *tahmin etmek* (goksel2005 §24.4.3.1).
- **Oblique objects**: *inanmak* (dative), *emin olmak* (ablative), *şüphe* with the dative: *Gelmeyeceğine şüphe yok* "There's no doubt he won't come"; *haberim yok* "I had no idea" with the ablative: *Ayşe'nin taşındığından haberim yoktu*.
- **Genitive with farkında olmak "be aware"**: *Geç kaldığımızın farkındayım* "I'm aware that we're late". The clause is the possessor of *fark* (goksel2005 §24.4.3.1).
- **Subject of a judgement of truth**: *doğru*, *yalan*, *belli*, *kesin*: *Onun hasta olduğu yalan* "It's a lie that he's ill".
- **Indirect questions** with any question word: *kim*, *ne*, *nerede*, *ne zaman*, *neden*, *nasıl*, *hangi*, *kaç*. Yes-no questions use *-(y)Ip … -mA* (tur-06-06).
- **"It's good that"**: *-DIK* + *iyi oldu* (goksel2005 §24.4.3.3).
- **Feelings** (*sevinmek*, *üzülmek*, *korkmak*): here *-DIK* and *-mA* are interchangeable: *Geldiğine sevindim* = *Gelmene sevindim* "I'm glad you came" (goksel2005 §24.4.5.2).
- **Seeing a fact versus watching an event**: *Düştüğünü gördüm* can mean "I saw that he had fallen" (a fact, perhaps found afterwards); *Düşmesini gördüm* means "I watched him fall" (goksel2005 §24.4.5.1).
- **"Know" or "think"**: with *bilmek*, stress on the verb gives "know"; stress inside the clause makes *bilmek* mean "think (wrongly)", like *sanmak* (goksel2005 §24.4.3.1).
- **Present versus past, made explicit**: *-DIK* alone covers both. To insist on "is doing" use *-(I)yor olduğu*; on "has done", *-mIş olduğu*: *Kitabı okumuş olduğunu sanıyorum* "I think he has read the book" (goksel2005 §24.4.7).
- **The same suffixes make relative clauses** ("the book that I read"), which is tur-06-05.

## Watch out

**Do not use -mA for a fact.** *\*Ali'nin gelmesini biliyorum* is wrong for "I know that Ali came". Knowing and saying take *-DIK*: *Ali'nin geldiğini biliyorum*. The reverse fails too: *Gelmeni istiyorum* is right, *\*Geldiğini istiyorum* is not (tur-06-02).

**Do not put case on a finite verb.** English and Spanish drop a full sentence after "that" or *que*. Turkish cannot add a case to a tensed verb: *\*Ali geldiyi biliyorum* is impossible. Make the verb a noun: *geldiğini*.

**Choose the tense from the main verb, not from now.** "He said he would come" is about a coming later than the saying: *Geleceğini söyledi*, not *\*Geldiğini söyledi*, which means "He said he had come".

**Do not backshift.** English "He said he *was* ill" moves "is" into the past; Spanish *dijo que estaba enfermo* does too. Turkish needs no change: *Hasta olduğunu söyledi*. Do not try *\*hasta olmuş olduğunu* for this; that means "had been ill".

**The possessive is the clause's subject, not the main subject.** "Ali said that I came" is *Ali geldiğimi söyledi*; "Ali said that he came" is *Ali geldiğini söyledi*. *\*Ali geldim söyledi* mixes a quotation into the sentence; quotations are tur-06-12.

**Supply olmak with a noun or adjective.** *\*Ali'nin doktordığını* and *\*hastadığını* are impossible. Say *doktor olduğunu*, *hasta olduğunu*.

**var does not take -DIK.** *Varmak* is a different verb, "arrive" (tur-04-05): *\*Süt vardığını sanıyordum* means nothing sensible. Say *Evde süt olduğunu sanıyordum* "I thought there was milk at home".

**Keep the k before the 3PL ending.** *\*Geldiğileri* is wrong; *geldikleri*. *-lArI* begins with a consonant, so *k* does not soften (tur-01-06).

**Do not leave out the accusative.** *\*Geldiğin biliyorum* is wrong: a possessed object takes the accusative (tur-03-01): *Geldiğini biliyorum*.

**Do not write the subject in the nominative when it is specific.** *\*Ali geldiğini biliyorum* is wrong for "I know that Ali came" (at best it reads "Ali, I know you came"). A specific subject of the clause is in the genitive: *Ali'nin geldiğini biliyorum*.

## Connections

Japanese and Korean report facts with a nominalised clause too: Japanese 君が来たことを知っている, Korean 네가 온 것을 안다. Like *-DIK*, the Japanese and Korean clause carries its own tense (来た, 온), measured from the main verb, and takes an object marker as *geldiğini* takes *-(y)I*. The resemblance is typological (dossier, section 6). Spanish and French use *que* with a finite verb and backshift the tense (*dijo que estaba*); Turkish does neither.

## Exercises

1. Say "I know that you are tired" and "I know that you will be tired". What changes, and why?
2. Build "We heard that Mehmet had won the match" step by step.
3. Take apart *gelmeyeceklerini* and *olduğuna*.
4. A learner writes *\*Ali'nin gelmesini biliyorum.* Correct it and explain.
5. Translate *Annem benim ne zaman döneceğimi sordu.*
6. Say "I don't know where they live."
7. Translate *Kapıyı kapattığından emin misin?* Who closed the door?
8. Why is *\*Geldiğileri* wrong?
9. Translate *Saatin bozuk olduğunun farkında değildim.* (*bozuk* "broken")
10. Say "He said that he was ill" without backshifting, and explain why Turkish does not need to.

## Worked answers

1. *Yorgun olduğunu biliyorum*; *Yorgun olacağını biliyorum*. An adjective needs *olmak* to carry the suffix; *-DIK* is for the same time as the knowing, *-(y)AcAK* for later (goksel2005 §24.4.3).
2. Inner sentence: *Mehmet maçı kazandı*. Subject in the genitive: *Mehmet'in*. The winning came before the hearing: *-DIK*: *kazandığ-*. 3SG possessive and accusative with pronominal n: *kazandığını*. Main verb: *Mehmet'in maçı kazandığını duyduk.* English "had won" needs nothing extra.
3. *Gel-me-yecek-leri-ni*: come, negative, future nominal, 3PL possessive (*k* kept before a consonant), accusative: "that they will not come". *Ol-duğ-u-na*: be, factive nominal, 3SG possessive, dative with pronominal n, as after *inanmak*.
4. *Ali'nin geldiğini biliyorum.* *bilmek* is a verb of knowing, so its clause states a fact and takes *-DIK*; *-mA* is for wanted or judged actions (tur-06-02).
5. "My mother asked when I would be back." An indirect question: *ne zaman* stays inside; *döneceğimi* is future relative to the asking, with the 1SG possessive; *benim* is the subject in the genitive.
6. *Nerede yaşadıklarını bilmiyorum.* The question word stays inside; *-DIK* for the present; 3PL possessive; accusative.
7. "Are you sure you closed the door?" The person addressed closed it: *kapattığından* is *kapat-tığ-ın-dan* with the 2SG possessive, in the ablative after *emin*.
8. *-lArI* begins with a consonant, so the *k* of *-DIK* stays: *geldikleri*. *k* becomes *ğ* only between vowels (tur-01-06).
9. "I wasn't aware that the clock was broken." *Bozuk olduğunun*: *olmak* for the adjective, *-DIK*, possessive, genitive, because the clause is the possessor of *farkında* (goksel2005 §24.4.3.1).
10. *Hasta olduğunu söyledi.* *-DIK* means "at the same time as the main verb", so the time of the illness is already fixed by *söyledi*; there is nothing to shift.
