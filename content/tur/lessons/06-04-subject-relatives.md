## The idea

A **relative clause** is a clause that describes a noun: "the children *who are playing in the garden*", "a guide *who speaks Turkish*". English, Spanish and French put the clause after the noun and start it with a relative pronoun: *who*, *que*, *qui*.

Turkish has no relative pronoun for this. It turns the verb of the clause into an adjective, a form called a **participle**, and puts the whole clause before the noun, where adjectives go (tur-05-01): *bahçede oynayan çocuklar*, literally "in-the-garden playing children". You build it the way you build *küçük çocuklar*: description first, noun last.

Which participle you use depends on what the noun does in the clause (goksel2005 §25.1). This lesson covers the case where the noun is the **subject** of the clause: the children *play*, the guide *speaks*. For that, Turkish uses ***-(y)An***. When the noun is the object or a place ("the book *I read*", "the town *where we live*"), the participle is *-DIK* or *-(y)AcAK* with a possessive, the next lesson (tur-06-05).

*-(y)An* is glossed PTCP. It takes no person ending, because the subject is the noun itself. It has no tense of its own either: *yüzen kadın* is "the woman who is swimming", "who swims" or "who swam", and the context or a time word decides (goksel2005 §25.4).

A participle is still an adjective, and an adjective can stand for a noun: *yeniler* "the new ones". So *-(y)An* forms can stand alone and take plural and case endings: *gelenler* "those who came", *bilmeyenlere* "to those who don't know". These are **headless relative clauses**: the noun they describe has been left out.

## How it is built

**Step 1: start from a sentence in which the noun is the subject.** *Çocuklar bahçede oynuyor* "The children are playing in the garden".

**Step 2: take the subject out and put it at the end.** *[bahçede oynuyor] çocuklar*.

**Step 3: replace the tense and person on the verb with -(y)An.** Two-way harmony, and *y* after a vowel: *oyna-yan*, *bil-en*, *gel-en*, *kır-an*. The negative stays in its slot: *bil-me-yen* "who doesn't know". Result: *bahçede oynayan çocuklar*.

**Step 4: the rest of the clause stays as it was.** Objects keep their accusative, places their locative: *Türkçe bilen bir rehber*, *yarışmayı kazanan öğrenci*. The noun phrase as a whole then takes whatever case the main sentence needs: *bahçede oynayan çocukları gördüm* "I saw the children playing in the garden".

**Nouns and adjectives as predicates.** "The friend who is a doctor": the clause *arkadaşım doktor* has no verb. Supply *olmak* (goksel2005 §25.1): *doktor olan arkadaşım*.

**The noun as possessor.** "My friend whose father is a doctor": in the sentence *Arkadaşımın babası doktor*, my friend is the possessor of the subject. Turkish relativises this with *-(y)An* too. The possessed noun keeps its possessive ending and starts the clause, with no genitive before it: *babası doktor olan arkadaşım* (goksel2005 §25.1.1.1). "A house with a garden" is built the same way, from *Evin bahçesi var*: *bahçesi olan bir ev*.

**Future.** *-(y)An* normally refers to the present or past. For the future, put *-(y)AcAK* on the verb and *olan* after it: *yarın gelecek olan misafirler* "the guests who will come tomorrow" (goksel2005 §25.4.1). In everyday speech *-(y)AcAK* alone also works as a participle: *gelecek misafirler*.

## Where it applies

- **The noun is the subject of an action**: *bahçede oynayan çocuklar*, *çocuğu kurtaran adam*.
- **The noun is the subject of a noun or adjective predicate**, with *olan*: *doktor olan arkadaşım*, *hasta olan öğrenciler* (goksel2005 §25.1).
- **The noun is the possessor of the subject**: *babası doktor olan*, *manzarası güzel olan*, *arabası olmayan* "who has no car" (goksel2005 §25.1.1.1). Such clauses often translate as "with" or "whose".
- **The noun is a possessor elsewhere in the clause, when the subject is indefinite**: *penceresinden müzik gelen ev* "the house from whose window music is coming" (goksel2005 §25.1.1.1). This is rarer; recognise it.
- **Headless, as a noun**: *gelenler*, *bilmeyenler*, *kazanan* "the winner". It takes plural, possessive and case like any noun: *gelenlere*, *kazananın adı*.
- **"Anyone who / no one who"**: headless *-(y)An* with *var*, *yok* or *olmak*: *Arayan yok* "No one has called"; *Bilen var mı?* "Does anyone know?" (goksel2005 §25.3).
- **Future**: *-(y)AcAK olan*, or *-(y)AcAK* alone in speech (goksel2005 §25.4.1).
- **Completed state**: *-mIş* as participle, *kırılmış bardak*; *-mIş olan* is a slightly more formal past: *dün gelmiş olan mektup* (goksel2005 §25.4.1).
- **Fixed participles that have become words**: *gelecek* "future; next" (tur-04-04), *geçen* "last" (*geçen hafta*). *Geçen* is *geç-en* "the passing (week)".

## Watch out

**Do not use -(y)An when the noun is the object.** *\*Okuyan kitap* means "the book that reads". If the noun is what someone reads, it is not the subject, and you need *-DIK* with a possessive: *okuduğum kitap* "the book I read" (tur-06-05).

**No relative pronoun.** By analogy with *que*, *qui* or "who", learners reach for *kim* or *ne*: *\*Adam kim geldi*, *\*Çocuklar ki oynuyor*. Turkish question words do not introduce relative clauses. Say *gelen adam*, *oynayan çocuklar*. (A Persian-style *ki* clause after the noun exists in formal writing; tur-06-12.)

**The clause goes before the noun.** *\*Çocuklar bahçede oynayan* is wrong; *bahçede oynayan çocuklar*.

**No person ending on -(y)An.** *\*Gelenim adam* or *\*oynayanlar çocuklar* are impossible. The participle is an adjective: no person, and no plural when a noun follows. The plural goes on the noun: *oynayan çocuklar*. Only a headless participle takes *-lAr*: *oynayanlar*.

**Do not put a genitive on the possessed noun's owner.** *\*Babasının doktor olan arkadaşım* and *\*Arkadaşımın babası doktor olan* are wrong. The owner is the head noun at the end; the possessed noun opens the clause with its possessive only: *babası doktor olan arkadaşım*.

**Use olan when the description has more than one word.** *Doktor arkadaşım* "my doctor friend" is correct, with *doktor* used like an adjective. But a place or time word put before it belongs to the whole sentence: *Ankara'da doktor arkadaşım var* is "I have a doctor friend in Ankara", not "the friend who is a doctor in Ankara". For that, make a clause: *Ankara'da doktor olan arkadaşım*. In the same way, "the students who are ill today" is *bugün hasta olan öğrenciler*; *\*bugün hasta öğrenciler* does not hold together as one phrase.

**Do not add tense to -(y)An.** *\*Geldien*, *\*gelecekan* do not exist. For the past, use a time word (*dün gelen*); for the future, *gelecek olan*.

**Watch the y.** After a vowel, *-(y)An* has *y*: *oynayan*, *arayan*, *bilmeyen*. *\*Oynaan* is wrong. *de-* and *ye-* write *i*: *diyen*, *yiyen* (TDK; dossier, part 4 notes).

## Connections

Japanese and Korean also put the relative clause before the noun with no relative pronoun: Japanese 庭で遊んでいる子供たち, Korean 정원에서 노는 아이들. Korean -는 and -(으)ㄴ are adnominal endings like *-(y)An*, though Korean uses the same ending whether the noun is subject or object, while Turkish switches to *-DIK* for objects (tur-06-05). The likeness is typological, not a sign of common descent (dossier, section 6). Headless *gelenler* matches Spanish *los que vinieron* and French *ceux qui sont venus*.

## Exercises

1. Turn *Kadın piyano çalıyor* into "the woman who plays the piano".
2. Say "a student who doesn't like football".
3. Build "my neighbour whose son is a policeman" step by step.
4. Translate *Bu yarışmaya katılanların hepsi ödül aldı.*
5. A learner writes *\*Okuyan kitap çok güzel* for "The book I'm reading is very nice." What is wrong, and which lesson has the fix?
6. Say "Is there anyone who speaks English?"
7. Translate *Yarın gidecek olan öğrenciler bugün bilet almalı.*
8. Correct *\*Adam kim kapıyı açtı benim babam.*
9. Translate *Kurallara uymayanlar oyundan çıkar.*

## Worked answers

1. *Piyano çalan kadın.* The woman is the subject of *çal-*: remove the tense and person and add *-(y)An*: *çal-an*; the clause goes before the noun.
2. *Futbolu sevmeyen bir öğrenci.* Negative *-mA*, then *-(y)An* with the buffer: *sev-me-yen*. *sevmek* takes the accusative for a general object it likes (tur-03-01).
3. Sentence: *Komşumun oğlu polis*. The neighbour is the possessor of the subject *oğlu*. Start the clause with the possessed noun: *oğlu polis*; add *olan* for the noun predicate: *oğlu polis olan*; head noun last: *oğlu polis olan komşum* (goksel2005 §25.1.1.1).
4. "Everyone who took part in this competition got a prize." *Katıl-an-lar-ın hepsi*: headless participle, plural, genitive, as the owner of *hepsi* (tur-05-02).
5. *Okuyan kitap* means "the book that reads", because *-(y)An* makes the noun the subject. The book is the object, so you need *-DIK* with a possessive: *Okuduğum kitap çok güzel* (tur-06-05).
6. *İngilizce bilen var mı?* A headless *-(y)An* as the subject of *var mı* (goksel2005 §25.3).
7. "The students who are leaving tomorrow should buy tickets today." *-(y)AcAK olan* makes the participle future; *-mAlI* gives the advice (tur-04-12).
8. *Kapıyı açan adam benim babam.* Turkish has no relative *kim*: the man is the subject of *aç-*, so *açan*, before the noun.
9. "Those who don't follow the rules are out of the game." *Uy-ma-yan-lar*: negative, participle, plural (headless). *uymak* takes the dative (*kurallara*); the aorist states a rule (tur-04-06).
