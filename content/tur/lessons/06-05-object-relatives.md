## The idea

Tur-06-04 described a noun by what it does: *kitap okuyan çocuk* "the child who reads books". Now the noun is on the receiving end: "the book *that the child reads*", "the city *where we live*", "the day *we met*", "the dog *I'm afraid of*". In each, the noun is not the subject of the clause. Someone else is.

That someone else has to be named, and Turkish names a subject the way it always does inside a verb-noun: with a genitive and a possessive (tur-06-02, tur-06-03). The participle for this is the same pair you used for facts: ***-DIK*** for the present or past, ***-(y)AcAK*** for the future, each with a possessive ending for the subject. *Çocuğun okuduğu kitap* is "the book the child reads (or read)", literally "the child's reading book" (goksel2005 §25.1.1.2).

So the rule for choosing the participle is one question: **is the noun the subject of the clause?** If yes, *-(y)An*, no possessive. If no, *-DIK* or *-(y)AcAK* with a possessive for the real subject (goksel2005 §25.1.1.3).

There is one more thing to notice. In "the dog I'm afraid *of*", English keeps the preposition. Turkish *korkmak* takes the ablative (*köpekten korkuyorum*), but in the relative clause that ablative disappears: *korktuğum köpek*. The same happens to the dative, the locative and *-(y)lA*. The verb tells you what relation is meant: you are afraid *of* something, you live *in* somewhere, you talk *with* someone. Turkish trusts you to work it out.

## How it is built

**Step 1: start from a sentence that contains the noun.** *Ali bu kitabı okudu* "Ali read this book". The noun to describe is *kitap*, the object.

**Step 2: is it the subject?** No: Ali is. So use *-DIK* or *-(y)AcAK*. The reading is in the past: *-DIK*.

**Step 3: take the noun out and put it at the end, without its case.** *[Ali okudu] kitap*.

**Step 4: subject into the genitive, verb into -DIK + possessive.** *Ali'nin okuduğu kitap* "the book Ali read". If the subject is a pronoun, the possessive alone says who: *okuduğum kitap* "the book I read", *okuduğun kitap* "the book you read". Add *benim*, *senin* only for emphasis or contrast.

**Step 5: the head noun takes the case of the main sentence.** *Ali'nin okuduğu kitabı ben de okudum* "I read the book Ali read, too". The accusative is on *kitabı*, at the end of the whole phrase.

The same steps work for any non-subject. "The city where we live": *Bu şehirde yaşıyoruz* → *yaşadığımız şehir*. "The day we met": *O gün tanıştık* → *tanıştığımız gün*. "The teacher I spoke to": *Öğretmenle konuştum* → *konuştuğum öğretmen*.

**Future.** *-(y)AcAK* with a possessive: *yarın izleyeceğimiz film* "the film we're going to watch tomorrow" (goksel2005 §25.4). Without a possessive, *-(y)AcAK* makes "something to be done": *okuyacak bir kitap* "a book to read", where nobody in particular is meant (goksel2005 §25.1.1.2 (v)). This is the source of the nouns *yiyecek* "food" and *içecek* "drink".

**Headless.** The head noun can be left out, as with *-(y)An*: *söylediğin* "what you said", *söyledikleriniz* "the things you said". With the plural of a thing, *-lAr* comes before the possessive: *söyle-dik-ler-in* "the things you said" (goksel2005 §25.3).

## Where it applies

- **Direct objects**: *okuduğum kitap*, *Ali'nin aldığı araba*.
- **Dative, ablative, locative and -(y)lA objects**, with the case dropped: *güvendiğim arkadaş* "the friend I trust" (from *arkadaşa güveniyorum*), *bahsettiğin dergi*, *konuştuğum öğretmen* (goksel2005 §25.1.1.2).
- **Places**: *yaşadığımız şehir*, *doğduğum köy*, *çalıştığı şirket*.
- **Times**: *tanıştığımız gün*, *geldiğin yıl*, *istediğin zaman*. The pattern *-DIğI zaman* "when" is tur-06-07.
- **Instruments and means**: *kapıyı açtığım anahtar* "the key I opened the door with" (from *anahtarla açtım*).
- **The possessor of a non-subject**: *oğluyla tanıştığım kadın*, *evini kiraladığımız adam* "the man whose house we rented" (goksel2005 §25.1.1.2 (iii)).
- **A possessed noun that is not the subject**, with the possessor in the genitive at the start: *Ali'nin kırdığın bardağı* "Ali's glass, which you broke" (goksel2005 §25.1.1.2 (iv)). Recognise this; it is not common.
- **Future**: *-(y)AcAK* + possessive, *yapacağımız iş* "the work we will do". Also a past plan: *dün göreceğim film* "the film I was going to see yesterday" (goksel2005 §25.4).
- **"Something to do"**: *-(y)AcAK* with no possessive: *yiyecek bir şey*, *okuyacak bir kitap*, *oturacak yer* "somewhere to sit".
- **Headless, as a noun**: *söylediğin* "what you said", *bildiğim* "what I know", *istediklerimiz* "the things we want". With *var* and *yok*: *Sana söyleyeceklerim var* "I have things to tell you" (goksel2005 §25.3).
- **Words made from -DIK**: *tanıdık* "acquaintance; familiar", from *tanımak* (goksel2005 §25.1.1.2 (v)).

## Watch out

**Is the noun the subject? Decide first.** *\*Okuyan kitap* for "the book I read" fails because *-(y)An* makes the book the reader. The opposite error, *\*Kitap okuduğu çocuk* for "the child who reads books", fails because *-DIK* says someone else is the subject, and the possessive then has no one to refer to. Say *kitap okuyan çocuk*.

**Drop the case of the head noun inside the clause.** By analogy with "the dog I'm afraid *of*", learners keep the case: *\*Köpekten korktuğum*, or put it on the head: *\*korktuğum köpekten* (when the main sentence does not need it). The relative clause ends in *-DIK* + possessive: *korktuğum köpek*. The head takes only the case of the main sentence.

**No pronoun for the head inside the clause.** By analogy with "the book I read *it*", which no one says in English either, learners sometimes add *onu*: *\*Onu okuduğum kitap*. Leave the head's slot empty: *okuduğum kitap*.

**The subject goes in the genitive, not the nominative.** *\*Ali aldığı araba* and *\*Ben okuduğum kitap* are wrong. Say *Ali'nin aldığı araba*, *(benim) okuduğum kitap*.

**The possessive belongs to the participle, not the noun.** "The book I read" is *okuduğum kitap*, not *\*okuduğum kitabım* or *\*okudu kitabım*. If the book is also mine, the sense is usually clear without it.

**Make the possessive agree with the clause's subject.** *\*Ali'nin aldığım araba* mixes two subjects. *Ali'nin aldığı araba*: Ali, 3SG.

**Do not confuse the relative clause with the fact clause.** *Ali'nin aldığı arabayı gördüm* is "I saw the car Ali bought". *Ali'nin araba aldığını gördüm* is "I saw that Ali bought a car". In the first, *-DIK* describes a noun; in the second, the clause itself is the object and takes the case (tur-06-03).

**Do not use English "that" or "which".** There is no word to translate it. *\*Kitap ki okudum* is not Turkish; *okuduğum kitap* is.

**Plural of a headless form: -lAr before the possessive.** *\*Söylediğinler* is wrong; *söylediklerin* "the things you said" (goksel2005 §25.3).

## Connections

Japanese and Korean make object and subject relatives with the same form: 子供が読んだ本 and 本を読んだ子供, Korean 아이가 읽은 책 and 책을 읽은 아이. Turkish distinguishes them, with *-(y)An* for subjects and *-DIK* plus a possessive for everything else. Like Japanese and Korean, it drops the case of the head noun inside the clause and uses no pronoun for it. The likeness is typological (dossier, section 6). Spanish *el perro del que tengo miedo* keeps the preposition; Turkish *korktuğum köpek* does not.

## Exercises

1. Turn *Ayşe bir mektup yazdı* into "the letter Ayşe wrote".
2. Say "the house where I was born". (*doğmak*, *ev*)
3. Build "the friend I trust" from *Arkadaşıma güveniyorum*, step by step.
4. Translate *Kiraladığımız araba çok eskiydi.*
5. Which is right for "the woman who is talking to Ali": *Ali'yle konuşan kadın* or *Ali'nin konuştuğu kadın*? What does the other mean?
6. Correct *\*Ben aldığım hediyeyi beğendin mi?*
7. Say "Did you understand what I said?"
8. Translate *Bu akşam yiyecek bir şey yok.*
9. Translate *Tamir ettiğin bisiklet yine bozuldu.* (*bozulmak* "break down")
10. Explain the difference between *Mehmet'in çizdiği resmi gördüm* and *Mehmet'in resim çizdiğini gördüm*.

## Worked answers

1. *Ayşe'nin yazdığı mektup.* The letter is the object, so *-DIK*; Ayşe is the subject: genitive, and 3SG possessive on the participle.
2. *Doğduğum ev.* The house is the place, not the subject: *doğ-duğ-um*, 1SG possessive; the locative of *evde doğdum* is dropped.
3. Sentence: *(Ben) arkadaşıma güveniyorum.* The friend is not the subject: *-DIK*. Take the noun out and drop its dative: *[güveniyorum] arkadaş*. Participle with 1SG possessive: *güvendiğim arkadaş*.
4. "The car we rented was very old." *Kirala-dığ-ımız*: object relative with 1PL possessive; *eski-y-di* past copula after a vowel (tur-04-08).
5. *Ali'yle konuşan kadın*: the woman is the subject of *konuş-*, so *-(y)An*. *Ali'nin konuştuğu kadın* means "the woman Ali is talking to", with Ali as subject.
6. *(Benim) aldığım hediyeyi beğendin mi?* "Did you like the present I bought?" The subject of a *-DIK* clause is in the genitive or left to the possessive; *ben* in the nominative is wrong.
7. *Söylediğimi anladın mı?* A headless relative, *söyle-diğ-im* "what I said", in the accusative as the object of *anla-*.
8. "There's nothing to eat this evening." *Yiyecek* without a possessive: "a thing to eat", no particular eater (goksel2005 §25.1.1.2 (v)).
9. "The bicycle you repaired has broken down again." *Tamir et-tiğ-in*: object relative, 2SG possessive.
10. The first is a relative clause: "I saw the picture Mehmet drew" (I saw the picture). The second is a fact clause: "I saw that Mehmet was drawing / drew a picture" (I saw the event or the fact). In the first, the case is on the head *resmi*; in the second, on the clause, *çizdiğini* (tur-06-03).
