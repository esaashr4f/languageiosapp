## The idea

So far, verbs in part 6 have become nouns (*-mAk*, *-mA*, *-DIK*) or adjectives (*-(y)An*). The third kind of non-finite verb is the adverb: a verb form that attaches its clause to the main verb the way *yavaş* or *dikkatlice* attach to it. Grammars of Turkish call these forms **converbs**: verb forms that make their clause into an adverbial part of another clause (goksel2005 §26.2).

This lesson covers the two you will use most.

***-(y)Ip*** means "and (then)". It joins two actions of equal weight, usually by the same person: *Eve gelip yattım* "I came home and went to bed". The interesting point is what it leaves out. The first verb has no tense and no person. It borrows both from the last verb in the chain: *gelip yattım* is "I came and I went to bed"; *gelip yatacağım* "I'll come and go to bed" (goksel2005 §28.2). Turkish says the tense once, at the end, which suits a language that puts the verb last. You could write *Eve geldim ve yattım*; it is correct, but sounds like two separate reports. *-(y)Ip* makes one connected event.

***-(y)ArAk*** answers "how?". It describes the manner or the means of the main action: *koşarak geldi* "he came running", *çalışarak kazandı* "he earned it by working". It can also mean "while" when two actions overlap (goksel2005 §26.3.8, §26.3.9). You have met one *-(y)ArAk* form already: *olarak* "as" (tur-05-01), which is *ol-arak* "being".

A third, older pattern repeats a verb with *-(y)A*: *koşa koşa* "running all the way", *gide gide* "by going on and on". It stresses that the action continues or repeats (goksel2005 §26.3.8). The proverb *damlaya damlaya göl olur* "dripping and dripping, it becomes a lake" uses it: small amounts add up.

## How it is built

**-(y)Ip.**

1. Take the verb stem: *gel-*, *al-*, *otur-*, *gör-*.
2. Add *-(y)Ip* with four-way harmony, *y* after a vowel: *gel-ip*, *al-ıp*, *otur-up*, *gör-üp*, *oku-yup*, *bekle-yip*. Before the vowel the final stop of a few stems voices, as before any vowel: *gid-ip* from *git-*, *ed-ip* from *et-* (wiktionary: gidip). *De-* gives *deyip* (TDK; dossier).
3. Put tense, mood and person only on the last verb: *Markete gidip ekmek aldım* "I went to the shop and bought bread".
4. A negative can go on the *-(y)Ip* verb itself only when the last verb is positive: *Evde oturmayıp dışarı çıktık* "We didn't stay in but went out". If the last verb is negative, its negative usually covers the whole chain: *Gelip yardım etmedi* "He didn't come and help" (goksel2005 §28.2).

**-(y)ArAk.**

1. Take the stem: *koş-*, *gül-*, *bağır-*, *dinle-*.
2. Add *-(y)ArAk* with two-way harmony, *y* after a vowel: *koş-arak*, *gül-erek*, *bağır-arak*, *dinle-yerek*. Stems change as before *-(y)Ip*: *gid-erek*.
3. Put the clause before the main verb, as an adverb: *Gülerek cevap verdi* "She answered with a smile".

**-(y)A -(y)A.** Add *-(y)A* to the stem twice, as two words: *koş-a koş-a*, *ağla-ya ağla-ya*, *damla-ya damla-ya*.

**Whether or not.** The doubled pattern of tur-06-03 is *-(y)Ip* plus the negative of the same verb: *gelip gelmeyeceğini sordum* "I asked whether he would come or not" (goksel2005 §20.1.3).

## Where it applies

**-(y)Ip**

- **Actions in sequence**, same subject: *gelip yattım*, *gidip aldım*. This is the normal way to say "and" between two verbs.
- **A whole chain**: *Kalkıp giyinip kahvaltı ettim* "I got up, dressed and had breakfast". Every verb but the last has *-(y)Ip*.
- **Inside a subordinate clause**: *-(y)Ip* replaces the subordinating suffix too: *Gelip gördüğünü söyledi* "He said he had come and seen it" (goksel2005 §28.2).
- **"Whether or not"**: *-(y)Ip* + the same verb negative, in a noun clause: *gelip gelmediğini*, *alıp almayacağını*, *gidip gitmemeye* (goksel2005 §20.1.3).
- **With *de* for contrast or "already"**: *Kitabı alıp da hiç açmamış* "He bought the book but apparently never opened it" (goksel2005 §28.2).
- **Different subjects**: possible but unusual, mostly with two third persons (goksel2005 §28.2). Prefer *ve* or two sentences.

**-(y)ArAk**

- **Manner**: *koşarak*, *gülerek*, *bağırarak*, *fısıldayarak* "in a whisper".
- **Means**: *biriktirerek*, *çalışarak*, *otobüse binerek* "by taking the bus".
- **Simultaneous action**: *müzik dinleyerek*, *el sallayarak* "waving".
- **Sequence, in formal style**: news and official prose use *-(y)ArAk* where speech uses *-(y)Ip*: *Müdür Japonya'ya giderek …* "The director went to Japan and …" (goksel2005 §28.2).
- **Lexicalised forms**: *olarak* "as" (tur-05-01), *giderek* "increasingly", a synonym of *gittikçe* (TDK; tur-05-10).

**-(y)A -(y)A**

- **Continued or repeated action, emphatic**: *koşa koşa*, *tekrarlaya tekrarlaya*, *damlaya damlaya*. Mostly the same verb twice (goksel2005 §26.3.8).

## Watch out

**Do not put tense or person on the -(y)Ip verb.** *\*Geldimip yattım* and *\*Geldiyip yattım* are impossible. Only the last verb is finite: *Gelip yattım*.

**Do not use -(y)Ip for "and" between nouns.** *\*Ali'yip Ayşe* means nothing. Nouns take *ve* or *-(y)lA*: *Ali ve Ayşe*, *Ali'yle Ayşe* (tur-03-13).

**Do not use -(y)Ip for manner.** *\*Koşup geldi* means "he ran and (then) came", two steps. "He came running" is *Koşarak geldi*. Ask: are there two events, or one event and how it was done?

**Watch the negative scope.** *Gelip yardım etmedi* usually means he did neither. If he came but did not help, say so with *de* or two clauses: *Geldi ama yardım etmedi*, or *Gelip de yardım etmedi* (goksel2005 §28.2).

**-(y)ArAk is not "after".** *\*Yemek yiyerek çıktık* does not mean "After eating, we left"; it says we left while eating. "After" is *-DIktAn sonra* (tur-06-07); a plain sequence is *yemek yiyip çıktık*.

**Spell the buffer y and the stem change.** *\*Okuip*, *\*bekleip*, *\*gitip*, *\*gitarak* are wrong. Write *okuyup*, *bekleyip*, *gidip*, *giderek* (wiktionary: gidip, giderek).

**Mind the harmony difference.** *-(y)Ip* has four-way harmony (*gelip*, *alıp*, *görüp*, *okuyup*); *-(y)ArAk* has two-way (*gelerek*, *alarak*, *görerek*, *okuyarak*). *\*Okuyurak* mixes them.

## Connections

*-(y)Ip* is close to Japanese て and Korean -고 / -아서: a non-final verb with no tense, which takes its time from the last verb (家に帰って寝た, 집에 와서 잤다). The match is typological (dossier, section 6). *-(y)ArAk* in the manner sense matches Spanish *-ando* and French *en …-ant*: *llegó corriendo*, *il est arrivé en courant*, *koşarak geldi*.

## Exercises

1. Join with *-(y)Ip*: *Kapıyı açtım. Dışarı baktım.*
2. Say "Tomorrow we'll get up early and go to the sea."
3. Which is right for "She left in tears": *ağlayıp gitti* or *ağlayarak gitti*? Why?
4. Translate *Pencereden el sallayarak bize bir şey bağırdı.*
5. A learner writes *\*Eve geldimip yemek yedim.* Correct it.
6. Say "I saved money by not eating in restaurants." (Hint: *-mA-yArAk*.)
7. Translate *Otobüsü kaçırıp işe yürüyerek gittim.*
8. Say "I don't know whether she will accept or not." (*kabul etmek*)
9. Take apart *bekleyip*, *giderek* and *koşa koşa*.

## Worked answers

1. *Kapıyı açıp dışarı baktım.* The first verb loses its tense and person, *aç-ıp*; *baktım* gives both (goksel2005 §28.2).
2. *Yarın erken kalkıp denize gideceğiz.* Future and 1PL only on the last verb.
3. *Ağlayarak gitti.* The crying goes with the leaving: one event and its manner, *-(y)ArAk*. *Ağlayıp gitti* would mean "she cried and then left".
4. "Waving from the window, he shouted something to us." *El salla-yarak*: simultaneous action; *bağırmak* with the dative of the person shouted to.
5. *Eve gelip yemek yedim.* No tense or person on the *-(y)Ip* verb.
6. *Lokantada yemek yemeyerek para biriktirdim.* The negative *-mA* comes before *-(y)ArAk*, with the buffer: *ye-me-yerek*.
7. "I missed the bus and walked to work." *Kaçır-ıp*: sequence; *yürü-yerek*: means, "by walking".
8. *Kabul edip etmeyeceğini bilmiyorum.* *-(y)Ip* on the verb, then the negative with *-(y)AcAK* and the 3SG possessive, in the accusative (goksel2005 §20.1.3). *Et-* becomes *ed-* before a vowel.
9. *Bekle-yip*: stem, buffer *y*, *-(y)Ip* with *i* after *e*. *Gid-erek*: *git-* with *t* voiced before the vowel, *-(y)ArAk* with front harmony. *Koş-a koş-a*: the doubled *-(y)A*, "running all the way".
