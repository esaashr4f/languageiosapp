## The idea

English has a word for each time relation: *when*, *while*, *before*, *after*, *until*, *since*. Turkish builds most of them from pieces you already have. Look at "after the meal": *yemekten sonra*, ablative plus the postposition *sonra* (tur-05-06). "After I ate" is the same thing with a verb-noun in place of the noun: *yedikten sonra*, *-DIK* plus ablative plus *sonra*. "When I came" is "at the time of my coming": *geldiğim zaman*, a relative clause on *zaman* "time" (tur-06-05). "Until you return" is *dönünceye kadar*, with the postposition *kadar* "as far as" (tur-05-05).

A few converbs exist only for time. The two most common are:

- ***-(y)IncA*** "when, once": one event follows another. *Ali gelince başladık* "When Ali came, we started" (goksel2005 §26.3.16 (i)).
- ***-(y)ken*** "while, when, as": the main event happens inside a stretch of time. It goes on the aorist of a verb (*çalışırken* "while working") or straight onto a noun or adjective (*çocukken* "when I was a child") (goksel2005 §26.2.3 (ii)). It contains the old copula *i-* "be", which is why it can follow a noun, like *-(y)DI* and *-(y)mIş* (tur-04-08).

One general rule covers all these clauses. Their subject, if named, is **not** in the genitive (goksel2005 §26.2.1): *Ali gelince*, *ben geldiğim zaman*, *Ali gittikten sonra*. That is the opposite of the relative clauses of tur-06-05, where *Ali'nin geldiği gün* has a genitive. An adverbial clause describes the time of the whole sentence; it does not "own" anything.

## How it is built

**-(y)IncA "when, once".** Stem + *-(y)IncA*, four-way harmony, *y* after a vowel: *gel-ince*, *al-ınca*, *otur-unca*, *gör-ünce*, *başla-yınca*. *De-* gives *deyince* (TDK). No tense and no person: the main verb gives both, and a separate subject stands before it in the nominative.

**-(y)ken "while".** For an action, aorist + *-ken*: *çalış-ır-ken*, *gel-ir-ken*, *oku-r-ken*; for a negative, *-mAz* + *-ken*: *bilmezken*. For a state, noun or adjective + *-(y)ken*: *çocuk-ken*, *hasta-yken*, *evde-yken* "while at home". *-ken* never harmonises and is unstressed (goksel2005 §4.3.2).

**-DIğI zaman "when".** *-DIK* + possessive for the subject + *zaman*: *geldiğim zaman* "when I came / come". Its typical use is a background: the main situation was going on when the event happened (goksel2005 §26.3.16 (ii)). The same meaning is carried by *-DIğIndA* (*geldiğimde*) and *-DIğI sırada* "at the moment when".

**-mAdAn önce "before".** Stem + *-mAdAn* + *önce*: *yat-madan önce* "before going to bed". *-mAdAn* contains the negative *-mA* (goksel2005 §4.3.2 (viii)); that is why its stress falls just before it, *YATmadan*. On its own, *-mAdAn* means "without": *yatmadan* "without going to bed". "Before going to bed" is therefore, literally, "not yet having gone to bed, earlier". *önce* may be left out (goksel2005 §26.3.16 (vi)).

**-DIktAn sonra "after".** Stem + *-DIK* + ablative *-DAn* + *sonra*: *gel-dik-ten sonra*, *yap-tık-tan sonra*. No possessive. It is the clause version of *yemekten sonra* (goksel2005 §26.3.16 (vi)).

**With a separate subject**, *-mAdAn önce* and *-DIktAn sonra* can also be replaced by *-mA* + possessive + ablative: *Ali'nin gelmesinden önce*, with a genitive subject because *-mA* is used (goksel2005 §26.3.16 (vi)). The short forms are more common.

**Three more.**

- ***-(y)IncAyA kadar*** "until; by the time": *-(y)IncA* + dative + *kadar*: *dönünceye kadar* (goksel2005 §26.3.16 (vii)).
- ***-DIğIndAn beri*** "since": *taşındığımızdan beri*.
- ***-Ar … -mAz*** "as soon as": positive and negative aorist of the same verb, *varır varmaz* (goksel2005 §26.3.16 (iv)).

And the one part 5 promised: ***-DIkçA*** means "whenever" or "the more": *dinledikçe* "every time I listen". *Gittikçe* "more and more" (tur-05-10) is *git-tikçe* "the more it goes".

## Where it applies

- **-(y)IncA**: one event triggers or follows another: *Ali gelince*, *ışıklar sönünce*. Also "now that, since (in the past)": *Kimse gelmeyince eve döndük* "When nobody came, we went home".
- **-(y)ken**: an action in progress (*pişirirken*), a state (*çocukken*, *hastayken*, *İstanbul'dayken*), and with a contrast, "whereas": *Ben çalışırken sen uyuyorsun* "I'm working while you sleep". *-mIşken* "having already done": *Buraya kadar gelmişken müzeyi de görelim* "Since we've come this far, let's see the museum too" (goksel2005 §26.2.3 (ii)).
- **-DIğI zaman / -DIğIndA / -DIğI sırada**: a background: *Telefon çaldığında duştaydım* "I was in the shower when the phone rang". Also for repeated events: *Ankara'ya gittiğim zaman onu ziyaret ederim* "When I go to Ankara, I visit him".
- **-(y)AcAğI zaman**: only for a time later than the main verb (goksel2005 §26.2.3 (iv)): *Gideceğin zaman bana haber ver* "When you're about to leave, let me know".
- **-mAdAn (önce)**: "before"; **-mAdAn** alone: "without". **-mAsIndAn önce**: before, with a genitive subject and a possessive.
- **-DIktAn sonra**: "after"; with a time span: *Mezun olduktan iki yıl sonra* "two years after graduating" (goksel2005 §26.3.16 (vi)).
- **-(y)IncAyA kadar**: "until" and "by the time" (goksel2005 §26.3.16 (vii)). In speech you also hear *-(y)AnA kadar*: *dönene kadar*; G&K mark it as informal.
- **-DIğIndAn beri, -(y)AlI**: "since": *taşındığımızdan beri*; *-(y)AlI* (*taşınalı*) is colloquial.
- **-Ar -mAz**: "as soon as".
- **-DIkçA**: "whenever" (*dinledikçe*), "the more" (*okudukça* "the more I read"), and the fixed *gittikçe*.
- **-DIğI sürece**: "for as long as": *Burada kaldığım sürece* "as long as I stay here" (goksel2005 §26.3.16 (ix)).

## Watch out

**No genitive on the subject.** By analogy with relative clauses, learners write *\*Ali'nin gelince* or *\*Ali'nin geldikten sonra*. In time clauses the subject is in the nominative: *Ali gelince*, *Ali geldikten sonra*, *ben geldiğim zaman* (goksel2005 §26.2.1). The exception is the *-mA* forms: *Ali'nin gelmesinden önce*.

**Do not put a person ending on -(y)IncA or -ken.** *\*Gelincem*, *\*çalışırkenim* do not exist. The person comes from the subject word or the main verb: *Ben gelince*, *çalışırken*.

**Do not use -(y)IncA for a future background with a different tense.** *-(y)IncA* has no tense; the main verb decides. *Ali gelince başlayacağız* "We'll start when Ali comes" is correct; *\*Ali gelecekince* is impossible.

**Do not drop the negative idea from -mAdAn önce.** It looks negative, and it is, inside, but the meaning is plain "before". Do not add a second negative: *\*Yatmamadan önce* is wrong; it is *yatmadan önce*. And *\*Yattıktan önce* is wrong: *önce* goes with *-mAdAn*, *sonra* with *-DIktAn*.

**Do not put a possessive on -DIktAn sonra.** *\*Geldiğimden sonra* is wrong in standard use; say *geldikten sonra*. If you need the subject, put it before: *ben geldikten sonra*.

**Put -ken on the aorist or straight on the noun.** For an action in progress the usual form is the aorist, *pişirirken*; *pişiriyorken* exists but is less common (goksel2005 §26.2.3 (ii)). On a noun, do not add a verb: *\*çocuk olurken* for "as a child" means "while becoming a child"; "as a child" is *çocukken*. And *\*pişirken* without the aorist is wrong.

**"When" in a question is ne zaman, not -DIğI zaman.** *Ne zaman geldin?* "When did you come?" A clause with *-DIğI zaman* never asks.

**Spanish *cuando* + subjunctive has no Turkish twin.** "When you arrive, call me" uses plain *-(y)IncA*: *Varınca beni ara*. Do not reach for the optative.

**Mind the buffers and stems.** *-(y)IncA* after a vowel: *başlayınca*, not *\*başlaınca*. *De-*: *deyince*. *Git-*: *gidince*, *gittikten*, *gitmeden*.

## Connections

Japanese and Korean also build time clauses from nominalised or adnominal verbs with words for "time", "before" and "after": Japanese 来た時, 寝る前に, 食べた後で; Korean 왔을 때, 자기 전에, 먹은 후에. *Geldiğim zaman* is close to 来た時 in build, and *-ken* resembles Japanese ながら or Korean -면서 for "while doing". The likeness is typological (dossier, section 6). In Spanish *antes de acostarme* and *después de comer*, the infinitive plays the role of Turkish *-mAdAn* and *-DIK*.

## Exercises

1. Say "When the film ended, we went home." (*bitmek*)
2. Say "While I was in Ankara, I visited my grandfather."
3. Build "before you leave" and "after you leave" from *git-*.
4. Translate *Elektrik kesilince bütün ışıklar söndü.* (*kesilmek* "be cut off")
5. Correct *\*Ali'nin gelince başladık.*
6. Translate *Hastalandığımdan beri hiç dışarı çıkmadım.*
7. Say "I'll wait until the water boils."
8. Translate *Kapıyı açar açmaz kedi içeri girdi.*
9. Explain the difference between *Ali gelince yedik* and *Ali geldiği zaman yiyorduk*.
10. Take apart *okudukça* and *gitmeden*.

## Worked answers

1. *Film bitince eve gittik.* One event, then the next: *-(y)IncA*; *film* is the subject, in the nominative.
2. *Ankara'dayken dedemi ziyaret ettim.* Noun with the locative + *-(y)ken*: a state; the main verb is a completed event inside it (goksel2005 §26.3.16 (iii)).
3. *Gitmeden önce*; *gittikten sonra*. *Git-* keeps *t* before consonants; with a subject, *sen gitmeden önce*, *sen gittikten sonra*.
4. "When the electricity was cut off, all the lights went out." *Kesil-ince*: sequence, *-(y)IncA*.
5. *Ali gelince başladık.* The subject of a time clause takes no genitive (goksel2005 §26.2.1).
6. "I haven't been out at all since I fell ill." *Hastalan-dığ-ım-dan beri*: *-DIK*, 1SG possessive, ablative, *beri*.
7. *Su kaynayıncaya kadar bekleyeceğim.* *-(y)IncA* with the buffer *y* after a vowel, dative, *kadar*.
8. "As soon as I opened the door, the cat came in." *Açar açmaz*: positive and negative aorist of the same verb, "as soon as"; the opener is understood from context, here "I" or "you".
9. *Ali gelince yedik*: we ate after Ali came, a sequence. *Ali geldiği zaman yiyorduk*: we were already eating when Ali came, a background (goksel2005 §26.3.16 (ii)); with a different main tense it can also mean "whenever Ali came, we would eat".
10. *Oku-dukça*: read + *-DIkçA*, "whenever / the more (someone) reads". *Git-meden*: *git-* + *-mAdAn*, "without going" or, with *önce*, "before going".
