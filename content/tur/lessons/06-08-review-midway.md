## Before you start

This review covers the first half of part 6: the infinitive *-mAk* (tur-06-01); the verbal noun *-mA* with a possessive (tur-06-02); *-DIK* and *-(y)AcAK* for facts (tur-06-03); relative clauses with *-(y)An* (tur-06-04) and with *-DIK* and *-(y)AcAK* (tur-06-05); the converbs *-(y)Ip*, *-(y)ArAk* and *-(y)A -(y)A* (tur-06-06); and time clauses (tur-06-07). Parts 2 to 5 are assumed.

Every non-finite verb in part 6 answers three questions, and the exercises keep asking them:

1. **What kind of word has the verb become?** A noun (*-mAk*, *-mA*, *-DIK*, *-(y)AcAK* with a case), an adjective (*-(y)An*, or *-DIK* / *-(y)AcAK* before a noun), or an adverb (the converbs and time clauses).
2. **Who is its subject, and how is that shown?** Not at all (*-mAk*, the converbs), by the head noun (*-(y)An*), or by a possessive with an optional genitive (*-mA*, *-DIK*, *-(y)AcAK*). In time clauses the subject is in the nominative.
3. **What case does the main verb need?** That case goes on the end of the clause.

Work each exercise before reading its answer. Every answer names the lesson to reread if you went wrong.

## Passage

**A letter after a move.** A constructed text. Words glossed in place: *emlakçı* "estate agent", *ev sahibi* "landlord" (literally "house owner"), *ayın başında* "at the start of the month", *uzun zamandır* "for a long time" (*zaman* + *-DIr*, as in tur-05-04).

1. Sevgili Mehmet,
2. Sana uzun zamandır yazmak istiyordum, ama taşındığımızdan beri hiç vaktim olmadı.
3. Yeni evimiz, eskiden çalıştığım şirketin yakınında.
4. Bahçesi olan küçük bir ev bulmak kolay değildi.
5. Ayşe manzarası güzel olan bir ev istiyordu; ben de bahçede oturmayı seviyorum.
6. Sonunda emlakçının gösterdiği üçüncü evi beğendik.
7. Ev sahibi kirayı her ayın başında ödememizi istedi.
8. Taşınmadan önce bütün eşyaları kutulara koyup arabaya taşıdık.
9. Taşındıktan sonra komşular gelip bize yardım ettiler.
10. Akşam yorgun olduğumuzu görünce bize yemek getirdiler.
11. Çocuklar bahçede oynarken biz de onlarla çay içip sohbet ettik.
12. Gelecek ay bizi ziyaret edeceğini biliyorum. Geldiğin zaman sana şehri göstereceğiz.
13. Gelip gelmeyeceğini bana yazmayı unutma!
14. Sevgiler, Ali

## Exercises

1. Translate lines 1 to 5.
2. Translate lines 6 to 10.
3. Translate lines 11 to 14.
4. Line 2: why is it *yazmak istiyordum* and not *yazmayı istiyordum* or *yazmamı istiyordum*? Take apart *taşındığımızdan beri*.
5. Line 3: take apart *çalıştığım* and say who worked where. Why is there no locative on *şirket* inside the clause?
6. Lines 4 and 5: explain *bahçesi olan* and *manzarası güzel olan*. Whose garden, whose view?
7. Line 5: why *oturmayı* and not *oturmak*?
8. Line 6: why *emlakçının* in the genitive, and why *gösterdiği* rather than *gösteren*? Where is the accusative, and why there?
9. Line 7: take apart *ödememizi*. Why could the landlord not have said *ödemek istedi* for this meaning?
10. Line 8: explain *taşınmadan önce* and *koyup*. Which verb gives *koyup* its tense and person?
11. Line 10: take apart *olduğumuzu* and *görünce*. Why is *komşular* (line 9) not in the genitive as the subject of *görünce*?
12. Line 11: what is the difference in meaning between *oynarken* and *içip*?
13. Line 12: why *edeceğini* in the first sentence but *geldiğin zaman* in the second, when both visits are in the future?
14. Line 13: explain *gelip gelmeyeceğini* and *yazmayı*.
15. Say "I want you to call me as soon as you arrive."
16. Say "The book you gave me is very interesting; I started reading it yesterday."
17. Say "Do you know who will win the match?"
18. Say "Those who want to come with us should get ready."
19. Correct *\*Seni gelmek istiyorum.*
20. Correct *\*Okuyan kitap çok güzel* (meaning "The book I'm reading is very nice").
21. Correct *\*Ali'nin gelince yemeğe başladık.*
22. Correct *\*Denizde yüzmek seviyorum.*
23. Explain the difference between *Ali'nin geldiğini gördüm* and *Ali'nin gelmesini istedim*.
24. Take apart *söylediklerin* and translate *Söylediklerin doğru değil.*

## Worked answers

1. "Dear Mehmet, I've been wanting to write to you for a long time, but since we moved I haven't had any time at all. Our new house is near the company where I used to work. It wasn't easy to find a small house with a garden. Ayşe wanted a house with a nice view; and I love sitting in the garden." *Uzun zamandır … istiyordum*: an action continuing up to a past point (tur-04-10 for the compound tense).
2. "In the end we liked the third house the estate agent showed us. The landlord asked us to pay the rent at the start of every month. Before moving, we put all our things in boxes and carried them to the car. After we moved, the neighbours came and helped us. In the evening, when they saw that we were tired, they brought us food."
3. "While the children were playing in the garden, we drank tea with them and chatted. I know you're going to visit us next month. When you come, we'll show you the city. Don't forget to write and tell me whether you're coming or not! Love, Ali"
4. Same subject (I want, I write): *-mAk*; *istemek* is the one verb that takes it bare (tur-06-01). *Yazmayı istiyordum* is possible but formal; *yazmamı istiyordum* is wrong, because *-mA* needs a different subject (tur-06-02). *Taşın-dığ-ımız-dan beri*: *-DIK*, 1PL possessive, ablative, *beri* "since" (tur-06-07).
5. *Çalış-tığ-ım*: *-DIK* with the 1SG possessive: "where I worked". The company is a place, not the subject, so *-DIK*; the locative of *şirkette çalıştım* is dropped in a relative clause (tur-06-05).
6. Both are *-(y)An* clauses in which the house is the possessor of the subject: "a house whose garden exists", "a house whose view is beautiful". The possessed noun opens the clause with its possessive and no genitive (tur-06-04).
7. *sevmek* takes an accusative object; only *istemek* takes a bare *-mAk* (tur-06-01).
8. The house is the object of *göster-*, not the subject, so *-DIK* + possessive; the agent is the subject, in the genitive (tur-06-05). *Gösteren ev* would be "the house that showed". The accusative is on the head, *evi*, because the whole phrase is the object of *beğendik*.
9. *Öde-me-miz-i*: verbal noun, 1PL possessive (we pay), accusative as the object of *istedi* (tur-06-02). *Ödemek istedi* would mean "he wanted to pay", with himself as the payer (tur-06-01).
10. *Taşın-madan önce*: "before moving", *-mAdAn* + *önce* (tur-06-07). *Koy-up*: "put and (then)", with no tense or person; *taşıdık* gives "past, we" to the whole chain (tur-06-06).
11. *Ol-duğ-umuz-u*: *olmak* for the adjective *yorgun*, *-DIK* for a fact, 1PL possessive, accusative after *gör-* (tur-06-03). *Gör-ünce*: "when (they) saw" (tur-06-07). Time clauses have a nominative subject (goksel2005 §26.2.1; tur-06-07).
12. *Oynarken*: an action in progress, the background (*-Ar-ken*, tur-06-07). *İçip*: one action in a sequence, joined to *sohbet ettik* (tur-06-06).
13. *Edeceğini* is a fact clause after *biliyorum*: later than the knowing, so *-(y)AcAK* (tur-06-03). *Geldiğin zaman* is a time clause: *-DIK* marks the coming as simultaneous with the showing, and the future is on the main verb (tur-06-07; goksel2005 §26.2.3 (iv)).
14. *Gel-ip gel-me-yeceğ-in-i*: "whether you will come or not": *-(y)Ip* plus the same verb negative with *-(y)AcAK*, 2SG possessive, accusative (tur-06-06, tur-06-03). *Yaz-ma-yı*: *-mAk* in the accusative after *unutmak* (tur-06-01).
15. *Varır varmaz beni aramanı istiyorum.* *-Ar -mAz* "as soon as" (tur-06-07); *-mA* + 2SG possessive + accusative for a different subject (tur-06-02).
16. *Bana verdiğin kitap çok ilginç; dün okumaya başladım.* Object relative with *-DIK* (tur-06-05); *başlamak* takes the dative of *-mAk* (tur-06-01).
17. *Maçı kimin kazanacağını biliyor musun?* Indirect question with *-(y)AcAK*; *kim* takes the genitive as the subject (tur-06-03).
18. *Bizimle gelmek isteyenler hazırlansın.* Headless *-(y)An*, plural (tur-06-04); *-mAk* after *istemek* (tur-06-01); 3SG imperative for "should" (tur-04-02).
19. *Gelmeni istiyorum.* A different subject needs *-mA* + possessive + accusative (tur-06-02).
20. *Okuduğum kitap çok güzel.* The book is the object, not the subject, so *-DIK* + possessive (tur-06-05).
21. *Ali gelince yemeğe başladık.* No genitive on the subject of a time clause (tur-06-07).
22. *Denizde yüzmeyi seviyorum.* *sevmek* needs the accusative (tur-06-01).
23. The first reports a fact or an event seen: "I saw that Ali had come" or "I saw Ali come" (*-DIK*, tur-06-03). The second is a wish about an action: "I wanted Ali to come" (*-mA*, tur-06-02). Facts take *-DIK*; wanted, asked-for or allowed actions take *-mA*.
24. *Söyle-dik-ler-in*: *-DIK*, plural, 2SG possessive: "the things you said", a headless relative with the plural before the possessive (tur-06-05). "What you said isn't true."
