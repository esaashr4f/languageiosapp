## The idea

Turkish conditions are unlike the other clauses of part 6 in one respect: they are almost finite. An "if" clause keeps its tense and takes the ordinary person endings of a main verb, not a possessive (goksel2005 ch. 27). What turns it into a condition is one suffix, ***-(y)sA***, which you met on nouns in tur-04-08 (*hastaysan* "if you are ill").

There are two conditional markers, and the difference matters:

- **The copular -(y)sA**, written after a tense suffix or a noun: *gel-ir-se* "if he comes", *gel-di-yse* "if he came", *hasta-ysa* "if he is ill". This lesson.
- **The verbal -sA**, straight on the verb stem, with no tense: *gel-se* "if he came (but he won't)", "if only he came". It is for imagined and unreal conditions, tur-06-11.

Both are glossed COND. The copular *-(y)sA* comes from the old verb *i-* "be", like *-(y)DI* and *-(y)mIş*; it can still be written as a separate word, *ise* (tur-04-08; goksel2005 §27.1.1).

Which tense goes before *-(y)sA*? It depends on whether the condition can be known yet (goksel2005 §§27.2, 27.3).

- **An open condition about the future**, which nobody can know yet: the **aorist** + *-(y)sA*. *Yağmur yağarsa evde kalırız* "If it rains, we'll stay in." English uses the present ("if it rains"); Turkish uses the aorist, the tense of what may happen in general (tur-04-06).
- **A condition about the present or past, or about a plan**, which is knowable in principle: the tense that the plain sentence would have, + *-(y)sA*. *Ali geldiyse* "if Ali has come", *çalışıyorsa* "if he is working", *gelmeyecekse* "if he isn't going to come (as planned)". Often the speaker has just heard it and takes it as given: "if, as you say, …".

***Eğer*** "if", borrowed from Persian, may open the clause. It adds nothing grammatically; it signals early that a condition is coming, which helps in a long sentence (goksel2005 §27.1.2). The suffix is never optional: *eğer* without *-(y)sA* is not a condition.

## How it is built

1. **Write the condition as a plain sentence, with the tense it needs.** Open future: aorist, *yağ-ar*; negative *yağ-maz*. Knowable: *gel-di*, *çalış-ıyor*, *gel-ecek*, *gel-miş*; with a noun predicate, the noun itself: *meşgul*, *evde*; existence: *var*, *yok*; negation of a noun: *değil*.
2. **Add -(y)sA.** Two-way harmony; *y* after a vowel: *yağarsa*, *geldiyse*, *çalışıyorsa*, *gelecekse*, *meşgulse*, *evdeyse*, *varsa*, *yoksa*, *değilse*.
3. **Add the person ending, k-set** (tur-04-08): *-m*, *-n*, nothing, *-k*, *-nIz*, *-lAr*: *gelirsem*, *gelirsen*, *gelirse*, *gelirsek*, *gelirseniz*. The 3PL *-lAr* comes before *-sA*: *gelirlerse* (wiktionary: giderlerse).
4. **Add eğer at the front, if you like.**
5. **Build the main clause.** For an open condition: the future if the result is certain, the aorist if it is likely, or a command, request or suggestion (goksel2005 §27.2.1.1). For a knowable condition: often an inference ("then …"), a suggestion, a duty, or a reproachful question (goksel2005 §27.3).

**Stress.** *-(y)sA* is unstressable, so the stress falls just before it: *gidérse*, *geldíyse*, *evdéyse* (goksel2005 §4.3.2).

## Where it applies

- **Open future conditions**: aorist + *-(y)sA*: *gelirsen*, *gelmezsen*. Main clause: future, aorist, imperative, optative (goksel2005 §27.2.1.1).
- **General truths and habits**: aorist + *-(y)sA* with an aorist or progressive main clause: *Vaktim olursa yürüyerek giderim* "If I have time, I walk". In a past story, only the main clause takes the past: *Vakti olursa yürürdü* "If he had time, he would walk" (goksel2005 §27.2.1.2).
- **Present conditions**: *-(I)yorsA*, noun + *-(y)sA*, *varsa*, *yoksa*, *değilse*: *Çalışıyorsa rahatsız etmeyelim* "If he's working, let's not disturb him".
- **Past conditions that may be true**: *-DIysA*, *-mIşsA*: *Ali gittiyse ben de giderim* "If Ali has gone, I'll go too"; *Ali gitmişse* "if Ali has gone, as they say".
- **Plans**: *-(y)AcAksA*: *Gelmeyecekse söylesin* "If he isn't coming, he should say so".
- **Taking up what someone said**: "if so, then": *Öyleyse gidelim* "In that case, let's go" (*öyle* + *-(y)sA*).
- **eğer**: anywhere a condition is, for emphasis or in long sentences; in speech also at the end: *Gelirsen eğer* (goksel2005 §27.1.2).
- **ise / -(y)sA after a noun or pronoun for contrast**, "as for": *Ben ise kalacağım* "As for me, I'll stay" (goksel2005 §11.1.1.3). Recognise this; it is not a condition.
- **Formal alternatives**: *-DIğI takdirde* "in the event that", *-mAsI halinde* "in case of" (goksel2005 §27.6.1).

## Watch out

**Use the aorist for an open future condition.** English "if it rains" and Spanish *si llueve* use the present; French *s'il pleut* too. Turkish does not use *-(I)yor* or the future here: *\*Yarın yağmur yağıyorsa evde kalırız* clashes, because *-(I)yorsA* means "if it is raining (now)"; *Yarın yağacaksa* is correct but means "if it's going to rain (as the forecast says)". For a plain open condition: *Yarın yağmur yağarsa*.

**Do not use the past for a future condition.** Spanish *si viniera* and English "if he came" can be about the future. *\*Yarın geldiyse* is wrong; *-DIysA* is a knowable past: "if he has come". For the future: *gelirse*; for a hypothetical "if he came", the verbal *-sA*: *gelse* (tur-06-11).

**k-set, not z-set, after -(y)sA.** *\*Gelirsesin* and *\*gelirsim* are wrong; *gelirsen*, *gelirsem* (tur-04-08).

**3PL: -lAr before -sA.** Wiktionary's tables give *giderlerse*, *geldilerse*. Do not write *\*gelirsalar*.

**eğer alone is not enough.** *\*Eğer yarın gelirsin, …* is wrong: the suffix is the condition, *eğer* only announces it. *Eğer yarın gelirsen …*.

**Do not put the future in the main clause of a habit.** "If I'm tired, I don't cook" is habitual: *Yorgunsam yemek yapmıyorum* or *yapmam*. *\*Yorgunsam yemek yapmayacağım* is a one-off promise.

**Do not confuse the verbal -sA with the copular -(y)sA.** *Gelse* has no tense and means "if he came (hypothetically)"; *gelirse* is a real possibility. *Yarın yağmur yağsa evde kalırız* is not wrong, but it is tentative, "were it to rain"; for a plain plan use *yağarsa* (goksel2005 §27.2.3).

**Stress the syllable before -(y)sA.** *gelírse*, not *\*gelirsé*: *-(y)sA* never takes stress (goksel2005 §4.3.2).

## Connections

Japanese and Korean also mark conditions on the verb, with no separate "if" required: Japanese 雨が降れば / 降ったら, Korean 비가 오면. Turkish *eğer*, like Japanese もし and Korean 만약, is an optional word that announces the condition. Turkish distinguishes the open future (aorist + *-(y)sA*) from the knowable past (*-DIysA*) more sharply than these languages do. The likeness is typological (dossier, section 6). Spanish keeps the present after *si* for open conditions, *si llueve*, where Turkish uses the aorist.

## Exercises

1. Say "If you come early, we'll have breakfast together."
2. Say "If you're hungry, there's bread in the kitchen."
3. Build "If they don't call, I'll go" step by step.
4. Translate *Buraya park ederseniz ceza ödersiniz.*
5. A learner writes *\*Eğer yarın vaktin var, bize gel.* Correct it.
6. Explain the difference between *Ali gelirse* and *Ali geldiyse*.
7. Translate *Fişiniz yoksa ürünü iade edemezsiniz.*
8. Say "If the shop is closed, we'll go to another one."
9. Translate *Satılık ev arıyorsanız bu mahallede birkaç tane var.*
10. Why is *\*Yarın yağıyorsa* odd for "if it rains tomorrow"?

## Worked answers

1. *Erken gelirsen birlikte kahvaltı ederiz.* Open future: aorist + *-(y)sA* + k-set *-n*; aorist main clause (goksel2005 §27.2.1.1).
2. *Açsan mutfakta ekmek var.* Adjective + *-(y)sA* + *-n*: a present condition (tur-04-08).
3. Plain sentence: *Telefon etmezler*. Negative aorist, 3PL: *etmezler*; add *-(y)sA* after *-lAr*: *etmezlerse*. Main clause: *giderim*. *Telefon etmezlerse giderim.*
4. "If you park here, you'll pay a fine." Open condition, 2PL: *park ed-er-se-niz*; the aorist main clause states a likely result.
5. *Eğer yarın vaktin varsa bize gel.* The condition is carried by *-(y)sA*; *eğer* alone does nothing (goksel2005 §27.1.2).
6. *Ali gelirse*: "if Ali comes", an open future, unknown. *Ali geldiyse*: "if Ali has come", a past event that can be checked; often "if, as I hear, he has come" (goksel2005 §27.3).
7. "If you don't have your receipt, you can't return the product." *Yok-sa*; *iade ed-eme-z-siniz*, impotential with the aorist *-z*.
8. *Dükkân kapalıysa başka birine gideriz.* Adjective + *-(y)sA*: a present, knowable condition. If you mean "if it turns out to be closed (when we get there)", *kapalı olursa* is also right.
9. "If you're looking for a house for sale, there are several in this neighbourhood." *Arı-yor-sa-nız*: present condition, k-set 2PL *-nIz*.
10. *-(I)yor* + *-(y)sA* is a present condition: "if it is raining". *Yarın* clashes with it. For an open future, use the aorist: *Yarın yağmur yağarsa*.
