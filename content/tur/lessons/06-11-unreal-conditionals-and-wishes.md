## The idea

Tur-06-10 dealt with conditions that may really happen: *yağarsa* "if it rains". This lesson deals with conditions that are imagined or false: "if I were rich", "if you had left earlier", "if only you were here". A condition that is contrary to the facts is called **counterfactual**.

For these, Turkish uses the **verbal -sA**: *-sA* straight on the verb stem, with no tense in front of it: *gel-se* "if he came", *ol-sa* "if it were". Then the main clause decides how unreal the whole thing is (goksel2005 §27.2.3):

- Main clause in the **aorist**: a hypothesis, possible but presented as imagined: *Taksiye binsek yetişir miyiz?* "If we took a taxi, would we make it?"
- Main clause in **aorist + past**, *-(A/I)rdI*: contrary to fact, now or then: *Zengin olsam dünyayı gezerdim* "If I were rich, I'd travel the world" (but I'm not).
- Condition in ***-sAydI*** (verbal *-sA* + past copula) and main clause in *-(A/I)rdI*: contrary to fact, usually about the past: *Erken çıksaydın treni kaçırmazdın* "If you had left early, you wouldn't have missed the train" (goksel2005 §27.2.4).

Why does a past marker make something unreal? The past copula *-(y)DI* here does not locate the event in the past: G&K point out that such a sentence can be about tomorrow, as in *Param olsaydı yarın gelirdim* "If I had the money, I'd come tomorrow" (goksel2005 §27.2.4). It marks distance from reality. English does the same with its past tense: "if I *had* time" is about now. So does Spanish: *si tuviera*. You already know *-(A/I)rdI* as "used to" (tur-04-10); "would" is its other job.

The same verbal *-sA* expresses wishes, often with ***keşke*** "if only", a Persian loan (TDK): *Keşke burada olsan* "I wish you were here"; with *-sAydI*, a regret about the past: *Keşke gelmeseydim* "I wish I hadn't come" (goksel2005 §21.4.4.1). And in the first person it makes gentle suggestions: *Yarın denize gitsek?* "What if we went to the seaside tomorrow?"

## How it is built

**Step 1: the condition, on the bare stem.** *gel-*, *ol-*, *bil-*. Negative first: *gel-me-*.

**Step 2: add -sA**, two-way harmony: *gel-se*, *ol-sa*. With *-(y)Abil*: *gel-ebil-se* "if he could come".

**Step 3: add -(y)DI for the past unreal**: *gel-se-ydi*. The *y* is the buffer of the copula.

**Step 4: add the person ending, k-set**, after everything: *gelsem*, *gelsen*, *gelse*, *gelsek*, *gelseniz*, *gelseler*; *gelseydim*, *gelseydin*, *gelseydi*, *gelseydik*, *gelseydiniz*, *gelselerdi* (wiktionary). Note that 3PL *-lAr* comes after the verbal *-sA* (*gelseler*), but before the copular *-(y)sA* (*gelirlerse*, tur-06-10).

**Step 5: the main clause.** Unreal: aorist + *-DI* + person: *gelirdim*, *gelmezdim*, *yapabilirdim*. Hypothetical: plain aorist. For a firm plan that was cancelled, *-(y)AcAktI*: *Vaktimiz olsaydı size gelecektik* "If we'd had time, we were going to come to you" (goksel2005 §27.2.4).

**Stress.** Unlike the copular *-(y)sA*, the verbal *-sA* takes the stress: *gel-sé-ydi* against *gel-ír-se* (goksel2005 §4.3.2).

**Wishes.** *Keşke* (optional but usual) + *-sA* for now or the future, *-sAydI* for the past.

## Where it applies

- **Contrary to present fact**: *-sA* … *-(A/I)rdI*: *Zengin olsam gezerdim*.
- **Contrary to past fact**: *-sAydI* … *-(A/I)rdI*: *Erken çıksaydın kaçırmazdın*. *-sA* … *-(A/I)rdI* can also refer to the past, especially with *olmak* and *bilmek* (goksel2005 §27.2.3.2): *Bilsem söylerdim* "If I knew / had known, I'd tell / have told you".
- **A hypothesis being weighed**: *-sA* … aorist: *Taksiye binsek yetişir miyiz?*
- **Advice and polite suggestions**: *-sA iyi olur*; *Senin yerinde olsam*; *Ben olsam* "if it were me".
- **Wishes**: *keşke* + *-sA* (now, future), + *-sAydI* (past). *Keşke* can also come at the end in speech (goksel2005 §21.4.4.1).
- **"At least"**: *bari* with the imperative or *-sA*: *Bari telefon etseydin* "You could at least have phoned" (TDK *bari*).
- **First-person suggestions and deliberation**: *gitsek*, *gitsek mi?*, *Acaba ne yapsam?* "I wonder what I should do?" (goksel2005 §21.4.4.4).
- **Plans that did not happen**: *-sAydI* … *-(y)AcAktI*.
- **Reported unreal conditions**: *-sAymIş* … *-(y)mIş*, "apparently he would have …": *Vakti olsaymış gelirmiş* "He says he would have come if he'd had time" (goksel2005 §27.2.5). Recognise this; it is not common.

## Watch out

**Do not use the copular -(y)sA for an unreal condition.** *Zengin olursam* means "if I become rich", a real possibility. "If I were rich (but I'm not)" is *Zengin olsam*. Likewise *\*Zenginsem dünyayı gezerdim* mixes a real present condition with an unreal result.

**The main clause of an unreal condition needs the past.** *\*Erken çıksaydın treni kaçırmayacaksın* is wrong: the result is unreal too. Use *-(A/I)rdI*: *kaçırmazdın*. Spanish *habrías* and French *tu aurais* are a single "would" form; Turkish builds it as aorist + past.

**Do not put a tense before the verbal -sA.** *\*Geldisem*, *\*gelirsedim* do not exist. Unreal: *gelsem*, *gelseydim*.

**Do not confuse -sAydI with -DIysA.** *Geldiyse* (tur-06-10) is "if he has come (perhaps he has)"; *gelseydi* is "if he had come (he didn't)". The order of the pieces tells you which: tense then condition, or condition then past.

**keşke needs -sA.** *\*Keşke buradasın* and *\*Keşke geliyorsun* are wrong. *Keşke burada olsan*; *Keşke gelsen*.

**3PL after the verbal -sA.** *Gelseler* "if they came", *gelselerdi* "if they had come". Do not borrow the order of *gelirlerse*: *\*gellerse* is impossible.

**Stress the verbal -sA.** *gelsé*, *gelséydim*; the copular *-(y)sA* of *gelírse* is unstressed (goksel2005 §4.3.2). *\*Gélse*, with the stress on the stem, is a mispronunciation.

## Connections

Spanish and French also use a past form for unreal conditions: *si tuviera dinero, viajaría*; *si j'avais de l'argent, je voyagerais*. Turkish *-sA* matches the *si* clause, and *-(A/I)rdI* matches the conditional "would". Japanese もし…たら / ば with ～のに for regret, and Korean -았/었더라면, also mark unreality on the verb; *keşke* is close to Japanese ～ばよかった and Korean -(으)면 좋겠다 in use. The likeness is typological (dossier, section 6).

## Exercises

1. Say "If I had a car, I would drive to work."
2. Say "If you had called, I would have come."
3. Translate *Keşke Türkçeyi çocukken öğrenseydim.*
4. A learner writes *\*Zenginsem bir ada alırdım.* Correct it and explain.
5. Build "If they knew the truth, they would be angry" step by step.
6. Translate *Senin yerinde olsam ona her şeyi anlatırdım.*
7. Explain the difference between *Ali geldiyse* and *Ali gelseydi*.
8. Say "What if we stayed at home this evening?"
9. Translate *Bari bir mesaj gönderseydin!*
10. Translate *Cesaretim olsaydı ona sorardım, ama olmadı.*

## Worked answers

1. *Arabam olsa işe arabayla giderdim.* *Var* becomes *olsa*; main clause aorist + past for a present unreal condition (goksel2005 §27.2.3.2).
2. *Telefon etseydin gelirdim.* *-sAydI* for a past that did not happen; *-(A/I)rdI* in the main clause (goksel2005 §27.2.4).
3. "I wish I had learned Turkish as a child." *Keşke* + *-sAydI*: regret; *çocukken* "when (I was) a child" (tur-06-07).
4. *Zengin olsam bir ada alırdım.* The condition is unreal, so it needs the verbal *-sA* on *ol-*; *zenginsem* is a real present condition ("if I am rich") and clashes with *alırdım*.
5. Condition: *gerçeği bil-*; verbal *-sA*, 3PL after it: *gerçeği bilseler*. Main clause: *kız-* in aorist + past + 3PL: *kızarlardı*. *Gerçeği bilseler kızarlardı.*
6. "If I were you, I'd tell him everything." *Yer-in-de ol-sa-m*: "if I were in your place".
7. *Ali geldiyse*: "if Ali has come", a real past condition that may be true (tur-06-10). *Ali gelseydi*: "if Ali had come", which he did not.
8. *Bu akşam evde kalsak?* First-person verbal *-sA* as a suggestion (goksel2005 §21.4.4.1).
9. "You could at least have sent a message!" *Bari* "at least" with *-sAydI* for a reproachful wish about the past.
10. "If I'd had the courage, I'd have asked her, but I didn't." *Cesaretim olsaydı*: past unreal possession; *sorardım* "would have asked"; *olmadı* "it didn't happen / I didn't (have it)".
